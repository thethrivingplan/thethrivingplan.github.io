The Evidence Board

A detective game about proof, not hunches. Collect clues, interview suspects, and literally string the evidence together on the corkboard — dot to dot. An accusation only stands if the string you built can hold it up. The stakes in every case are the same: the truth, and clearing the wrongly accused. There is never any money in it.

Every case here is original fiction, written for this page. The country-house case is labelled fiction in big letters, contains no gore, and is a logic puzzle — the mystery is the point.

How to work a case

1 · Gather

Collect the physical clues at the scene and interview every suspect. Interviews add their statement cards to the board. A statement is a claim — not yet a fact.

2 · String it together

On the board, tap one card, then tap another to run string between them. Tap the pair again to cut the string. Hold a card to read it in full. Only connect what you can show connects.

3 · Accuse — with a chain

Pick a suspect in the Accuse panel and make the accusation. It stands only if your string links that suspect to the key evidence. A guess gets refused, and the refusal tells you why.

Consequences, stated up front

A wrong or unsupported accusation rattles an innocent person and costs the case: each one costs 2 energy (when the budget is on) and adds a strike. Three strikes and the case goes cold — witnesses stop talking and you must start the case over. The board never lies to save you.

Evidence vs assumption

A connection you can show (“the register has his name at 3:55”) is evidence. A connection you merely believe (“he seems shifty”) is an assumption. String is for the first kind only.

Motive is not proof

Motive says someone wanted it done. It never says they did it. Every case here plants a loud motive on an innocent person. Check their alibi before you string them up.

Timelines

Lay the times end to end. Most lies die on a timeline: a person cannot be carrying a violin case at 4:22 and “never near the boiler room” at the same time.

Alibi checking

An alibi is a claim until something independent backs it — a witness, a record, a camera. A backed alibi clears a suspect even when their motive screams.

Cases — in order, they ramp

Case 1 is a theft with 3 suspects. Case 2 adds a fourth suspect, an energy budget, and a trap about shared knowledge. Case 3 is the country-house murder mystery: 5 suspects, 11 pieces of evidence (6 at the scene, 5 statements), and the loudest motive in the drawer belongs to an innocent woman.
