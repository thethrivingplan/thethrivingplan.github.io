The Ruler's Test — Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, President of Brazil

Subject: Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva — President of Brazil 2003–2010 and January 1, 2023–present; won a fourth term in the October 25, 2026 runoff against Senator Flávio Bolsonaro (Reuters, Oct.–Nov. 2026 reporting; election coverage, Oct. 25, 2026). Graded: November 2026, on the documented public record. Method: Both shelves are shown for every test — the strongest evidence that he passes, then the strongest evidence that he fails. Allegations are labeled ALLEGATION and never stated as verdicts. Contested items are named as contested, with who disputes them. Seat-holders and public figures are named; people below the public eye appear by role only, unless the documented record itself establishes their wrongdoing. Grades: HOLDS / FAILS / MIXED / NOT ENOUGH EVIDENCE.

A note on his own case, stated exactly: Lula was convicted in the Operation Car Wash cases (2017, Judge Sérgio Moro), imprisoned April 2018–November 2019 (580 days), and barred from the 2018 election. The convictions were annulled in 2021: the STF ruled the Curitiba court had lacked jurisdiction, and separately ruled that Judge Moro had been biased in his case. The UN Human Rights Committee reached the same conclusion on Moro's bias in April 2022. Annulment is not an acquittal on the merits and not a conviction that stands — it is the record, stated both ways, and it is graded nowhere below except where the brief directs.

Test 1 — GIVES BY ONE LAW

Same rules for allies and opponents, insiders and outsiders; no favorites at the source.

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • The prosecution of the opposing camp ran through institutions, not his hand. The coup-plot case against former President Jair Bolsonaro was investigated by the Federal Police, charged by the Prosecutor-General, and tried by the Supreme Court's First Panel, which convicted Bolsonaro on September 11, 2025 and sentenced him to 27 years and 3 months; the series of coup trials produced 29 convictions in total, including senior military officers (AP, Sept. 11–12, 2025; International IDEA democracy tracker, Sept.–Dec. 2025). Lula neither directed the case nor pardoned anyone in it; when Congress passed a "Dosimetry Bill" in December 2025 to cut the January 8 convicts' sentences — a bill that would have benefited Bolsonaro directly — Lula publicly pledged to veto it and chose January 8, 2026, the anniversary of the attacks, to do so (Reuters, Sept. 18, 2025; MercoPress, Dec. 18, 2025; presidential statements, Dec. 2025).
  • The law reached inside his own government, on his watch. In April 2025 his Communications Minister stepped down after being charged with bribery (charges concerning 2022 conduct; the case is an ALLEGATION in process, not a verdict) (Reuters via Pension Policy International, May 5, 2025). In April–May 2025, a Federal Police and Comptroller-General operation (Operation No Discount) exposed the INSS pension-fraud scheme — an estimated R$6.3 billion in unauthorized deductions from pensioners, begun in 2019 under Bolsonaro but with 64% of the losses falling in 2023–24, under Lula — and Lula ordered the INSS president dismissed; his Social Security Minister resigned days later (Reuters, Apr.–May 2025; INSS fraud scheme record, Federal Police/CGU figures). No documented instance exists of Lula ordering either investigation stopped.
  • Historically, the same judiciary prosecuted his own party when he led it: the mensalão (vote-buying) trial of 2012 convicted senior Workers' Party figures, including his former chief of staff, in a Supreme Court proceeding; the petrolão (Petrobras) scheme likewise produced convictions of party officials. Those prosecutions were not his doing, but they were not blocked by him either — the law, in those years, reached his own house (STF mensalão judgment, 2012; Car Wash court records).

Evidence against:

  • He placed his own personal lawyer on the court that judges everything. In June 2023 Lula appointed Cristiano Zanin — the lawyer who had led his Car Wash defense and won the annulment of his convictions — to the Supreme Court, over opposition criticism that the relationship posed a conflict of interest; the Senate confirmed him by a large majority. Lula had himself, as a candidate, criticized exactly this practice: "trying to meddle with the Supreme Court to place a friend, to place a partner, to place a supporter is a setback." He then publicly shifted his description of Zanin from "my friend" (March 2023) to "he was not a friend, he was my lawyer" (July 2023) (AP reporting, June–Aug. 2023). He also appointed his sitting Justice Minister, Flávio Dino, to the Court in 2024 — a second appointment of a close political associate through the constitutional process (administration announcements; Senate confirmation records).
  • The legacy cuts the other way as well: the mensalão and petrolão schemes themselves operated under Workers' Party governments he led or founded. The documented record of those scandals — convictions of party treasurers, a chief of staff, and Petrobras directors — is the standing counterweight to any claim that his movement policed itself from inside (STF and Car Wash court records, 2012–2019).
  • Public judgment is part of the record, labeled as polling, not fact: in the first Quaest poll after the 2026 election, 57% of respondents described Lula as "not honest," against 34% who said he is (Quaest, Oct. 30–Nov. 2, 2026).

Test 2 — HOLDS WITHOUT GRIPPING

Do Congress, the courts, the states, the media, and the military keep their autonomy — or is independence punished and control centralized?

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • Congress beats him in the open, regularly, and he governs anyway. The legislature overrode most of his vetoes of the marco temporal (Time Limit) Indigenous-lands law in December 2023 (Legal 500 / law record, Dec. 14, 2023); it diluted his December 2024 spending-cut package from ~R$70 billion to ~R$50 billion in savings (Senate Independent Fiscal Institution analysis; market reporting, Dec. 2024); it passed the Dosimetry Bill over his opposition (Senate, Dec. 17, 2025, 48–25). He responded with vetoes and negotiation, not with any documented move against the institution.
  • The courts rule against his preferences and stand. The Supreme Court struck down the marco temporal law as unconstitutional in December 2025 — a ruling that rescued his veto's substance after Congress had overridden it, from a court he does not control in any documented operational sense (Le Monde, Oct. 31, 2026, summarizing the STF ruling). The 2024 suspension of X in Brazil — often attributed abroad to "Lula's Brazil" — was a Supreme Court order by Justice Alexandre de Moraes, not an act of his government; attribution matters, and the record assigns it to the judiciary (STF orders, Aug.–Oct. 2024).
  • The central bank kept its statutory autonomy against his open displeasure. Lula publicly attacked the bank's interest-rate policy and its Bolsonaro-appointed president for two years, but he did not move against the 2021 autonomy law; his own appointee took the bank's presidency through the normal succession in January 2025, and his 2026 campaign pledged no change to central-bank autonomy (central bank record; campaign platform reporting, Sept. 2026).
  • The military was returned to its barracks role without a purge: after January 8, 2023, he dismissed the army commander who had resisted removing protester camps — an exercise of ordinary presidential authority — and the armed forces accepted the civilian chain of command throughout the coup trials that convicted their own generals (presidential acts, Jan. 2023; STF coup-trial record, 2025).

Evidence against:

  • His two Supreme Court appointments (Test 1) are also grip evidence: a personal lawyer and a sitting minister are the appointments of a president comfortable blurring the line between his circle and the institution meant to check him, whatever the appointees' qualifications (AP, 2023; Senate records).
  • He pressed a regulator to bend on his signature contradiction. Le Monde's review of the term documents that Lula "exerted heavy pressure on Ibama" — the federal environmental agency — to authorize Petrobras oil exploration at the mouth of the Amazon River, succeeding in October 2025 against the agency's earlier technical refusal (Le Monde, Oct. 31, 2026).
  • State-company independence bent once, visibly: in May 2024 he replaced Petrobras's CEO with a closer ally after a dispute over dividends and investment direction — lawful presidential power over a state-controlled company, exercised in a direction markets read as intervention (Reuters, May 2024).

Test 3 — ANSWERS THE WOBBLE

When courts, voters, Congress, or events push back, does he adjust openly — or deny and double down?

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • On the largest external shock of the term, he absorbed the blow and negotiated rather than capitulated or escalated. When President Trump imposed a 50% tariff on Brazilian goods in July 2025, explicitly tying it to the Bolsonaro prosecution and demanding it end, Lula refused the judicial interference publicly — "Brazil is a sovereign country with independent institutions… We don't want an emperor" — while declining immediate retaliation and pursuing negotiation, meeting Trump in Kuala Lumpur in October 2025 (Trump tariff letter, July 9, 2025; Lula statements, July 2025; Brazil–U.S. dispute record, 2025).
  • He adjusted his fiscal course under market and congressional pressure, twice: the December 2024 spending-cut package after the real's slide, and — days after his 2026 re-election — authorizing a second package of measures to curb mandatory spending, which Congress's lower house approved in November 2026 (Reuters, Nov. 24, 2026).
  • He accepts institutional defeats as final. His vetoes were overridden on marco temporal; his answer was to let the constitutional process run to the STF, which settled it — not to attack the Court or the Congress that beat him (law record, 2023–2025).
  • His Ukraine rhetoric softened in the open: the 2022–23 framing that Kyiv shared responsibility for the war — which drew formal Ukrainian and Western criticism — was, in the Inter-American Dialogue's assessment, "substantially softened" as his diplomacy proceeded (Inter-American Dialogue analysis, 2023; EULAS network review, 2026).

Evidence against:

  • The INSS scandal showed the slow, blame-shifting version of him. The Social Security Minister had been alerted to the pension-fraud complaints in 2023; the government's public response when the scheme broke in April 2025 was widely judged — including by poll respondents, half of whom said the government acted too slowly — as more concerned with establishing that the fraud began under Bolsonaro than with the pensioners. The minister's resignation came nine days after the police operation, not before it (Irish Times, May 15, 2025; AtlasIntel polling, May 2025).
  • On January 8 accountability, his line never moved an inch in either direction — no amnesty, ever, restated at every pressure point including a fast-tracked congressional amnesty push in September 2025 and street mobilization on both sides. Consistency is recorded here on both shelves: his supporters read it as principle; the convicted rioters' families and the opposition read the refusal of any sentence relief for rank-and-file defendants — many of them people below the public eye convicted of the January 8 attacks — as a wobble refused (Reuters, Sept. 18, 2025; Senate record, Dec. 2025). CONTESTED: whether the STF's January 8 sentences were proportionate remains disputed between the Court's defenders and the opposition.
  • His environmental record contains an unabsorbed contradiction he has never publicly reconciled: record-low deforestation (Test 5) alongside his personal pressure for Amazon-mouth oil drilling and his October 23, 2026 debate boast about advancing the BR-319 highway through the rainforest (Le Monde, Oct. 31, 2026).

Test 4 — COUNTS EVERYONE

Does the accounting include the whole flock — the poor, Indigenous peoples, Afro-Brazilians, business, his opponents' voters — or are some counted out?

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • The poor are counted first, and measurably. He restored Bolsa Família in 2023 at a R$600 minimum plus R$150 per young child, rebuilt the Food Acquisition Programme and the national food-security council (Consea, abolished under Bolsonaro, reinstated 2023), and in July 2025 the FAO removed Brazil from the UN Hunger Map — undernourishment below 2.5% — two years after 33.1 million Brazilians had been counted in hunger (FAO SOFI report, July 28, 2025; PENSSAN network count, 2022). Severe food insecurity fell from 6.6% (2021–23) to 0.6% (2023–25) on FAO figures.
  • Indigenous peoples received institutional form, not only words: he created Brazil's first Ministry of Indigenous Peoples, led by an Indigenous minister, resumed land demarcations stalled under his predecessor, launched the federal operation against illegal mining in Yanomami territory, and vetoed the core of the farm lobby's marco temporal bill in October 2023 (Reuters, Oct. 20, 2023).
  • Business is inside the count in practice: agribusiness GDP grew more than 17% across the term on Climate Observatory figures, exports set records, and unemployment reached a year-low 5.3% in 2026 — his model taxed dividends (a new dividend tax took effect January 2026) while the productive sectors he is accused of opposing expanded (Climate Observatory, Oct. 2026; Reuters tax-revenue reporting, Nov. 2026; Kalshi/Reuters election analysis, Sept. 2026).

Evidence against:

  • The Indigenous count is undercut by his own hand on the two largest Amazon decisions of the term: the oil license at the river's mouth (October 2025) and the BR-319 highway, both pressed over Indigenous and environmental objection (Le Monde, Oct. 31, 2026). And his marco temporal veto was overridden — the protection Indigenous peoples actually received came from the STF in December 2025, not from his office's power.
  • Nearly half the country experiences itself as outside his count, and the division is documented rather than alleged: 81% of voters in the post-election Quaest poll said Brazil emerged from the 2026 election more divided, and 42.2% of AtlasIntel respondents said they believed there was electoral fraud in the runoff he won — a belief for which no evidence has been produced and which election authorities reject, recorded here because a ruler's count includes whether the losing side believes it was counted at all (Quaest, Nov. 8, 2026; AtlasIntel/Bloomberg, Nov. 5–9, 2026). The fraud belief is an ALLEGATION without supporting evidence; the breadth of the belief is a documented fact.
  • The pensioners defrauded in the INSS scheme — among the poorest people in his count — were failed by his government's slow detection on his watch: 64% of the R$6.3 billion in losses fell in 2023–24 (Federal Police/CGU figures, 2025).

Test 5 — KEEPS HIS WORD OVER TIME

Signature promises vs. the record, with dates.

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • Hunger — his first promise, kept on the measure he chose. In 2022 he pledged to end hunger and guarantee three meals a day; Brazil left the UN Hunger Map in July 2025, as it had previously done in 2014 at the end of the policy arc his first governments built (FAO, July 2025).
  • The Amazon — kept, and then some. He promised to end illegal deforestation by 2030 and revived the PPCDAm action plan in January 2023. INPE's official satellite record: Amazon deforestation fell from 11,594 km² (2022, Bolsonaro's last year) to 3,335 km² (Aug. 2025–July 2026) — a 71% decline and the lowest annual figure since monitoring began in 1988; the Cerrado also hit its record low (INPE/PRODES data released Oct. 16, 2026).
  • The 2026 electorate audited the record and returned him: on October 25, 2026 he won the runoff with roughly 51.8% of valid votes, becoming the first Brazilian elected president four times (election results, Oct. 25, 2026).

Evidence against:

  • Fiscal word vs. fiscal record. His 2023 fiscal framework promised a trajectory to a 1% primary surplus by 2026; the targets were pursued mainly through revenue, revised in practice, and gross public debt rose more than 11 percentage points on his watch to 82.9% of GDP, with an interest bill near 8.7% of GDP — the gap his own November 2026 corrective package implicitly concedes (Reuters, Nov. 24, 2026; Senate Independent Fiscal Institution, Nov. 2024).
  • The Amazon's other ledger. "Zero illegal deforestation by 2030" stands on the deforestation numbers; against it stand his administration's pursuit of oil at the Amazon's mouth (license granted October 2025 after his pressure on the regulator) and BR-319 — commitments to a different future for the same forest, made by the same man (Le Monde, Oct. 31, 2026).
  • Ukraine: the "peace club" was promised as Brazilian-led mediation (February 2023); it produced a Brazil–China six-point framework that omitted Ukrainian territorial integrity, which President Zelensky called "destructive," and no negotiation followed. By 2025–26 Brazil was abstaining on every UN Ukraine resolution. The promise of an active peace role narrowed, in practice, to abstention (EULAS network review, 2026; UN voting record).
  • The one-term suggestion. Through 2022 he repeatedly suggested a third term would be his last, citing his age; in 2026 he ran again and won a fourth term, which begins January 2027 and would end days after he turns 85 (2022 campaign statements; election record, Oct. 2026).

Overall Summary

One law: MIXED · Without gripping: MIXED · The wobble: MIXED · Counts everyone: MIXED · His word: MIXED.

Lula's record refuses both available caricatures. The documented core is real: hunger measurably beaten twice in one lifetime, the Amazon's destruction cut 71% to its lowest recorded level, a coup prosecuted through courts rather than by decree, and a Congress and central bank that beat him regularly and stood. Against it stand his own hand's marks: his personal lawyer placed on the Supreme Court after he had condemned that very practice, a pension-fraud scheme that grew on his watch and was answered slowly, a debt that climbed eleven points past his own framework's promises, and oil sought at the mouth of the river whose forest he saved. The system applied to him lands where it lands: nothing fails outright, and nothing holds clean.
