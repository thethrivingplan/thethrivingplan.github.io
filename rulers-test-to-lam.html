The Ruler's Test — Tô Lâm, General Secretary of the Communist Party of Vietnam and President of Vietnam

Subject: Tô Lâm — General Secretary of the Communist Party of Vietnam (CPV) since 3 August 2024, succeeding Nguyễn Phú Trọng on his death; unanimously re-elected for a full five-year term at the 14th National Party Congress on 23 January 2026. President of Vietnam from 22 May 2024 to October 2024, when he relinquished the state presidency to Lương Cường; elected President again by the National Assembly on 7 April 2026 (495 of 495 deputies voting in favour), and as of November 2026 holds the party leadership and the state presidency concurrently for the 2026–2031 term — a dual tenure without precedent in post-war Vietnam outside his own brief 2024 overlap. Before that he was Minister of Public Security from 2016 to 2024, the office that ran the investigative machinery of the "blazing furnace" anti-corruption campaign. Because Vietnam is a one-party state in which the general secretaryship is the seat of real power, he is graded as the country's paramount leader, and "opponents" in these tests means rival factions and predecessors' networks inside the Party, and ordinary citizens measured against officials. Graded: November 2026, on the documented public record. Method: Each test is graded only on the evidence listed under it. Both shelves are shown — the strongest evidence that he passes, then the strongest evidence that he fails. Allegations are labeled ALLEGATION and never stated as verdicts. Contested items are named as contested, with who disputes them. Seat-holders and public figures are named; people below the public eye appear by role only, unless the documented record itself establishes their wrongdoing. Grades: HOLDS / FAILS / MIXED / NOT ENOUGH EVIDENCE. This record is correctable: new evidence changes grades, in the open.

Test 1 — GIVES BY ONE LAW

Same rules for allies and opponents, insiders and outsiders — no favorites at the source. In a one-party state: does discipline and prosecution fall evenly across factions and predecessors' networks, and does the law treat the official and the citizen by the same measure?

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • Party discipline under his leadership reached the highest figures in the country's modern history, and it reached men who had already left office. On 13 December 2024 the Politburo issued warnings to former Prime Minister and President Nguyễn Xuân Phúc and former Standing Deputy Prime Minister Trương Hòa Bình, and a reprimand to former Politburo member Trương Thị Mai; a warning to former National Assembly Chairman Vương Đình Huệ had been issued the previous month (Politburo decisions as reported by the government portal, November–December 2024). On 18–19 July 2025 the 13th Central Committee, meeting under his chairmanship, went further: Phúc, former President Võ Văn Thượng, Huệ and former Deputy Prime Minister Lê Minh Khải were removed from all Party positions, and former Health Minister Nguyễn Thị Kim Tiến was expelled from the Party (12th Plenum announcement, VNA, 19 July 2025). No former president of Vietnam had previously been stripped of every party post.
  • The criminal process continued to convict senior officials below the Politburo line across regions and networks: in the Phúc Sơn Group case, former provincial chairmen and vice-chairmen of Vĩnh Phúc, Phú Thọ and Quảng Ngãi were convicted of bribery and bid-rigging, with sentences adjusted on appeal in 2025 only after the court recorded that losses exceeding 1.1 trillion đồng had been fully remedied (Supreme People's Court appeal ruling, as reported, 2025); in January 2026 a Hanoi court sentenced 55 defendants in the Ministry of Health food-safety bribery network, including a former food administration director jailed for 20 years (court reporting, January 2026).
  • One major legal reform of his tenure was written as a general rule and applied to everyone it covered, friend and stranger alike: on 25 June 2025 the National Assembly voted 429 of 439 to abolish the death penalty for eight offences — embezzlement, bribery, espionage, drug transport, producing counterfeit medicine, sabotaging state property, undermining peace and activities aimed at overthrowing the government — cutting capital offences from 18 to 10 from 1 July 2025, with all existing death sentences for those offences converted to life imprisonment (Law No. 86/2025/QH15; UN OHCHR statement welcoming the vote, 27 June 2025). The change spared, among hundreds of others, property tycoon Trương Mỹ Lan, whose April 2024 death sentence for embezzling some $12.5 billion from Saigon Commercial Bank had been upheld on appeal on 3 December 2024; her lawyer confirmed the sentence converts to life under the general law (AFP/AP reporting, June 2025).

Evidence against:

  • At the very top, the instrument that fell on the leaders was Party discipline, not law. Phúc, Thượng, Huệ, Khải and Bình were warned, reprimanded or stripped of party posts; none has been criminally charged in the documented record through November 2026. Since đổi mới began in 1986, exactly one Politburo member — Đinh La Thăng, in 2018 — has ever been criminally prosecuted, and that was under Trọng, not Lâm. Reporting in December 2024 that Phúc "looks set to face criminal prosecution" over the Đại Ninh eco-tourism investigation (Radio Free Asia, analyst commentary by Zachary Abuza, 29 December 2024) has not been followed by any documented indictment; the specific claim that Phúc received a $3 million bribe in that case is an ALLEGATION carried in that commentary, is not the finding of any court, and is recorded here as unadjudicated.
  • The pattern of who fell is CONTESTED, and the contest is on the record. Analysts including Abuza and David Hutt have documented that the officials removed in the 2022–2025 sequence were drawn overwhelmingly from networks other than Lâm's own Ministry of Public Security, and describe the campaign, in Abuza's words, as the "weaponization of the Ministry of Public Security to force the resignation of rivals" (VOA and RFA analyst reporting, 2024). The Party's stated position is that discipline follows the Central Inspection Commission's findings regardless of faction. Both characterizations are printed here; the undisputed fact underneath them is that the investigative machinery that produced the files — the Ministry of Public Security and the inspection commissions — was the apparatus Lâm himself led or now chairs: he heads the Central Steering Committee for Prevention and Control of Corruption, Wastefulness and Negative Phenomena.
  • The law's measure is not the same for the citizen as for the official. While leaders' violations are handled by negotiated resignation and internal discipline, citizens who criticise officials face criminal process: between 2018 and February 2025 courts sentenced at least 124 people under Penal Code Article 331 ("infringing on the interests of the state"), and at least 32 more in 2025 alone, including people who had raised corruption, land and religious-freedom grievances on social media (Human Rights Watch, April 2025 report and World Report 2026). One law at the top is a party rulebook; one law at the bottom is a prison sentence.
  • The "setting an example" standard was enforced against others and never applied to his own best-documented lapse. Phúc and Huệ were disciplined in part for breaching the regulation on exemplary conduct by senior officials. In November 2021, while Vietnam was under COVID-19 lockdown hardship and Lâm was in Britain for the COP26 summit, a video showed the celebrity chef Nusret Gökçe ("Salt Bae") hand-feeding him a gold-encrusted steak at his London restaurant, where such a steak was priced at about £1,450; the video went viral, public anger was widely reported, and no statement, apology or disciplinary process concerning Lâm appears anywhere in the documented record (Reuters, BBC and HRW reporting, November 2021 and after). The episode's criminal sequel is graded under Test 4.

Test 2 — HOLDS WITHOUT GRIPPING

Do the courts, media, the National Assembly, provincial governments and the state bank keep what autonomy the system allows — or is independence punished and control centralized further?

GRADE: FAILS

Evidence for:

  • For the first fourteen months of his party leadership the formal distribution of power held. In October 2024 Lâm gave up the state presidency to Lương Cường, leaving the "four pillars" — general secretary, president, prime minister, National Assembly chairman — in four different hands, and the pillars other than his own were filled by figures from outside his ministry: Prime Minister Phạm Minh Chính (until April 2026) and Assembly Chairman Trần Thanh Mẫn.
  • The counter-analysis exists and is serious: East Asia Forum's June 2026 assessment ("Vietnam is not China in the making") argues that factional balance, the military's institutional weight and the Central Committee's collective forms still constrain him, and that his re-election and presidency were transacted through those forms — unanimous Central Committee and Assembly votes — rather than imposed outside them (East Asia Forum, 19 June 2026). The National Assembly did debate and amend major legislation in this period, and the June 2025 constitutional amendment and penal-code reform passed through it as law, not decree.
  • No judge, editor, central banker or provincial leader is documented as having been removed for ruling, publishing or deciding against his wishes; the State Bank's governorship did not change hands as a purge, and the courts' commutation of existing death sentences under the June 2025 law was carried out by the Supreme People's Court as a legal act.

Evidence against:

  • The seat itself was doubled. On 7 April 2026 the 16th National Assembly elected him President for 2026–2031 with 495 of 495 votes, making him the first leader in post-war Vietnam to hold the general secretaryship and the presidency together for a full term — a break with the collective-leadership norm that the Associated Press, Al Jazeera and regional analysts all recorded as the central fact of the vote (AP, 7 April 2026). With the presidency he also chairs the Council for National Defence and Security; he was already Secretary of the Central Military Commission.
  • His own ministry grew on his watch, at the expense of others. The February 2025 restructuring of government — ministries cut from 18 to 14 and government agencies from 8 to 5, effective 1 March 2025 — transferred additional state functions to the Ministry of Public Security, including driver testing and licensing, criminal-record certification and aviation security, and placed the state telecom operator MobiFone under the ministry (government restructuring decisions, February 2025). Human Rights Watch's World Report 2026 records the conclusion plainly: the 2025 restructuring "further enhanced the already considerable power of the Ministry of Public Security, an agency active in the government's harsh repression of dissidents."
  • The personnel pattern matches the institutional one. Analysts counted six of the fifteen Politburo members as coming from the public-security apparatus by late 2024 (David Hutt, RFA commentary, September 2024); his protégé Lương Tam Quang succeeded him as Minister of Public Security in June 2024 and entered the Politburo; and the prime minister elected in April 2026, Lê Minh Hùng, is a former central bank governor and party organisation chief from a public-security family background, identified across the analysis as Lâm's ally. These are analysts' characterizations of documented appointments; the appointments themselves are not disputed.
  • The local tier that might have checked the centre was halved and shortened. On 1 July 2025 Vietnam merged its 63 provinces and centrally-run cities into 34 and abolished the district level of government entirely, moving to a two-tier (province–commune) system, dissolving roughly two-thirds of wards and communes (National Assembly resolutions, June 2025; constitutional amendment, June 2025). Whatever its administrative merits (graded as policy under Tests 3 and 5), as a distribution-of-power fact it removed an entire elected tier and half the provincial leadership posts through which regional figures rose to national office (East Asia Forum, 3 September 2025).
  • The judiciary and press kept no autonomy to lose, and none was conceded. Human Rights Watch records that Vietnam's courts operate under Communist Party control and "routinely deny defendants their due process rights" (World Report 2026); all media are state-licensed; and the unanimity of the decisive votes — 495 of 495 for his presidency, a unanimous Central Committee re-election on 23 January 2026 — is itself the documented measure of how much independent weight the checking bodies brought to the questions put to them.

Test 3 — ANSWERS THE WOBBLE

When economic actors, foreign partners, the public or events push back, does he adjust openly — or deny and double down?

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • Faced with the largest external shock of his tenure, he moved first and moved fast. On 2 April 2025 the United States announced a 46% "reciprocal" tariff on Vietnamese goods, among the highest rates in the world. On 4 April 2025 Lâm spoke directly with President Trump — among the first world leaders to call — and said Vietnam was ready to cut its tariffs on US goods to zero (Reuters, 4 April 2025). The negotiation he opened produced the 2 July 2025 framework: a 20% US tariff on Vietnamese goods, 40% on transshipped goods, and zero tariffs for US goods entering Vietnam (announcements by both governments, 2 July 2025). Vietnam absorbed the terms rather than retaliate; exports to the US nonetheless rose by nearly 28% in 2025, and the reciprocal trade agreement remained under negotiation through seven rounds as of October 2026 (Ministry of Industry and Trade statements).
  • The administrative revolution was run with its costs acknowledged and paid, not denied. The streamlining he announced in November 2024 — in his own words a "revolution" in the apparatus — was implemented on the announced date of 1 July 2025 after public consultations on the provincial mergers; by late 2025 about 145,000–146,800 public employees had left their posts, and the state spent roughly 130 trillion đồng (about US$5 billion) in 2025 on severance and benefit packages for them (Ministry of Home Affairs figures, as reported). A wobble answered by denying the displaced existed would have been cheaper; the record shows compensation instead.
  • On the anti-corruption drive's economic side-effects, he adjusted the message and the method in public: from his first meetings as party chief in August 2024 he assured officials and business that the campaign would not be allowed to stall economic work, and the reform program explicitly reframed anti-corruption from high-profile crackdown toward prevention through institutional redesign (East Asia Forum, 3 September 2025).
  • One adjustment arrived late but is documented as underway: on 17 November 2026 the Traffic Police Department announced it is studying a replacement for Decree 168 that would warn, rather than heavily fine, first-time red-light offenders who stopped late or turned without causing danger, while keeping full penalties for deliberate violations (Traffic Police Department statement, 17 November 2026).

Evidence against:

  • Decree 168 is the domestic wobble he refused for nearly two years. The decree, effective 1 January 2025, multiplied traffic fines overnight — running a red light in a car rose from 4 million to 18–20 million đồng — and produced widespread public anger among the country's millions of riders, documented traffic paralysis, and open criticism that the fines were calibrated to revenue rather than safety (RFA and domestic reporting, January–February 2025). The response from the apparatus he built was not adjustment but discipline of the critics: a truck driver who posted a TikTok asking opponents of the draft decree to share their thoughts was fined 5 million đồng for "fabricated information," and a Hanoi resident was fined 7.5 million đồng for critical Facebook comments (Ministry of Public Security reporting via RFA, January 2025). The November 2026 softening study comes twenty-two months after the anger, and only as a study.
  • Pushback from below is answered with prosecution as a matter of pattern, not exception. In 2025 Vietnam held more than 160 political prisoners and arrested at least 40 more people for criticising the government; in December 2025 courts convicted five journalists and dissidents under Article 117 in what Human Rights Watch described as hastily convened trials, two of them in absentia (HRW World Report 2026). A leadership that adjusts openly does not need the December docket.
  • On the golden-steak video of November 2021 — the single most widely discussed public rebuke of his own conduct — the documented response, then and since, is silence: no statement, no accounting, and the video suppressed from domestic platforms while the parodist went to prison (see Test 4). Denial by erasure is the opposite of answering.
  • Where a foreign court's protection stood against his security apparatus's demand, his government pressed to the end: Montagnard activist Y Quynh Bdap, a UNHCR-recognised refugee in Thailand convicted in absentia in Vietnam of terrorism, was ordered extradited by a Thai court on 28 November 2025 despite the documented risk of torture, and was returned to Vietnam, where his whereabouts were unknown at last reporting (USCIRF FoRB database; Front Line Defenders, November–December 2025). The terrorism conviction is Vietnam's stated position; Bdap and his organisation deny involvement in violence, and no open trial tested the charge. The extradition itself is graded here as the refusal to absorb an external check.

Test 4 — COUNTS EVERYONE

Does the accounting include ethnic minorities, religious communities, workers, the poor and dissenters — or are some counted out?

GRADE: FAILS

Evidence for:

  • The material count genuinely widened, and the numbers are official and large. On 28 February 2025 the Politburo decided to waive all tuition fees in public schools from kindergarten through high school; the National Assembly approved it in June 2025 and from September 2025 more than 23 million students attend public school tuition-free, at a state cost of roughly 30 trillion đồng (about US$1.2 billion) a year, with subsidies extended to private-school pupils (Politburo decision, 28 February 2025; National Assembly resolution, June 2025). The same programme of resolutions set health goals in the same direction: Resolution 72 (September 2025) commits the system toward free basic health services, with Lâm himself pressing in March 2025 for hospital fees to be exempted by 2030.
  • Poverty, on the state's own multidimensional measure, fell to 1.93% of households in 2024 — about 600,000 households, down from 58.1% in 1993 — and per-capita GDP reached US$5,026 in 2025, moving Vietnam into the upper-middle-income group (VNA/MOLISA figures; National Statistics Office, January 2026). The 145,000-plus officials displaced by his streamlining were counted too, in the limited sense that their severance was budgeted and paid rather than their existence denied (Test 3).
  • Growth itself was broadly shared in its headline form: 297,500 enterprises were newly established or resumed operations in 2025, up 27.4% (National Statistics Office), and the private economy his Resolution 68 champions employs about 82% of the workforce.

Evidence against:

  • Ethnic and religious minorities are counted out by prosecution. Vietnam's Montagnard (Central Highlands) Christians face documented, systematic pressure to abandon independent house churches for the state-recognised Evangelical Church of Vietnam–South: USCIRF's record for 2024–2025 includes Montagnard believers sentenced to 4.5 and 13 years, a believer found dead after complying with a police summons, pastors arrested in October 2025 for leading an unregistered Montagnard church, and Khmer Krom Theravada Buddhist monks sentenced on 26 November 2024 to between two and six years after declaring independence from the state-controlled Buddhist church (Vietnam FORB Roundtable joint letter, 31 January 2025; USCIRF FoRB victims database, 2025). The government's position is that it protects freedom of belief and acts only against lawbreakers; the documented pattern of sentences for unregistered worship stands against the claim.
  • The external referees' verdict on religion worsened on his watch and he did not answer it. In its 2026 Annual Report (covering 2025), USCIRF recommended — again — that Vietnam be designated a Country of Particular Concern for "systematic, ongoing, and egregious" violations; the State Department has kept Vietnam on its Special Watch List since December 2023 (USCIRF, March 2026; US State Department designations). Y Quynh Bdap's extradition (Test 3) is the same file at its sharpest: a refugee from the Montagnard minority, returned to a 10-year in-absentia sentence, whereabouts unknown.
  • Dissenters are counted out by definition. More than 160 political prisoners were held in 2025; at least 124 people had been sentenced under Article 331 between 2018 and February 2025 and at least 32 more were sentenced in 2025, among them journalist Trương Huy San (Huy Đức) — arrested 1 June 2024 and sentenced on 27 February 2025 to 30 months for thirteen Facebook posts — and lawyer Trần Đình Triển, sentenced in January 2025 to three years after criticising the then chief justice (HRW, April 2025 and World Report 2026; CPJ, 27 February 2025).
  • The noodle vendor is the count in miniature. Bùi Tuấn Lâm, a Đà Nẵng beef-noodle seller, posted a parody video in November 2021 sprinkling green onions in imitation of Salt Bae, days after the video of Minister Tô Lâm's gold-encrusted steak caused public outrage. He was summoned by police that month, arrested in September 2022, and on 25 May 2023 sentenced to five and a half years in prison plus four years' probation under Article 117 for "propaganda against the state"; the appeal court upheld the sentence in August 2023. The ministry that arrested him was Tô Lâm's. In December 2025 Amnesty International issued an urgent action reporting that since April 2025 he has been held in solitary confinement without clean water, electricity or light, and was shackled for ten days after a hunger strike (Amnesty International Urgent Action ASA 41/0526/2025, 12 December 2025; HRW, 31 May 2023; Reuters, 25 May 2023). A citizen who mocks the ruler's dinner is, on this record, counted as an enemy of the state.
  • Workers remain counted as a resource, not as a voice: independent trade unions remain prohibited, and Vietnam again failed in 2025 to ratify ILO Convention 87 on freedom of association despite having pledged to do so (HRW World Report 2026).

Test 5 — KEEPS HIS WORD OVER TIME

Stated promises and positions vs. the record since 2024 — and his tenure as Minister of Public Security, 2016–2024, where it bears on the tests — with dates.

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for (promises kept or credibly in motion):

  • The 8% promise — kept, on the official count. His government set a minimum 8% GDP growth target for 2025 (Government Resolution 25/NQ-CP) above the National Assembly's more cautious range, and the National Statistics Office reported 2025 growth of 8.02% — the second-highest rate since 2011 — with GDP of US$514 billion (NSO, 5 January 2026).
  • The streamlining "revolution" — delivered on the date named. He announced the apparatus overhaul in November 2024 as a revolution to be executed before the Party Congress; the cabinet was cut on 1 March 2025 and the two-tier local system, with 34 provinces, took effect on 1 July 2025, the date the plan had set (National Assembly resolutions, February and June 2025).
  • The private-sector word — kept in policy. Resolution 68-NQ/TW, issued by the Politburo on 4 May 2025, declares the private economy "one of the most important driving forces of the national economy" — reversing decades of state-sector-first doctrine — and targets 2 million enterprises and 55–58% of GDP from the sector by 2030; in October 2026 Regulation 224 went further, encouraging Party members outside state employment to run businesses (Resolution 68 full text, 4 May 2025; VietnamPlus reporting on Regulation 224, October 2026). Enterprise formation rose 27.4% in 2025.
  • Free tuition — promised February 2025, in force September 2025 (Test 4), and the death-penalty reduction he championed as legal modernisation was passed and applied retroactively as written (Test 1).

Evidence against (promises not kept, receding, or kept only in the letter):

  • The double-digit promise is running behind its own arithmetic. The 14th Congress (January 2026) adopted his target of at least 10% average annual growth for 2026–2030, and the government set at least 10% for 2026. Official figures put the first nine months of 2026 at 9.01% (Q1 8.15%, Q2 8.81%, Q3 an estimated 9.95%), and the IMF's October 2026 projection is 8.4% for the year, the World Bank's 7.4% — both below the promise, with the full-year verdict still open (NSO quarterly releases; IMF World Economic Outlook, October 2026). The 2045 high-income pledge that frames the target is, at this distance, a direction rather than a testable word.
  • The collective-leadership word was kept for fourteen months. In October 2024 he relinquished the presidency in the name of the Party's collective arrangement, and Party statements through 2025 praised the four-pillar distribution as the system's safeguard. In April 2026 he took the presidency back for a full term alongside the general secretaryship — the arrangement he had been praised for vacating (AP, 7 April 2026). No public statement in the record explains the change in terms of the principle previously stated.
  • The anti-corruption word and the anti-corruption instrument diverge. He pledged the furnace would burn with "no forbidden zones." The discipline record at party level supports the pledge (Test 1); but no sitting or former Politburo member has faced a criminal court under him, the campaign's files are produced by the security ministry he led for eight years, and named analysts (Abuza; Hutt) record their assessment that the drive has tracked factional lines. The pledge is graded here as kept in rhetoric and in party discipline, unproven in law.
  • The security-ministry record is part of his word's history, and it does not match the exemplary-official standard he enforces on others. As Minister of Public Security (2016–2024) he presided over the apparatus that arrested the parodist Bùi Tuấn Lâm and that German proceedings touched in the 2017 Trịnh Xuân Thanh affair — a Vietnamese agent was convicted in a Berlin court in 2018 of aiding an abduction from German soil; Vietnam officially denied state involvement, and Lâm's personal direction of the operation is an ALLEGATION attributed to analysts (including David Hutt), never adjudicated. The November 2021 steak video (Tests 1 and 4) drew no acknowledgment from him at any point in the five years since. A ruler's word over time includes what he has never said.

Overall Summary

One law: MIXED · Without gripping: FAILS · The wobble: MIXED · Counts everyone: FAILS · His word: MIXED.

Tô Lâm is the most powerful Vietnamese leader in decades, and the documented record shows a seat being deliberately rebuilt around one man: the party leadership and the presidency held together for a full term, his former ministry enlarged in function and stocked through the Politburo, half the provinces and an entire tier of government abolished by his signature reform, and a legislature whose decisive votes are unanimous. Against that stand genuine and unusual counterweights for a one-party state: an anti-corruption discipline that stripped two former presidents of every party post, a general law that abolished the death penalty for corruption itself, free public schooling for 23 million children delivered on schedule, 8.02% growth against an 8% promise, and a trade shock answered by negotiation within forty-eight hours rather than defiance. But the tests measure the seat, not the programme. The law that reaches the top is a party rulebook wielded by his own apparatus, while the law that reaches the citizen is a prison term for a Facebook post or a parody video; the minorities of the Central Highlands and the Mekong Delta are prosecuted out of the count; and the one rebuke aimed at the man himself — a gold-encrusted steak eaten in lockdown — has never been answered at all.
