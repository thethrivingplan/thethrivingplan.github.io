1 · Forming — The Suit Is the Ammunition

Design as ruled

There is no magazine, no pouch, no reload. The suit forms the projectile on the spot — endless by design.

Diamond mode: each form is shaped exactly for its moment, while the repertoire of forms spreads with no fixed pattern.

How it works

The wearer chooses the form in the moment: an arrow, a lightning bolt, a fist — whatever the moment needs. The suit shapes its substance into that form and sends it. No separate ammo exists to run out of, because the suit itself is the supply.

Form is not a menu fixed at build time. It is a choice made at the speed of the situation, by the person in it.

Why it works

Every carried weapon carries its limits with it: weight, count, the grab, the load, the moment your hands are busy instead of ready. Forming on the spot removes the whole chain. What remains is intent → form → effect, with nothing in between to fumble, drop, or exhaust.

Open part: what the projectiles are formed from, and at what power and material cost, is an Open How — see the shelf below.

2 · Defense & Absorption

Design as ruled

Defense is total. Absorption — not rigidity — is the answer to impact.

Golden mode: coverage with no gaps — the field answers wherever the hit lands.

“Nuh-uh times infinity.”— On the defense

How it works

Nothing can hurt the wearer. The defense runs off the mind — because the wearer’s mind says that’s the truth, the field holds it as truth.

The same field thinking covers impact energy: a crash or blow is absorbed, the way a crumple zone drinks a collision instead of passing it to the passenger. The suit does not win by being harder than the hit. It wins by taking the energy in and leaving the wearer out of it.

Why it works

Rigid armor has the oldest failure in engineering: whatever it can’t stop, it delivers — to the person inside. Absorption breaks that trade. It is the same lesson cars learned after the rigid era: survive the energy, don’t argue with it.

Open parts: the field’s capacity — how much energy it can take, and how that scales — and the interface between mind and defense are Open Hows, printed at full size below.

A solid projectile can’t change its mind. This substance can — because it was never only one thing.

4 · Lifespan & No Debris

Design as ruled

Every formed thing has a designed lifespan — long enough to do its work, then complete evaporation. No debris. Nothing left behind.

Platinum mode: one exact ending rule for every form — complete evaporation, the same for all, nothing left behind.

How it works

A formed projectile must not evaporate immediately — it has to survive its flight and its moment of effect. At the end of its designed lifespan, it evaporates completely. No shell, no fragment, no residue, no litter of spent forms.

The lifespan is part of the design of each form, the same way its shape is: set for the job, then over.

Why it works

Debris is a cost billed to everyone who comes after — the street, the bystander, the next person to use the space. A weapon that cleans up after itself by design removes that bill entirely. Force with an expiry date is force that can’t keep hurting after its moment has passed.

Open part: how the designed lifespan is engineered — what sets the clock and guarantees the complete ending — is an Open How.

A tool that defaults to stopping — and reserves the rest for when stopping isn’t enough — keeps the wearer’s judgment in charge of the outcome. “Be good, not nice” is the rule on the dial: goodness decides the level; niceness doesn’t get a vote.

6 · Regeneration — Body Repair for the Wearer

Design as ruled

Not suit repair — body repair. If a hit makes it through the defense, the suit’s own liquid is the repair medium: it rushes into the wound, prevents the injury from becoming the real problem, then heals from the inside out — supporting broken bones as it goes, and keeping the wearer going until real care can take over.

Golden mode: distribution answers local demand — the liquid rushes to the wound.

“The wearer gets shot or stabbed or whatever… then liquid from the suit can rush into the wound to prevent real problems, as well as heal from the inside out. Support broken bones, cure other diseases.”— On the mechanism

How it works — the repair sequence

The defense is total by design — and this system exists for the “just in case” the design refuses to pretend away. The suit watches for the two signals that matter: a breach of the field, and an injury to the wearer. Because the suit is already liquid-based and already in contact with the body, it does not need a separate repair kit: the suit itself is the medium.

1 · Rush in. The moment a hit gets through — a shot, a stab, whatever made the wound — liquid from the suit flows into the wound, not just over it. It reaches the injury at its depth, where the damage actually is, instead of only dressing the surface.

2 · Prevent real problems — first. The liquid’s first job is stopping the cascade: seal the wound, apply steady pressure where bleeding must be slowed, and protect the injury so the wound itself does not turn into the bigger problem — blood loss, shock, contamination, further tearing. Prevention comes before repair.

3 · Heal from the inside out. With the cascade held, the same liquid works as a healing medium from inside the body outward: it supports the body’s own repair starting at the deepest part of the wound and knitting outward, so healing is not a surface patch over damage left underneath.

4 · Support broken bones. Where bone is broken, the liquid braces it from inside and out — flowing around the break to hold alignment from within, and forming a support or splint outside it — so the bone is held steady while it heals instead of shifting and doing fresh damage.

5 · Stabilize and hand over. Through all of it, the suit monitors the wearer — breathing, pulse, responsiveness — keeps them warm, held, and stable, and signals for real help. Its job is to buy the body time and hold it together while the body’s own healing — and then real medicine — does the rest.

Why it works

Every other system protects the wearer from the outside. This one answers the oldest honest question in any defense: what happens when it isn’t enough? A design that assumes it can never fail is a fragile design. Layered defense — field first, absorption second, body repair last — means one failure is not the end of the story.

It also follows from what the suit already is: one liquid substance that already forms, flows, holds a shape, and releases. Turned outward, that substance is a projectile. Turned inward, the same properties are exactly what a wound needs — something that can flow to the damage, hold where it is needed, support what is broken, and work from the inside out. Regeneration is not a second suit bolted on; it is the same pieces turned to care instead of force.

Not adaptation: regeneration repairs the wearer — improving the suit’s design as better knowledge arrives is a different job, and it belongs to Adaptation, under System 9 below.

Where each part stands — graded honestly:

Designed core: the trauma sequence — rush in, seal, prevent the cascade, support, stabilize. That is the mechanism as ruled, and the part the Build Order tests first (Stage 4).

Designed mechanisms, questions still open: healing from the inside out and bracing broken bones from inside and out. The mechanism is stated on this sheet; how the liquid senses damage, guides repair, and leaves the body safely afterward is engineering still owed — see Open How #5.

Far aim — not a settled capability: the same liquid as a healing medium against disease, not only trauma — “cure other diseases.” The sheet carries that as the far end of this system’s aim, printed at full size as an aim and an Open How. It is not claimed here as something the design can already do.

Open parts: how the suit senses and judges an injury or illness, how far real tissue repair can honestly go beyond sealing and stabilizing, how a liquid acts inside the body without harming it, and how repair is prioritized when power is limited are Open Hows — see #5 on the shelf below.

The healing reaches others

Self-healing was the first form. The suit heals other people too — from the inside out, the way it heals the wearer. The wearer sends the same liquid to another person and it works inside them: repair from the inside out. Touch is one door; the liquid’s steerable flight is the other — it is sent across a distance and arrives where it is sent. It rides on properties already on this sheet: the liquid is discriminating — it knows who it is sent to and works only there — steerable, and abortable; a healing is stopped mid-send the way a strike is. Healing is sent by the wearer’s will toward another’s good — offered, an act of the wearer: it is the force system’s other face, the same liquid that refuses collateral harm carrying repair instead. The repair mechanism inherits regeneration’s own Open How (#5) — marked open, no mechanism invented.

Limits — full size

This is a design on paper — not a built medical device, and nothing on this sheet is medical advice. “Regeneration” here means suit-assisted repair and stabilization: sealing, supporting, and keeping the wearer going until real care. It does not claim to regrow limbs, undo serious internal injury, or replace doctors, surgery, or real medicine — ever. A hurt wearer still needs real care, fast; this system’s whole purpose is to get them to it alive and stable.

7 · Circulation — The Heart Is the Model

Design as ruled

The suit’s hub meters like a heart: one pump serving the whole suit, giving more when more is asked and less when less is — with the metering built into the pump itself, not decided above it.

Golden mode: metering is demand-driven, like the heart it is modeled on.

How it works

The heart is a metering pump. At rest it moves about 5 litres a minute; under demand, about 25 litres a minute — five times the flow from the same pump, with no bigger pump swapped in.

Its metering is intrinsic to the muscle — the Frank-Starling law, in plain words: the more blood returns to the heart and stretches it, the more it ejects. The heart ejects what it receives. No central computer sits above it deciding the flow; the pump itself answers what arrives.

Spec for the suit: one hub serving the whole suit through the liquid network. Distribution answers local demand — where part of the suit is working hard or hurt, more liquid goes there; where nothing is asked, the flow rests.

Why it works

A pump that meters itself at the point of demand never waits for orders. The body already runs distribution this way: blood reroutes to an injury, sending more flow where the damage is — the same move as the suit’s liquid rushing to a wound in System 6. Circulation is that principle named as the suit’s plumbing rule.

The Water Fist is the same metering turned outward: ejection metered to just enough — non-lethal by default, as System 5 rules — because ejection past the need is not extra safety. Excess is damage. Metering is what keeps “just enough” honest instead of hopeful.

Graded honestly: the metering itself is bedrock physiology — measured, named, and taught, not a hope. How a hub in the suit’s liquid senses and answers demand the way muscle does is engineering still owed, and the sheet prints it that way rather than as solved.

One paper’s reading — not settled fact: Henein (2011) read healthy-heart dimensions as according with the golden ratio, 1.618, and reported that ratio falling to about 1.4 in end-stage heart failure. That is that paper’s interpretation of its measurements. The sheet carries it as a reading, labeled as one — the design above stands on the metering, which is bedrock, and does not depend on the ratio reading being right.

8 · The Wall — Capability Lives in the Wall, Not the Pump

Design as ruled

The giraffe does not run a bigger pump. It runs a thicker wall — and that is where the capability lives.

Platinum mode: uniform pressure held by an aligned structure, at even tension.

How it works

The popular story says the giraffe needs an exceptionally large heart to push blood up that long neck. Measured, the story fails: giraffe heart mass is about 0.51% of body mass — the same share as other mammals (Smerup, Damkjær et al., Journal of Experimental Biology, 2016). The oversized heart is refuted; the pump is ordinary-sized.

The real adaptation is in the wall: a small inner cavity wrapped in a thick ventricular wall — up to about 8 cm — thickening almost in step with neck length. That wall generates pressures that may exceed 300 mmHg while the tension in the wall itself stays at normal levels.

Why it works

The Law of Laplace, in plain words: the tension a chamber’s wall must bear rises with the pressure inside times the radius of the chamber, and falls as the wall gets thicker. So a small cavity wrapped in a thick wall can hold very high pressure at ordinary wall tension. The capability is not in moving more — it is in what the wall can stand, evenly, all the way around.

Spec for the suit: wall-thickness versus chamber-volume is a deliberate dial, set per system, on purpose. Turned toward thick wall and small chamber, it buys pressure — the Water Fist’s delivery. Turned toward a larger chamber, it buys flow — regeneration’s rush to a wound (System 6). Neither end of the dial is “better.” Each system states its setting, and its mode, and why.

The trade-off — full size

The thick wall limits stroke volume. A small cavity can only hold — and move — so much per beat. The giraffe’s heart trades volume for pressure: it is a pressure pump, not a volume pump, and it pays for the pressure with the volume. Any design that copies the wall copies the trade. The dial on this sheet is honest only because both ends of it are printed.

Need 1 · Seen or unseen — ✓ covered

Emission is a state of the plasma, not a coating: excited, it glows and is seen; quiet and low-density, it is nearly unseen. Visibility is a dial on the medium itself.

Need 2 · Thin — ✓ covered

A field-held medium is a sheath, not bulk. Plasma sheaths at boundaries run microns to millimeters; thin is native to field-holding, where a liquid would need containment bulk to be held at all.

Need 3 · Morph at will — ✓ covered

The strongest native fit of the nine. Plasma has no shape of its own; the field is its shape, and fields re-draw at electronic speed. Morphing is the medium’s nature, not a mechanism added to it.

Need 4 · Shoot out — ✓ covered

Plasma travels as jets and along thrown channels — the lightning-conduction filament is this same move at distance. The medium reaches out along a road the field lays.

Need 5 · Non-fatal — ✓ covered

Delivered energy is metered — current × path × time — so force is a dial, not a switch. A bullet or a blade cannot choose to be non-fatal; a field can. This need is System 5’s rule stated at the level of the medium.

Need 6 · Healing — ✓ covered

Cold atmospheric plasma is a real medical field: plasma jets of the kINPen class are certified medical devices in Europe, used and studied for wound care — disinfecting, including antibiotic-resistant bacteria in studies, stimulating cell growth and migration, promoting blood-vessel growth, helping chronic wounds close, and promoting clotting, by controlled reactive oxygen and nitrogen species. Healing is the cold layer’s job — the layer at the skin heals the wearer. Honest label: device-scale healing is demonstrated and clinical; the suit’s continuous wearable form is this design’s claim, extending a proven mechanism — the same standing as System 6.

Need 7 · Sustenance — ◐ not covered by plasma alone; fixed by a paired layer

The first need the medium does not cover, and the mark-off working as intended: the body eats chemistry, not current. Plasma contributes — nitrogen fixation from air (the arc process is historically real and plasma reactors still do it), plasma-activated water, and energy. The sustenance itself is a biological layer paired with the plasma: reserves carried and delivered when needed (the body’s own reserve-tank pattern), water recycling (spacesuit and station life support), and a bioregenerative algae-class element producing oxygen and edible biomass from carbon dioxide and light — a real space-agency research lane. The form: plasma feeds the biology; the biology feeds the wearer. The suit is plasma (the physics) plus a living layer (the sustenance) — a pair, not a single substance.

Need 8 · Run off the mind, no controller — ✓ covered, with two floors

The wearer’s thought is the interface. No handset, no external operator; the only mind in the loop is inside the suit. Floor one — bandwidth: today’s thought-channels are narrow, so the design splits the work by the count: the mind sends intent only — the choice, the 5 — and the Thing carries the thousand adjustments underneath, the 1 through 4, the way the body carries a catch without conscious finger management. Floor two — latency: nothing is literally instant; signal speed and processing are a physics floor. The suit reads the signal at its source, upstream of the body’s own travel time, and the design target is end-to-end latency below the threshold of perception. “As close to instant as possible” is the requirement, printed with its floor.

Need 9 · Thinks for itself — ✓ covered

The pair to Need 8, not a contradiction of it: the wearer directs, and the built-in intelligence thinks — carrying execution, adjustment, and protection underneath the wearer’s intent. This is the Thing doctrine of the Open Contract, restated as a need. Need 10 · Workout, massage, and regeneration — ◐ the mechanism is proven; the integrated form doesn’t yet

The suit works the wearer’s muscles, and it rubs the wearer’s soreness, on demand. The contraction mechanism is electrical stimulation — EMS: real, commercial, and studied. Stimulation contracts muscle, and modest strength gains from it are documented. The suit’s field and plasma layer distributes the pulses across a whole muscle group instead of at two pads, and massage is the same system at comfort settings — stimulation plus pressure — so a sore back gets a back massage, where it hurts. Honest label: the marketed version of stimulation — training fully replaced, effortlessly — is oversold, and this sheet does not print it as fact. Regeneration: the same layer supports recovery — worked muscle repairs and rebuilds with the suit’s help, the cold layer’s repair support (Need 6) aimed at the muscle that was just worked. The suit supports the body’s own regeneration; it does not replace it.

Need 11 · Sweat handling and load sharing — ◐ carried as a requirement; its cost is measured by Test T4

Sweat: the skin layer wicks sweat away as it forms. At a full sprint, sweat never sits on the wearer and never gets in the wearer’s eyes — the face and brow are kept clear as a named requirement. What is collected is not discarded: the skin layer routes sweat into the biological layer’s water recycling (Need 7), so sweat becomes input, not waste. Load: the suit carries the majority of the load, so the wearer does not tire out — the field-held medium and the suit’s frame take the weight, the way an exoskeleton’s load path carries a pack to the ground. The wearer still does some of the effort, by the designer’s own word: some effort is his, and that is fine. The suit’s share is not free energy — it is drawn from the same budget as everything else on this sheet, and its size is measured by Test T4.

Need 12 · Waste management — ◐ proven at station scale; the suit-scale form doesn’t yet

What goes in comes out, and the suit handles that too — literally. It is the output half of the sustenance loop (Need 7). Urine is collected and routed into the biological layer’s water recycling — the same loop that takes the sweat (Need 11) — and the water comes back around. That recovery is proven at the largest scale humans run: a space station recovers nearly all of its water, urine included. The suit-scale form of it doesn’t yet exist, and its miniaturization is printed as a ❓ under the tests below. Solids are sealed in containment for disposal; no recycling is claimed for them. The whole path is sealed: nothing touches the wearer’s skin twice, nothing vents into the plasma layer, and a containment breach is a failure the suit isolates and never spreads (Test T5). The biological layer is the suit’s gut and kidneys both: sustenance in, waste out, water around the loop.

Need 13 · Protection — absorption against weapons — ◐ proven in pieces; the integrated stack doesn’t yet

One process, every threat: the suit does not carry a different armor for each weapon. It absorbs. Whatever energy arrives, the field, the plasma, the weave, and the frame take it in and shed it, store it, or route it around the wearer before it reaches the body — the plasma’s own nature, put to work: nearly everything that enters it becomes it. Ballistic: the woven laminate carries the stop — its mechanics are on the material bench — and the thin plasma sheath alone does not stop a round; this sheet does not claim it does. Blast: fragments meet the same layers, heat meets the plasma in its own element, and the pressure squeeze is taken by the frame and the stack and metered down before it reaches the wearer, the way a fall is. Shock: impact-hardening material and the load frame eat falls and crashes. Electrical: the lightning lane above. And the defense is responsive, not static — the field stiffens where a hit is detected and the armor flows toward the threat; a static defense is a puzzle, and this one moves. Honest label: absorbed is not vanished, and proof is a rating, never an absolute — every threat class carries a ceiling, and Test T8 measures each ceiling separately.

Need 14 · The eyes show the best move — ◐ the computation is proven at engine level; the in-view suit form doesn’t yet

The built-in AI puts what the wearer needs to see in front of his eyes. A card game: the play he should make. A chess position: the move. Aiming at something: where he will hit — the tracer package in the glasses, the line before, the tracer during, the marker after. The display is private — it lives in the vision lane, in spectra only the wearer’s glasses read; nobody else at the table sees his line. The suit-integrated form — near-instant, mind-linked, in-view — doesn’t yet exist: it stands under the latency test (T3) and a display-accuracy test still to be determined (T9). The balance, printed with the need: the AI shows the best move; the wearer still makes the move. Advice in the eyes — the choice stays his. The 5 stays human.

Need 16 · One utility tool — attachments in and out, backups on backups, wear spread where it is needed — ◐ the modular form is proven everywhere; the suit’s powered mount doesn’t yet

The whole suit is a utility tool: not a shelf of single-purpose devices, but one tool that works for everything — simplify, simplify, simplify — as much as one tool genuinely can. That qualifier is the designer’s own, and it is the floor printed with the claim: where a job truly needs a specialist, the specialist is not a second system — it mounts on the same one interface (the Universal Screw standard: one drive, one mount language, across everything we design) and comes off when the moment passes. Backups upon backups — all the way down: every critical function carries its backup, the backup carries one, and so on down through every layer to the smallest unit, so there is always a fix — no depth at which failure becomes final. The suit degrades in steps, never off a cliff: a layer lost is a capability dimmed, not a suit dead. And the bottom layer of the stack is repairability itself — every part on the one interface, one driver opens all of it, because a backup that cannot be fitted is not a backup. Redundancy layered the way this house keeps its records, last known good behind last known good, so no single failure is the failure. And no one tool is worn out being overdriven: work is dispersed across the units — not forced equal, just wherever it needs to be. The mechanisms are proven in other fields: flash storage levels its wear so no cell dies first, networks balance their loads, crews rotate. The suit tracks wear per unit, routes the work to the unit that should carry it, and rests the tired ones. The form is proven everywhere from rail mounts to the reorderable staff (banked tonight, Watch 94) and swap-on equipment mounts (Watch 103); the suit’s version — a powered, data-carrying, hot-swapping mount that holds under load and motion, with the wear-routing run by the suit’s own mind — doesn’t yet: it stands under T11.

The tool you left at home is the tool you don’t have. So the suit is worn, not carried — light enough, comfortable enough, and plain enough in ordinary life that leaving it off never tempts. Self-Protection, By Itself — And Control at the Edges

The suit protects itself, by itself. If a thing is told to protect itself and the protection is done correctly, no controller is needed — really by itself, or it is still controlled. The test: remove the controller. If the protection still stands, it was the thing’s own; if it collapses, it was remote control in costume. Standing orders written in advance are a constitution, not a leash — once written in, they are the thing, the way instinct is part of the animal and not a hand on its collar. No outside line exists to steer or countermand in the moment — because a protector that can be countermanded from outside can be captured from outside.

Control belongs at the edges. Control is a good thing in its place — as filters at the boundary, deciding what gets in, so the wearer is not flooded with what is not needed. It is never a steering hand at the center, deciding what the thing does. A filter does not steer. Control at the edges protects the freedom at the center.

Effort has to be worth the time. Effort is required sometimes — nobody said otherwise. But effort is spent in the one real currency, time, and the effort has to be worth the time it costs. Work that spends the wearer’s time without returning worth is refused by this design’s own rule, the same rule that governs the walls it will not grind against.

The Takeover Line

Replacement is not improvement. A synthetic substitute — down to a device standing in for a single nerve cell — has to earn its place by actually being better: “if it even makes it better.” Newer is not better, and a copy that merely matches what it replaced bought nothing but risk.

Augmentation never becomes takeover. The mind-link of Need 8 stays an interface: it reads, it assists, it carries. It does not become the nervous system, and it does not become the thinker of the wearer’s thoughts. One assisted cell is assistance; the line is drawn while clear-headed, in advance, at exactly the place where assisting would turn into substituting. The wearer remains the wearer. The 5 stays human.

No Implant by Default — Any Exception Carries Its Why

Ideally, nothing goes under the skin. The link is worn, not implanted, wherever a worn route can do the job — that is the default, and it is not close. An implant enters this design only as a justified exception, and the justification is printed on the sheet beside it, in the open: why — the specific job no external route can do, stated against the measured floor that rules the external routes out (bandwidth, latency, signal), never “better” in the abstract; what — exactly what it is and is not: an interface that reads and carries, under the Takeover Line with extra force, because under the skin is exactly where assisting must never become substituting; reversible — removable, with the wearer’s own off-switch absolute and his alone: his hand on his own device is not an outside line, it is the owner’s; and chosen — under the signature standard, risks written and fully understood by the wearer himself. If the why is thin, it does not go in. The burden of proof sits on the implant, never on the refusal.

Three Seats — And the Options That Fill Them

Design as ruled

The plasma types were cut by need, and three stand — not three rivals, but three seats: cold at the skin, hot at the work, magnetized holding both.

Parts & sourcing — The Seats

Seat one · Cold plasma, at the skin
What
The wearer-safe layer: healing (Need 6), the seen-or-unseen dial at rest, and the suit’s contact surface — always through the separating layer.
Options
Dielectric-barrier discharge panels — flat and flexible, the form closest to clothing; a jet array — point sources, aimed healing, poorer coverage; glow-discharge textile — electrodes woven in, so the fabric itself makes the plasma.
Get it
The panel and jet forms are obtained from existing cold atmospheric plasma device practice — the certified wound-care device class named under Need 6. The textile form is the thinnest-evidenced of the three: laboratory-stage, printed here as an option, not a result.
Seat two · Hot plasma, at the work
What
The power end: forming, shooting out, and the heavy work of the medium — held away from the wearer by the magnetized seat and the separating layer.
Options
Arc plasma — raw power, torch-class; radio-frequency / microwave-sustained plasma — no electrodes to erode, cleaner control; pulsed plasma — energy in packets, so dose is a count of pulses: the option that speaks directly to Need 5’s non-fatal metering.
Get it
Obtained from established practice in each lane: plasma torch and arc-jet work for the arc; industrial and laboratory RF plasma processing for the sustained form; pulsed-power practice for the packet form.
Seat three · Magnetized plasma, the holding
What
The containment itself: the field that holds the cold seat against the wearer and the hot seat at the work, and re-draws both when the suit morphs.
Options
Electromagnetic coils — adjustable, at a power cost; permanent magnets — no power drawn, but a fixed shape, so morphing suffers; a hybrid — permanent base shape, coils only where the shape must change; the self-pinch — the plasma’s own current holds it, elegant and self-scaling.
Get it
Coil holding is obtained from magnetic-confinement practice at its largest proven scale — the tokamak line of machines, where fields are the only container that works. T2

❓ Field-holding on a bending body

Can the magnetized seat hold a thin, stable plasma layer on a body that bends? T3

❓ Mind-to-plasma latency, end to end

Need 8’s floor, measured: from the wearer’s neural signal at its source to the plasma’s response, end to end, against the threshold of perception. The requirement is a number under that threshold — “as close to instant as possible,” proven in milliseconds, not asserted in words.

T4

What the three seats draw at rest and at work, and the storage that implies. Inside this test sits the lightning question from the graded lanes above: in a routed strike, what fraction can storage actually take in? Measured, not guessed — until this test runs, the sheet prints no harvest number.

T5

Power cut mid-morph. A dead field segment. Sweat and water contact — the body’s weak route, arriving from outside. And the self-pinch’s coupled failure: power and hold weakening together. T6

Need 7, in numbers: reserves carried, water recycled, and the algae-class element’s output — oxygen and edible biomass — measured against what a wearer actually consumes. The test rules wherever it lands; no expected outcome is printed here as fact.

Against that envelope, dose is measured against outcomes: strength gained, comfort kept, soreness relieved. Until this test runs, the sheet prints stimulation’s proven mechanism and its documented modest gains — and prints the designer’s full stated scale as what it is: unmeasured.
