Pairs

The memory matching game — the old Concentration form. Cards lie face down in a grid. Flip two: a match stays up, a miss flips back. Clear the whole grid, and in solo play do it in as few flips as you can.

The one rule that matters: every card you have ever seen is a clue, whether it matched or not. The game is won by the memory you build from your misses. All the pictures on the cards are our own drawings, made for this page.

Bot honesty — how its memory really works

The bot has no hidden knowledge. It only ever “knows” cards that have been flipped face-up where it could see them — the same cards you saw. Each time a card shows, the bot gets one memory roll at its level’s rate: roll under the rate and the card sticks; roll over it and the bot plays as if it never saw it. A card shown twice gets two chances to stick. The bot never peeks at face-down cards it hasn’t seen.

LevelChance one seen card sticksChance it recalls a whole seen pair*

*Plain reasoning, shown rather than asserted: recalling a pair needs both copies remembered, and the two rolls are independent, so the chance is the rate multiplied by itself (rate²). Learning: 0.40 × 0.40 = 0.16 — it will usually miss pairs it has technically “seen.” Pro: 1.00 × 1.00 = 1.00 — perfect recall of everything shown, but it still knows nothing about cards never flipped.

The honest note about memory

Memory is trainable — not as a slogan, but measurably, and this page measures it. Your record tracks flips per pair game after game: when that number falls, your memory for places is genuinely improving, because the game, the grids, and the counting never change. Slow weeks happen and the record shows those too. What improves is a real skill — holding locations in mind — and it carries off this page into kitchens, car parks, and workshops.
