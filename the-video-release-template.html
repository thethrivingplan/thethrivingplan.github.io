The Rule

Post the video AS IT IS. Nothing burned in, nothing added, no treatment — unless the person releasing it says otherwise.

“As it is” means as it arrives. We do not burn captions or titles into the picture, lay music over it, cut it, clean it up, or dress it in graphics. The video that goes up is the video that was handed over — the same frames, the same sound, the same words.

The one exception is the releaser’s own call: if the person releasing the video says to treat it, that is their say-so, and it gets stated on the release. Silence is never treated as a yes.

Why as-is? ▾

A video is a record of what somebody actually said and did. Every treatment is an edit to that record — a caption burned in can change a word, a cut can change a meaning, music can change how a moment lands. Once it is treated, nobody downstream can tell what was original and what was added.

Posting as-is keeps the chain clean: the speaker owns their words, the viewer sees the real thing, and if anyone questions the release later, the answer is simple — nothing was touched.

The Pack

Every release carries three boxes — a title, a description, and tags — each in its own separate box, click-to-copy, so each can be pasted on its own. These are the rules for what goes in each one.

BOX 1

Title

The hook line, in the speaker’s own words.

If the video has a line that lands — the sentence people would repeat — the title is that line, as spoken. Not a rewrite of it, not a teaser written around it, not a claim stretched past what the speaker said. The speaker already wrote the title; the box just carries it.

Speaker says: “I was around that age when it just quit working.”
Title box: “It just quit working” — their words, trimmed, never reworded.

BOX 2

Description

What the video actually says — nothing inflated.

A plain account of what is in the video: who is speaking, what they talk about, what happens. If the video does not say it, the description does not say it. No hype words doing work the video doesn’t do, no summary that promises more than the minute delivers.

Honest: “A one-minute talk about knowing your value and not apologizing for it.”
Not this: “The most powerful message you will hear all year!!”

BOX 3

Tags

The real topics — no stuffing.

Tags name the topics actually in the video, so the right people can find it. A tag for something the video never touches is a small lie told to a search engine, and it is not told here. Fewer true tags beat a long list of borrowed ones.

In the video: self-worth, speaking up, knowing your value.
Not in the box: trending topics the video never mentions.

Why three separate boxes? ▾

Every platform takes a title, a description, and tags in three separate fields. When the pack travels as one block of text, the person posting has to cut it apart by hand — and hand-cutting is where lines get lost, tags land in the description, and wording drifts from what was approved.

Separate boxes end that. Copy one, paste one. What was written is what gets posted, field for field, every time, on every platform.
