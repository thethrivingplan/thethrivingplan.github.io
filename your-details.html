What it is

One small record — name, birthday, birth time, birthplace — kept by this browser, on this device, under one shared name every page here knows. Pages read it so they can fill themselves in. This page is where you write it, change it, or wipe it. Four fields is the whole of it; this page will never ask for more, and never for a password or a secret code.

What it means

Nothing about you is kept on any server, because there is no server copy to keep. The wall between browsers and devices is real: a different browser or a new phone starts empty, and that emptiness is the honest price of nothing being held anywhere else. The seed link above is the bridge across that wall — carried by you, in the link’s tail, the one part of an address browsers never send to a server.

What it could mean for you

Ten seconds here buys back every retyping everywhere else on this door: charts that arrive pre-filled, forms that start finished. Change a detail and the next page that reads it gets the new one. Forget it here and it is gone from this device entirely — no copy anywhere else to chase.

The microphone is off. One tap starts it, a second tap stops it — and it stays on while you scroll and use the whole page.

How the mic works, honestly: one tap starts it (your browser asks permission once), and it stays on while you scroll and use the whole page until you tap stop. This page itself records nothing and sends nothing while you talk — your words become text in the box above, on your device, appearing live as you speak, and only what you choose to submit travels, exactly as with typing. The transcription itself, though, is done by your own browser’s speech service, not by this page and not entirely on your device: most browsers — Chrome included — send your voice audio to their maker’s servers to turn it into text. So “nothing is sent anywhere” would not be true, and this page will not claim it. Where a browser or an in-app viewer cannot do live dictation, the control above says so plainly and typing works as always — a real browser (Chrome or Safari itself) is the reliable door.
