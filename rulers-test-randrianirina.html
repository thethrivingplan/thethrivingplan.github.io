The Ruler's Test — Michael Randrianirina, President of the Refoundation of the Republic of Madagascar

Subject: Colonel Michael Randrianirina — commander of the CAPSAT army corps, sworn in on 17 October 2025 as President of the Refoundation of the Republic of Madagascar. The seat graded is the transitional presidency, and it is graded as it is actually held: he holds executive power not by election but as chairman of the Council of the Presidency for the Re-Foundation of the Republic of Madagascar, a body of officers drawn from the army, the gendarmerie, and the national police, after the High Constitutional Court (HCC) on 14 October 2025 — the day the National Assembly voted 130 to 1 to impeach President Andry Rajoelina, who had fled the country as CAPSAT took control of the capital — invited him to exercise the functions of head of state. The uprising that carried him in began on 25 September 2025 as decentralized, Gen Z–led protests against chronic water and electricity failures in Antananarivo, and widened into a nationwide demand for Rajoelina's resignation over corruption, poverty, and the suppression of civil liberties; the UN High Commissioner for Human Rights reported on 29 September 2025 that at least 22 people had been killed and hundreds injured, most by security forces loyal to the then-government (UN OHCHR, 29 September 2025; CIVICUS Lens, 2025). On 11 October 2025 CAPSAT refused orders to fire on protesters and turned against the president; Rajoelina left Madagascar on 12–13 October 2025 aboard a French military aircraft (AFP reporting, October 2025). Randrianirina, 51, is himself a product of the machinery he now runs: CAPSAT is the same unit that installed Rajoelina in the 2009 coup, and Randrianirina was imprisoned from November 2023 to February 2024 under Rajoelina on an alleged coup plot, released with a suspended sentence for attacking state security (Reuters factbox, October 2025). Because the seat is a transition in progress, every grade in this dossier is provisional by the nature of the seat: the transition's own terminal promises — a constitutional referendum and a presidential election in 2027 — are still inside their window, and the record that will finally grade this seat has not finished being written. His predecessor Rajoelina (president 2019–2025) appears as context where the record Randrianirina inherited, or the undertakings the transition made in his place, bear directly on a test. Graded: November 2026, on the documented public record, some thirteen months into the transition. Method: Each test is graded only on the evidence listed under it. Both shelves are shown — the strongest evidence that he passes, then the strongest evidence that he fails. Allegations are labeled ALLEGATION and never stated as verdicts. Contested items are named as contested, with who disputes them. Seat-holders and public figures are named; people below the public eye appear by role only, unless the documented record itself establishes their wrongdoing. Grades: HOLDS / FAILS / MIXED / NOT ENOUGH EVIDENCE. This record is correctable: new evidence changes grades, in the open. The ethic printed on the Test card governs the reading: "How you play the game matters. Play by the rules. Stay true to your word. And if another option becomes available, at least consider it." The mandate rule applies in the form this seat allows: Randrianirina holds no electoral platform, so the platform of record is the transition's own charter of undertakings — his public announcements of 14 October 2025, his first national address of 17 November 2025, and the Refoundation programme published by the Council of the Presidency on 28 February 2026. Governing on those undertakings is not itself a failure and is never graded as one; carrying them out is evidence on the FOR shelf of Test 5. Marks are lost only for HOW: a second law for his own side (Test 1), gripping the referees (Test 2), refusing to consider another option when one becomes available (Test 3), counting out those who did not install him (Test 4). One constraint is recorded at the outset under the no-good-choice clause, and bounded to where it belongs: in the seventy-two hours of 12–14 October 2025 the options were a military council, a vacuum in which loyalist gendarmerie were still in the streets, or a civilian figurehead with no force behind the signature. The seizure's first days are graded against that constraint. The thirteen months since are not: what he built inside the constraint is the record, and the record is what is graded.

Test 1 — GIVES BY ONE LAW

Same rules for allies and opponents, insiders and outsiders — no favorites at the source.

GRADE: FAILS

Evidence for:

  • The anti-corruption machinery was switched on immediately and pointed, at least in its stated remit, at the public finances as a whole. On taking power he ordered the Court of Accounts to audit the management of public funds; the audits identified hundreds of millions of US dollars lost to corruption and mismanagement (Amnesty International, November 2026). He pledged publicly to "pursue corrupt individuals and thieves" and bring them to justice, and stated that Madagascar wished to prosecute Rajoelina itself: "everything that happened in Madagascar stemmed from the president's initiatives and orders, including the shootings" (Africa Press, October 2025).
  • The flagship prosecutions have run through courts and prosecutorial process rather than by decree, and the process has visibly constrained the executive's own side of the argument at points. On 28 October 2026 the HCC ruled that the Anti-Corruption Pole (PAC), a specialised criminal court, may prosecute Rajoelina, former prime minister Christian Ntsay, and several former ministers and officials — holding that corruption for personal gain is not part of exercising a presidential or ministerial mandate, so the files (alleged fictitious contracts for school desks; the management of the sovereign fund between 2022 and 2025) need not go to the High Court of Justice; the ruling is about jurisdiction, not guilt, and no trial date has been set (HCC ruling reported 28–30 October 2026). In the separate Boeing 777 case — five aircraft provisionally registered in Madagascar and found in Iran in July 2025 — a first trial involving 32 Malagasy, Iranian, and Indian defendants opened in November 2026, and on 23 November 2026 PAC prosecutors requested arrest warrants for Rajoelina and Ntsay on charges of complicity in financing terrorism and the acquisition of heavy weapons; the warrants had not been issued at grading, because an investigating judge must first approve them — the prosecution's request is not a warrant and not a declaration of guilt, and every charge in both proceedings is an ALLEGATION (Afrikactus, 24 November 2026; AFP, November 2026).
  • The case against his predecessor's closest business adviser is not Madagascar's alone: Maminiaina Ravatomanga was arrested in Mauritius in October 2025 by that country's Financial Crimes Commission on suspicion of laundering some USD 163 million through Mauritian assets — an ALLEGATION he has not answered in court — and is also a defendant in the Boeing proceedings (Africanews, 25 October 2025; Afrikactus, November 2026).

Evidence against:

  • The enforcement map is the political map. Amnesty International's one-year assessment finds the fight against corruption "applied selectively" and "mainly weaponized against opponents": former officials and political figures of the previous administration and their families have faced intimidation, illegal searches, arbitrary detention, and prolonged pretrial detention, with dozens forced into exile, and at least 20 individuals remained arbitrarily detained for politically motivated reasons at the assessment's writing, including former presidential candidates Sylvain Rabetsaroana and Paul Rabary Andrianirina (Amnesty International, November 2026). These are a monitor's findings, printed as findings; the government has not published a competing account of the detentions in the record assembled here.
  • The audit's own frame shields the auditor. The Court of Accounts exercise he ordered was limited to public finances covering 2022 to 2025 — a window that ends where his administration begins, which Amnesty identifies as strategically shielding his administration from the same accountability (Amnesty International, November 2026). A law that audits the predecessor's years and stops at the door of one's own is two laws.
  • The one body of wrongdoing for which the evidence is on film has produced no process at all. Soldiers, police, and gendarmes are recorded in extensive video footage shooting at protesters and bystanders during the September–October 2025 protests, killing and severely injuring them; no investigations have followed under his administration (Amnesty International, November 2026). Those killings were ordered under Rajoelina — the fact is stated in Test 5's context — but the gendarmerie that did much of the shooting now sits inside his own governing council's forces, and thirteen months of impunity for them, alongside terrorism-financing charges for the exiled camp, is the sharpest one-law failure in the file: the law reaches the camp that lost and does not reach the uniforms that won.
  • The citizenship stripping was executed by executive decree against one named man. On 24 October 2025, a week after the swearing-in, the new government decreed Rajoelina stripped of his Malagasy nationality, on the ground that he had voluntarily acquired French nationality in 2014, which Article 4 of the nationality code provides causes loss of Malagasy nationality (Africanews, 25 October 2025; HCC, October 2026). The legal basis exists and is printed on the FOR side of the ledger in the Summary; what Test 1 grades is the manner — a general law, dormant through the six years in which the fact was publicly contested while Rajoelina governed, activated by decree of the successor government against the one person it displaced, with no court process documented in the record assembled here.

Why the grade lands here: The prosecutions of the old camp run through real courts, and that is credited in full above. But one law means the same law in every direction, and the documented record runs in one direction only: audits that stop at his own door, detentions that land on the predecessor's people and on his own critics, exile for the old camp's families, and no process of any kind for the security forces filmed shooting the crowds whose uprising seated him. Selectivity this consistent is not a backlog; it is the shape of the enforcement itself.

Helped: The Malagasy public, to the extent the Court of Accounts audit established on the record that hundreds of millions of dollars were lost under the previous administration; the PAC and the ordinary courts, whose jurisdiction over the old regime's files was confirmed rather than bypassed (HCC, 28 October 2026). Hurt: Former officials and political figures of the Rajoelina administration and their families — intimidation, searches Amnesty characterizes as illegal, prolonged pretrial detention, and exile for dozens (Amnesty International, November 2026); the at least 20 people Amnesty counts as arbitrarily detained, including Sylvain Rabetsaroana and Paul Rabary Andrianirina; the families of the 22 dead of September–October 2025, whose killers face no investigation; Andry Rajoelina, stripped of citizenship by decree of the government that replaced him.

Test 2 — HOLDS WITHOUT GRIPPING

Do the courts, the electoral commission, the media, the prosecution, and the central bank keep their autonomy — or is independence punished and control centralized?

GRADE: FAILS

Evidence for:

  • The referees he announced dissolved in October 2025 are, in the documented record of 2026, functioning — and have ruled in ways that structure his own transition. The army's first announcement suspended the constitution and dissolved the Senate, the HCC, and the electoral commission (CENI), but this was partly reversed: the HCC and the commission continue to work, and the HCC still rules on the basis of the 2010 constitution (electos.net country briefing, November 2026). The HCC entrusted legislative power to the National Assembly alone after the Senate's term ended in January 2026 (the senatorial election planned for December 2025 having been cancelled); it confirmed on 4 November 2026 that the constitutional referendum may be held before the presidential vote; and in November 2026 it entertained a petition by opposition MP Antoine Rajerison asking it to remove Randrianirina himself for alleged treason and repeated constitutional violations in appointments — and dismissed it as inadmissible for not meeting the constitutional requirements, a procedural ruling against a legal challenge to a military ruler, reached in the open (electos.net, November 2026; Mogaz News, November 2026).
  • The National Assembly was retained — Rajoelina's dissolution decree against it was treated as void — and it has bitten the hand that kept it. In an extraordinary session opened 25 August 2026 the government tabled bills on combating cybercrime (bill 042/2026) and on electronic communication (bill 040/2026); after public outcry over their restrictive provisions, the Assembly decided on 4 September 2026 to withdraw both bills from consideration for further examination (CIVICUS Monitor, 2026). The Assembly also continues to conduct its own internal affairs by its own rules: on the night of 23–24 November 2026 it removed its president, Siteny Randrianasoloniaiko, by 112 votes to 37 — the two-thirds threshold was 109 — on a motion citing "serious misconduct" and breaches of the rules of procedure. Malagasy reporting records disagreements between Siteny and Randrianirina, including over possible electoral ambitions; the same reporting states plainly that the record does not establish direct intervention by the executive in the procedure, and this dossier prints it as exactly that — adjacent and contested, not as his act (Afrikactus, 24 November 2026; 2424.mg reporting).
  • The electoral machinery is being rebuilt in the open rather than captured in the dark: a complete rebuilding of the electoral register began in July 2026 and is due to finish in April 2027, with the commission expecting 13 to 14 million voters (electos.net, November 2026). Critical media and monitors — RFI, Midi Madagasikara, Newsmada, Amnesty International, the CIVICUS Monitor — publish detailed indictments of his government from inside the country, and the Guardian quotes Malagasy civil-society figures criticizing him by name (CIVICUS Monitor, 2026; Amnesty International, November 2026).

Evidence against:

  • The seat's own instrument is the grip. Sovereignty sits in a Council of the Presidency composed of security officers, which he chairs; Amnesty records that he "elevated four highly ranked officials in the gendarmerie and the army to the level of 'head of state', despite the lack of constitutional grounds upon which to justify this, and populated key government positions with present or former public security officials" (Amnesty International, November 2026). The constitution whose suspension he announced on 14 October 2025 has never, in the record assembled here, been formally un-suspended by any instrument; the HCC rules on its basis in practice while its formal status remains what the colonel announced. A referee who officiates at the pleasure of an announcement is not yet an autonomous referee.
  • The electoral referee's membership turns over under his decree. In March 2026 eight members of the CENI resigned together, their letters lodged with the HCC on 5 March 2026; under the procedure reported at the time, replacements are appointed by decree of the president (Gasikara News, 12 March 2026). The resignations were announced as voluntary; public reaction recorded at the time treated them with open skepticism, and the circumstances are printed here as contested. What is not contested is the destination of the power: the body that will run the referendum and the 2027 election is being reconstituted by the signature of the man whose transition those votes will judge.
  • The executive has reached into individual cases. Amnesty records that, as a precondition for their release, Randrianirina personally pressured the parents of detained Gen Z activists to prevent their children from participating in protests (Amnesty International, November 2026) — the head of the executive bargaining over detainees' liberty with their families, outside any court. The same assessment records ministerial appointments made in disregard of constitutional process, including a minister facing corruption allegations (an ALLEGATION, unnamed in the assessment and unnamed here), and appointments in some instances based on polygraph examinations rather than institutional processes established in law (Amnesty International, November 2026).
  • His government drafted the surveillance architecture even though the Assembly stopped it. Bill 042/2026 would have created a Digital Investigation Unit with access to the information systems of telecommunications operators and internet providers, and allowed emergency blocking of content deemed "manifestly unlawful" on a police request validated by a prosecutor, with no possibility of appeal; bill 040/2026 would have let the regulator ARTEC suspend a communication service for up to a year without prior judicial authorization (CIVICUS Monitor, 2026). Amnesty adds that the cybercrime proposal would have required government critics to back opinions with "actual and solid proof" and given the state permanent access to telecommunications data without judicial oversight (Amnesty International, November 2026). The bills' withdrawal is credited on the FOR shelf and in Test 3; the drafting is graded here, where it belongs: it is the executive's own statement of how much of the referee it wanted to hold. Meanwhile the draft law on freedom of public information, tabled in one form or another since 2007, was notably absent from the same session's agenda (CIVICUS Monitor, 2026).

Why the grade lands here: The referees function — that is the strongest thing on the FOR shelf, and it is what keeps this grade within reach of repair. But Test 2 grades the holding, and the holding is structural: a council of officers as the sovereign body, four security chiefs raised to head-of-state level without constitutional grounds, a constitution suspended by announcement and never formally restored, the electoral commission's membership renewed by his decree, detainees' releases bargained with personally, and a surveillance law drafted to bypass judges. The referees still whistle; the hand that announced their dissolution has not let go of the whistle's cord.

Helped: The National Assembly as an institution, retained and demonstrably capable of withdrawing the government's bills; litigants and the public who still have a functioning HCC and CENI to appeal to, including MP Antoine Rajerison, whose petition against the president was heard and answered rather than punished in any way the record shows. Hurt: The CENI as an independent body, its membership vacated en masse and refilled by presidential decree; the detained activists whose release was conditioned on a parent's promise extracted by the president personally; Malagasy internet users and journalists, for whom the surveillance bills were drafted and remain, after withdrawal "for further examination," an announced intention rather than a dead letter.

Test 3 — ANSWERS THE WOBBLE

When courts, voters, parliament, the street, foreign partners, or events push back, does he adjust openly — or deny and double down?

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • Parliamentary and public pushback on the surveillance bills produced an open adjustment. After civil society and public criticism of bills 040/2026 and 042/2026, the National Assembly withdrew both from consideration on 4 September 2026 to allow further examination of their provisions (CIVICUS Monitor, 2026; Midi Madagasikara reporting). The withdrawal was the Assembly's act; that it happened inside his transition, against his government's own bills, without documented retaliation against the critics, is the system's answer and is credited here.
  • Institutional pushback from outside was met with process rather than defiance. The African Union suspended Madagascar for unconstitutional change of government and set conditions — a Malagasy-led inclusive dialogue, a credible roadmap, elections; SADC's communiqué of 17 December 2025 called for inclusive national consultations and periodic reporting, and its Panel of Elders ran shuttle missions in January–February, February–March, and May 2026. His government submitted its dialogue readiness report and transition roadmap by the AU's 28 February 2026 deadline, publishing the 24-month Refoundation programme the same day (Torch Media Africa, 2026; Council of the Presidency, 28 February 2026). Madagascar remains suspended by the AU in the record as of November 2026 — the adjustment is engagement with the referees, not yet restoration by them.
  • When his own machinery stalled, he intervened in the open and restated the commitment. On 19 November 2026 he convened the Prime Minister's office, the Refoundation ministry, the FFKM church council, and the technical committee at the Iavoloha palace over the national consultation's delays — only 8.33% of allocated consultation funds had been disbursed, through public-finance procedures — and reiterated the commitment to elections within two years and a return to constitutional order before the end of 2027, with the referendum first (Madagascar-Tribune, 20 November 2026).
  • He has gone looking for the other voices rather than waiting to be pushed. On 28 July 2026 he received former presidents Marc Ravalomanana and Hery Rajaonarimampianina at the Iavoloha palace to work on the transition's most sensitive unfinished business — establishing a functioning Constitutional Council, the body that validates laws and arbitrates crises, effectively dormant as settled machinery since the takeover — and on 5 August 2026 received the civic organization Komity Mpiaro ny Tolona; he had previously consulted Norbert Lala Ratsirahonana, a former head of state once close to Rajoelina (Torch Media Africa, July–August 2026). Under this Test's standing rule, this is what considering another option when one becomes available looks like in its early form: the composition and selection method of the council remain unannounced, so the consideration is documented, the conclusion is not.
  • Smaller wobbles were answered without force. A teachers' strike running since 25 September 2025 was mediated by a member of the presidential council, Colonel Gervais Andriamiarisoa, and settled by a memorandum of understanding between the education minister and the teachers' unions (CIVICUS Monitor, 2026). On 6 May 2026, when residents of Antananarivo's Tsiadana neighbourhood protested repeated power outages spontaneously, security forces arrived, no incidents were reported, and power was restored thirty minutes after the protest began (CIVICUS Monitor, 2026; RFI). Lawyer and party founder Harosilavo Rakotoson, arrested on 24 August 2026 for "undermining state security" after podcast comments on corruption — no official reason was given — was released the next day following a spontaneous social-media mobilisation in his support (CIVICUS Monitor, 2026).

Evidence against:

  • The street that made him was answered with arrests. On 10 April 2026, dozens of young people protested in Antananarivo against the opaque transition process and the failure to dismantle institutions they regard as corrupt, demanding the dissolution of the Assembly, the HCC, and the CENI. On 12 April 2026 four youth activists — Herizo Andriamanantena, a known Gen Z Madagascar member, Carine Rakotomanantoanina, Rija Andriamalala, and Nomena Elia Ratsihorimanana — were arrested and accused of "criminal conspiracy," "threat to national security," and "destabilisation of the state" (ALLEGATIONs, unproven); Andriamanantena was reportedly held incommunicado for three days. They were released between 24 and 29 April 2026, one unconditionally and three under judicial supervision requiring them to report every fifteen days (CIVICUS Monitor, 2026; Amnesty International Urgent Action, 21 April 2026). Amnesty counts at least nine Gen Z leaders arrested in April 2026, some for being in a WhatsApp group or exchanging messages with an opposition figure or a whistleblower, and reports that some were subjected to acts that may amount to torture, including revolvers forced down their throats (Amnesty International, November 2026). Transparency International Madagascar's Ketakandriana Rafitoson named the pattern in the Guardian: "It is a pattern we saw under the previous administration and one that many hoped would be broken with the transition. So [last] Friday's protests were a test for this regime and they failed it" (The Guardian, via CIVICUS Monitor, 2026).
  • The demands underneath the April protests were refused a look. The protesters' specific institutional demand — dissolve the Assembly, the HCC, and the CENI as beholden to the former regime — was not adopted, and no documented engagement with it by the president appears in the record assembled here; the answer documented is the arrests. Under this Test's rule, an option declined with stated reasons is not against-evidence; an option answered with conspiracy charges is not a declining.
  • His own promised instrument for hearing the country was starved in his own house. The national consultation, launched 10 December 2025 under the FFKM churches with a stated six-month duration, was postponed several times; its operative timeline was only published in August 2026, running September 2026 to March 2027, and by 19 November 2026 only 8.33% of its allocated funds had been disbursed, with the UN, AU, and SADC expressing concern about the impact on the electoral timeline (CIVICUS Monitor, 2026; Madagascar-Tribune, 20 November 2026). The November meeting to unblock it is credited above; that it needed unblocking, eleven months in, in the presidency's own finance procedures, is the wobble he did not answer until it was a year old.

Why the grade lands here: The pattern splits cleanly by who is pushing. Pushback that arrives through institutions — the Assembly withdrawing his bills, the AU and SADC setting conditions, former presidents across the old divides, a court entertaining a petition against him — is answered with process, deadlines met, and doors opened. Pushback that arrives through the street — the same street, using the same methods, that carried him into the palace — is answered with incommunicado detention and conspiracy charges. A ruler who adjusts to referees and doubles down on crowds is neither entry whole; the grade is MIXED, and the April 2026 file is the weight on its failing side.

Helped: The Malagasy public and press, who kept the cybercrime and electronic-communication bills off the books in 2026; teachers, whose strike was mediated to a signed agreement; the consultation process itself, unblocked at the November 2026 meeting with the two-year commitment restated. Hurt: The April 2026 protesters and the nine-plus Gen Z leaders arrested that month, three of whom remained in pretrial detention at the November assessment (Test 4); Harosilavo Rakotoson, arrested overnight for podcast speech before release; the consultation's intended participants nationwide, whose promised hearing slipped a year inside the presidency's own procedures.

Test 4 — COUNTS EVERYONE

Does the accounting include the Gen Z protesters who made the transition, the old government's people, the regions, and the poor — or are some counted out?

GRADE: FAILS

Evidence for:

  • The consultation is designed, on paper, to count the whole country. The Refoundation programme's first phase is nationwide consultations to gather citizens' proposals on institutional reform, organized not by the ruling camp but by the FFKM, the council of Christian churches, and the programme publishes even the electoral model itself as an open question — direct universal suffrage in two rounds, a French-style indirect model, or a US-style indirect model (Refoundation programme, 28 February 2026; Ecofin Agency, March 2026). The electoral register is being rebuilt from scratch rather than inherited, with 13 to 14 million voters expected (electos.net, November 2026). Former presidents from every side of Madagascar's old divides — Ravalomanana, Rajaonarimampianina, Ratsirahonana — have been received and consulted (Torch Media Africa, 2026).
  • The transition's founding act counted the street's grievance as legitimate. CAPSAT's refusal to fire on protesters on 11 October 2025, and the transition's own framing of the uprising's demands — reliable electricity, clean water, an end to corruption — as the mandate of the Refoundation, put the protesters inside the account at the start (CIVICUS Lens, 2025; Refoundation programme, 2026).

Evidence against:

  • The movement that made the transition is now counted as a suspect class. At least nine Gen Z leaders were arrested in April 2026, some merely for membership in a WhatsApp group or messages exchanged with an opposition figure or whistleblower; three remained arbitrarily held in pretrial detention at November 2026, including Rakotoarijaona Vonimahery, known as Mahery — arrested at his workplace on 21 April 2026 by gendarmes in civilian clothes over a WhatsApp conversation about the transition and the lack of reforms, and kept, per Amnesty, in deplorable conditions in one of the country's most overcrowded prisons (Amnesty International, November 2026; CIVICUS Monitor, 2026). NGO director Harnelle Rakotobe of Ivorary faced repeated intimidation by the cybercrime unit and military personnel linked to the presidency, accused among other things of receiving funds to finance the president's assassination — on the basis, per Amnesty's Urgent Action, of her friendship with a relative of a former senator and her public speech about the transition (Amnesty International, 21 April 2026). The wife of a detained activist, Gabriella Ranjamalalasoa, was detained at Ivato International Airport and her passport confiscated to prevent her travelling to the 89th Ordinary Session of the African Commission on Human and Peoples' Rights (Amnesty International, November 2026).
  • The old camp is counted as a file, not as citizens. At least 20 people remained arbitrarily detained for politically motivated reasons at November 2026, including former presidential candidates Sylvain Rabetsaroana and Paul Rabary Andrianirina; dozens of former officials' family members have been forced into exile; government critics face harassment and travel restrictions, particularly those attempting to engage regional and international organizations (Amnesty International, November 2026). Whatever the old regime's documented corruption — and the audit's findings are on Test 1's FOR shelf — a count that reaches a man's family and drives them abroad has stopped counting persons and started counting lineages.
  • The land's defenders are counted out at the local level. Berthine Elignesoa, vice-president of a farmers' civil-society organization in the Bas-Mangoky region, was arrested on 9 May 2026 and sentenced on 7 July 2026 by the Morombe Court of First Instance to eighteen months in prison for "contempt of a magistrate," days after a television broadcast in which she criticized unjust court rulings in a land-allocation dispute affecting some 8,000 hectares of an African Development Bank–funded project; Malagasy land-rights organizations state the prosecution follows her activism on behalf of the farmers the project was meant to benefit (CIVICUS Monitor, 2026; Collectif TANY, CONCRET-S and SIF statement, 2026). The conviction is a court's, and is printed as a court's; its place in a pattern is what this Test counts.
  • The dead of the founding uprising are uncounted in the only ledger that could honour them. The UN's count stands separate and unreconciled: at least 22 killed and hundreds injured in September–October 2025 (UN OHCHR, 29 September 2025) — killed under Rajoelina's orders, as Randrianirina himself has said — and no investigation into the shootings has followed under the government the uprising installed (Amnesty International, November 2026). No competing official toll appears in the record assembled here; the two counts are not blended, and the absence of any accounting is itself the finding.
  • The budget counts the palace before the grievance. Amnesty records that salaries and the bureaucratic machinery swallowed the bulk of the 2026 national budget to the detriment of the services that brought the nation into the streets; health received about 6 per cent, roughly USD 5 per person for a year, less than under Rajoelina, while investment in the president's personal security is estimated in the millions of dollars and Russian security agents have been granted to him and other senior officials (Amnesty International, November 2026). Thirteen months in, the grievances remain unresolved on the monitors' record: the April and May 2026 protests in the capital were about the same water and electricity failures as September 2025's (Amnesty International, November 2026; CIVICUS Monitor, 2026).

Why the grade lands here: A transition counts everyone when the people who made it, the people who lost to it, and the people who merely live under it all appear in its account as persons. The documented record shows the opposite motion in each direction: the Gen Z movement counted as a conspiracy, the old camp counted by family and driven into exile, a land defender imprisoned after criticizing judges, the founding dead uninvestigated, and a budget that funds the presidency's security at multiples of the nation's health. The consultation and the rebuilt register are real counting machinery — aimed at a future electorate. In the present tense, this seat counts its friends and files everyone else.

Helped: Future voters, if the rebuilt register and the consultation complete — 13 to 14 million expected registrants, and a published choice of electoral models; the former presidents and civic organizations received into the Constitutional Council consultations. Hurt: Gen Z activists — at least nine arrested in April 2026, three still in pretrial detention in November 2026, Mahery among them; Harnelle Rakotobe and Arimamy Todisoa, threatened with the same charges; Gabriella Ranjamalalasoa, stopped at the airport; the at least 20 political detainees and the dozens of exiled family members of the old administration; Berthine Elignesoa, imprisoned for eighteen months; the families of the 22 dead of 2025, still without an investigation; and the users of Madagascar's water, power, and health systems, whose services the 2026 budget placed behind the bureaucracy's salaries and the president's security.

Test 5 — KEEPS HIS WORD OVER TIME

The transition's undertakings vs. the record since 17 October 2025, with dates; his predecessor's completed word stated as context.

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for (undertakings kept or credibly in motion):

  • Elections within two years — the central word, still inside its window and repeatedly restated. On 14 October 2025 he pledged a referendum within two years and elections to return to civilian rule; on 10 November 2025 he told Sky News elections would be held within 18 to 24 months after "refoundation and recovery"; in his first national address on 17 November 2025 he laid out the sequence — nationwide consultation, referendum, presidential election within two years. The Refoundation programme of 28 February 2026 fixed the presidential election for September 2027, inside the two-year bound; the CENI in May 2026 placed the referendum in June 2027 and the presidential election in October 2027, at the bound's edge (CIVICUS Monitor, 2026). On 19 November 2026 he publicly recommitted to elections within two years and constitutional order before the end of 2027 (Madagascar-Tribune, 20 November 2026). The two dates in circulation — September 2027 in his own programme, October 2027 in the commission's announcement — are printed as both appear in the record; either keeps the letter of the founding pledge, and neither has yet been met or broken. This grade is provisional precisely here.
  • A civilian government quickly — kept in form. He pledged a civilian government under the Council of the Presidency within two weeks of taking power; a civilian businessman, Herintsalama Rajaonarivelo, was appointed prime minister within days of the swearing-in and remains the government's head in the record as of November 2026 (CIVICUS Lens, 2025; Madagascar-Tribune, November 2026). The form was kept; that the council chairing it is military is graded in Test 2, not double-counted here.
  • The roadmap delivered to the referees on time. The AU's deadline of 28 February 2026 for a dialogue readiness report and transition roadmap was met the day it fell due, with the full 24-month programme published (Torch Media Africa, 2026).
  • Prosecute the corrupt — in motion through courts. The Court of Accounts audit was ordered on taking power; the PAC proceedings against the old regime's principals have advanced through an HCC jurisdictional ruling (28 October 2026), an opened trial (November 2026), and warrant requests placed before judges rather than executed by decree (23 November 2026) (Test 1). No verdicts exist; the word was to pursue, and the pursuit is documented.
  • The predecessor's word, as context for the seat: Rajoelina's completed record is the ground the transition stands on — a president who won re-election in 2023 in a vote the opposition largely boycotted, whose government answered the September 2025 protests over water and power with live fire (22 dead on the UN's count), who dissolved his own government on 29 September 2025 and appointed a general as prime minister on 6 October in a last attempt to hold the seat, and who fled when his own army corps refused his orders. The transition's undertakings were made against that record, and the street that extracted them is the constituency to whom they are owed.

Evidence against (undertakings broken, slipping, or hedged):

  • The consultation timetable — the sequence's first link, a year late. The consultation was launched on 10 December 2025 with a stated duration of six months; it was postponed repeatedly, its operative window became September 2026 to March 2027, and by November 2026 only 8.33% of its funds had been disbursed (CIVICUS Monitor, 2026; Madagascar-Tribune, 20 November 2026). The word was not merely that a consultation would exist but that it would found the new order in time for the 2027 votes; on the record, the founding instrument spent its first year mostly unheld — and, by November 2026, only 8.33 per cent funded, through his own administration's procedures.
  • The services word — failing in the present tense. The Refoundation's founding claim was that the grievances of September 2025 — water, electricity, corruption — would be answered. Thirteen months on, Amnesty's assessment is that they "remain unresolved," and the capital protested the same outages in April and May 2026 (Amnesty International, November 2026; CIVICUS Monitor, 2026). A promise about the future can be pending; a promise about the taps and the grid is graded by the taps and the grid.
  • The candidacy word — hedged, neither kept nor broken. Asked by France 24 on 5 December 2025 whether he would stand in the election he is organizing, he answered: "Right now, I am working, and I am not thinking about being a candidate at this time" (France 24, 5 December 2025). Headlines rendered this as a renunciation; the sentence is not one — it is a statement about the present, with the future expressly left open, and no instrument in the transition's documents bars the chairman of the Council of the Presidency from the ballot he is scheduling. The file is recorded as open: the word that would settle it has not been given.
  • The roadmap's internal dates have drifted once already. Phase one of the 28 February 2026 programme runs consultations from March 2026 to January 2027; the consultations' actual published window is September 2026 to March 2027 — a six-month slip inside the programme's first phase, absorbed so far by the programme's own warning that "implementation deadlines may be revised" (Refoundation programme, 2026; Ecofin Agency, March 2026). A timetable that pre-authorizes its own revision is a weaker word than the one given to Sky News.

Why the grade lands here: The transition's central undertaking — referendum and election inside two years — is not yet due, has been restated at every checkpoint including November 2026, and still has a published path to being kept; that is why this Test is not failed. Around that centre, the supporting words are slipping in the present tense: the consultation a year behind its own launch promise and starved of funds, the services unanswered, the candidacy left deliberately open, the roadmap's phases quietly re-dated. The grade is MIXED, and it is the most provisional grade in this dossier: September and October 2027 will grade it finally, one way or the other.

Helped: Voters and the AU/SADC referees, who hold a published roadmap delivered on deadline and a terminal date restated in November 2026; the courts, whose processes the prosecution word has been routed through. Hurt: The consultation's intended participants, whose founding hearing slipped a year; households still living the outages the Refoundation was promised to answer; and the transition's own credibility — every hedged word (the candidacy) and slipped date (the consultation) is spent from the same account the 2027 election will need to draw on.

Overall Summary

One law: FAILS · Without gripping: FAILS · The wobble: MIXED · Counts everyone: FAILS · His word: MIXED. All five grades provisional, by the nature of the seat.

Michael Randrianirina is the second consecutive Malagasy ruler in this series to arrive through CAPSAT — the unit that installed Rajoelina in 2009 installed his successor in 2025, and the colonel it carried in had himself spent the winter of 2023–24 in Rajoelina's prisons on a coup allegation. He took the seat in the one configuration the no-good-choice clause exists for: a fled president, an impeaching Assembly, a court handing him the functions, and loyalist gendarmerie still in the streets. The clause covers those seventy-two hours. It does not cover the thirteen months since, and the thirteen months have a shape. On Test 1, the law runs in one direction — audits bounded at his own door, prosecutions and pretrial detentions for the old camp, exile for its families, and no investigation of the filmed shootings of the very crowds that seated him; the PAC proceedings are real and run through courts, and they do not cure the direction. On Test 2, the referees demonstrably function — an Assembly that withdrew his government's surveillance bills, a court that ruled on his referendum's sequencing and heard a petition against his own tenure — yet they function inside a frame he announced suspended, under a sovereign council of officers, with the electoral commission's membership refilled by his decree and detainees' releases bargained with personally; the grip is structural, and the functioning referees are the file that can move the grade. On Test 3, the split is the story: institutions that push are answered with process — the AU roadmap delivered on its deadline, former presidents consulted, bills withdrawn after outcry, a blocked consultation unblocked in public — while the street that pushes is answered as his predecessor answered it, with conspiracy charges and incommunicado cells, which is why the grade is MIXED and why Transparency International Madagascar's verdict on the April protests ("a test for this regime and they failed it") is printed in full. On Test 4, the count excludes the counters: the Gen Z movement prosecuted as a WhatsApp conspiracy, three of its people still in pretrial detention, the old camp counted by lineage into exile, a land defender imprisoned, the 22 dead of the founding uprising uninvestigated, and a 2026 budget that funds the presidency's security ahead of the nation's health at roughly five dollars a person. On Test 5, the word that matters most is still alive — referendum and election inside two years, restated in November 2026 with dates (September 2027 in his programme, October 2027 in his commission's) that both fit the pledge — while the words around it slip: the consultation a year late and 8.33% funded, the taps and grid unanswered, the candidacy hedged in a sentence built to be outgrown.

The two axes cross in this dossier in all four of their shapes, and are recorded independently. Kept his word and it helped: the roadmap and dialogue report delivered to the AU and SADC on the 28 February 2026 deadline, and the civilian prime minister appointed within days as pledged — process words, kept on schedule, that kept the transition legible to its referees. Kept his word and it still hurt: the prosecution pledge — "pursue corrupt individuals and thieves" — is being kept, and its keeping, as documented by Amnesty, is the instrument by which the old camp's families have been searched, detained, and exiled; the keeping earns its Test 5 credit and the hurt stands in the ledger in full. Went against his word and it helped: the dissolution he announced on 14 October 2025 — constitution suspended, Senate, HCC, and CENI dissolved — was not carried through; the referees survived his announcement of their abolition, and everything on Test 2's FOR shelf, from the bills' withdrawal to the referendum ruling, exists because the announced act was quietly abandoned. Broke his word and it hurt: the consultation, promised as the founding act and launched with a six-month duration in December 2025, slipped a year inside his own administration's procedures while the grievances it was meant to hear were being protested again in the capital's streets — and answered there with arrests. The open files that will move these grades are named: whether the CENI he reconstitutes runs a referendum and an election the losers accept; whether the Constitutional Council he is consulting into existence is built by consensus or by appointment; whether the September/October 2027 dates hold; whether the candidacy hedge resolves into a renunciation or a campaign; whether the 2025 shootings are ever investigated; and whether Mahery and the remaining detainees are tried, released, or simply kept. The seat is a promise with a deadline. The deadline is the grade.
