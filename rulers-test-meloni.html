The Ruler's Test — Giorgia Meloni, Prime Minister of Italy

Subject: Giorgia Meloni, President of the Council of Ministers of Italy (in office since 22 October 2022); leader of Brothers of Italy (Fratelli d'Italia). On 4 September 2026 her government became the longest-serving administration in the history of the Italian Republic (Reuters factbox, 31 August 2026). Graded: November 2026, on the documented public record. Method: Each test is graded only on the evidence listed under it. Both shelves are shown — the strongest evidence that she passes, then the strongest evidence that she fails. Allegations are labeled ALLEGATION and never stated as verdicts. Contested items are named as contested, with who disputes them. Seat-holders and public figures are named; people below the public eye appear by role only, unless the documented record itself establishes their wrongdoing. Grades: HOLDS / FAILS / MIXED / NOT ENOUGH EVIDENCE.

Test 1 — GIVES BY ONE LAW

Same rules for allies and opponents, insiders and outsiders; no favorites at the source.

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • The criminal law reached her own camp, in open court, while she governed. Her Deputy Prime Minister Matteo Salvini was prosecuted for kidnapping over the 2019 Open Arms blockade — the Senate authorized the trial, prosecutors sought six years — and a Palermo court acquitted him on the merits on 20 December 2024 ("the fact does not exist"); the Supreme Court upheld the acquittal on 17 December 2025, rejecting the prosecutors' direct appeal (Reuters, 20 December 2024 and 17 December 2025). A system that tries the deputy premier and acquits him on the facts is applying one law in both directions.
  • Her Justice Undersecretary Andrea Delmastro was prosecuted and convicted — at first instance and again on appeal — receiving a suspended eight-month sentence for disclosing official secrets in the Cospito case (Italian Times review of Fratelli d'Italia legal cases, 2026; EUalive, March 2026). The prosecution of a sitting undersecretary of the very ministry of justice proceeded without documented interference.
  • When she finally moved, she moved against her own: in March 2026, after the referendum defeat, Meloni publicly asked Tourism Minister Daniela Santanchè to resign over her legal troubles, obtained the resignation within roughly a day, and accepted Delmastro's resignation the same week (DevDiscourse/Reuters reporting, March 2026; UIS Journal, 26 March 2026).
  • On state-company appointments, her stated standard was "competence, not political affiliation," and the 2023 round kept Eni's Claudio Descalzi — a pre-existing appointee — for a fourth term while installing experienced corporate managers at Enel and Leonardo; in February 2026 her government signaled continuity and further renewals for the same CEOs rather than rotation of loyalists (Reuters analysis, 13 April 2023; Reuters, 25 February 2026).

Evidence against:

  • For over a year, the standard for insiders was visibly slower than for outsiders. Santanchè remained Minister of Tourism while on trial for false accounting in the Visibilia case, facing a possible referral for aggravated fraud against the social-security agency INPS over €126,468 in COVID wage-support funds, and under investigation for fraudulent bankruptcy in the Bioera collapse — all ALLEGATIONs, which she denies — and she left office only in March 2026, after the referendum was lost, when Meloni publicly requested it (Italian Times, 2026; DevDiscourse, March 2026). Opposition parties argue the timing shows the removal waited until it was politically costless; the government frames it as respect for the presumption of innocence (UIS Journal, 26 March 2026 — CONTESTED as to motive).
  • Delmastro likewise kept the justice undersecretary post for more than a year after his first-instance conviction, and through his appeal conviction; his March 2026 resignation was triggered by a separate scandal — his founding stake in a restaurant company co-owned with the daughter of a businessman definitively convicted of mafia-aggravated fictitious asset registration. Delmastro is not under investigation in that probe and says he did not know his partner's background; he divested fully in February 2026. Until hours before the resignation, the government — including Justice Minister Carlo Nordio — had ruled out any step back (EUalive, March 2026).
  • The 2023 state-company round was, on Reuters' documented account, a coalition partition negotiated among the three governing parties: Enel's new chairman "owed his appointment to Berlusconi," its CEO had Salvini's backing, and Meloni dropped her own preferred candidates to keep coalition peace (Reuters analysis, 13 April 2023). Competence was served; so was the spoils table — both are on the record.
  • On public media, the Media Freedom Rapid Response consortium (March 2026 mission) found that government influence over RAI — politicized appointments, axed shows, departing journalists — amounts in its view to a violation of Article 5 of the European Media Freedom Act, and that "the scale and intensity of the current government's overhaul stands out in modern Italian history." RAI and the government dispute the characterization (MFRR, March 2026; C21Media report of RAI's rebuttal). The finding is an expert consortium's assessment, CONTESTED by RAI — recorded here at that strength.

Test 2 — HOLDS WITHOUT GRIPPING

Do courts and magistrates, RAI/public media, the Bank of Italy, the regions, and her own coalition partners keep their autonomy — or is independence punished and control centralized?

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • The judiciary's autonomy is not theoretical under Meloni; it has repeatedly bitten her government and survived. Rome's courts refused to validate the Albania-protocol detentions from October 2024 onward; the Constitutional Court struck down key parts of her coalition's differentiated-autonomy law, leaving it "in limbo" (Reuters factbox, 31 August 2026); and the magistrates' own convictions of her undersecretary (Test 1) stand. No judge is documented to have been removed, transferred, or sanctioned for ruling against her.
  • The Bank of Italy kept its voice: Governor Fabio Panetta used his May 2026 annual address to warn publicly that Italy's underinvestment in human capital is driving a "self-reinforcing cycle of decline" — a direct criticism of a core national failure on her watch — with no documented retaliation (Bank of Italy address, 29 May 2026, as reported).
  • Her coalition partners keep their own spin. Salvini and Tajani hold the deputy-premierships and real portfolios; Meloni's documented method is mediation — "granting Deputy Prime Ministers Matteo Salvini and Antonio Tajani small victories here and there" — and in the 2023 appointments she abandoned her own favorites rather than impose them on her partners (Reuters analysis, April 2023; American Purpose/Persuasion review, October 2026).
  • The premierato — direct election of the prime minister, her "mother of all reforms" — was not forced through: after Senate first approval in June 2024 it stalled in the Chamber, expert hearings had not advanced since July 2025, and it will not be enacted before the next election (European Commission 2026 Italy Rule of Law Report; Reuters factbox, 31 August 2026). The centralizing instrument exists as a draft, not as a grip.

Evidence against:

  • The justice reform she did pass through parliament would have restructured judicial self-government at its root: separating judge and prosecutor careers, splitting the CSM into two councils whose magistrate members are drawn by lot, and creating a new disciplinary court — a design the National Association of Magistrates opposed with a one-day strike joined by over 80% of its members, arguing it weakens independence and increases political influence over prosecutors (Reuters, 19 February and 11 March 2026). The government's counter — that sortition breaks the factional "clans" exposed by the 2019 Palamara wiretap scandal, a real documented scandal — is also on the record (Reuters, 11 March 2026). Voters settled the question in March 2026 (Test 3); the intent to restructure the magistrates' house is the documented part.
  • RAI is the clearest grip. In January 2025 the RAI chief executive circulated a directive designating "editorial managers" for all national programmes, which the RAI journalists' union USIGRAI called an attempt to control the editorial line (European Parliament written question E-000339/2025). RAI journalists struck in May 2024 over what they called "suffocating control." The parliamentary oversight commission (Vigilanza) then collapsed in 2025–26: its president and every opposition member resigned, accusing the majority of holding the broadcaster "hostage" for almost two years over the blocked RAI presidency appointment; the majority answers that the two-thirds rule was cynically used to block its candidate (Italy News/ANSA reporting, 2026). Whoever is right about the blockage, the oversight body meant to guarantee the broadcaster's pluralism ceased to function on her watch.
  • The October 2026 electoral law (Law 180/2026, approved 8 October 2026, 227–164) rewrites the rules of the next election in the incumbent coalition's favor: a fully proportional system plus a bonus of 70 deputies and 35 senators to any pre-declared alliance reaching 42% of the vote, with signature requirements for new parties quadrupled from 1,500 to 6,000. The opposition calls it a "scam law" and has vowed a Constitutional Court challenge; Meloni defends it as a stability guarantee and had warned her coalition that defeat of the bill could open a government crisis (EU Insider, October 2026; CIVICUS/LUISS interview, November 2026; Law 180/2026 text). A rule change that converts 42% of votes into an absolute majority, enacted by the coalition it is designed to benefit, is a grip on the board itself — contested in court, not yet adjudicated.

Test 3 — ANSWERS THE WOBBLE

When courts, voters, markets, or events push back, does she adjust openly — or deny and double down?

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • The market wobble she answered completely. Facing the 2022 spread crisis inheritance, her governments ran primary surpluses and cut the deficit from 7.1% of GDP (2023) to 3.4% (2024) to 3.1% (2025), with 2.9% projected for 2026 — inside the EU's 3% limit for the first time since 2019. The BTP–Bund spread narrowed from 251 basis points in September 2022 to 59 by January 2026, the lowest since 2008; S&P raised Italy to BBB+ in April 2025, Fitch followed in September 2025, and Moody's raised Italy to Baa2 in November 2025, its first upgrade in 23 years, citing political stability and prudent fiscal management (QNB Economics commentary, 15 November 2026; Fitch and Moody's rating actions, 2025). When the 2023 bank windfall tax rattled lenders, the government watered it down rather than fight the market (Mace News, 2023).
  • The voters' wobble she answered formally and in the open. When the justice referendum failed on 22–23 March 2026 — roughly 54% No to 46% Yes — Meloni conceded the result the same day, called it a "lost opportunity," said she would respect the voters' decision, and governed on; she had announced beforehand that she would not resign in defeat, and did not (Reuters, 23 March 2026; referendum synthesis, March 2026). The reform died with the vote; there was no attempt to re-enact it by another door in the same form.
  • On Albania, after the courts' repeated refusals, the government adjusted the instrument rather than defying the judges: a March 2025 decree converted the Albanian facilities from sea-rescue processing centers into ordinary detention/return centers also usable for irregular migrants already in Italy (Decree-Law 37/2025), and the legal fight was carried through the Court of Justice of the EU — where Advocate General Emiliou's opinion of 23 April 2026 held the protocol compatible in principle with EU law, a partial vindication the government obtained by litigation, not by pressure on judges (Reuters, 23 April 2026).

Evidence against:

  • The adjustment on Albania was preceded by an attempt to legislate around the judges: after Rome's courts refused to validate detentions in October 2024 on safe-country grounds, the government issued Decree-Law 158/2024 writing its own safe-country list into law — and the Court of Justice ruled on 1 August 2025 (joined cases C-758/24 and C-759/24) that the mechanism was unlawful, because a state may not designate a country safe where it is not safe for the whole population and designations must be open to judicial scrutiny (CJEU judgment, 1 August 2025; Mondo Internazionale legal review, 2026). Meloni's office called that ruling "surprising" and said it "weakens policies to combat mass illegal immigration" (PM's office statement, August 2025).
  • Her rhetoric toward the wobble's source is documented and repeated: in February 2026, after a Palermo court ordered €76,000 compensation to the NGO Sea-Watch, Meloni asked in a video message whether "whatever law is passed… a politicised section of the judiciary is ready to obstruct it," and senior ministers urged a Yes referendum vote as the answer to "a dysfunctional judiciary" (Reuters, 19 February 2026). President Mattarella appealed for institutional calm; the magistrates' association answers that courts are "simply applying the law" (Reuters, February 2026). Answering a ruling is legitimate; framing the referees as a faction, while campaigning to restructure their house, is the documented pattern.
  • On the fiscal wobble, one seam: the 2027 budget plans the deficit back up to 3.4% of GDP for defense and energy spending in the election year, and S&P (13 November 2026) noted debt stabilization will be "less pronounced" as a result — discipline held for four years, loosening as the vote approaches (S&P affirmation note, November 2026; QNB, November 2026).

Test 4 — COUNTS EVERYONE

Does the accounting include the whole flock — migrants and asylum seekers, the South, young people, her opponents' voters, minorities — or are some counted out?

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • Legal migrants are counted in at scale, on her government's own numbers: Italy issued 426,956 new residence permits in 2025, up 47.2% on 2024, under expanded labor-migration quotas (Interior Ministry data, as compiled November 2026). The strategy distinguishes sharply between irregular arrivals — which fell from 157,651 (2023) to 66,617 (2024) to 66,296 (2025), and halved again to 28,510 by 19 November 2026 — and legal channels, which were widened (Interior Ministry dashboard, November 2026; ANSA/Viminale annual assessment, 2025).
  • The South was given a named instrument: the single Special Economic Zone (ZES unica) for the Mezzogiorno, replacing the fragmented southern zones with one tax-credit regime for investment across the whole South — the government's flagship answer to the regional divide (government legislation, in force 2024).
  • Her opponents' voters were counted when they spoke: the March 2026 referendum No — carried heavily in the South, with Campania rejecting her reform by over 65% (Interior Ministry results via Eligendo, March 2026) — was accepted without any move to discount or rerun it (Test 3).

Evidence against:

  • Asylum seekers were counted offshore, at high cost and low use. The Albania centers — estimated at €650 million over five years (ANSA, reported by InfoMigrants) — stood empty for months after the courts blocked the transfers; an ActionAid/University of Bari study found construction costs seven times those of comparable facilities in Italy, and a complaint was filed with the Court of Auditors (Mondo Internazionale, 2026). The human ledger also runs: an estimated 1,342 people died or went missing in the central Mediterranean in 2025, one for every 49 who arrived (UNHCR/ReliefWeb Italy Sea Arrivals Overview 2025). The government's NGO-rescue restrictions (the 2023 Piantedosi decree, requiring rescue ships to head to port after a single rescue) remain criticized by rescue organizations as costing lives — a characterization the government disputes (InfoMigrants, 2024).
  • Young Italians are leaving the count. ISTAT records 141,000 citizens emigrating in 2024 and 109,000 in 2025 (provisional); the 25–34 age band lost a net 161,000 people between 2019 and 2024, 78,000 of them graduates, and CNEL prices the education of the 630,000 young people who left in 2011–2024 at €159.5 billion of lost human capital (ISTAT migration reports, 2026; CNEL, September 2026). Bank of Italy Governor Panetta's May 2026 warning — that low pay and underinvestment keep driving talent out — was delivered to her government, not by it. Youth unemployment (20.3% for under-25s, August 2026) remains well above the EU average, though the NEET rate improved to 13.3% in 2025 from 15.2% in 2024 (Eurostat/ISTAT data).
  • The South's own graduates keep draining north: between 2019 and 2025 southern Italy lost 150,000 graduates aged 25–34, over four in five of them to the Centre-North (ISTAT, 2026). Her coalition's differentiated-autonomy law — which southern critics and the opposition argued would lock in the funding gap between rich and poor regions — was passed in 2024 and then gutted by the Constitutional Court; the ZES exists, but the documented flows have not yet turned (Reuters factbox, August 2026; ISTAT, 2026).
  • The poorest lost their floor and got a narrower one: the Citizenship Income was abolished and replaced by a more restricted inclusion allowance — a deliberate policy choice her government defends as targeting, and the opposition attacks as counting out the working poor (Mace News, 2023; budget records). The promised €1,000 minimum pension (Test 5) was not delivered to the elderly poor who were promised it.

Test 5 — KEEPS HER WORD OVER TIME

Campaign promises vs. the record, with dates; her own explanations on the shelf.

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • The central promise of the 2022 campaign — control of irregular migration — was substantially delivered on its own metric: sea arrivals fell from 157,651 in 2023 to roughly 66,300 in both 2024 and 2025 and to 28,510 by mid-November 2026, with repatriations in 2026 (7,813 by 17 November) the highest since 2018 (Interior Ministry dashboard, November 2026). The means differed from the slogan (below), but the numbers moved the way she said they would.
  • The stability promise was kept in full view of the markets: she pledged in 2022 that Italy would be governable and fiscally credible, and delivered the Republic's longest-serving government, three rating upgrades, and a spread at 2008 lows (Test 3). Her own explanation — that credibility is earned by not spending what Italy does not have — matches the budgets actually passed (budget laws 2023–2026).
  • On the EU and the euro, the word she gave in the 2022 campaign was kept: the Brothers of Italy programme and her campaign statements pledged Atlanticism, NATO loyalty, and no euro exit, and in office she made Brussels her first foreign trip, built a working relationship with Commission President von der Leyen, sustained military support to Ukraine against the sympathies of parts of her own coalition, and never picked the fights with the EU that were predicted (Britannica, updated 10 November 2026; IISS analysis, 2024).

Evidence against:

  • The euro/EU reversal is real — it is just older than the campaign. Before office she called the euro a wrong currency for Italy, led a Eurosceptic bloc (president of the European Conservatives and Reformists from 2020), and in 2018 congratulated Vladimir Putin on his re-election and praised Russia as a defender of European and Christian values (IISS, 2024; ORF analysis). In office she became, in IISS's words, a "moderate, pro-EU, pro-NATO Atlanticist." Her explanation — that she is a "euro-realist," that governing taught her the constraints, and that she never promised an exit she did not attempt — is on the record (Britannica, 2026). Voters who backed the earlier Meloni got a different foreign policy than the one she had sold for a decade; voters who backed the 2022 programme got what it said. Both facts stand.
  • The flat tax — a signature pledge of a general flat tax — was not delivered: what arrived was an extension of the flat regime for the self-employed and incremental IRPEF bracket cuts, not the universal flat tax of the campaign; opposition promise-audits mark it "partially fulfilled" at best (coalition programme 2022; budget laws 2023–2026; promise audit, November 2026).
  • Pensions: the coalition promised to abolish the Fornero pension law and raise minimum pensions to €1,000. Neither happened. The Fornero law remains in force — in the 2027 budget process her own party confirmed there will be no repeal, the retirement age has risen, and the Opzione Donna early-retirement scheme was eliminated (government budget statements and opposition responses, October–November 2026). Her defense — that the numbers never allowed it — is the same fiscal discipline credited in Test 3, applied against her own voters' expectation.
  • The "naval blockade" of migration, promised in the campaign, was never attempted; what replaced it — deals with Tunisia and Libya and the Albania protocol — her critics call a quieter name for the same goal and her defenders call the promise kept by other means (InfoMigrants, 2024; Interior Ministry data). The slogan was specific; the substitute was not announced as a substitute.
  • The "mother of all reforms," the premierato, was abandoned without a vote of the people or a full parliamentary decision — allowed to stall in committee after 2024 and formally undeliverable before the 2027 election (Reuters factbox, 31 August 2026). The October 2026 electoral law, which requires coalitions to name their prime-ministerial candidate in advance, delivers a piece of its effect by ordinary statute — a substitution the opposition contests as the premierato by the back door (CIVICUS/LUISS, November 2026).

Overall Summary

Meloni's sheet is the most mixed in this series so far, and the pattern is consistent: where an institution could answer back — the markets, the courts, the voters in a referendum — she adjusted, complied, or conceded, and Italy's fiscal record under her (deficit 7.1% to 3.1% of GDP, three upgrades, the longest-serving government of the Republic) is documented fact. Where the grip was hers to take quietly — RAI's editorial line, the timing of her own ministers' resignations, the 2026 electoral law's 42% bonus — she took it, and the Santanchè and Delmastro files show insiders' law running on political time. Her word split by era: the 2022 programme's core (migration numbers, stability, Atlanticism) was substantially kept; the older sovereigntist promises and the domestic pledges on flat tax, Fornero, and €1,000 pensions were not. Grades: One Law — MIXED. Holds Without Gripping — MIXED. Answers the Wobble — MIXED. Counts Everyone — MIXED. Keeps Her Word — MIXED.
