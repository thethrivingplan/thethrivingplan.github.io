The Ruler's Test — Scott Bessent, Secretary of the Treasury (United States)

Subject: Scott K. H. Bessent — the 79th Secretary of the Treasury, in office since 28 January 2025, and still in office at grading: Treasury's own press releases name him Secretary on 6 November 2026 (readout of his call with China's Vice Premier He Lifeng on implementation of the Busan arrangement), and he was giving on-record interviews as Secretary on 20–22 November 2026 (CNBC, 20 November 2026; CBS Face the Nation, 22 November 2026). The premise was verified before grading, because the series grades whoever actually holds the seat: there has been no change of hands in 2025–2026. President-elect Donald Trump announced Bessent's nomination on 22 November 2024; Bessent, a global macro investor (founder of Key Square Group, formerly of Soros Fund Management) and a Trump campaign economic adviser and fundraiser, testified before the Senate Finance Committee on 16 January 2025; the committee advanced the nomination 16–11 on 21 January 2025; the Senate confirmed him on 27 January 2025 by 68–29 (Congress.gov, nomination PN11-1; Senate roll call vote 19, 119th Congress); he was sworn in on 28 January 2025. His is therefore a full, ongoing tenure of roughly 22 months, graded in progress — not a completed tenure — and the file states that where it matters. The seat graded is the standard executive seat for the nation's finances: managing federal debt and the debt limit, overseeing tax administration through the Internal Revenue Service (a Treasury bureau), running sanctions and economic statecraft through the Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC), chairing the Financial Stability Oversight Council (FSOC), and stewardship of the dollar and the Treasury market. Where the record belongs to the President or to Congress rather than to Bessent personally, the dossier says so; where Bessent was the officer who executed, defended, or publicly owned a decision, it is graded as his. Graded: November 2026, on the documented public record through 24 November 2026. Method: The ethic, printed verbatim: "How you play the game matters. Play by the rules. Stay true to your word. And if another option becomes available, at least consider it." Each test is graded only on the evidence listed under it. Both shelves are shown — the strongest evidence that he passes, then the strongest evidence that he fails. Allegations are labeled ALLEGATION and never stated as verdicts. Contested items are named as contested, with who disputes them. Conflicting figures (debt, deficit, and tariff-revenue numbers from different sources) are printed as separate sourced figures, never blended. Carrying out the platform the voters elected is not inherently a failure and is never graded as one: it is evidence on the FOR shelf of Test 5, and marks are lost only for HOW it was done — a second law for his own side (Test 1), gripping the referees (Test 2), counting out those who did not elect his administration (Test 4). Seat-holders and public figures are named; people below the public eye appear by role only, unless the documented record itself establishes their wrongdoing. Grades: HOLDS / FAILS / MIXED / NOT ENOUGH EVIDENCE. This record is correctable: new evidence changes grades, in the open.

Test 1 — GIVES BY ONE LAW

Same rules for allies and opponents, insiders and outsiders — no favorites at the source.

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • The sanctions instrument — the sharpest one-law tool in the seat — was applied across the ally/adversary line, not only against declared enemies. Under Bessent, OFAC's maximum-pressure campaign against Iran reached facilitators in allied and partner jurisdictions: on the record assembled here, Treasury designated eight Canada-based money services businesses enabling the Iranian regime (Treasury press release, November 2026, coordinated with Canada's FINTRAC), and Bessent personally cited action against an Egyptian bank operating in Dubai that he said facilitated $1.8 billion for Iran, a major Turkish bank, and international branches of a leading Russian bank (Bessent remarks at Heritage Foundation Forum 2026, 13 November 2026, via contemporaneous reporting). Canada, Türkiye, Egypt, and the UAE are partners, not adversaries; the rule was applied inside the tent as well as outside it.
  • The sanctions calendar itself was broad and rules-based in form: designations ran continuously from February 2025 onward against Iranian oil and shadow-fleet networks, North Korean IT-worker fraud, ransomware infrastructure (jointly with Australia and the United Kingdom, 11 February 2025), cartel and fentanyl networks, and China-based hackers (OFAC press-release index, February–March 2025 and continuing). Operation Economic Outcast, launched August 2026, extended the same instrument to Iranian digital-asset exchanges (Nobitex, Wallex, Bitpin, and Ramzinex designated 2 June 2026; Aban Tether and Shelbit designated 7 August 2026 — Treasury press releases, 2026).
  • Where a court ruled that a prior designation exceeded the law, Treasury complied rather than carving out an exception for itself: OFAC delisted the Tornado Cash mixer on 21 March 2025, following the Fifth Circuit's 26 November 2024 ruling that OFAC had exceeded its statutory authority (OFAC, 21 March 2025; Legal 500 sanctions update, 24 March 2025). One law, applied to the government's own earlier action.
  • The debt itself was accounted for under one set of books, audited clean: the Government Accountability Office found the Bureau of the Fiscal Service's Schedules of Federal Debt for fiscal years 2025 and 2026 "fairly presented in all material respects," with effective internal control and no reportable noncompliance (GAO-27-108795, November 2026). Whatever the fiscal policy, the counting of it was not rigged by constituency.

Evidence against:

  • Tax law enforcement — the one law every ordinary earner lives under — contracted sharply and unevenly on his watch. The Treasury Inspector General for Tax Administration (TIGTA) records that the IRS workforce fell from approximately 103,000 in January 2025 to approximately 74,000 in January 2026 (a 30 percent reduction), that the FY2026 enacted budget cut IRS funding by approximately 20 percent, and that the IRS started 30 percent fewer taxpayer examinations in FY2025 than in FY2024, with examination closures and proposed assessments in the Small Business/Self-Employed Division notably declining as the cuts landed (TIGTA, FY2027 Major Management Challenges, 15 October 2026). IRS Criminal Investigation's investigations into abusive tax schemes — the schemes used by sophisticated, wealthy evaders — fell 63 percent in 2025 to a level roughly 40 percent below any other year in the past decade (IRS-CI 2025 Annual Report, as cited in a letter from Senators Whitehouse, Wyden, Warren, and colleagues to Bessent, 28 January 2026). The senators' characterization of the cause is their own; the underlying IRS-CI and TIGTA numbers are the agencies' own.
  • A reporting law was split in two by nationality of the company. Treasury's own 2025 year-in-review lists as an accomplishment: "Removed BOI reporting requirement under Corporate Transparency Act for domestic entities and individuals" (Treasury, Year in Review 2025). Beneficial-ownership reporting — an anti-money-laundering rule — thereafter applied to foreign entities while domestic entities, the larger population, were exempted: one transparency law for outsiders, a lighter one for insiders, by design and announced as such.
  • Taxpayer confidentiality, the statutory promise (26 U.S.C. § 6103) that tax information is shared with no one except under specified exceptions, was applied selectively against one group. On 7 April 2025 the IRS and the Department of Homeland Security entered a memorandum of understanding to share taxpayer addresses with Immigration and Customs Enforcement (MOU of 7 April 2025, as recited in Community Economic Development Center v. Bessent, D. Mass.). The IRS's own Chief Risk Officer, Dottie Romo, declared under oath on 11 February 2026 that the agency had disclosed current addresses even where ICE-supplied data contained entries such as "Failed to Provide," "Unknown Address," and "NA NA," and in response to addresses that were jails or detention facilities (Romo declaration, Center for Taxpayer Rights v. IRS, D.D.C., 11 February 2026; Washington Post reporting, 11 February 2026). The U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia concluded in November 2025 that the IRS's disclosure of taxpayer data to ICE was unlawful, and the District of Massachusetts found DHS's requests unlawful in multiple respects and enjoined DHS from using the data (rulings as recited in the Congressional Hispanic Caucus letter to Bessent, 26 February 2026, and the D. Mass. memorandum of 5 February 2026). Bessent is a named defendant in that litigation in both his capacities — Secretary and then-Acting Commissioner. A confidentiality law that holds for most filers and bends for immigrant filers is two laws.

Why this grade: The sanctions and debt-accounting record shows a real one-law streak — partners' banks designated, a court's ruling against his own department obeyed, the books audited clean. The tax record shows the opposite shape at larger domestic scale: enforcement against complex evasion collapsed by the agencies' own counts while confidentiality protection was withdrawn from one class of filer, in disclosures a federal court concluded were unlawful. Both shelves are documented; neither cancels the other. That is MIXED.

Helped / Hurt ledger:

  • Helped: U.S. and allied enforcement against Iranian, North Korean, and cartel finance networks, and the public that relies on sanctions meaning the same thing in Toronto and Dubai as in Tehran; honest taxpayers to the extent the audited debt schedules stayed clean.
  • Hurt: Immigrant taxpayers — including, on the IRS Chief Risk Officer's sworn account, people misidentified through defective ICE data — whose statutory confidentiality was breached; honest wage earners and small businesses, who kept filing under full enforcement while investigations of abusive schemes fell 63 percent (IRS-CI, 2025); future taxpayers, who inherit the revenue the unworked tax gap represents (the IRS's last published tax-gap estimate was nearly $700 billion for 2022, as cited in Senate correspondence, June 2025).

Test 2 — HOLDS WITHOUT GRIPPING

Do the courts, the electoral commission, the media, the prosecution, and the central bank keep their autonomy — or is independence punished and control centralized? For this seat the referees are the Federal Reserve, the IRS's Senate-confirmed leadership, the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau, FSOC's member regulators, and the Treasury payment systems themselves.

GRADE: FAILS

Evidence for:

  • On the Federal Reserve — the referee this seat most often grips — Bessent repeatedly acted as a brake, not a hand. When President Trump pressed for Chair Jerome Powell's removal, Bessent urged the President not to fire him, warning in an April 2025 interview that early removal could trigger financial instability (Wall Street Journal reporting, 2025; Politico interview, 17 April 2025). In July 2025, with the President publicly calling for Powell's resignation, Bessent said: "There's nothing that tells me that he should step down right now. His term ends in May. If he wants to see that through, I think he should" (Fox Business, July 2025). He described the Fed's monetary-policy autonomy as "a jewel box that should be walled off" (Bessent post on X, July 2025, via contemporaneous reporting). Powell served out his term.
  • He accepted a statutory limit on his own grip when it expired: Bessent served as Acting IRS Commissioner from 8 August 2025; the Federal Vacancies Reform Act's 210-day window expired on 6 March 2026, and the IRS then stated publicly that his "service as Acting Commissioner of the IRS under the Federal Vacancies Act has expired, and he has not served in that capacity since that time" (IRS press release, March 2026, via Fox Rothschild tax-controversy analysis, March 2026). The limit was named and observed, albeit at the deadline and after senators raised it (below).
  • FSOC under his chairmanship continued to meet, publish, and speak in its members' own voices: its 2025 annual report was approved unanimously and still names Treasury-market fragility as a central risk, recommending central clearing and monitoring of stablecoin effects on the Treasury market (FSOC 2025 Annual Report, December 2025; FSOC minutes, 4 June and 10 September 2025).

Evidence against:

  • Within days of taking office he personally took the chair of an independent regulator and stopped its work. On 3 February 2025 Bessent was named acting director of the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau — a bureau Congress deliberately funded outside the appropriations process to insulate it — and ordered staff to immediately halt rulemaking and enforcement work pending review (CFPB chronology, February 2025; National Law Review analysis, February 2025). His successor as acting director, Russell Vought, extended the same pause into a broader stand-down; the opening move — a Treasury Secretary dual-hatted over the consumer referee, halting its whistle — was Bessent's. Treasury's own year-in-review later listed among its accomplishments that FSOC was "reprioritized to focus on sustainable long-term economic growth" and "reputation risk" was ended in bank supervision (Treasury, Year in Review 2025) — the stability council's remit rewritten toward the growth agenda of the administration it is meant to watch.
  • The IRS spent most of his tenure without its Senate-confirmed referee, and the vacancy was filled by Bessent himself and then by an invented office. The agency had seven commissioners or acting commissioners in 2025 alone (Bloomberg Tax, August 2025). After President Trump removed confirmed Commissioner Billy Long on 8 August 2025 — less than two months after his confirmation — Bessent served as Acting Commissioner while simultaneously holding the Cabinet office, for the full 210 days the Vacancies Act allows and, on the Senate Finance Committee's account, with no nominee ever submitted to replace Long (letter from Senators Wyden, Schumer, and Warren, March 2026). Day-to-day control passed to a newly created IRS "chief executive officer" post — held by the Social Security Commissioner, Frank Bisignano — which the senators describe as "a fake job designed to sidestep the Senate's vetting and confirmation process" (Senate Finance Committee, March 2026). That characterization is the senators'; the underlying facts — seven leaders in a year, a dual-hatted Secretary, an unconfirmed chief executive — are not disputed in the record assembled here. Most of the IRS's 28 "top official" positions were vacant or filled by acting officials as of late 2025 (same letter).
  • The Treasury payment systems themselves were opened to an outside team in his first weeks. In February 2025, personnel associated with the Department of Government Efficiency were granted access to Treasury's payment infrastructure; Bessent's letter to Congress described one engineer's access as read-only without mentioning a second, Marko Elez, who resigned after racist social-media posts surfaced and was later rehired (DOGE Track chronology, February 2025, summarizing Treasury's letter and contemporaneous reporting). Federal courts subsequently restricted that access. The episode is graded here for what it shows about the seat: the nation's payment rails — the referee of last resort for every other obligation — were treated as an administrative convenience in the Secretary's first month.
  • His pressure on the Fed, though short of removal, was sustained and institutional: in July 2025 he called publicly for an exhaustive review of the Fed's non-monetary operations and its $2.5 billion headquarters renovation, and by December 2025 he was proposing, in a published essay and interviews, a comprehensive review of the Fed "as an institution," including its rate-setting authority and a proposal that it relinquish bank supervision (Bessent post on X, 21 July 2025; Reuters, 4 February 2026 hearing coverage; December 2025 essay as summarized in contemporaneous reporting). A review is not a firing; a Treasury Secretary campaigning to shrink the central bank's mandate while his President demands rate cuts is pressure on the referee by other means, and the shelf records it as such.

Why this grade: The Powell defense is genuine and earns its place on the FOR shelf — he kept the most important referee in his seat. But the pattern across the other referees runs one way: the consumer bureau halted by his own hand in week one, the tax agency run for over a year without confirmed leadership with himself in the chair and an unconfirmed invented office beneath him, the stability council reprioritized toward the administration's growth agenda, and the payment systems opened to an outside team. A holder who protects one referee's term while personally occupying two others is gripping. That is FAILS, with the Powell record named as the strongest counter-evidence.

Helped / Hurt ledger:

  • Helped: The Federal Reserve's continuity — Powell completed his term, and market participants who price U.S. assets on central-bank independence; the administration's deregulatory program, which gained a compliant FSOC and a paused CFPB.
  • Hurt: Consumers whose complaints and cases sat under a halted CFPB; taxpayers of every politics, whose tax agency ran leaderless-by-design through two filing seasons; Senate-confirmed governance itself — the confirmation process was routed around at the IRS for over a year (Senate Finance Committee, March 2026).

Test 3 — ANSWERS THE WOBBLE

When courts, voters, parliament, the street, foreign partners, or events push back, does he adjust openly — or deny and double down? This test also grades whether, when another option became available, he at least considered it openly: considering it and declining with stated reasons is not against-evidence; refusing to look at it at all is.

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • The sharpest market wobble of the tenure was answered with adjustment, in the open, within a week. After the 2 April 2025 tariff announcement, the S&P 500 fell about 12 percent in four days and, more dangerously for this seat, Treasury yields spiked — the 30-year recording its largest three-day rise in nearly 40 years (market reporting, April 2025). On 9 April 2025 the administration paused the higher "reciprocal" tariffs for 90 days at a 10 percent baseline; Bessent fronted the announcement to reporters, describing country-by-country "bespoke" negotiations to follow (AP reporting, 9 April 2025). The President himself credited the bond market — "I was watching it… people were getting a little queasy" (Trump remarks, 9 April 2025) — and Bessent, the former bond investor, was the administration's public face of the adjustment.
  • The China escalation was walked back by negotiation he personally led. After tit-for-tat tariffs reached 145 percent (U.S. on China) and 125 percent (China on U.S.), Bessent led the U.S. side in Geneva on 10–11 May 2025; the joint statement of 12 May 2025 cut both sides' rates by 115 percentage points for 90 days (U.S. to 30 percent, China to 10 percent), and Bessent stated the shared conclusion plainly: "neither side wants to decouple" (White House joint statement, 12 May 2025; Bessent press remarks, Geneva, 12 May 2025). The channel stayed open through his tenure: he was still working implementation of the later Busan arrangement with Vice Premier He Lifeng on 6 November 2026 (Treasury readout, 6 November 2026).
  • When the bond market wobbled again in August 2026 — the 30-year yield crossing 5 percent — Treasury adjusted its own operations rather than blaming the market: on 19 August 2026 Bessent announced plans to double long-bond buyback operations to relieve duration pressure, alongside an issuance tilt toward shorter maturities (Treasury announcements and market reporting, August 2026).
  • The debt-limit wobble of 2025 was handled by the book and named in advance. The limit was reached on 1 January 2025 — before Bessent took office — at $36.1 trillion; Treasury ran extraordinary measures, and in May 2025 Bessent told Congress plainly that without action Treasury probably could not pay the government's bills in full and on time beginning in August (Bessent testimony and letters to Congress, May 2025). Congress raised the limit by $5 trillion, to $41.1 trillion, in the One Big Beautiful Bill Act signed 4 July 2025. No default occurred. This is also the file's no-good-choice case, graded by its mechanics: the options actually available were extraordinary measures (harm: accounting strain and a shrinking margin for error), public warning (harm: market anxiety), or default (harm: catastrophic and unprecedented). He took the least-harm doors that were open, said what he was doing, and the trap itself — a limit reached before he was sworn in — was not of his building. The origin caveat is recorded in Test 5: the law that raised the limit also added, on CBO's estimate, $3.4 trillion to federal debt over the decade, a choice his department championed.

Evidence against:

  • The Supreme Court's pushback was answered by replacement, not acceptance. On 20 February 2026 the Court held 6–3, in Learning Resources, Inc. v. Trump, that the International Emergency Economic Powers Act does not authorize the President to impose tariffs (Supreme Court, 20 February 2026). Within hours the administration revoked the IEEPA orders and imposed a new global tariff under Section 122 of the Trade Act of 1974 — announced at 10 percent, raised to 15 percent the next day — with Bessent stating the plan to rebuild the same wall under Sections 122, 232, and 301 (JPMorgan analysis of the decision and response, February 2026; Legal 500 client alerts, February 2026). The refunds the ruling set in motion were not volunteered: the administration signaled it did not plan to issue refunds voluntarily, the President said the question would be litigated for years, and a refund mechanism had to be ordered into being by the Court of International Trade (Judge Eaton's order, 4 March 2026; Legal 500, March 2026). By 14 July 2026 Treasury figures showed $81 billion refunded (Treasury monthly statement reporting, July 2026) — paid, but under court compulsion, months after the ruling. When the referee ruled against the policy, the response was to find another statute and keep the policy; the option of accepting the ruling's substance was, on this record, not considered openly — the replacement was announced the same day.
  • His own deficit commitment was redefined under questioning rather than owned as missed. At his June 2025 testimony he still held the 3 percent target: "we would like to have a three in front of the deficit to GDP by 2028" (House testimony, 11 June 2025). By June 2026, with the administration's own budget projecting deficits over 5 percent of GDP through 2029, he answered Senator Cortez Masto: "I said something with a three in front of it and we had a fiscal contraction last year so we are already in the range" (Senate Finance Committee hearing, 3 June 2026). Moving the definition of the promise when the numbers push back is the wobble answered by rewording.
  • The February 2026 prediction he made on the day the tariffs fell did not survive contact with the year, and no correction appears in the record assembled here: Bessent stated in Dallas on 20 February 2026 that Treasury's estimates showed replacement authorities "will result in virtually unchanged tariff revenue in 2026" (Bessent remarks, Economic Club of Dallas, 20 February 2026). CBO's October 2026 review found customs duties of $182 billion for FY2026, an 11 percent decline from 2025, after roughly $130 billion in refunds, and customs collections about $250 billion below CBO's February projection (CBO Monthly Budget Review, October 2026). A separate Treasury-reported figure puts net tariff income at about $173 billion after roughly $145 billion in refunds (Treasury figures via Reuters, October 2026). The two figures are printed separately, as sourced; both contradict "virtually unchanged."

Why this grade: Against markets and foreign partners, Bessent adjusted fast, openly, and by negotiation — the April 2025 pause, Geneva, the August 2026 buybacks are real adjustments with his name on them. Against the courts and against his own stated numbers, the pattern reverses: a struck-down tariff rebuilt the same day under a new statute, refunds paid only under court order, a deficit promise redefined in the hearing room. The wobble is answered where the bond market delivers it and evaded where judges and arithmetic deliver it. That is MIXED.

Helped / Hurt ledger:

  • Helped: Market participants, exporters, and consumers on both sides of the Pacific by the April 2025 pause and the Geneva de-escalation; federal beneficiaries and creditors protected by the orderly 2025 debt-limit handling; importers who ultimately received $81 billion in refunds by July 2026 (Treasury figures).
  • Hurt: Importers and their customers who waited months for refunds of duties a court had ruled unlawfully collected, and who faced a rebuilt tariff wall within 24 hours of the ruling; holders of long-dated Treasuries and mortgage borrowers during the episodes when yields spiked before the adjustments came (30-year yield above 5 percent, August 2026; 10-year at 5.10 percent on 16 October 2026, per market reporting).

Test 4 — COUNTS EVERYONE

Does the accounting include those who did not elect this administration — the poor, the misidentified, the foreign civilian under sanctions, the future taxpayer — or are some counted out?

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • The core counting function of the seat — paying everyone the government owes, without picking favorites among creditors and beneficiaries — was maintained through two debt-limit episodes' worth of strain. No payment was missed; the 2025 limit was raised before the August X-date Bessent had named, and the FY2026 debt schedules were audited clean (GAO-27-108795, November 2026). Social Security recipients, federal employees, bondholders large and small were all paid on the same terms: that is counting everyone in the seat's most literal duty.
  • The next wobble was disclosed early rather than sprung: by November 2026 the Bipartisan Policy Center, working from Treasury cash-flow data through October 2026, could project the $41.1 trillion limit being reached in February–March 2027 with six to nine months of extraordinary measures beyond it — a timeline built on Treasury's published numbers and a record-high cash balance the department had deliberately accumulated (Bipartisan Policy Center, The Debt Limit Returns in 2027, November 2026).
  • Sanctions were framed and, in the Iranian case, aimed at the regime's finances rather than its population's subsistence: Operation Economic Outcast's stated targets are the IRGC's financial channels, oil-smuggling networks, and sanctions-evading exchanges (Treasury press releases, August–November 2026). In November 2026 Treasury also announced fraud-prevention measures in Minnesota aimed at protecting public-benefit funds (Treasury remarks, 30 October 2026) — enforcement directed at misuse of money meant for beneficiaries, announced as protection of the counted-in.

Evidence against:

  • Immigrant taxpayers were counted out of the confidentiality every other filer receives (Test 1): addresses disclosed to ICE under the April 2025 MOU included, on the IRS Chief Risk Officer's sworn declaration, disclosures triggered by garbage data — "Failed to Provide," "Unknown Address," jail addresses (Romo declaration, 11 February 2026). The people hurt by a misidentification of that kind are, by definition, people the data said nothing reliable about. A federal court concluded the disclosures were unlawful (D.D.C., November 2025). The community organizations that sued — representing immigrant communities in Massachusetts and nationally — are the plaintiffs of record in Community Economic Development Center v. Bessent (D. Mass., filed 2025; memorandum, 30 September 2026).
  • The cost of the tariff program fell, as tariffs' costs do, on the importing country'​s own buyers — and the seat's own numbers show the burden was real while the revenue case for it shrank. The average effective U.S. tariff rate rose from 2.3 percent in January 2025 to 7.1 percent in August 2026 (Penn Wharton Budget Model, August 2026). FY2026 net customs revenue was $182 billion on CBO's count — about 3 percent of the $5.40 trillion in total federal receipts (CBO, October 2026) — while the deficit the tariffs were said to help close widened (Test 5). Who paid the difference between gross collections and refunds, in higher prices at the register, is distributed across every consumer in the country, weighted toward those for whom import prices are a larger share of a smaller income; the dossier records the incidence as economists across the coverage describe it, not as a verdict on any single price.
  • The interest bill now counts against everyone who uses public services, and it crossed a line on his watch: net interest exceeded $1 trillion in FY2026 for the first time, with gross interest on publicly held debt above $1.3 trillion (Treasury Monthly Treasury Statement for September 2026, via contemporaneous reporting, 16 October 2026; CBO figures put FY2026 net interest $81 billion, or 8 percent, above FY2025 — Bipartisan Policy Center, November 2026). Interest is the one line in the budget that buys nothing for anyone; its growth is a transfer from every counted person to the debt's holders.
  • The Iranian population was counted as a pressure surface, in his own words. Bessent described the goal of the August 2026 campaign as to "collapse" the Iranian economy and called it "the greatest economic isolation operation in the history of the world" (Bessent remarks, August–September 2026, via contemporaneous reporting). Whatever the regime's culpability — and the designations target its military and its evasion networks — an announced strategy of economic collapse counts a country's civilians as leverage, and the dossier records that as the strategy's stated design, not as an allegation. The threats against Bessent personally that followed (he relocated to military housing in November 2026 on Secret Service advice, per administration officials) are recorded as context for the campaign's intensity, not as evidence on this test.

Why this grade: The seat's universal duties — pay everyone, publish the numbers, warn early about the next limit — were performed for everyone alike, and that is not nothing; it is the floor of this test, met. Above the floor, specific groups were counted out in documented ways: immigrant filers out of confidentiality (a court said unlawfully), consumers into a tariff burden whose fiscal justification thinned as the year went on, an entire foreign population named as an economic target, and every future service-user taxed by a trillion-dollar interest line. The floor holds; the counting above it is partial. That is MIXED.

Helped / Hurt ledger:

  • Helped: Every recipient of a federal payment, paid in full and on time through the tenure; beneficiaries of the Minnesota fraud-prevention measures as announced; U.S. negotiators' constituents — exporters and importers both — when de-escalation (Geneva, Busan implementation) lowered barriers.
  • Hurt: Immigrant taxpayers, including the misidentified, whose addresses went to ICE (Romo declaration, February 2026); U.S. consumers, who bore a 7.1 percent average effective tariff rate by August 2026 (Penn Wharton); Iranian civilians, the named surface of a stated collapse strategy; future taxpayers, who inherit FY2026's $1 trillion-plus net interest (Treasury/CBO, October 2026).

Test 5 — KEEPS HIS WORD OVER TIME

Confirmation-hearing commitments and public statements vs. the record since 28 January 2025, with dates. Under the mandate rule, carrying out the platform the voters elected is FOR-shelf evidence here; the two axes — word kept or broken, and people helped or hurt — are graded independently, and all four crossings are named where the record shows them.

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for (promises kept or credibly in motion):

  • The tax platform — delivered as run on. Bessent was nominated to make the 2017 tax cuts permanent and carried the administration's side of the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (H.R. 1), signed 4 July 2025, which made those cuts permanent and raised the debt limit (Treasury, Year in Review 2025: "Led negotiations on One Big Beautiful Bill (H.R. 1)"). Under the mandate rule this is a kept word, full stop; its costs are graded on Tests 1 and 4 and in the ledger below, not by relabeling the keeping as a failure. Crossing 2 — kept his word AND it hurt people — is named here in full: CBO estimates the Act's tax and spending provisions will add $3.4 trillion to federal debt over the decade before interest (CBO estimate, July 2025), and corporate tax receipts fell about $23 billion (6 percent) below projection in FY2026, primarily due to the Act's investment deductions (Bipartisan Policy Center analysis of CBO/Treasury data, November 2026). The keeping earns Test 5 credit; the hurt stands in the ledger unlaundered.
  • Maximum pressure on Iran — delivered at the promised scale, past his own earlier calibration. At his confirmation he advocated tougher sanctions on Russian oil and Iranian networks; from February 2025 the designations ran continuously, escalating to Operation Economic Outcast in August 2026, which he personally fronted (OFAC index, 2025–2026; Treasury, August 2026). Crossing 2 again, on the foreign shelf: the promise was kept, and its stated object was the collapse of an economy of some 90 million people (Test 4).
  • No default, dollar defended — kept. He committed to U.S. obligations being honored and to the dollar's reserve status; through the 2025 limit episode and the FY2026 borrowing program (total federal debt $40.2 trillion at 30 September 2026 — GAO, November 2026), every obligation was paid, and he repeated the strong-dollar commitment on the record, including at his February 2026 House hearing (Reuters, 4 February 2026).
  • The Geneva word — kept. The 12 May 2025 joint statement's 90-day rollback was implemented on schedule (rates effective 14 May 2025), the consultation mechanism was established as promised, and Bessent was still working the successor arrangement's implementation with He Lifeng on 6 November 2026 (Treasury readout). Crossing 1 — kept his word AND it helped people: de-escalation lowered costs for traders, exporters, and consumers in both countries.
  • The stablecoin framework — delivered. He championed dollar-backed stablecoin legislation as a dollar-dominance measure; the GENIUS Act was signed 18 July 2025, establishing the framework (Treasury, Year in Review 2025).

Evidence against (promises not kept, or receding):

  • The 3-3-3 centerpiece — the deficit leg is off track and the words moved. Bessent's signature plan, stated in 2024 and affirmed at confirmation and in written answers, was a deficit of 3 percent of GDP by 2028, 3 percent real growth, and 3 million additional barrels of oil per day. The deficit record, printed as separate sourced figures and never blended: FY2025's deficit was $1.775 trillion, 5.8 percent of GDP on Treasury's calculation (Treasury figures via Reuters, October 2026) — though Treasury's calendar-year presentation claims $1.67 trillion, down 17 percent (Treasury, Year in Review 2025), and JPMorgan analysts calculated the FY2025 figure at more than $1.9 trillion, above 6 percent of GDP, once a student-loan accounting change is unwound (JPMorgan analysis, January 2026). FY2026's deficit was $1.992 trillion, 6.2 percent of GDP, up 12 percent from FY2025 (Treasury Monthly Treasury Statement, October 2026; CBO Monthly Budget Review, October 2026).
