4 · The Names

What the names themselves carry — before any one person wore them.

Bugenis

RECORD ◇ root unverified

Lithuanian surname, about 129 bearers worldwide (Forebears, 2014): Lithuania 78 · United States 37 · Argentina 8 · Brazil 2 · Poland 2 · Estonia 1 · Norway 1.

Meaning is not listed in standard sources. Candidate root, unverified: Lithuanian būgnas = drum — possibly “the drummer's” name. It sits as a beautiful maybe until the Lithuanian surname dictionary is read directly.

Gaylord

RECORD

Variant of Gaylard, Anglicized French Gaillard: Old French gaillard = vigorous, powerful, brave, lively, cheerful. Behind it, Germanic Gailhard — cheerful + hard/firm. Brought to England by Huguenots; also a documented Americanized form of Gaillard directly.

House lore: the line traced to Normandy and Château Gaillard, and a William Gaylord sailing to America in 1630. Tristan's specific branch: unproven — and he notes no known relation to the prominent Gaylord, Texas line.

Tristan

RECORD

A Knight of the Round Table in Arthurian legend — Tristan and Isolde. The name was reshaped in French by triste (sad), and is kin to tryst: an appointed, secret meeting — originally a hunter's waiting-place. Sad knight, hidden meetings, in one name.

Michael

RECORD

Hebrew Mikha'el — “Who is like God?” A challenge, not a statement. The archangel who fights the dragon and weighs souls. Lithuanian form: Mykolas. Family lore: the men may have been Mykolas / Michaelis on the old documents before America made them Michael. Mikalojus sounds close but is Nicholas — a different name.

Your code — what it does, and why you might not need one

What the code does

It seats you at a table as your claimed name — the table checks the code's fingerprint, never the code itself. It is the only key that can release your name, rename it, or hand it to someone. And it lets you collect messages left for your name at a standing table.

Why you might not need one

Claiming is an option, not a requirement — nothing at the table is closed to unclaimed names. And a name claimed without a code is still protected forever, just frozen — you cannot prove it, release it, rename it, or hand it on.

There is no back door and no reset — for anyone, ever. Lose the code and the name stays yours forever, unprovable and unreleasable. That is the design, printed plainly, not a flaw being hidden.

Free doors for your own tree

Start here: start from one person you know, follow the obituaries — every obituary names a whole generation.

  • Published obituaries — funeral-home and Dignity Memorial pages
  • WWII draft registration cards — U.S. National Archives
  • VA gravesites / national cemetery records
  • City marriage-license indexes — Reclaim The Records
  • Ship passenger lists — including the Massilia, 1929
  • Parish jubilee books — e.g. St. Mary of the Angels, Brooklyn, 1963
  • State surname archives — Lithuania's is free online
  • FamilySearch (free account) — census records and manifests
  • Facebook Family members lists (free, already built) — a relative's own profile often already names their brother, mother, uncle, aunts and cousins with the relationship spelled out. Grab it exactly as listed, keep living people's details light, and grade it FAMILY until a record backs it. That's how Anna Katherine Myers's household was placed on this tree on Nov 10, 2026.
  • Free family-tree sites — shared trees on FamilySearch and similar free sites may already have your lines built by somebody else. Grab what's standing there, then grade every grabbed link against your own records before you trust it — a ready-made tree is a lead, not proof.
  • Old professional journals — e.g. Texas Architect
  • Newspaper archives via search; surname counts via Forebears

6 · The Rules of This Tree

Grades

RECORD A document says it — obituary, draft card, cemetery record, marriage index, passenger list.

FAMILY The family says it — Tristan's own words, kept in his words, uncertainty included.

SHAPE It fits — ages, places and names rhyme — but no single document closes it yet.

OPEN Asked, unanswered. An open door is a good shelf, not a failure.

Living people

Living people appear with connections but light details, marked “Living — details kept light on purpose.” These trees are roots and records. Nobody on them gets contacted or outreached on the strength of a chart.

DNA — read this plainly

Police and consumer DNA databases are NOT public records — no open door exists, and this tool never collects DNA data. A person's own private copy may carry a private on-device note (“test taken”, “match confirmed”) — link it privately, never broadcast it. In each deep-dive below there is a private-note box that lives only in this tab, for exactly that.

Privacy

No private contact details are published here. Living status always carries its date and its grade. If a fact can't be verified, it is printed as unverified — never dressed up.
