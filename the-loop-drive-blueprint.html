§1

How it moves

Ride and glide first. Force second.

Not just force — glide

Existing water jet packs — Flyboard, JetLev and their kin — work by force alone: a pump shoves a huge volume through a hose and the rider balances on top of it. Tethered, loud, hungry.

The Loop Drive shapes the flow. The Glide Chamber is a wing for water: it guides the stream so the medium does part of the work, the way a wing lets air lift and a manta ray crosses an ocean on slow wingbeats. The pump pushes. The shape multiplies what the push is worth.

  • Screw Intake lifts water from the surrounding medium up into the tank — no blast, continuous flow (see Fig. 2 below).
  • Glide Chamber accelerates and shapes tank water — smooth, directed, low-turbulence. A circulation screw inside the return keeps the loop moving through the chamber without slamming it.
  • Nozzles release it. Thrust points opposite the output. Aim the output, choose the motion.

Direction is distribution

Up, down, left, right, forward, back, sideways — none of these need a different engine. They need a different split.

  • All nozzles even → the vehicle holds, hovers, stays level.
  • More to the rear → forward. More below → up.
  • More to one side → sideways slide, without turning the nose.
  • Opposite corners up-weighted → correction — the Distributor kills a tilt before the rider feels it.

Stability is not an add-on. Stability is the Distributor, balancing output many times a second across every nozzle.

FIG. 2 — THE MECHANISM INSIDE THE LOOP ✓ PROVEN — NOT A GUESS

The Archimedes Screw — the loop's lift & circulation

How to read it: each pocket of water sits trapped between two turns of the blade. The water itself barely moves — the pocket travels. One full turn carries every pocket one step up the tube.

A helical blade turning inside a tilted tube traps pockets of water and carries them upward with each revolution. That is the whole mechanism. It states what it does, plainly:

  • No valves. Nothing opens and slams shut.
  • No blast. No high-pressure jet does the lifting.
  • Continuous flow. As long as it turns, water climbs — pocket after pocket, steady.
  • It does not need to be perfectly watertight. It works as long as more water is lifted than leaks back. A little leak is a cost, not a failure.

Its job in this drive: the screw is what replenishes the tank and keeps the loop circulating. At the intake it lifts water from the medium into the tank. In the return / Glide Chamber circuit, the same turning keeps water circulating through filter and chamber, so the loop never stalls waiting on pressure.

Run in reverse, the same screw is a generator. Water flowing down through it turns it, and the turning makes power — proven in hydroelectric use. In the Loop Drive that means a descending or current-pushed form returns charge to its pack instead of only spending it.

Why this screw, in the designer's own reading: this is the mechanism he pointed at when he said existing water jet packs are “looking at force instead of actually riding gliding.” The screw does not blast water up — it carries it. The screw carries water the way the design carries the rider. Slow, continuous, held the whole way up.

Honest label: The screw itself is PROVEN — in use from ancient irrigation to modern wastewater treatment, and as a hydroelectric generator. Its place inside this drive is the design's own assembly. The mechanism is proven. This combination is the blueprint.

§2

The forms

Same loop. Three mounts named — and any vehicle after them.

🛟
Form A — Ride it

The Platform

A flat deck with the Loop Drive underneath it. The rider stands, sits, or loads cargo on top. The Archimedes screw lifts water from below into its tank; in air, it runs on tank water. Big and stable enough to ride — stability comes from wide nozzle spacing and the Distributor balancing across them.

🎒
Form B — Wear it

The Jet Pack

The same loop, shrunk until the platform is you. Tank rides high and close to the spine, nozzles at hips and shoulders, Distributor at the center of mass. Controls are in the hand grips. The honest limit is printed here: the pack lives or dies on how much water it carries and how fast it moves it — that is why the loop recycles instead of spending it.

🚗
Form C — Install it

Any Vehicle

Put it in a car and it drives the roads — nozzles become directed drive units at the corners. Put it in a hull and it is a boat or a sub. Put it in a flyer and it goes up, down, left, right and sideways. The vehicle changes. The loop, the tank, and the Distributor do not.

ROAD NOTE: On land there is no surrounding water to scoop — the car form runs fully on its tank loop, and refills at any water source. In water and in air near water, the intake tops the tank up as it goes.

§3

Distributor & controls

Where power goes, how hard it works, and where it's headed.

“And that can be user controlled like if you gotta fly into the wind you know you might need a little more effort you gotta fly with the wind a little less.” — The designer, on effort control
Effort Control — Mode 1

MANUAL — the operator sets the effort

The operator sets the effort level by hand, reading the wind or the current themselves.

  • Into the wind / against the current: effort up.
  • With the wind / with the current: effort down — let the medium carry its share.
  • Uphill, loaded, or hovering: effort up. Downhill, light, descending: effort down.

One control does it: a thumb wheel / grip lever on the pack, a dial / pedal on the platform and vehicle forms. Push it, the whole Distributor scales together — direction does not change, effort does.

Effort Control — Mode 2

AUTOMATED — the Distributor meters it

But that can also be automated. We already have driving cars. Self driving cars. Self driving drones.

In Automated mode the Distributor reads the medium itself — wind, current, and slope — and meters effort itself, the way self-driving cars and self-driving drones already manage their own power.

  • Sensors read headwind / tailwind, water current, incline, and load.
  • The Distributor raises effort into resistance and eases off when the medium helps.
  • It holds speed and stability steady while conditions change around the vehicle.

Both modes ship on every form. The person remains the decider.

Every form — platform, jet pack, car, boat, sub, flyer — ships with both modes fitted, not one or the other. The operator chooses the mode. The operator switches modes at any time. The human takes the effort control back at any instant, in any mode — one grab of the manual control overrides automation immediately, with no menu, no confirmation, no delay. The machine assists. The person remains the decider.

Navigation — GPS

Tell it where to go — it goes there

Effort control decides how hard it works. GPS decides where. The operator names a destination — an address, a pin on the map, a saved place — and the Distributor flies the route itself: GPS position feeds the controller, the controller biases the nozzles, and the vehicle steers itself there, holding level the whole way.

  • Set the destination once. Type it, tap it on the map, or say it. The route is drawn before it moves.
  • Self-driving, self-flying. The same sensing that meters effort — wind, current, slope, obstacles — steers around conditions on the way, the way self-driving cars and drones already navigate.
  • It arrives and holds. At the destination the Distributor settles into an even split and hovers / idles in place until told otherwise.
  • The takeback still rules. One grab of any manual control cancels the route instantly. GPS proposes the path; the person remains the decider, at any second, for any reason.
The Skin — Plasma Coat

Covered in a plasma-like skin that protects itself

The whole vehicle wears an outer skin of plasma-like material — an energized, flexible coat over the frame. It is not paint and it is not armor plate. It is an active layer that meets trouble before the machine does.

  • Impact spreads, not lands. A strike hits the skin and the energy disperses across the coat instead of denting through to the frame, the tank, or the rider.
  • It closes behind damage. The layer flows and reseals over scrapes and punctures, so a knock does not become a leak or a tear that grows.
  • It shields the loop. Tank, screw, and Distributor sit inside the skin — the water stays in, grit and spray stay out.
  • Crashes become non-events. With the skin taking the hit and the Distributor killing the tilt, the design's standard is plain: a crash is a thing of the past — contact happens, damage does not.

Honest label: GPS navigation and self-driving control are proven technology in cars and drones today; this design routes them through the Distributor. The plasma skin is this design's own layer — stated as what it does, not yet built or crash-tested.

§4

Tank & water care

It replenishes itself. You can still dump it.

The self-replenishing tank

The tank is a water tank that replenishes itself. Moving through water, the Archimedes screw at the intake lifts water up into the tank continuously — pocket by pocket, with no valves and no blast. Spray and return flow are caught and routed back through the filter into the tank. Nothing is carried that the medium will not replace.

  • Screw lift (✓ PROVEN) does the replenishing — it works even if it is not perfectly watertight, as long as more is lifted than leaks back.
  • Filter stage strips grit and debris on every pass.
  • Level sensor reads the tank and speeds or slows the screw when it runs low.
  • Overflow returns clean excess to the medium.

Dump & replace

If you really need to, you dump out the water and replace the water — depending on how dirty it gets, or whatever the water has picked up.

  • Dump valve at the tank's lowest point — one universal-screw panel, no tools beyond the one driver.
  • Flush cycle: dump, rinse from any clean source, refill, run.
  • Rule printed on the tank: if it looks dirty, smells, or the filter light stays on — dump it. Fresh water is everywhere this machine works.
Scented Water

It runs on water — so it can smell like lemongrass

Nothing burns in this drive, so nothing smells burned. The exhaust is water and mist, and water takes scent the way it takes everything else dissolved in it. A scent port on the tank takes a few drops of essential oil — lemongrass is the designer's pick — and the loop carries it through every pass.

  • A few drops is the dose. Oil goes in at the scent port, disperses through the tank, and rides the return mist — the vehicle trails a light scent instead of fumes.
  • Any oil, your call. Lemongrass today, cedar, mint, or nothing tomorrow. Dump and replace the water (§4) and the scent resets with it.
  • No diesel, ever. No combustion means no exhaust stink, no fuel smell on the rider's clothes, no fumes in the garage. The machine smells like what you put in the water — because water is all that comes out.

§6

Hitting GO

So covered that just hitting go works.

  1. Check the tank. Full, or let the intake fill it. Dirty? Dump and replace first (§4). Want it to smell like something? A few drops of lemongrass at the scent port, now, while you're standing here.
  2. Pick your effort mode. Manual if you want it in your hand. Automated if you want the Distributor reading the medium for you. You can switch mid-run, and Manual always wins the instant you grab it.
  3. Set direction by distribution — or just tell it where to go. Controls bias the Distributor; it keeps you level while it moves you. Or set a GPS destination and let it fly the route itself (§3).
  4. Hit GO. The Distributor balances, the loop recycles, the plasma skin stands guard, the medium does its share. Adjust effort for the wind — or let Automated do it — and ride the glide.
