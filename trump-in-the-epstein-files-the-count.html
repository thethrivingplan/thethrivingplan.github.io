People v. Trump — falsifying business records

CONVICTED

New York Supreme Court, Manhattan · Indicted March 2023 · 34 counts

Manhattan District Attorney indictment charging falsifying business records in the first degree (NY Penal Law §175.10), based on 34 documents — invoices, checks, and ledger entries — that recorded reimbursements to Michael Cohen as "legal expenses." Prosecutors alleged the reimbursements repaid Cohen's $130,000 payment to Stormy Daniels during the 2016 campaign and that the records were falsified to conceal another crime. Trump denied wrongdoing and denied Daniels's account.

Decision: convicted on all 34 counts by a Manhattan jury, May 30, 2024. On January 10, 2025, Justice Juan Merchan imposed an unconditional discharge — no prison, no fine, no probation — leaving the conviction in force.

Status as of Oct 28, 2026: the conviction stands, with two appellate tracks open — a direct state appeal pending before the Appellate Division, First Department (both sides' briefs filed; no decision yet), and a federal-removal track in which a U.S. district judge denied removal for a third time on Aug 28, 2026; Trump appealed that denial the same day.

United States v. Trump — classified documents

DISMISSED

U.S. District Court, Southern District of Florida · Indicted June 2023 · 40 counts

Federal grand-jury indictment over classified documents kept at Mar-a-Lago after Trump left office — willful retention of national-defense information under the Espionage Act, plus obstruction and false-statement counts. A superseding indictment in July 2023 brought the total to 40 counts. Trump pleaded not guilty.

Decision: dismissed July 15, 2024. Judge Aileen Cannon dismissed the case in full, ruling that Special Counsel Jack Smith's appointment violated the Constitution's Appointments Clause. There was no trial and no ruling on the evidence.

Status as of Oct 28, 2026: closed. The Special Counsel appealed, then abandoned the appeal as to Trump on Nov 25, 2024, under the Justice Department's longstanding policy against prosecuting a sitting president. Judge Cannon's dismissal stands untested on appeal.

United States v. Trump — 2020 election (federal)

DISMISSED

U.S. District Court, District of Columbia · Indicted August 1, 2023 · 4 counts

Federal indictment charging conspiracy to defraud the United States, conspiracy to obstruct an official proceeding, obstruction of and attempt to obstruct an official proceeding, and conspiracy against rights — over efforts to overturn the 2020 election results. After the Supreme Court's July 2024 presidential-immunity ruling, the Special Counsel filed a narrower superseding indictment (Aug 27, 2024) keeping the same four counts. Trump pleaded not guilty.

Decision: dismissed without prejudice, November 25, 2024. Judge Tanya Chutkan granted the Special Counsel's request to dismiss, made under the Justice Department's policy that a sitting president cannot be prosecuted, after Trump won the 2024 election. In his filing, the Special Counsel wrote that the prohibition "does not turn on the gravity of the crimes charged, the strength of the Government's proof, or the merits of the prosecution, which the Government stands fully behind." There was no trial.

Status as of Oct 28, 2026: closed. "Without prejudice" means the charges could in theory be refiled after Trump leaves office; no refiling has occurred.

State of Georgia v. Trump — election racketeering (RICO)

DISMISSED

Fulton County Superior Court, Georgia · Indicted August 14, 2023 · 13 counts as indicted

Fulton County indictment of Trump and 18 co-defendants under Georgia's RICO Act and related counts, over efforts to overturn Georgia's 2020 election results. Trump faced 13 counts; Judge Scott McAfee struck 5 of them during the case (3 in March 2024, 2 in September 2024), leaving 8. In December 2024 the Georgia Court of Appeals disqualified District Attorney Fani Willis and her office; the case passed to the Prosecuting Attorneys' Council of Georgia, whose executive director, Pete Skandalakis, took it over. Trump pleaded not guilty.

Decision: dismissed in its entirety, November 26, 2025. Judge McAfee granted prosecutor Skandalakis's motion to drop the case, Skandalakis writing that there was "no realistic prospect that a sitting President will be compelled to appear in Georgia to stand trial" and that Georgians would not be served "by pursuing this case in full for another five to ten years." There was no trial and no finding of guilt or innocence.

Status as of Oct 28, 2026: closed — the last of the four cases to end.

How this page works

  • Three counts, kept apart. Appearing in a document (Section 1), being charged with a crime (Section 2), and the money (Section 3) are different things, counted in different sets, and never added together.
  • Money is totaled only within one basis. Section 3 prints a sub-total only where every figure in it rests on the same basis — FEC filings, or one financial disclosure — and the basis is named in the same line. There is no grand total of "the money," because no honest one exists.
  • Every number names its set. "17" is the reviewed set, listed in full. "4 cases / 91 counts" is every criminal case that has charged Donald Trump personally, as of October 28, 2026. Civil matters and uncharged allegations are in their own labeled boxes, outside those numbers.
  • Both directions at the same size. The reviewed set's exculpatory statements and its accusatory ones are printed in matching boxes on the surface — neither in a footnote, neither larger.
  • Statuses are dated and can move. Appeals were open on this page's date. A later ruling changes a status, not the record of what was decided before it.
