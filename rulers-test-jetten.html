The Ruler's Test — Rob Jetten, Prime Minister of the Netherlands

Subject: Rob Jetten — Prime Minister of the Netherlands since 23 February 2026, when his cabinet was sworn in by King Willem-Alexander after the general election of 29 October 2025. Leader of Democrats 66 (D66) since 12 August 2023; previously Minister for Climate and Energy Policy in the fourth Rutte cabinet (January 2022 – July 2024) and a member of the House of Representatives (2017–2022). He leads a minority coalition of D66, the VVD and the CDA holding 66 of 150 seats in the House of Representatives and 22 of 75 in the Senate — the first Dutch minority cabinet in decades. He is the youngest prime minister in Dutch history and the first from D66. His predecessor, Dick Schoof, headed the PVV–VVD–NSC–BBB cabinet that fell on 3 June 2025 when the PVV withdrew over asylum policy, and then served as caretaker until Jetten's inauguration. Because Jetten has held the seat for nine months, several files below are graded on a short record, and the dossier says so where the evidence is thin. Graded: November 2026, on the documented public record. Method: Each test is graded only on the evidence listed under it. Both shelves are shown — the strongest evidence that he passes, then the strongest evidence that he fails. Allegations are labeled ALLEGATION and never stated as verdicts. Contested items are named as contested, with who disputes them. Seat-holders and public figures are named; people below the public eye appear by role only, unless the documented record itself establishes their wrongdoing. Grades: HOLDS / FAILS / MIXED / NOT ENOUGH EVIDENCE. This record is correctable: new evidence changes grades, in the open.

Test 1 — GIVES BY ONE LAW

Same rules for allies and opponents, insiders and outsiders — no favorites at the source.

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • The one personal-integrity case of his formation was resolved against his own party's nominee, at his own insistence on the standard. Nathalie van Berkel, a newly elected D66 MP designated as State Secretary for Finance, withdrew on 16 February 2026 — a week before the cabinet was sworn in — after reporting showed her CV overstated her education (a claimed Leiden master's degree she had not completed). Jetten publicly held that the credibility of ministers and state secretaries is essential, particularly in a minority cabinet; Van Berkel also gave up her seat in parliament (contemporaneous reporting, February 2026). The rule was applied to an ally, before office, at a cost to his own launch.
  • No case is documented in his nine months of a prosecution opened against an opponent, a proceeding halted for an ally, or an appointment made as a personal favor. The state's enforcement machinery has not been visibly pointed in either direction.
  • On the November 2026 tax plan, the cabinet as a body advised the House against the PRO/Volt box-3 amendment on stated, rule-like grounds — that its financial coverage was not watertight — even though two of the three coalition factions stood to gain politically from it (government statements during the 12–16 November 2026 crisis, as reported by NL Times/DutchNews).

Evidence against:

  • Inside his own coalition, the rules of dealing were not applied evenly. On 12 November 2026 the parliamentary parties of his own D66 and the CDA struck a deal with left-wing opposition parties (PRO, Volt, and smaller parties) to amend the government's 2027 tax plan — roughly €1 billion in additional wealth taxation in box 3, offset in income tax — without their coalition partner the VVD, whose MPs then voted against their own government's bill. The VVD leader and deputy prime minister, Dilan Yeşilgöz, said her party had been blindsided and called the manner of the vote "appalling" (ontluisterend); Jetten himself admitted that "things have not gone well recently" and that relations had been damaged (reporting of the 13–16 November 2026 crisis talks; Jetten's press conference, 13 November 2026). A partner inside the government was treated by a different rule than partners outside it.
  • His coalition agreement committed the cabinet to carry over and implement "in full" the asylum legislation of the Schoof cabinet — bills the Advisory Division of the Council of State had advised against introducing in that form (advice of 10 February 2025, finding the explanatory memorandum had not made it plausible the measures would limit inflow or speed procedures, while clearly burdening the immigration service and the judiciary), and which the Council for the Judiciary warned could generate some 20,000 additional cases. The bills were Schoof-era in origin (introduced 7 March 2025), but the decision to adopt them as his own government's program, advisory record notwithstanding, was Jetten's (coalition agreement, 30 January 2026; Council of State advice, February 2025).

Test 2 — HOLDS WITHOUT GRIPPING

Do the courts, the Council of State, the central bank, the media, municipalities, and his own coalition partners keep their autonomy — or is independence punished and control centralized?

GRADE: HOLDS

Evidence for:

  • The Senate exercised its independence against his cabinet's program, in public, and he let it. On 21 April 2026 the Senate rejected the Asylum Emergency Measures Act (31–44) — the centerpiece of the asylum package his coalition agreement had pledged to implement — after first rejecting (37–38) the amending bill that would have softened its criminalization of irregular residence. The Two-Status System Act passed (41–34). The decisive votes against the Emergency Act included his own party's Senate faction: D66 senator Boris Dittrich stated that "the Senate is not bound by the coalition agreement" and that criminalizing people for lacking papers remained, in his faction's view, inhumane. Jetten declined to lean on them, saying before the vote: "I don't think it helps if I, as Prime Minister, comment on how senators will vote" (Senate record, 21 April 2026; Trouw and DutchNews reporting, April 2026). No senator, of any party, is documented as punished or pressured by him afterward.
  • The courts rule and are obeyed: in October 2026 The Hague district court accepted the new family-reunification framework in the cases before it while requiring fresh decisions where applicants' interests had not been adequately weighed — ordinary judicial correction of the executive, absorbed without public attack from the government (Rechtbank Den Haag rulings, October 2026).
  • The central bank's autonomy is visible in print: De Nederlandsche Bank publishes housing-market analysis that identifies government policy constraints (nitrogen rules, grid congestion, land costs) as causes of the housing shortage his cabinet owns politically. No interference with DNB, the Council of State, or the judiciary is documented (DNB housing-market analysis, 2026).
  • Coalition partners' autonomy is not theoretical: in November 2026 the VVD voted against its own government's tax plan, its leader publicly called the minority cabinet "a failed experiment" and demanded a "fundamental reset" of the coalition agreement and the cabinet's composition — and remained in the government, unpunished, after crisis talks ended on 16 November 2026 with trust formally restored (reporting of the crisis talks, 13–17 November 2026). The coalition's response to its governability problem was institutional: Sybrand Buma, vice-president of the Council of State and the formation's earlier informateur, was recruited to advise the cabinet on securing parliamentary majorities in a structured, transparent way, with a month to report (Brussels Signal and Dutch reporting, 16–17 November 2026).
  • No pressure on media is documented. Public broadcasters, newspapers, and the ANP news agency have covered his cabinet's crises, his party's internal dissent, and opposition attacks without any recorded retaliation, funding threat tied to coverage, or access restriction by his government.

Evidence against:

  • The one documented attempt to move a contested instrument through the system was inherited but adopted: the Asylum Emergency Measures Act began, under the Schoof cabinet in autumn 2024, as an attempt by the then asylum minister to use emergency state powers — an instrument for war and natural disaster — for migration policy; documents later released under freedom-of-information law show civil servants steering the minister away from emergency powers. Jetten's cabinet did not revive the emergency route, but it did commit to the resulting bills "in full," against the Council of State's advice, and defended them until the Senate stopped the main act (Statecraft reconstruction from released documents; Council of State advice, 10 February 2025; Senate vote, 21 April 2026). The grip was attempted by adoption rather than by origin, and it failed by the constitution's ordinary means.
  • Structural caveat, stated plainly: he governs with 66 of 150 House seats and 22 of 75 Senate seats. Much of the autonomy recorded above is guaranteed by his arithmetic as well as by his conduct. The test measures the seat's behavior on the record, and on the record every check that has been applied to him has been accepted; but nine months of minority government is a thinner file than a full term of majority power would be, and this grade is correspondingly provisional.

Test 3 — ANSWERS THE WOBBLE

When courts, voters, coalition partners, or events push back, does he adjust openly — or deny and double down?

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • He names his own government's weakness in public rather than denying it. After the cabinet meeting of 10 July 2026 he acknowledged openly that the minority cabinet lacked secure backing for key budget measures, especially planned social-security cuts; the cabinet nonetheless submitted its multi-year budget on schedule and negotiated in the open (NOS reporting, July 2026).
  • In the November 2026 crisis he absorbed the rebuke and changed the method. After four days of talks he accepted a formal restoration-of-trust process, agreed to bring in Buma to structure future majority-seeking, and stated the lesson himself: on 18 November 2026 he said the cabinet would choose its negotiating partners earlier. His public line during the crisis was concessionary — "there are also things that are not going well… that can and must be better — substantively, politically and personally" (Jetten statement, 13 November 2026; NL Times, 18 November 2026).
  • On tax design he adjusted to the referees: the 2027 tax plan as revised in November 2026 scrapped the household "freedom contribution" for 2027, partially restored the inflation adjustment the contribution would have limited (59.8% instead of 48%), postponed the 2028 decision, delayed the box-3 reform to 2029 in line with Tax Administration feasibility warnings and Council of State advice, and extended the deduction for specific healthcare costs to 2030 after opposition parties raised the impact on people with high care costs (Deloitte summary of the adopted 2027 Tax Plan; AD reporting via EuropeSays, November 2026). Pushback changed the policy, in the open, before enactment.
  • The pattern predates the premiership. As climate minister, when the Netherlands Environmental Assessment Agency's projections showed the 2030 target would be missed, he responded with a larger instrument rather than a denial: the April 2023 climate package of some €28 billion across roughly 120 measures, aimed at a 55–60% reduction by 2030 (cabinet presentation, 26 April 2023).

Evidence against:

  • The largest wobble of his tenure was self-inflicted and was answered late. The 12 November 2026 box-3 deal was struck by his own and the CDA's factions with the left opposition while his VVD partner was, on its leader's account, unaware; the adjustment — crisis talks, a trust-restoration process, an outside adviser — came only after the cabinet had spent four days at the edge of collapse and the VVD had demanded a renegotiation of the coalition agreement itself (crisis reporting, 12–17 November 2026).
  • The budget wobble ran for months before it was answered. Through the summer and autumn of 2026 the cabinet cycled through broad outreach, then fragmented one-on-one talks with opposition parties, attached "decisive" dates (the week of 17 August 2026) that produced no deal, and sought Council of State advice on how a minority government can work more effectively with parliament — an implicit acknowledgment, as the coverage put it, that the bargaining model had failed (NOS and V3 News reconstructions, July–November 2026).
  • On nitrogen, the referee's warning stands unanswered in substance. On 12 March 2026 the Environmental Assessment Agency (PBL) reported that existing policy would lift the share of nature below its critical nitrogen load only from about 30% to about 33% by 2030 — against a January 2025 court judgment ordering the state to halve deposition on the most vulnerable nature by the end of 2030 or pay a €10 million penalty. The cabinet's answer, the June–July 2026 package, sets its binding targets at 2035, not 2030 (see Test 5). Whether it satisfies the court order is CONTESTED: the cabinet states the package is strong enough to meet the Greenpeace judgment; the action group MOB has calculated the percentages are too low and signaled new litigation (PBL, 12 March 2026; Boerenbusiness and Kurums reconstructions, June–July 2026).

Test 4 — COUNTS EVERYONE

Does the accounting include the whole flock — asylum seekers, farmers, the Groningen earthquake victims, the childcare-benefits victims, his opponents' voters — or are some counted out?

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • The childcare-benefits (toeslagen) repair — the state's largest standing debt to a named group of its own citizens — continued to completion milestones under his cabinet rather than stalling. The 22nd progress report (12 February 2026) recorded that every registered parent had received their integral assessment; the recovery organization counts 43,056 recognized victims among 69,443 applicants, each guaranteed at least €30,000; statutory interest payments to large groups of parents began in April 2026; by the 23rd progress report (September 2026) some 4,500 parents had also completed the additional-damage route, and the responsible state secretary projected financial recovery complete for most parents by the end of 2026 and additional-damage compensation by the end of 2027, with the average total compensation per family budgeted at roughly €190,000. The children's scheme had reached 111,714 children with payments (UHT progress reports 22–23, February and September 2026; State Secretary Palmen's answers to the Senate). The scandal itself (roughly 2004–2019, under earlier cabinets) is not graded against him; his handling of the inherited file is, and the handling is a functioning repair, not an abandonment.
  • Groningen's victims remain inside an operating compensation system: the gas field was permanently closed on 19 April 2024 (under the Schoof caretaker period's predecessor decisions), and the independent Institute Mining Damage Groningen had handled more than 500,000 damage reports and paid over €2.5 billion by the end of 2024; the system continued under his cabinet. Groningen-specific delivery figures for 2026 were not established in this research, and the file is graded as continuity, not acceleration — stated plainly, the evidence here is thinner than for toeslagen.
  • His opponents' voters are seated and heard. The PVV, tied with D66 at 26 seats, sits in parliament with full rights; its leader's unfounded election-fraud claims (see Test 5) were answered by the municipalities concerned through ordinary channels — Zaanstad publicly denied the specific claims as fabricated — and no PVV voter, member, or official is documented as excluded from any public service or process by his government. The PVV's exclusion from the cabinet itself was a stated position of all three coalition parties, given on the record before and during the formation: they would not govern again with Wilders after he had collapsed the previous cabinet in June 2025 (see Test 5 for the reciprocal exclusions).
  • Farmers, the group his nitrogen policy burdens most, are counted in money and in sequence: the 2026 package reserves some €20 billion — €9 billion for farm transformation, €2.7 billion for a voluntary exit scheme, €2.2 billion for nature restoration, €2 billion for innovation — and the first nitrogen space freed goes to the PAS-melders, farmers left in legal limbo by the state's own earlier permitting policy (cabinet package, June–July 2026).

Evidence against:

  • Asylum seekers are counted down by design, on his signature. The coalition agreement carried over most of the Schoof cabinet's asylum program; the Two-Status System Act, in force from 2026, divides protection holders into refugees (A status) and beneficiaries of subsidiary protection (B status) with materially lesser rights — for B-status holders, family reunification generally requires a two-year wait plus income and housing conditions, and reunification for all is restricted to the core family. The EU Pact implementation legislation (in force 12 June 2026) sets asylum permits at three years and ends permanent asylum permits. D66's own campaign had proposed a "Canadian model" of managed migration and processing applications outside Europe. The sharper instrument — criminalization of irregular residence itself — was in the package his agreement endorsed and was stopped by the Senate, with his own party's senators among those who stopped it (coalition agreement, January 2026; Senate votes, 21 April 2026; IND guidance, 2026).
  • The fiscal consolidation is distributed downward in specific, documented places: the coalition agreement raises the healthcare deductible from €385 to about €460 by 2027 (with per-treatment safeguards), shortens unemployment-benefit duration, and ties the state pension age to life expectancy from 2033 — costs falling on the chronically ill, the unemployed, and future pensioners, taken to fund the defense build-up and the 2%-of-GDP deficit ceiling the coalition set itself (coalition agreement, 30 January 2026).
  • The housing shortage still counts out the young and the renting: the shortfall stood at roughly 396,000 homes in mid-2025 (ABF Research, the government's own modeler) and about 410,000 by early 2026 (Capital Value); completions in 2025 were 69,200 — the third consecutive year below the 100,000-a-year ambition. His housing minister answered in 2026 with a 50-point acceleration plan (presented 21 September 2026), €7 billion in funding, and a partial relaxation of the rent cap; delivery against the target is not yet visible in the figures (ABF Research, July 2025; Capital Value, February 2026; Taskforce Versnelling Woningbouw, September 2026).

Test 5 — KEEPS HIS WORD OVER TIME

Campaign and program promises vs. the record, with dates — his nine months as prime minister, and the prior roles where the same promises were made.

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for (promises kept or credibly in motion):

  • The collaborative method — kept as method. He campaigned on Het kan wel ("Yes, we can") and on building a "broad political centre"; after the election he said voters had "clearly indicated the need for cooperation" and that he would work "for all Dutch people." He first sought the broad coalition he had described, including GroenLinks–PvdA; when the VVD refused that combination, he formed the minority cabinet and has governed by assembling ad-hoc majorities — including, in July 2026, passing his nitrogen package through the House with the left opposition PRO's votes (86 of 150) (election-night and formation reporting, October 2025 – February 2026; House vote, 2 July 2026).
  • Education — kept. The coalition agreement reversed the previous cabinet's planned education cuts and added roughly €1.5 billion for schools and research, a stated D66 priority (coalition agreement, January 2026).
  • Defense — in motion as promised. The agreement commits to 2.8% of GDP by 2030 and 3.5% by 2035, an envelope of about €19 billion a year at full build-out, part-funded by the "freedom contribution" (vrijheidsbijdrage) — which was in fact legislated in the 2027 tax plan, in the form of a limited inflation adjustment of tax brackets, before being partially softened (Reuters, 30 January 2026; Deloitte, November 2026).
  • Nitrogen — the promise to break the deadlock was kept, in form. After seven years without a comprehensive package, his cabinet produced one and passed it through the House: agricultural emissions down 42–46% by 2035 against 2019, a livestock-density cap of 2.6 livestock units per hectare, buffer zones around 100 nature areas, €20 billion of support, and a permitting route intended to unfreeze housing and infrastructure; the bills were formally submitted to the House on 3 November 2026 and still require the Senate (cabinet package, June–July 2026; submission, November 2026).

Evidence against (promises not kept, traded, or receding):

  • The asylum reversal — his party's stated position, traded at the formation table. D66 had been among the fiercest parliamentary critics of the Faber asylum bills; its Senate spokesman called criminalizing undocumented residence inhumane. As prime minister-designate, Jetten defended adopting the bills wholesale: "We're not fans of it, but sometimes you have to go the extra mile. The Netherlands needs clarity when it comes to asylum policy" (Nieuwsuur interview, February 2026). The word given in opposition was exchanged for the coalition agreement; the Senate later removed the sharpest part.
  • The mortgage interest deduction — a signature D66 position, surrendered. D66's long-standing program was to phase out the mortgage interest deduction; the coalition agreement maintains it in full, a stated VVD requirement (coalition agreement, January 2026).
  • The freedom contribution — announced at €5 billion a year, delivered diluted and postponed. Announced in January 2026 as a surcharge on income and corporate taxes raising about €5 billion annually for defense (Reuters, 30 January 2026), it entered the 2027 tax plan only as a limited indexation adjustment, was partially reversed for 2027 by amendment (indexation at 59.8% rather than 48%), the household component for 2027 was scrapped outright in the November 2026 revision, and the decision for 2028 was postponed to next year (Deloitte summary of the adopted 2027 Tax Plan; AD reporting, November 2026).
  • 100,000 homes a year and "ten new cities" — the headline campaign pledge, not yet met. D66's 2025 campaign promised 100,000 homes a year and building ten new towns/cities. Completions were 69,200 in 2025 (pre-dating his cabinet but defining the baseline he promised to change), the shortage estimate rose to about 410,000 by early 2026, and as of November 2026 the government's own instruments are an acceleration plan and funding rather than a completion rate at target. His party's claim to be "on track" for the first 100,000 (D66 MEP Gerben-Jan Gerbrandy, autumn 2026) is CONTESTED by the completion figures above.
  • Stability — the meta-promise, strained by his own deputy's words. He took office promising a stable, ambitious government after two collapsed cabinets; nine months in, his deputy prime minister publicly called the cabinet "a failed experiment" that "cannot go on like this," and Jetten himself conceded that things "have not gone well recently." The cabinet survived the November crisis; the promise of calm competence is, on his own account, not yet kept (crisis reporting, 13–17 November 2026).
  • Context — the 2025 election itself, stated exactly. D66 and the PVV each won 26 seats; D66 took the popular vote by roughly 15,155 votes (ANP count). Wilders did not concede, called Jetten "arrogant" for claiming victory before the Electoral Council's formal declaration, and amplified claims of voting irregularities sent to him from around the country, saying they should be investigated while acknowledging he did not know whether they had substance; the municipality of Zaanstad, named in the claims, denied them as untrue, and no irregularity affecting the result has been established (ANP and contemporaneous reporting, 31 October – 3 November 2025). The formation that followed was shaped by reciprocal exclusions, each stated openly by its holder: the three coalition parties ruled out the PVV; the VVD ruled out GroenLinks–PvdA, which Jetten had wanted to include; and JA21, which had gained eight seats, was "blocked by Jetten" from joining, on the reporting of the formation (formation reporting, November 2025 – January 2026).

Overall Summary

One law: MIXED · Without gripping: HOLDS · The wobble: MIXED · Counts everyone: MIXED · His word: MIXED.

Rob Jetten holds the Dutch premiership with the weakest hand any Dutch prime minister has held in decades — 66 of 150 seats, 22 of 75 in the Senate — and the documented record of his first nine months is largely the record of that weakness being exercised on him, and accepted by him. His own party's senators killed his government's flagship asylum act and he would not pressure them; his coalition partner voted against his tax plan and called his cabinet a failed experiment and stayed; the courts, the central bank, the Council of State and the press have all operated on him without a single documented act of retaliation. Against that stand the debits of a formateur's compromises: he adopted, wholesale, asylum laws his party had opposed and the Council of State had advised against; he surrendered his party's positions on mortgage relief and accepted a two-tier asylum system that counts the war-fleeing as less than the persecuted; his signature defense tax shrank within a year of its announcement; and his government's gravest crisis was caused not by any opponent but by his own factions dealing around their partner. The repair files he inherited — the toeslagen parents above all — are being carried to completion on his watch, and the nitrogen deadlock that froze Dutch building for seven years has a passed House bill with his name on it. The file is nine months long, graded on what is documented, and correctable in the open: a full term, or a majority, would test several of these grades harder than 2026 has.
