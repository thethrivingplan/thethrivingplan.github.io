The Ruler's Test — Recep Tayyip Erdoğan, President of Türkiye

Subject: Recep Tayyip Erdoğan — Prime Minister 2003–2014; President 2014–present (in the seat since 2014; ruling Türkiye since 2003). Graded: November 2026, on the documented public record. Method: Each test is graded only on the evidence listed under it. Both shelves are shown — the strongest evidence that he passes, then the strongest evidence that he fails. Allegations are labeled ALLEGATION and never stated as verdicts. Contested items are named as contested, with who disputes them. Seat-holders and public figures are named; people below the public eye appear by role only, unless the documented record itself establishes their wrongdoing. Grades: HOLDS / FAILS / MIXED / NOT ENOUGH EVIDENCE.

Test 1 — GIVES BY ONE LAW

Same rules for allies and opponents, insiders and outsiders; no favorites at the source.

GRADE: FAILS

Evidence for:

  • The machinery of prosecution does sometimes reach his own camp's periphery: after the February 2023 earthquakes, prosecutors opened thousands of building-collapse investigations — 612 suspects under investigation and 184 jailed within three weeks, including contractors who had grown rich in the AKP-era construction boom (Justice Minister Bekir Bozdağ, February 25, 2023, reported by AP). Courts later convicted contractors and, in January 2026, three former officials in the Grand İsias Hotel case (trial record, Adıyaman).
  • His governments have repeatedly submitted their record to competitive elections whose results were not pre-written — including elections he lost (see Test 3) — and anti-corruption law on the books applies formally to everyone.
  • The corruption allegations against Istanbul Mayor Ekrem İmamoğlu are the state's case, formally brought: prosecutors allege he ran a "profit-oriented criminal organization" involving bribery, extortion, bid-rigging and unlawful data collection. These are ALLEGATIONs, contested by İmamoğlu and the CHP, who call the case political; the main İBB trial is ongoing (436 defendants, 46 in pretrial detention as of November 2026; next hearing January 4, 2027 — court reporting via Habertürk, November 12, 2026). The charges exist in legal form and are being tried in open court.

Evidence against:

  • The founding asymmetry is December 2013. Police detained the sons of three cabinet ministers, the head of state-owned Halkbank, and businessman Reza Zarrab in bribery and sanctions-evasion probes. Erdoğan denounced the investigations as a coup attempt by the Gülen movement; the prosecutors and police chiefs running them were removed or reassigned, police refused to execute the second wave of detention orders, and by October 2014 prosecutors had dropped the case against all 53 suspects. In January 2015 parliament's AKP majority voted against sending the four implicated former ministers to trial (case record; Istanbul Chief Public Prosecutor's 2014 non-prosecution decision). Zarrab later pleaded guilty in a New York court and testified he had bribed the former economy minister — testimony in a U.S. court, never tested in a Turkish one, because the Turkish case had been closed (U.S. v. Zarrab/Atilla proceedings, SDNY, 2017–18).
  • A former senior judge testified in court that Erdoğan, then prime minister, personally telephoned him in December 2013 asking him to stop prosecutors reaching his son — testimony given under oath at the Court of Cassation and reported in the Turkish press; Erdoğan has not addressed it specifically, and it is recorded here as one witness's sworn account, contested by implication by the government's position that the probes were a plot (İbrahim Okur testimony, reported by Cumhuriyet).
  • Against that shelf, the İmamoğlu process: Türkiye's most popular opposition figure was detained on March 19, 2025 — four days before the CHP was to nominate him as its presidential candidate, and one day after Istanbul University annulled his university diploma, a constitutional prerequisite for the presidency (the Council of State rejected his final appeal against the annulment on November 19, 2026). He has been jailed pending trial since March 23, 2025 — 607 days as of November 20, 2026 — and has accumulated further convictions while in prison: a July 2025 sentence of 1 year 8 months for insulting a prosecutor, and on November 18, 2026, a sentence of 2 years 1 month plus a political ban in the CHP-congress case (appealable; Reuters via TRT Haber, November 18, 2026). Whether the underlying corruption ALLEGATIONs are true is for the trial; the documented pattern is that the full weight of the process — pre-trial detention, diploma annulment, serial cases — falls on the one man positioned to beat the president at the ballot box, while the 2013 files on the president's own ministers were closed by removing the investigators.
  • The same asymmetry runs through the trustee system (Test 2): the Interior Ministry removes elected opposition mayors on terrorism ALLEGATIONs — charges, not convictions — while no AKP mayor has been replaced by a trustee on comparable allegations (HRW, November 7, 2024).
  • In November 2026 the pattern extended to media: opposition broadcasters Halk TV (October 2026) and Sözcü TV (November 17, 2026) were placed under state trusteeship (March 19 Platform weekly record, November 20, 2026).

Test 2 — HOLDS WITHOUT GRIPPING

Do courts, the central bank, media, universities, municipalities, and the military keep their autonomy — or is independence punished and control centralized?

GRADE: FAILS

Evidence for:

  • His power rests on elections that remain genuinely contested: he won the May 2023 presidential runoff with 52.2% against a united opposition, and his party has lost real elections — Istanbul and Ankara in 2019, and the nationwide local vote in 2024 (see Test 3). Opposition parties organize, campaign, hold parliament seats, and run Türkiye's largest cities where trustees have not intervened. No comparable dossier subject who grades FAILS here has faced voters this often.
  • The 2025–26 Kurdish peace process has been run partly through parliament — a cross-party commission and a framework law (Law No. 7595, August 2026) — rather than by decree alone (parliamentary record, 2026).
  • After 2023 he delegated economic management to orthodox technocrats — Finance Minister Mehmet Şimşek and central bank governors Hafize Gaye Erkan, then Fatih Karahan — and, as of November 2026, has let their tightening program run for over three years (Reuters, June 2023; CBRT decisions 2023–26).

Evidence against:

  • The judiciary: after the July 2016 coup attempt, 4,006 judges and prosecutors were removed by the judicial council (HSYK/HSK), and the 2017 constitutional amendments restructured that council so its members are chosen by the president and a parliament his alliance controls (Stockholm Center for Freedom purge tally, 2026; 2017 constitutional amendments). The European Court of Human Rights found violations in 66 of 74 judgments on Türkiye in 2025 alone, and Türkiye carries 448 unimplemented ECtHR judgments (ECtHR statistics, end-2025).
  • The purges generally: 113,290 public servants were dismissed by emergency decree in 2016–18, and the armed forces expelled 26,206 members including 150 of 326 generals and admirals — dismissals by list, without individualized judicial review (emergency decree record; SCF tally). The ECtHR's 2025 Demirhan judgment found 239 fair-trial violations in a single case arising from convictions based on alleged use of the ByLock messaging app (ECtHR, 2025).
  • Municipalities: the trustee (kayyum) system, created by Decree Law 674 in September 2016, has run in three waves — 94 municipalities seized in 2016–17, 48 of the 65 the HDP won in 2019, and 13 more in 2024–25, including the CHP's Esenyurt district in Istanbul and the DEM Party's Mardin, Batman, Halfeti, Van, Siirt and Tunceli municipalities (HRW counts, 2016–2025). No removed DEM mayor had returned to office by October 2026. Elected councils are dissolved and replaced by the local governor — an appointed official of the Interior Ministry.
  • The central bank: between 2019 and 2021 Erdoğan dismissed three central bank governors in succession — Murat Çetinkaya (2019), Murat Uysal (2020), and Naci Ağbal (March 2021, fired days after raising the policy rate to 19%). His successor cut rates from 19% to 8.5% while inflation rose to a 24-year peak above 85% in October 2022 (CBRT record; Reuters, June 8, 2023). The appointment power over the bank passed to the president personally under the 2018 presidential-system decrees.
  • Media: in March 2018 the Doğan Group — owner of Hürriyet, CNN Türk and Kanal D, the last large independent media house — sold its media assets for $1.1 billion to the Demirören Group, widely regarded as close to the government, after years of pressure including a 2009 tax fine of roughly $3.3 billion against Doğan (Columbia Journalism Review, 2019; Reuters, 2018). Roughly 90% of national media is now held by pro-government groups, over 150 outlets were closed by decree after 2016, and Türkiye fell to 159th on the RSF Press Freedom Index by 2025 (RSF; sector tallies).
  • Universities: since 2016, university rectors are appointed by the president; his January 2021 appointment of an outside rector to Boğaziçi University triggered months of protests and mass detentions of students (Reuters, 2021).
  • The defiance of the external court: the ECtHR ordered Osman Kavala's release in 2019; Türkiye did not release him, the Council of Europe opened infringement proceedings in 2022 — only the second such case in its history — and a Turkish court sentenced Kavala to aggravated life imprisonment in the Gezi trial in April 2022 (ECtHR, Kavala v. Türkiye, 2019; Council of Europe, 2022). Selahattin Demirtaş, the former HDP co-chair, remains in Edirne Prison as of November 2026 despite the ECtHR Grand Chamber's 2020 judgment ordering his immediate release (ECtHR GC, Demirtaş v. Türkiye (No. 2), 2020; Demirtaş's own published writings from prison, November 2026).

Test 3 — ANSWERS THE WOBBLE

When courts, voters, markets, earthquakes, or allies push back, does he adjust openly — or deny and double down?

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • The economic U-turn is real and sustained. After winning re-election in May 2023 with inflation near 40% and reserves depleted, Erdoğan reversed the signature policy of his later rule: he appointed Şimşek, let the central bank raise rates from 8.5% to 50%, and kept the orthodox program through local-election pain and into 2026, by which time inflation had fallen below 30% for the first time since 2021 (29.7%, September 2026 — TÜİK). He has publicly asked voters for patience with the program rather than abandoning it (Reuters, 2023–26).
  • The 2024 defeat was accepted in words and in fact. On March 31, 2024, the CHP won the nationwide local vote (~37.8% to the AKP's ~35.5%) — his party's worst result in over two decades in power. Erdoğan addressed supporters the same night: "This is not an end for us, but actually a turning point… If we made a mistake, we will fix it," and called the result the victory of "our democracy, the national will." The results stood; CHP mayors took office across the country (Reuters, April 1, 2024).
  • The earthquake apology. After the February 6, 2023 earthquakes killed 53,537 people in Türkiye (official count), Erdoğan acknowledged "shortcomings" in the initial response on February 8 and on February 27, 2023, in Adıyaman, said publicly: "We were not able to work the way we wanted in Adıyaman for the first few days. I apologize for this" (AFP/Reuters, February 27, 2023).
  • The Van correction: in April 2024, when the election board stripped the DEM Party's winning Van mayoral candidate of his certificate and handed the office to the losing AKP candidate, mass protests followed — and the Supreme Election Council reversed itself and reinstated the elected mayor within days (AFP, April 2024).
  • The peace opening: in 2025 he backed his nationalist ally Bahçeli's overture that led to Öcalan's February 27, 2025 call, the PKK's May 2025 announcement that it would dissolve and disarm, and an ongoing verified-withdrawal process through November 2026 — a reversal of his own post-2015 hard line, undertaken openly (Reuters/AP, 2025; process reporting, November 2026).

Evidence against:

  • The earthquake's accountability stopped at contractors. The same government that apologized had, in 2018, passed a zoning amnesty ("zoning peace") that registered some 10 million non-compliant buildings for a fee, safety left to the owner's declaration; 294,166 buildings in the ten destroyed provinces held amnesty certificates (TMMOB count; 2018 amnesty law). Erdoğan had campaigned on the amnesty in 2019 as having "solved the problems" of hundreds of thousands of property owners (campaign remarks, 2019). After the quakes, thousands of contractors were investigated — but as of March 2024, permission to investigate public officials had been granted in only three cases, with most authorities refusing even to answer information requests (HRW/Citizens' Assembly, March 27, 2024). In the first days he had also called critics of the response "dishonest, immoral and vile" and "dishonorable people spreading lies" (speeches, February 2023).
  • The rate doctrine was denied for years before it was reversed. The 2023 pivot followed five years in which every market signal was answered by firing the messenger (Test 2): inflation above 85%, the lira losing over 80% of its value in five years, and the policy defended as religious obligation ("nas") as late as December 2021. The correction came only after an election fought on the cost of living, and Erdoğan has never acknowledged the doctrine itself was wrong — the same man who reversed it still describes interest as "the mother and father of all evil" (2018 remark, repeated since).
  • 2019 Istanbul: when İmamoğlu won the Istanbul mayoralty in March 2019, the election board — under AKP objection — annulled the result; the re-run in June 2019 returned İmamoğlu with a far larger margin, which Erdoğan then accepted. The annulment is the against-item; the acceptance of the re-run is on the for-shelf of history, but the first instinct was to void a lost vote (YSK decision, May 6, 2019; re-run, June 23, 2019).
  • Court rulings he dislikes are defied, not absorbed: the Kavala and Demirtaş release orders (Test 2) have been refused for six and seven years respectively, at the cost of Council of Europe infringement proceedings.
  • The 2025 wobble was answered with the grip: the mass protests after İmamoğlu's March 2025 arrest — the largest since Gezi — were met with protest bans, mass detentions of demonstrators, and hundreds of protesters put on trial (HRW, April 2025).

Test 4 — COUNTS EVERYONE

Does the accounting include the whole flock — Kurds, Alevis, secular Turks, refugees, earthquake victims, his opponents' voters — or are some counted out?

GRADE: FAILS

Evidence for:

  • Refugees, for a decade: Türkiye hosted the world's largest refugee population — some 3.6 million Syrians at peak — and Erdoğan defended the policy for years against domestic backlash in explicitly moral terms, saying Türkiye would not close its doors to people fleeing death (presidential statements, 2016–2021). Whatever its later turn, the hosting itself was a decade in which millions of non-citizens were inside the count, fed, schooled and treated at state expense.
  • Earthquake victims, materially: the state mounted a vast reconstruction program and reports handing over its 455,000th rebuilt home in December 2025 (Interior Ministry statement, February 2026) — the victims of the quake zone, many of them his own voters but many not, were rebuilt for at scale.
  • The Kurdish opening, twice: his government ran the 2013–15 "solution process" with the PKK, and in 2025 backed the new process that has the PKK dissolving — an acknowledgment, in policy, that Kurdish citizens' grievance is part of the national account (process records, 2013–15 and 2025–26).
  • Early-era inclusion: his first governments lifted the state of emergency in the southeast, permitted Kurdish-language broadcasting (TRT Kurdî, 2009), and ran "Alevi workshops" (2009–10) — documented openings toward both communities.

Evidence against:

  • Kurdish voters' choices are annulled by appointment. Three waves of trustees (Test 2) mean that in much of the Kurdish-majority southeast, voting for the DEM Party and its predecessors has not produced DEM government: 94 municipalities seized in 2016–17, 48 of 65 won in 2019, 13 more in 2024–25. HRW calculated the November 2024 removals alone stripped over half a million voters of their chosen representatives (HRW, November 7, 2024). The practice continued during the 2025–26 peace process — disarmament was negotiated while the elected mayors of the disarming side's political movement sat removed, and DEM's stated demands for their restoration remained unmet as of November 2026 (DEM Party statements; process reporting).
  • The Kurdish political leadership is in prison. Demirtaş has been imprisoned since November 2016 — a decade — despite the ECtHR Grand Chamber's release order; hundreds of HDP/DEM politicians have been prosecuted in the Kobani case and others (ECtHR GC, 2020; trial records).
  • Alevis remain unrecognized. The 2009–10 workshops produced no legal status for cemevis as houses of worship; the 2022 creation of an Alevi-Bektaşi Culture and Cemevi Directorate attached Alevi institutions to a state ministry — a structure Alevi federations rejected as control without recognition, and the ECtHR's earlier Cem Foundation line of judgments on Alevi religious rights remains unimplemented in its core demand (government decrees, 2022; Alevi federation statements).
  • His opponents' voters are governed as a problem. The CHP won the 2024 local elections; within a year its Istanbul mayor was jailed, its Esenyurt and Şişli mayors replaced by trustees, 500+ CHP mayors, councillors and officials arrested or investigated by opposition counts (CHP tally, July 2025 — an interested party's count, consistent in scale with the court reporting), and nine opposition municipal leaders who had replaced jailed CHP mayors defected to the AKP in November 2026 under what the CHP describes as judicial pressure — described here as the CHP's characterization, contested by the AKP (November 2026 reporting).
  • Refugees, later: from 2019 onward the policy turned to returns — Erdoğan announced plans to return one million Syrians, deportations were documented by rights groups, and after the fall of the Assad government in December 2024 returns accelerated with official encouragement. The people counted in as guests in 2015 are counted as departures in the government's 2025–26 reporting (presidential statements; UNHCR return figures).

Test 5 — KEEPS HIS WORD OVER TIME

Signature promises and stated positions vs. the record across two decades.

GRADE: FAILS

Evidence for:

  • He delivered the system he promised: he campaigned for an executive presidency, won the April 2017 referendum (51.4%), and built exactly the centralized presidency he had described to voters — supporters and critics agree the product matches the pitch (2017 referendum record).
  • Several signature cultural promises were kept across decades: the headscarf ban in universities and public employment was lifted (2010–13), Hagia Sophia was reconverted to a mosque as his movement had long demanded (2020), and the Sweden/Finland NATO bargain he struck in 2023–24 was executed as stated.
  • The 2025 peace process follows his own stated condition — no negotiations while armed attacks continue, movement only after the PKK's dissolution decision — and he has kept Şimşek's economic program, and its pain, after promising voters in 2023 he would restore economic stability, accepting the political cost in the 2024 local elections rather than sacking the program (Reuters, 2024).

Evidence against:

  • "This is my last election." Campaigning in March 2024, Erdoğan told supporters: "For me, this is a final. This election is my last election" (remarks, March 8, 2024). In September–October 2026, his chief legal adviser Mehmet Uçum stated publicly that the 2028 elections will "almost clear[ly]" be moved to March–April 2028 and that "President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan will run for the final time" — using the constitution's early-election clause (Article 116) to reset his eligibility, with the Justice Minister adding that Erdoğan "will be reelected in 2028." Erdoğan has not disavowed the plan (Reuters, October 15, 2026; adviser statements, September–October 2026). It is the third resetting of his term count: the 2017 system's "new system, new count" ruling by the election board let him run in 2023 despite a 2014–18 presidential term, over opposition objection (YSK ruling, 2023).
  • The EU promise inverted. Erdoğan's first governments made EU accession the centerpiece of the state: reform packages, abolition of the death penalty, and the opening of accession negotiations on October 3, 2005. The process has been frozen since 2018, and Erdoğan has since described the EU in adversarial terms and threatened to "part ways" with it (Council of the EU conclusions, 2018; presidential statements, 2023). The destination he sold voters in 2003–05 was abandoned without ever being formally renounced or put back to them.
  • The economic doctrine reversed without acknowledgment. He pledged for years that low rates would defeat inflation — cutting rates into rising inflation from 2021 — and reversed to the world's steepest tightening in 2023 (Test 3). Voters who saved in lira on the first promise lost over 80% of its value in five years (market record). A promise reversed after the bill arrives is graded on the record of both halves.
  • The 2013–15 peace process: he owned the Kurdish opening, then disowned it after the June 2015 election cost his party its majority — stating "there is no Kurdish problem" and that he did not recognize the Dolmabahçe agreement his own government had announced weeks earlier (statements, March–July 2015). The pattern — an opening kept while it pays, disavowed when it costs — recurs in the EU and rate files above.
  • Institutional promises of the early era — "zero tolerance for torture," the Copenhagen criteria renamed the "Ankara criteria" as a pledge to meet them regardless of Brussels (2002–04 statements) — stand against the ECtHR statistics of Test 2: 66 violation findings in 74 judgments in 2025, 448 leading judgments unimplemented.

Overall Summary

Grades: One Law — FAILS. Holds Without Gripping — FAILS. Answers the Wobble — MIXED. Counts Everyone — FAILS. Keeps His Word — FAILS.

Erdoğan's file divides cleanly in two. As an electoral politician he is the most tested ruler in this series: he has won and lost real elections for two decades, accepted the worst defeat of his career in 2024 with a promise to fix his mistakes, apologized on camera for the earthquake response, and executed a genuine, costly economic U-turn that has run for three years. As a holder of institutions, the record runs the other way: the 2013 corruption files on his own ministers were closed by removing the investigators while his chief rival sits in pre-trial detention 607 days and counting; judges, central bankers, rectors, editors and elected mayors all hold office at his pleasure; two European Court release orders have been refused for six and seven years; Kurdish voters have had their municipalities seized in three waves; and the man who called the 2024 vote "my last election" is, by his own adviser's account, preparing to run again in 2028. The wobble grade is mixed because the adjustments are real. The rest fail because the grip is the system, not an episode in it.
