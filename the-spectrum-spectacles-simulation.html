DESIGN STUDY · SIMULATION + BLUEPRINTS · SHEET SS-01

The Spectrum Spectacles

One pair of glasses, every band of light. The lens doesn't get better — the key changes: a catcher for each band, a translator that re-keys it into the one window your eye owns (about 400–700 nm), and a dial on the bridge that chooses the layer. Spin the dial slowly and you read layer by layer, like Franklin's lenses in National Treasure. Spin it fast and the layers fuse — the way Newton's disc fuses the spectrum into white.

Honest label, printed on the frame: this is a design on paper and a simulation — not a built device. Every view below is a translation, not the world "as it really looks." False color is honest only while it's labeled, so every view in the simulator carries its band and its key on its face.

What you're watching

Single layer is Franklin's method: one lens in, one hidden layer out. The crow is black, the flower is plain red, the person is just a person.

Spinning key-ring cycles the bands. Raise the speed and persistence of vision starts stacking the layers on your retina — heat over pattern over color — until the separations blur. The whole, at the price of the parts.

Hubble stack is how the famous space pictures are actually made: three bands (here infrared, visible, ultraviolet), each assigned a visible color, woven into one still view. About three bands is the comfortable count — past that, layers paint over each other.

The voltage slider runs the liquid lens: a sealed droplet of oil and water whose curve bends under voltage — focus and zoom with no moving parts. That's the morph. The lens stays in the frame; the liquid inside it becomes the dial.

02

How It Works — Four Parts

No single material can catch the whole line, so the device is not one lens. It's four parts doing four jobs.

1

Many Eyes — Catch

One sensor per band: silicon for visible, InGaAs / microbolometer for infrared, UV-enhanced silicon with quartz optics for ultraviolet, an antenna for radio. The mantis shrimp runs this design — twelve-plus channels, not one better eye.

2

The Translator

Every band outside 400–700 nm is decoded into that window: heat becomes orange, UV patterns become violet markings. A mapping — a key — chosen, swappable, and printed on the frame next to the view it produced.

3

The Dial

The bridge dial (or the spinning key-ring) selects the layer — or the blend. Slow: layers one at a time. Fast: fused, Newton-style. The wearer chooses; the choosing is the wearer's, every time.

4

The Un-Morphed Eye

The last lens never changes: your eye, a ball of water with a soft gel lens. The world gets re-keyed into the eye — never the other way around. Any band can be brought to you. Not all bands at once, in detail.

03

Blueprints

Three sheets. Drawn to be built from: every part named, every fastener the one universal drive — one driver opens all of it.

Sheet A — Front Elevation

DWG SS-A · SCALE: FULL · UNITS: MM

Reading the sheet: two round frames and a bridge — the spectacles shape — with the hub where the nose would be turned into the control. Sensor pods sit above the lenses because the catchers don't need to be where the eye looks; only the translated image does. The wearer's view is a micro-display / electrochromic layer over each liquid lens.

Sheet B — Liquid Lens Cell, Cross-Section

DWG SS-B · SECTION THROUGH ONE EYE

Why liquid: the eye itself is liquid optics — a gel lens squeezed by muscle. Electrowetting lenses already ship in barcode scanners and industrial cameras: a droplet of water and oil in a sealed cell, bent by voltage, instantly, with nothing to wear out. This cell adds the translator's display layer in front of it.

Sheet C — Signal Path

DWG SS-C · CATCH → TRANSLATE → DIAL → EYE

The bridge rule: nothing reaches the eye untranslated and unlabeled, and every decode is recorded as it's made — so the seeing can be checked later by somebody who wasn't wearing the spectacles.

The Pathway — Three Phases

There's always a pathway; it just has to be found — and most of this one is already walked. Each phase below exists somewhere today; the unbuilt piece is wearing them together.

PHASE 1 · EXISTS NOW

The Bench Rig

A camera on a rotating filter wheel — the way telescope cameras have worked for decades: one exposure per band, then stack and blend.

  • Filter wheel + UV/IR filters on a scientific camera
  • Software assigns each band a visible color (the key)
  • Proves the translations on the crow, the flower, the sky
PHASE 2 · EXISTS IN PIECES

The Wearable Translator

Night vision and thermal scopes already translate one band each, live, on a face. Phase 2 puts several catchers on one frame with the dial between them.

  • Sensor pods + micro-displays in a spectacles frame
  • Bridge dial swaps keys; recorder stamps every frame
  • Fixed-focus lenses — the morph comes in Phase 3
PHASE 3 · THE UNBUILT PIECE

The Morphing Liquid Key

Electrowetting cells replace the fixed lenses. Voltage bends the droplet; the fluid and its curve become part of the key itself.

  • Liquid cells tuned per band, driven by the dial
  • Spin mode: key-ring cycling fast enough to fuse
  • The full simulation above, worn

The Limits — Stated Plainly

  • Any band, not all bands at once. The visible window is narrow and the spectrum is endless; pour everything in simultaneously and it stacks into mush. About three woven bands is the comfortable count — the dial, and the wearer, still have to choose.
  • A decode is not the thing itself. The crow doesn't see a white crow, and neither would you — you'd see crow, translated. Every eye is a key, and there is no view from no key at all.
  • Shape was never the wall. A glass lens bent into any shape is still glass. The wall is catching: each band needs its own catcher and its own window material (Table 2).
  • X-ray and gamma are not wearable bands in this revision — they need shielding and non-lens optics. They're on the spectrum bar for completeness, and the honest drawing leaves them off the face.
  • The label is load-bearing. The moment a translated view loses its printed key, it becomes the white-crow picture: a guess wearing a photograph's clothes.

The Spectrum Spectacles — a simulation and blueprint set, Rev A. Design on paper; nothing here is a built or medical device. Precedents named on the sheets are real and checkable: Newton's disc, DLP color wheels, telescope filter wheels and Hubble band-stacking, electrowetting liquid lenses, Herschel's infrared (thermometer past the red end) and Ritter's ultraviolet (past the violet). There's a pathway to get there. We find it.
