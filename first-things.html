For Grown-ups

The teaching underneath the games — one tap down, there when you want it, out of the way when you don’t.

How recognition builds

1

Name it

Hear and see the thing named, over and over, in play: “That’s red.” “That’s a three.” “That’s B.”

2

Find it

Pick it out from a few others: “Can you find the red one?” Finding is the first real proof it’s landing.

3

Say it

In their own time, the word comes back out. Pointing first is fine — saying follows finding, not the other way round.

Every game on this page walks that same path: the page names it, the game asks them to find it, and the praise says it once more.

Honest notes on pace and development Tap to open

Ranges are wide, and wide is normal. Many children name a few colours somewhere around age two to three, count objects with one-to-one tapping around three to four, and learn letter names across three, four, and five — but “somewhere around” hides a lot of perfectly ordinary variation. A child months ahead on words may be months behind on colours, and the other way round.

Every child at their own pace. What matters is the direction — more finding, more naming, over weeks and months — not where they sit next to another child, a chart, or a cousin.

This is play, not a test. There are no scores on this page, no streaks, no levels, and nothing about your child is kept — not here, not anywhere. A missed tap teaches as much as a hit: the page names the colour that was tapped and invites another try.

When to mention it: if a child isn’t responding to their name, isn’t picking up new words over months, or loses skills they had, that’s worth raising with their doctor or health visitor — not because a game says so, but because you know your child. Trust that.

Volume & together-time Tap to open

Volume: the games speak through your device’s own voice, at your device’s volume. Set it at a comfortable talking level before you hand the device over — if you have to raise your voice to be heard over it, it’s too loud for little ears at close range. Keep the speaker a reasonable distance from tiny ears, and use the Voice ON/OFF button above whenever quiet play is better.

Together-time: these games are built to be played with a grown-up, not instead of one. Sit together, say the words along with the page, and let your child tap — your voice saying “red!” right after the page does is worth more than the tap itself.

  • Short and often beats long and rare: a few happy minutes, then stop while it’s still fun.
  • Follow their lead: if they want the same colour ten times running, that repetition is the learning.
  • Take it off the screen: after “find the red one,” find red things in the room. The game is the warm-up; the world is the lesson.
  • Done means done: wandering off is a complete sentence. There’s nothing to finish and nothing is lost by stopping.
What this page does with your child’s information Tap to open

Nothing. There is no record. No account, no sign-in, no analytics, no score, no history. The games run entirely on your device; when you close the page, the play is gone with it. The only sound that leaves the device is none — the voice is your device’s own built-in speaker voice, generated on the spot.

The download button at the top saves a copy of this page to your device so it works with no connection at all — on a plane, in a waiting room, wherever. Same page, same promise.
