The Tech Tree — Blueprints

Games like Civilization lay out the whole story of human technology as a tree — each technology unlocking the next. This page is that tree, except every node is a real, free blueprint sheet: what it is, how it works, why it works, and how people actually do it — gathered in one place, free. The things that work have already been documented; they're just spread all over the web. Here they stand on one tree.

Free. Always free. No account, no paywall, nothing held over anybody. Take the whole page with you — it works offline.

Why? →

Because the knowledge is scattered. Every technology on this tree was figured out by somebody, written down somewhere, and then buried in a different corner of the web — so each new person re-derives it, pays for it, or goes without. Why is it scattered? Because nobody gathered it. That is the whole problem, and it is a gathering problem, not a knowledge problem.

How? →

Era by era, in waves, the way the games climb the tree. Each sheet carries what the technology is, how it works, why it works, and how people actually do it — graded honestly: proven, contested, or still an open how. A node marked “in the waves” is an honest not-yet, never a dead end. An unknown is not a no.

The weapons line

Weapons technologies get sheets like everything else — their history, their principles, and their why, at full educational size. A tree of human technology that skips the weapons is lying about the tree: bows, powder, and fission changed the world whether we print them or not, and understanding them is how they stop being magic.

And every weapons sheet points at the defense answer — the field, the suit — because the honest answer to a weapon is protection that works, standing on the same tree, one branch over.

The house rule, no exceptions: these sheets teach; they do not hand anyone a buildable weapon. No formulations, no proportions, no construction steps for functional weapons or explosives — on any sheet, ever.

The tool rule — what a sheet is

A sheet is a blueprint: documented knowledge, gathered and graded — honest about what is proven, what is contested, and what is still an open how. If a sheet cannot yet say how a thing works, it says so at full size and labels the gap an open how, the way the UFO sheet labels propulsion. Guesses wear labels; they never wear costumes. That grading is what makes the tree safe to build on.

What are we missing?

The tree is a map, and maps get corrected by the people walking them. A technology that belongs on a branch and isn't? A line on a node that's wrong, or a wave that should move up? Write it here — copy it and bring it to us, and the tree grows by it.
