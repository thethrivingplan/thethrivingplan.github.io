Last Card

Shed every card in your hand before the bots shed theirs. Match the top card by colour or number, bend the turn with action cards — and whatever you do, call “Last Card!” before you play down to one.

A homage to the classic colour-and-number shedding games that have filled kitchen tables for decades — they work, so we borrowed the shape and built our own: our own name, our own colours (Ember, Ocean, Leaf, Sun), our own card faces and art. Nothing here is copied from any published game.

    Why it works — the teaching layer

    The one trap this game teaches: high cards left late

    When a hand ends, the winner scores the value of every card left in everyone else’s hand. Number cards score their face. Action cards — Skip, Reverse, Draw Two — score 20 each. Wilds score 50 each. So the card that feels most powerful to hold is exactly the card that costs you most when somebody else goes out first.

    That is why shedding high cards late is a trap: a Wild Draw Four sitting in your hand on the last turn is not strength, it is 50 points you are about to hand to the winner. The strong play is usually the opposite of the greedy one — spend your heavy cards while the hand is young, keep cheap numbers for the endgame, and save a wild only when you can name the turn you will spend it on.

    Why the call rule exists

    Calling “Last Card!” is a memory-and-honesty rule: it tells the table you are one card from winning, so they can aim their Skips and Draw Twos at you. Forgetting is not cheating — it is a slip — so the price is cards, not blame: draw the penalty and play on. Watch the bots: Pip the novice genuinely forgets about one time in three, and pays for it, same as you.

    Why matching colour or number

    Two ways to match means a hand is rarely fully stuck, but the choice matters: playing the same colour keeps the colour you are long in; switching colour with a matching number can strand an opponent who has been hoarding the old one. The flow of the table — who is forced to draw, who gets skipped — is set by these small choices, the same pacing-and-contrast logic as a set list or a meal: tension, release, and knowing what comes next.

    Rules in full — tap to open, tap again to close

    Goal

    Be first to play your last card. The winner of a hand scores the value of all cards left in the other players’ hands. First to the target score (default 200, changeable in Settings) wins the match.

    Your turn

    Play one card that matches the top card by colour, by number, or — for action cards — by symbol. Wild cards match anything. If you cannot or will not play, tap the draw pile: draw one card; if it can be played you may play it at once, otherwise your turn passes.

    The cards (all our own designs)

    Numbers 0–9 in Ember, Ocean, Leaf and Sun. Pass (Skip) — the next player loses their turn. Turnabout (Reverse) — play reverses direction. Draw Two — the next player draws two and loses their turn. Wild — play it on anything and name the new colour. Wild Draw Four — name the colour and the next player draws four and loses their turn. With stacking on (Settings), a player facing a draw may answer with their own Draw Two or Wild Draw Four and pass the whole growing pile along.

    The call

    Before you play the card that leaves you holding exactly one card, tap the Call “LAST CARD!” button. Play down to one without calling and you immediately draw the penalty (default 2 cards). Bots play by the same rule, at their own honest forgetfulness rates.

    Scoring

    Numbers score face value · Pass, Turnabout and Draw Two score 20 · Wild and Wild Draw Four score 50. The hand winner collects the total from all three other hands.

    The bots, honestly

    Pip — Novice: plays a random legal card, names a random wild colour, forgets the call about 1 time in 3. Marlo — Casual: plays a sensible card, keeps wilds back, names the colour it holds most of, forgets the call about 1 time in 10. Vera — Sharp: sheds its highest-value cards first, saves a Wild Draw Four for when an opponent is nearly out, and never forgets the call. No bot sees your hand; no bot cheats.

    Settings — the rules are settings

    New settings apply from the next hand. Nothing here is locked: house rules are yours to bend.
