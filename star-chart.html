Aspects

What an aspect is, in plain words: an aspect is the angle between two bodies, measured around the wheel. When two bodies sit at one of a few special angles to each other, the tradition says the two are in conversation — how friendly or tense that conversation is depends on the angle. There are five kinds that count as major:

Conjunction (about 0° apart) — the two sit together and act as one. Sextile (60°) — easy cooperation, an opportunity that works when you use it. Square (90°) — tension and friction that force growth. Trine (120°) — easy flow, a natural talent. Opposition (180°) — two sides pulling against each other, seen clearly through the contrast.

Orb is how far an aspect is from the exact angle, in degrees — a smaller orb means closer to exact, and the tradition reads it as stronger. This chart has 23 major aspects, tightest first. The tightest is the very first line: Chiron sextile the North Node, off exact by barely a tenth of a degree. Chinese zodiac

Fire Tiger 虎
What it is

The Chinese calendar is lunisolar, and its years are named on a sixty-year cycle: twelve animals crossed with five elements. The year turns at Lunar New Year, not January 1 — the Tiger year began on February 9, 1986, so a birth on December 28, 1986 falls safely inside it, nowhere near the border. Animal Tiger, element Fire: the Fire Tiger, a combination that last came round in 1926 and won’t again until 2046.

What the tradition says

The Tiger is read as bold, independent, protective, and born to lead — courageous, unpredictable, allergic to being fenced in, and fierce about the people it counts as its own. Fire laid over the Tiger turns the heat up: more passionate, more expressive, more restless, quicker to act on a conviction and slower to cool down.

What could this mean for you

Held up next to the Western chart, this mirror shows a different facet of the same face. The tropical chart is mostly earth and water — the builder, the deep feeler — while the Tiger reading is all forward motion and protective fire. It could be read as the heat the calm Taurus surface keeps banked underneath: the independence that won’t be managed, the protectiveness that shows up the moment somebody of yours is threatened. Two traditions, one recurring note — self-reliant, and loyal at full strength.

Mayan Tzolk’in

4 Oc — the Dog
What it is

The Tzolk’in is the Maya 260-day sacred calendar: twenty day-signs turning against thirteen numbers, a combination that repeats every 260 days and was used for naming, timing, and divination. By the standard correlation between the two calendars (the GMT correlation), December 28, 1986 is the day 4 Oc — number four, day-sign Oc, the Dog.

What the tradition says

In the day-sign readings that descend from the Tzolk’in, Oc the Dog is loyalty, guidance, and the heart — the dog is the companion who guides travellers (in the old stories, even through the underworld), faithful, warm, and brave on behalf of its pack. The number four is stability and definition: the four directions, the four corners — a foundation number, the day given a frame.

What could this mean for you

Of all the mirrors on this page, this is the warmest one: where the Western chart emphasises the builder and the deep water, the Tzolk’in day reads as the loyal guide — the one who walks with people through their hard passages and doesn’t leave. The four underneath it could be read as the structure that loyalty stands on: not wandering devotion, but devotion with corners — a pack, a place, a frame. If the Fire Tiger is the heat and Capricorn the climb, 4 Oc is the company kept on the way up.

Celtic tree calendar

Birch — Dec 24 to Jan 20
What it is

Printed with its honest label: the “Celtic tree calendar” — thirteen tree months — is a modern tradition, popularised by Robert Graves in The White Goddess (1948), not a documented ancient Celtic system. It survives because people enjoy it, which is reason enough to include it as what it is. In it, a December 28 birth falls in the first tree month: Birch, which runs from December 24 to January 20.

What the tradition says

The birch is the pioneer tree — the first to leaf in spring, the first to recolonise cleared or burned land, growing where nothing else has yet managed. The tradition built on it reads Birch people as new beginnings: resilient starters, renewers, the ones who can begin again on bare ground and make it look natural.

What could this mean for you

This mirror rhymes with the chart’s Aries threads — the North Node’s go-first direction of growth, the rising sign’s border with the first sign of the zodiac. It could be read as the starter’s gift stated plainly: beginning on cleared ground, whether that’s a project, a room, or a whole plan, and being willing to be the first thin green thing up. A modern tradition, an old tree, and — like everything on this page — a mirror, not a verdict.

Only systems that can be worked out faithfully from a birth date, time, and place are on this page. A tradition that needs information we don’t have — or whose dating can’t be checked — doesn’t get invented to fill the space.
