Find your pitch

Before a song, warm up here. Pick a target note, hear it, then sing and hold it. The reading tells you the note you're actually on and how far off you are — a cent is one hundredth of the space between two notes, so under about 25 cents most ears hear "in tune."

How the grading works

No mystery score. The same three things a patient singing teacher listens for:

1 · Are you on the note?

Your voice is measured many times a second while each note plays. The middle reading for that note is compared with the target: within about half a note-step counts as on pitch, a little wider counts as close, and anything further is a miss.

2 · The edges don't count

Everybody slides into a note. The first and last slice of each note is ignored, so how you land and settle is what's graded — not the scoop getting there.

3 · Polish never grades you

Reverb, echo, and warmth change how you sound, never how you're scored. The grade reads your raw voice, so a good score here is a real one — and the polish is just the room you're singing in.

Duet rooms

Sing with another person for real — invite a friend, pick who takes the lead and who takes the harmony, and hear each other as you go. Live voices between two phones need a connection that carries sound both ways with almost no delay; that's the piece that gets built next.

Later

The VR stage

The full version: a headset puts you in the same room — a stage, lights, your duet partner standing next to you, an audience if you want one. Same songs, same grading, same polish; the room is what changes. It grows out of the duet rooms, not instead of them.

What are we missing?

A song you wish was here, a voice effect you reach for, a way of singing with people we haven't thought of — say it on the Poster Board and it goes on the list. This studio grows the way the rest of the plan does: somebody names what's missing, and it gets built.
