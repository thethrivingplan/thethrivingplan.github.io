Bumpers are rails along the gutters: a ball that would drop into the gutter bounces back into the lane instead. Standard (no bumpers) is the default. The setting applies to every game started from this device — same-device, vs the bot, and rooms you host — and the bot plays by it too. Saved on this device only. When bumpers are on, the game says so on the lane for the whole game: a bumper game's score is a bumper game's score, never dressed as standard.

Oil is real bowling: the pattern decides where the ball skids and where it grips, so it really changes where and how hard your ball hooks — spin does more on a dry lane, less on a slick one. The pattern is the same for every player and the bot. House is the standard pattern. Any other pattern in play is labelled on the lane for the whole game, so its scores are never dressed as house-pattern scores. Saved on this device only.

The shot helper draws your projected line on the lane while you aim — where the ball is headed, hook included, so you can line up the shot before you throw. On is the default. Turn it off if you want the raw lane: no line, just you and the pins. It changes nothing about the physics — only what you see. Saved on this device only.

      How online play works, in plain words: connections are peer-to-peer (PeerJS free broker) — no account, no sign-up, nothing is stored anywhere. On your turn you aim and throw on your own device; your device sends the result to everyone else, and every scorecard updates identically.       How to play & how scoring works — tap to open

      Throwing: 1) Set your start position and aim angle with the sliders (or drag on the lane). 2) Tap to lock your aim. 3) Stop the sweeping power meter — the red zone near the top is a strike-power throw, but it’s harder to control. 4) Stop the spin meter to add hook: the ball curves late, the way a real hook does. Hit the pocket — just beside the head pin — for your best strike chance. Miss the lane and it’s a gutter ball.

      Throw by touch (or mouse): the lane itself is a playing board, and every part of the shot has a touch equal. Drag the ball along the bottom of the lane to place it. Drag up the lane to set your angle — the projected line follows your finger. Drag sideways across the top of the lane to dial in spin, and watch the spin readout under the lane as you do. Or do it all in one motion: swipe up the lane to throw — the swipe’s speed sets the power, its direction sets the angle, and a curving swipe adds spin. The step-by-step sliders, meters and keyboard all stay as the other doors; use whichever you like, throw by throw.

      Rules you can change (in the Rules settings on the setup screen): bumpers — rails along the gutters that bounce a would-be gutter ball back into the lane, for every player and the bot; the lane oil pattern — House is the standard pattern, and Sport, Dry and Slick really change where and how hard the ball hooks, for you and the bot alike; and the shot helper — the projected aim line — which you can turn off for the raw lane. Standard settings are the default. A game played with a non-standard setting says so on the lane the whole game, and its scores are that setting’s scores.

      Scoring (real bowling scoring): A strike (all 10 with your first ball) scores 10 plus your next two balls. A spare (all 10 in two balls) scores 10 plus your next ball. The 10th frame gives you up to three balls, with fresh pins after a strike or a spare. A perfect game is 300.

      Keyboard: ← → move, A/D angle, Space locks aim and stops the meters.
