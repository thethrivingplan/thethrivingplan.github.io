The Chain

You and the bot trade one word at a time. Each new word must connect to the word just before it. Links are judged from our own written association bank — common, everyday associations written for this page, not a scraped list. Grades: Close (most people would say it), Stretch (real, but sideways — often the clever ones), Broken (no link in the bank), or Unfiled (a word our bank doesn't hold yet — that's our gap, not your fault).

🧠 How minds file words

Hint: A link can live in three drawers at once — meaning, sound, and memory.

Explain: Meaning — same family (ocean → wave), opposites (hot → cold), part-of (wheel → car). Sound — rhymes and echoes (moon → spoon). Memory — phrases and moments (peanut → butter; birthday → cake). Close links usually come from meaning. Clever links usually come from sound or memory — they feel like a small jump, then click.

Deep Dive: Why does the same link feel obvious to one person and random to another? Because the memory drawer is personal. “Hospital” may file next to “birth” for one person and “fear” for another. Neither filing is wrong — the bank here grades the common filing, and says so. When your link is graded Stretch or Broken, read the why: it tells you which drawer the bank expected, so you can see the gap between your filing and the common one. That gap is the game.

One-Word Clue

One word is the target (ringed in gold). The other five are decoys. Give the bot guesser exactly one word to steer it to the target — without steering it to a decoy. You are scored on how clean the steer is: a clue tied tightly to the target and loosely to everything else scores highest. Your clue can't be the target itself, contain the target, or be one of the words on the board.

🎯 Why one word is hard

Hint: The best clue is not the closest word to the target — it's the word closest to the target and far from the decoys.

Explain: “Wet” points at ocean — but also at river and rain if they're on the board. A clean clue checks every decoy before it leaves your mouth. That's the whole skill: hold six filings in mind, not one.

Deep Dive: Different guessers file differently, so the same clue lands differently. A meaning-first guesser hears “salty” and goes to ocean; a sound-first guesser hears “salty” and may drift to “sandy.” This bot shows its full ranking after each round, so you can see exactly which drawer your clue pulled on — and adjust for the guesser in front of you, which is what good clue-givers do with people, too.

Learn — the three drawers

1 · Meaning

Words filed by what they are: families (fruit: apple, banana), opposites (day, night), parts (petal, flower), and jobs (knife cuts, clock tells time). Meaning links are the fastest and most shared — that's why they grade Close.

2 · Sound

Words filed by how they sound: rhymes (cat, hat), same starts (sun, sand), echoes (whisper, whistle). Sound links feel playful and are easier to miss — a meaning-first listener may call them Broken when they're really Stretch.

3 · Memory

Words filed by what they've been through together: set phrases (salt & pepper), places (beach → towel), and personal moments. Memory links are the most personal drawer — and the main reason the same clue lands differently for different people.

The why under the why: association isn't trivia. It's a map of how a mind is organised. Playing these games, you watch your own map — which drawer you reach for first, and which drawers other minds reach for. That noticing is the skill that transfers: to clues, to conversations, to spotting when someone's words are steering you.

About & sources

The association bank was written for this page from common, everyday associations — the kind documented for over a century in word-association research (the “first word that comes to mind” exercise used since the early 1900s). No proprietary word list was scraped or copied. The bank is small and honest about it: words it doesn't hold grade Unfiled, never Broken.

The clue game is our own version of the documented one-word steering form: one clue, one target, decoys, scored on the cleanliness of the steer.

Bot honesty: the three bot levels genuinely pick differently — Sunny only takes Close meaning links; Mix takes any bank link; Weaver prefers Stretch sound & memory links. The guesser ranks board words from the same bank you are graded by, and shows its ranking.
