About & the honest print

The genre's worst feature, named

Idle games learned a bad trick: monetized waiting. The game slows down on purpose, then sells you the skip — gems, boosts, "limited offers" — so the waiting you were enjoying becomes a toll booth. This page answers it the plain way: no purchases, no ads, no pay-to-skip, ever. There is nothing to buy and nothing watching you. Waiting is the game here, not a paywall, and the numbers grow at exactly the speed printed on them.

Where this form comes from

The incremental game is a documented form with a short, public history. Around 2010 a satirical browser toy about clicking a cow showed that clicking itself could be the joke and the game. By 2013 the form had flowered: a browser game about clicking one big cookie became a phenomenon, candy- and cosmos-themed clickers followed, and a text-only incremental about lighting a fire in a dark room proved the loop worked with no pictures at all. The shared skeleton those games settled — tap to earn, buy producers that earn on their own, prices rising on an exponential curve, upgrades that multiply, and prestige: resetting for a permanent bonus — is the documented standard form, and it is the form this page follows. Where this page departs from it, the departure is labelled on the spot (the prestige-off variant is the one deliberate departure, and it wears a banner).

Offline earnings, in plain words

When you come back, the game pays your per-second rate for the real time you were gone — capped at 8 hours, your setting, printed here and in Settings. Away for a weekend? You get the cap, not the weekend. The cap is not a punishment; it is what keeps "check in twice a day" from becoming the optimal strategy in a game about patience.

How the math works (Tap to open)

Each producer's next price is its base price × 1.15owned, rounded up. 1.15 looks small; it is not. Ten purchases multiply a price by about 4, twenty by about 16, fifty by over 1,000. Meanwhile each producer pays a flat rate per second, multiplied by your upgrades and your prestige bonus (+3% per Patience Star, added together, not compounded). A purchase's payback period — price ÷ the income it adds per second — tells you how long that purchase takes to earn its own price back. Short payback is not always right: a dearer producer can pay back slower yet carry you further, which is exactly the argument the Coach keeps having with you, using your numbers.

What is saved, and where (Tap to open)

Your game, your settings, and your record are saved in this browser, on this device only, so the game can continue and pay offline earnings. Nothing leaves the device. The Forget button in Your Record wipes the record with one more confirming tap; the reset button in Settings wipes the game itself.
