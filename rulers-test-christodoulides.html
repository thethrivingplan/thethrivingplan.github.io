The Ruler's Test — Nikos Christodoulides, President of the Republic of Cyprus

Subject: Nikos Christodoulides — President of the Republic of Cyprus since 28 February 2023. The premise was verified first: the seat has not changed hands. Christodoulides, a former foreign minister who broke with the ruling Democratic Rally (DISY) to run as an independent, won the presidential runoff on 12 February 2023 with 51.97% (204,867 votes) against Andreas Mavroyiannis's 48.03% (189,335 votes), after leading the first round on 5 February 2023 with 32.04% to Mavroyiannis's 29.59% (2023 presidential election results; Reuters, 12 February 2023). Turnout was 72.05% in the first round and 72.45% in the runoff, printed here as separate sourced figures and never blended. He was sworn in on 28 February 2023 as the eighth president, for a five-year term; the next presidential election is scheduled for February 2028 (European Parliament Research Service briefing, January 2026). Cyprus is a presidential republic: the president is both head of state and head of government, appoints the Council of Ministers, and is graded here as the executive. He was still in office in November 2026 — meeting Canada's prime minister on 12 November 2026, addressing a school in Paphos on 5 November 2026, and scheduled to meet the Turkish Cypriot leader again on 24 November 2026 (Prime Minister of Canada readout, 12 November 2026; Cyprus government reporting, November 2026). This is therefore a mid-tenure grading, on roughly three years and nine months in office, not a completed-tenure file. The northern administration is not graded: it is not a sovereign seat for this list, and it appears only where its record bears directly on a test — principally the reunification talks, where the Turkish Cypriot leadership changed in 2025 from Ersin Tatar to Tufan Erhürman. His predecessor Nicos Anastasiades (president 2013–2023) appears as context where the record Christodoulides inherited — the Vasiliko LNG contract, the citizenship-by-investment ("golden passports") scheme — bears directly on a test. Graded: November 2026, on the documented public record. Method: The Ethic: "How you play the game matters. Play by the rules. Stay true to your word. And if another option becomes available, at least consider it." Each test is graded only on the evidence listed under it. Both shelves are shown — the strongest evidence that he passes, then the strongest evidence that he fails. Allegations are labeled ALLEGATION and never stated as verdicts. Contested items are named as contested, with who disputes them. Seat-holders and public figures are named; people below the public eye appear by role only, unless the documented record itself establishes their wrongdoing. Governing on the platform the voters elected is not inherently a failure and is never graded as one: the Test grades the holding, never the platform, and carrying out what he ran on is evidence on the FOR shelf of Test 5. Grades: HOLDS / FAILS / MIXED / NOT ENOUGH EVIDENCE. This record is correctable: new evidence changes grades, in the open.

Test 1 — GIVES BY ONE LAW

Same rules for allies and opponents, insiders and outsiders — no favorites at the source.

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • The inherited flagship corruption file was investigated by outside referees his government did not block. The European Public Prosecutor's Office (EPPO) opened an investigation in July 2024 into the Vasiliko LNG import terminal — a €542 million project, about €101 million of it EU-funded — on suspicion of procurement fraud, misappropriation of EU funds and corruption, prompted by the Cyprus Audit Office's January 2024 report and information from the European Climate, Infrastructure and Environment Executive Agency (CINEA) (EPPO statement via IENE and Cyprus press, July 2024). The deputy government spokesman stated the president's instruction was "to have a full investigation into this matter" (Cyprus press reporting, July 2024). The contract itself was awarded in December 2019, under Anastasiades, to the CPP-Metron consortium — a fact stated because Test 1 grades the source, and the source here predates him.
  • The January 2026 "Videogate" allegations against his own circle were put to an investigator rather than buried. After the 8 January 2026 video appeared (see Evidence against), the state appointed former Supreme Court judge Andreas Paschalides to lead an independent investigation, and the Attorney General's office was left to review its findings for any further action (Reuters, 20 July 2026). His chief of staff — Charalambos Charalambous, who is also his brother-in-law — resigned on 12 January 2026, and the First Lady, Philippa Karsera, stepped down as chair of the management committee of the Independent Social Support Body on 11 January 2026 (FES Cyprus Newsletter No. 158, January–February 2026; Irish Times, 15 January 2026). Whatever the video's truth, the people closest to the president did not keep their seats while it was examined.
  • The older "golden passports" file ended in court, not in a pardon. On 17 February 2026 the Nicosia Criminal Court found former House President Demetris Syllouris and former AKEL MP Christakis Giovanis not guilty in the citizenship-by-investment case (FES Cyprus Newsletter No. 158, January–February 2026). An acquittal is recorded here as what it is: the case was prosecuted to a verdict under his government, and the verdict was the court's.
  • The reform that would harden one law into the prosecution system was advanced by his cabinet: on 2 July 2025 the cabinet approved a package of bills splitting the Attorney General's dual role — state legal adviser and head of prosecutions — creating an independent Director of Public Prosecutions, granting the Audit Office financial independence, and fixing single non-renewable eight-year terms for the top legal and audit officials (Justice Minister Marios Hartsiotis, cabinet reporting, July 2025; European Commission 2025 Rule of Law Report, Cyprus chapter). The bills' fate in parliament is graded under Test 2; their sponsorship is the Test 1 fact.

Evidence against:

  • The Videogate video, on its face, describes a second law for those with cash and access. The eight-minute secretly filmed video, posted on X on 8 January 2026 by an account in the name "Emily Thompson," showed Charalambous, former energy minister Giorgos Lakkotrypis, and a construction company chief executive speaking with people posing as investors about cash donations bypassing the €1 million campaign-spending cap, donations channelled through the First Lady's charitable body in exchange for access to the president, and help for a Russian-linked businessman to evade EU sanctions (Irish Times, 15 January 2026; Reuters, 20 July 2026). Every element of that description is an ALLEGATION: no court has ruled on it, all three men denied wrongdoing, and the government's position — that the published cut was maliciously edited and spliced — is partly borne out by the investigator's finding that roughly 26 hours of original footage were genuine but the eight-minute published version was "not authentic since it was edited" (Reuters, 20 July 2026).
  • The investigation's conclusion is itself contested, and the contest is printed with its disputants named. Paschalides' office concluded on 20 July 2026 that the video "was a form of hybrid attack to harm the Cypriot state," that the statements in it were "empty talk and bluster," and that none of those filmed did anything wrong (Reuters, 20 July 2026). Black Cube, the Israeli private-intelligence firm that admitted in April 2026 it had produced the sting without naming its client, replied that it "stands firmly behind its evidence uncovering corruption in Cyprus" and that Cypriot authorities had verified the evidence's authenticity (Reuters, 20 July 2026). Two opposition parties, AKEL and ALMA, decried the investigator's verdict as a whitewash (Reuters, 20 July 2026). The dossier records all three positions; it adopts none as a verdict. What is not contested is the structural fact the video exposed to view: the president's brother-in-law held the presidency's gatekeeper office, and the First Lady chaired a state support body that received donations — arrangements in which access and family sat at the source, whatever was or was not sold there.
  • The outcome measure has not moved. Cyprus scored 55 of 100 in Transparency International's 2025 Corruption Perceptions Index (49th of 182, released February 2026), down from 56 in 2024 and below the EU average of 64; Transparency International's Cyprus brief attributes the score to "loopholes that remain in the country's anti-corruption system" and the failure to reform laws separating business and government (Transparency International, CPI 2025; CIREN Cyprus summary, February 2026). Three years into an anti-corruption mandate, perceived one-law enforcement is flat to slightly worse — a perception measure, recorded as such, not a verdict on any case.

Why: The grade lands at MIXED because both shelves carry documented weight and neither cancels the other. On one side, the two biggest corruption files of his tenure — Vasiliko and Videogate — were handed to investigators outside his household, his own chief of staff and his wife lost their roles within days, and his cabinet sponsored the structural reform that would separate prosecution from government advice. On the other side, the Videogate allegations — unproven, contested, and labeled as such above — describe exactly the offence Test 1 grades, the arrangements that made them plausible (family at the gate, a presidential-family charity receiving donations) were his arrangements, the investigator's exoneration is disputed by the firm that made the film and by two opposition parties, and the independent outcome measure has not improved. A HOLDS would require the contested file to be settled; a FAILS would require treating an allegation as a verdict, which this Test does not do.

Helped / Hurt ledger:

  • Helped: the Audit Office and EPPO process, which were allowed to run against a flagship state project; ordinary applicants and investors without palace access, to the extent the DPP-split reform would level the source if enacted.
  • Hurt: public trust in the presidency itself — the resignations of Charalambos Charalambous and Philippa Karsera from their roles are documented facts whatever the video's truth; Charalambous, Lakkotrypis and the businessman filmed, whose recorded words were published in an edited cut before any court heard them.

Test 2 — HOLDS WITHOUT GRIPPING

Do the courts, the electoral commission, the media, the prosecution, and the central bank keep their autonomy — or is independence punished and control centralized?

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • Elections ran, and his side lost ground in them, without interference. The House of Representatives election of 24 May 2026 was administered and its result stood: DISY won a plurality of 17 of the 56 elected seats (27.15%), AKEL 15 (23.86%), ELAM doubled to 8, DIKO took 8, and two new parties — ALMA and Direct Democracy Cyprus — entered with 4 seats each, on turnout of 66.91% (2026 legislative election results; Who Governs Europe analysis, May 2026). Two of the parties that had backed his presidency, EDEK and DIPA, lost all their seats; the government publicly accepted the result and said it would seek new agreements rather than disputing it (government spokesman Konstantinos Letymbiotis via RIK, May 2026). Annita Demetriou of DISY — the opposition — was re-elected President of the House on 4 June 2026 (2026 in Cyprus chronology).
  • Parliament checks him in the open and he governs without a majority. Cyprus's presidential system means the election did not form his government, but the House passes his laws and budgets; after May 2026 his original support base shrank and legislation depends on issue-by-issue bargains, a constraint he has operated under rather than overridden (Who Governs Europe, May 2026). The tax reform and teacher-evaluation reform passed in late 2025 with strong majorities assembled across the chamber (Phileleftheros interview reporting, 29 December 2025).
  • The courts decided the term's most politically charged personnel case themselves. Auditor-General Odysseas Michaelides was removed on 18 September 2024 by an eight-judge panel of the Supreme Constitutional Court, ruling unanimously that he had conducted himself inappropriately; the application had been filed by Attorney-General George Savvides in April 2024 (Cyprus press reporting, September 2024). Christodoulides publicly refused to involve himself while it was before the court: "Any decision the courts make will be respected by me to the fullest extent" (Christodoulides statement via Cyprus Mail reporting, 2024). The removal instrument was a court judgment, not a presidential dismissal — the president has no power to dismiss the Auditor-General.
  • His government carried the Cyprus Presidency of the Council of the EU (1 January – 30 June 2026) as a broker rather than a grip: the presidency closed dossiers across defence, competitiveness, enlargement and the EU budget framework, and handed over to Ireland on schedule (President's review speech, Cyprus Presidency, June 2026; European Parliament briefing, January 2026).

Evidence against:

  • The Michaelides removal is contested at its root, and the contest goes to the presidency. Michaelides alleged — an ALLEGATION, recorded with its maker named — that Christodoulides and Savvides "colluded" to have him removed after his audit found the president should repay €18,176 in leave payments, and that the Attorney-General acted "as if he were Christodoulides' private lawyer"; he announced an appeal to the European Court of Human Rights (Michaelides letter to the House audit committee and Phileleftheros interview, 2024–2025). The political parties' reaction is documented fact: AKEL called the removal "a serious blow to transparency, public accountability and oversight," DIKO — his own coalition partner — called it a blow to the institution of the Auditor-General, and Volt said the bar for firing independent officials had been greatly lowered (Cyprus press reporting, 18 September 2024). The court's ruling stands as the legal record; the allegation of presidential motive is unproven; but the documented effect is that the state's most aggressive auditor left office mid-term after auditing the president, and the institution's independence is weaker in public standing than it was.
  • The prosecution reform that would loosen the executive's oldest grip stalled with the gripped office objecting. The Law Office bills have been pending before parliament since July 2025; Attorney-General Savvides raised constitutional reservations about splitting his own office's dual role; parliament went into recess in late April 2026 for the elections with the debate suspended, and the European Commission's 2026 rule-of-law reporting records the bills still pending (European Commission Rule of Law Reports 2025 and 2026, Cyprus chapters). A president who sponsors a reform and then lets it sleep while the affected office-holder objects has not gripped the referee — but he has not freed the referee either.
  • The media environment narrowed on his watch, on documented monitors' counts. Cyprus fell 12 places in RSF's 2025 World Press Freedom Index, from 65th to 77th of 180, with RSF citing government-proposed legislation that could limit media freedom (RSF World Press Freedom Index 2025). The government's European Media Freedom Act transposition bill was attacked by the Cyprus Union of Journalists, the Journalistic Ethics Committee and the House interior committee chair as giving state-appointed authorities power to demand journalists reveal sources and to impose fines — the committee chair called the bill in its current form "unacceptable" (Cyprus Mail editorial and committee reporting, 2026). Separately, the journalists' union raised alarm over three criminal investigations against journalist and author Makarios Drousiotis — false news, personal-data and fundraising charges — warning of a chilling effect; the investigations are police and prosecutorial matters, recorded here as the environment, with no documented presidential direction of them (Cyprus Journalists' Union statement via EUalive, 2026).
  • The counterweight he himself cited cuts both ways: in July 2025 he wrote to the EU's leadership protesting Turkey's reported summons of Turkish Cypriot journalist Şener Levent as a "flagrant violation of freedom of expression" (Cyprus News Agency, 12 July 2025) — defending a critic's press freedom abroad while his own government's media bill drew the same charge at home.

Why: MIXED, because the formal referees demonstrably still work — elections he could not control cost his allies their seats, an opposition speaker presides over the House, courts and not the president decided the Auditor-General's fate, and no documented act shows him directing a prosecution, a court or the central bank. But two referees are weaker in fact or in standing than he found them: the audit institution lost its head in a process the removed man alleges — unproven — was payback for auditing the president, and the press faces a government-drafted bill its own profession and a House committee call a control instrument, alongside a 12-place fall in the international index. Sponsoring the DPP reform earns credit; leaving it pending since July 2025 while its subject objects caps that credit.

Helped / Hurt ledger:

  • Helped: voters and opposition parties, whose May 2026 verdict was executed in full, including the elimination of two pro-government parties; the House as an institution, which gained real bargaining power over the executive.
  • Hurt: the Audit Office as an institution and Odysseas Michaelides personally, removed mid-term (by court ruling; motive contested); journalists as a class, facing the EMFA transposition bill as drafted and, in Makarios Drousiotis's documented case, three concurrent criminal investigations.

Test 3 — ANSWERS THE WOBBLE

When courts, voters, parliament, the street, foreign partners, or events push back, does he adjust openly — or deny and double down?

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • The biggest wobble of the tenure was answered with de-escalation, stated openly. On 1–2 March 2026, during the Iran war that began with Israeli–US strikes on 28 February 2026, a Shahed-type loitering munition struck a hangar at RAF Akrotiri — the British sovereign base on Cyprus — causing minor damage and no deaths; further drones toward Cyprus were intercepted on 1 and 4 March, two of them by Greek F-16s in Lebanese airspace, and Larnaca airport briefly closed on 4 March (2026 strikes on Akrotiri and Dhekelia chronology; UK and Cyprus government statements, March 2026). Attribution was contested — Cyprus's foreign minister said the drone was Iranian-manufactured and launched from Lebanon; UK officials confirmed only that the weapon was Iranian-made — and both positions are printed here with their makers. Christodoulides stated repeatedly that Cyprus was not a participant in any military operation and did not intend to become one, corrected early reports of Iranian missiles fired at Cyprus ("no indication that there was a threat to the country," government spokesman, 1 March 2026), and took the matter up with London over the terms of the bases' use rather than retaliating or grandstanding (Cyprus government statements, March 2026). This was a no-good-choice situation in the clause's sense: the options actually open were (a) protest the bases' use and risk the UK security guarantee Cyprus relies on, (b) silence, and absorb strikes connected to a war Cyprus did not join, or (c) public non-belligerence plus a private renegotiation of terms. He took (c). No evidence in the record shows he built this trap — the bases' status dates to the 1960 treaties and the war was not his — and he was honest inside it about Cyprus's non-participation. Graded as the best available choice in the moment, not as a demerit.
  • When the other side's politics changed, he treated it as an opening rather than a threat. Tufan Erhürman — who favours resuming talks on the agreed UN basis — replaced the two-state advocate Ersin Tatar as Turkish Cypriot leader in October 2025. If another option becomes available, the Test asks whether the ruler at least considered it openly: Christodoulides met Erhürman in the UN-facilitated format through 2026, presented his own five-point proposal at the 28 January 2026 trilateral with envoy María Ángela Holguín (reaffirm the agreed basis, list the Crans-Montana convergences, convene an enlarged meeting, resume talks from the convergences, open four crossing points), accepted Holguín's judgment that no enlarged meeting was yet justified, hosted UN Secretary-General António Guterres's first visit to the island in 16 years on 27–29 July 2026, and on 9 November 2026 agreed with Erhürman and Holguín to open two new crossing points, coordinate emergency responses and share archival materials, with a further leaders' meeting set for 24 November 2026 (Security Council Report, January 2026; FES Cyprus Newsletters No. 158 and No. 161, 2026; Reuters reporting via Regional Balance, 9 November 2026). The January meeting ended without agreement and he said so; the process continued anyway.
  • Voters pushed back in May 2026 and the answer was acceptance, not denial of the result's meaning at the level of fact: the government did not contest a single seat, the spokesman acknowledged the loss of EDEK and DIPA from parliament, and the presidency announced talks with other forces to build new support (RIK interview, May 2026). The EU Council presidency's own wobble — taking the chair on 1 January 2026, days before Videogate broke — was answered by running the presidency to a documented conclusion on 30 June 2026 rather than pleading the scandal as an excuse (Cyprus Presidency review, June 2026).
  • The Schengen miss was owned and re-dated in public: when the 2026 accession target slipped (Test 5), the government stated the new expectation openly — technical readiness confirmed, the Council vote outstanding, 1 July 2027 named as the target by the president on 12 November 2026 (Cyprus Mail and IMI Daily reporting, October–November 2026).

Evidence against:

  • The first answer to Videogate was a frame, not an inquiry. Within hours of the video's release on 8 January 2026, government sources were describing it as a "malicious" hybrid attack bearing a foreign state's fingerprints — before any investigator had seen the 26 hours of original footage, and four days before the president's own first statement ("I have nothing to fear," 12 January 2026) (Irish Times, 15 January 2026; BytesEU reporting of the Guardian, January 2026). The hybrid-attack conclusion may yet prove right — the appointed investigator reached it in July 2026 — but it was asserted by the accused government first and investigated second, and when the investigator agreed with the government's opening frame, AKEL and ALMA's "whitewash" charge (Reuters, 20 July 2026) was the predictable cost. Deny-first is the pattern Test 3 grades against, even where the denial later finds support.
  • On Vasiliko, assurances outran adjustment. After the CPP-Metron consortium terminated the LNG contract on 18 July 2024, the president's public line was that the consortium "should have never been given" the project and that "the project will be implemented" (Associated Press via Natural Gas World, July 2024). Two years on, the terminal remained unfinished, the FSRU Prometheus sat abroad awaiting the pier, the contractor's arbitration claims ran to about €150 million, and his former energy minister Giorgos Papanastasiou was publicly attributing the delays to "politics and vested interests" — prompting AKEL to ask the anti-corruption authority to investigate the former minister's claims in November 2026 (Cyprus press reporting, November 2026). The inherited contract explains the trap's origin; it does not explain two years of completion assurances with no completion.
  • The street's and the buffer zone's pushback was met with a closed door. In spring–summer 2024, after declaring a migration "state of serious crisis" in April 2024 and suspending Syrian asylum processing, the government refused entry to nearly 30 asylum seekers, including children, stranded in the UN buffer zone — the president's stated reason being to avoid "the creation of a new route for illegal migration" — while UNHCR urged Cyprus to process their claims (Mixed Migration Centre, Quarterly Update Q2 2024). The policy was explained; it was not adjusted, and the people in the zone remained in limbo in extreme heat on the documented record.

Why: MIXED, because the tenure's two largest shocks were answered in the open and without doubling down — the March 2026 strikes were met with stated non-belligerence and correction of false reports, and the change of Turkish Cypriot leadership was treated as an option to consider, producing the November 2026 crossings agreement — while the two domestic scandals were answered frame-first: Videogate was labelled a hybrid attack by the accused before it was investigated, and Vasiliko drew completion assurances for two years without a terminal. The May 2026 electoral wobble sits between: the result was fully accepted, though the government publicly declined to read it as a verdict on its management.

Helped / Hurt ledger:

  • Helped: Cypriot civilians and the island's security position in March 2026, kept out of a regional war; both communities' daily movement, if the two crossings agreed on 9 November 2026 open as announced; EU partners, who got a completed Council presidency.
  • Hurt: the asylum seekers, including children, held in the buffer zone in 2024; electricity consumers, who continue to pay oil-fired prices while the gas terminal that would cut generation costs by an estimated 15–25% (government estimate) remains unfinished; the presidency's own credibility on Videogate, spent on a conclusion announced before the evidence was examined.

Test 4 — COUNTS EVERYONE

Does the accounting include the Turkish Cypriots, the communities of the island, migrants and asylum seekers, women, and the poor — or are some counted out?

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • Turkish Cypriots are counted in his diplomacy as citizens, not only as a negotiating counterparty. In July 2025 he wrote to the European Commission, Council and Parliament presidents protesting Ankara's reported summons of Şener Levent, stressing that Turkish Cypriots "as citizens of the Republic of Cyprus and of the European Union, are entitled to full protection of their fundamental rights" (Cyprus News Agency, 12 July 2025). The confidence-building track with Erhürman is explicitly framed by the UN as measures "that could improve daily life for all Cypriots," with new crossing points the key item; four of the six initiatives agreed in 2025 were achieved by July 2025 — a technical committee on youth, environment and climate work, cemetery restoration, and a demining agreement pending technical details — and two more crossings were agreed on 9 November 2026 (Security Council Report, January 2026; UN Secretary-General's statements, July 2025 and July 2026; Reuters, 9 November 2026).
  • The fiscal record counts downward as well as up. The tax reform in force from 1 January 2026 was designed with progressive per-child exemptions rising with each child; child allowance rose 5% and income and property limits were removed for families with five or more children; optional all-day schools grew from 222 in 2022–23 to 326, with 60 more planned for 2026–27; social expenditure rose about 6% in 2024–25 with a further 6% planned for 2026; and a support package of over €200 million from April 2026 cut fuel tax and electricity VAT and zero-rated basic foods (President's Annual Planning 2026 address, 5 February 2026; large-families event address, May 2026; Phileleftheros interview, December 2025). The macroeconomic frame those measures sit in — growth of 3.8% in 2025, a surplus of 2.6% of GDP, debt down to 55% of GDP, a return to "A" credit ratings for the first time since 2011 — is the government's stated record (CNA reporting, 2026).
  • Women were counted in law even where the cabinet pledge fell short (Test 5): a Gender Mainstreaming Law was enacted, the Commissioner for Gender Equality's role was strengthened by legislation, gender officers sit in every ministry since March 2023, maternity leave was increased and parental leave extended, and childcare and informal-care periods are to count as insured periods under the pension reform, including for women who never worked (Commissioner for Gender Equality speeches, gov.cy, 2026; President's address via Deputy Minister, May 2026).

Evidence against:

  • The island's constitutional accounting still counts Turkish Cypriots out of the room where laws are made, and his tenure has not changed that structure: 24 of the House's 80 seats, allocated to the Turkish Cypriot community, have remained vacant since 1964, and the May 2026 election filled only the 56 Greek Cypriot seats (2026 legislative election record). This predates him by six decades and no president can fill those seats alone — it is recorded as the standing structure his "president of all Cypriots" pledge (victory speech, 12 February 2023) operates inside, and against which the crossings-and-committees progress above is the partial counterweight, not a settlement. No substantive negotiation on the settlement itself had resumed by November 2026; the last talks remain those that collapsed at Crans-Montana in 2017 (Security Council Report, January 2026).
  • Migrants and asylum seekers were counted as a flow to be reversed, and the reversal is the government's own headline. The documented results: asylum applications down 69% between 2022 and 2024, departures exceeding arrivals for the first time (10,941 departures in 2024, a 179% departures-to-arrivals ratio, the EU's highest), processing cut to one to three months, benefits terminated immediately on rejection, and irregular sea arrivals at zero from May 2024 (Interior Minister Constantinos Ioannou and Deputy Migration Minister Nicholas Ioannides, government figures, January 2025). This was his elected platform and is graded as kept word under Test 5, not as a platform failure. What Test 4 grades is the counting: the April 2024 suspension of Syrian asylum-claim processing, the buffer-zone refusal documented in Test 3, and the summer 2025 scheme offering Syrian families cash to withdraw claims and return — with one household member permitted to stay and work — sit on the hurt side of the ledger for the people concerned (Politico reporting via Kamumedya, December 2025; MMC Q2 2024). Roughly 40 Syrians a day were requesting return from 9 December 2024, on the government's count (Ioannides, January 2025) — a figure printed as the government's, since voluntariness under a suspended asylum system is precisely what is contested by the NGOs cited in the migration reporting.
  • The property file cuts the other way across the divide and he defended the cut: in November 2026 he publicly refuted opposition criticism of his government's handling of the case of Israeli developer Simon Aykut, prosecuted in the Republic over development of Greek Cypriot-owned property in the north (Cyprus News Agency, 23 November 2026). The prosecution counts Greek Cypriot owners' rights as live law; Turkish Cypriot residents and the northern administration count the same prosecutions as intimidation. Both countings are recorded; the dossier notes only that his government chose the owners' side of the ledger and defended the choice openly.
  • The poorest pensioners are still waiting on the flagship promise made to them: the minimum pension of about €510 that the president himself called "unacceptable" in December 2025 remained the figure he was still promising to raise through 2026, with low-income pensioner support extended by cabinet decision only to 30 June 2026 at a cost of €13.5 million for about 24,000 households (Phileleftheros interview, December 2025; cabinet reporting, 2025–2026). The pension reform itself had not been enacted by grading (Test 5).

Why: MIXED, because the counting genuinely runs in both directions at once. Turkish Cypriots are defended by name as rights-holding citizens and served by a working, if slow, confidence-building track that produced crossings, committees and demining — while remaining outside the legislature and no nearer a settlement. Families, children and low-income households received documented, progressive transfers inside a strong fiscal record — while the poorest pensioners still live on the €510 floor the president himself condemned. And the migration file, kept as promised, counts arrivals and departures with precision while the people in the buffer zone and the suspended Syrian caseload were, on the documented record, counted as a route to be closed rather than as claims to be heard.

Helped / Hurt ledger:

  • Helped: large families and parents (child allowance, per-child tax exemptions, all-day schools); crossers of the Green Line in both communities, if the November 2026 crossings open; Turkish Cypriot journalists and citizens whose EU-citizen rights the president asserted against Ankara; households receiving the €200 million-plus 2026 support package.
  • Hurt: asylum seekers in the buffer zone in 2024 and Syrian applicants under the processing suspension; Syrian families who withdrew claims under the return-incentive scheme (voluntariness contested); low-income pensioners, still on the unreformed minimum while the flagship reform remains pending.

Test 5 — KEEPS HIS WORD OVER TIME

Program pledges and public statements vs. the record since 28 February 2023, with dates.

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for (promises kept or credibly in motion):

  • The tax reform — delivered, on the platform he was elected on. He ran on modernising the state and reforming taxation; parliament approved the tax transformation in late 2025 and it entered into force on 1 January 2026, the first comprehensive tax reform in over two decades, with progressive child-linked exemptions (President's New Year message, 31 December 2025; CNA, October 2026). Crossing 1 — kept his word AND it helped people: households, the middle class and families are the reform's documented beneficiaries, on the government's design and the parliamentary majority that passed it.
  • The teacher-evaluation reform — delivered against union pushback. Approved by parliament alongside the tax reform in late 2025, the first change to teacher evaluation in decades; he stated the government "would not retreat" under union reaction, and did not (Phileleftheros interview, 29 December 2025).
  • The migration programme — delivered as written. The platform's tougher migration stance produced the documented reversal graded in Test 4: applications down 69% (2022–2024), departures exceeding arrivals, processing in one to three months (government figures, January 2025). Crossing 2 — kept his word AND it still hurt people: the keeping earns full Test 5 credit here; the hurt — the buffer-zone families, the suspended Syrian caseload — is recorded in Test 4's ledger in full and is not laundered by the keeping.
  • The EU Council presidency — delivered as promised. Pledged as a "national mission" run under the motto "An Autonomous Union. Open to the World," it ran 1 January – 30 June 2026 and closed with the MFF negotiating box, partial general approaches on key instruments, and dossiers advanced across defence, competitiveness, enlargement and social cohesion, on the presidency's own review (President's review speech, June 2026).
  • Reunification readiness — the word was "ready even tomorrow," and the conduct matches the word as far as his side controls it.
