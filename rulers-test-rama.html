The Ruler's Test — Edi Rama, Prime Minister of Albania

Subject: Edi Rama — Prime Minister of Albania since September 2013 and chairman of the Socialist Party (PS) since 2005; previously Minister of Culture (1998–2000) and Mayor of Tirana (2000–2011). He is the only Albanian prime minister to win four consecutive terms (2013, 2017, 2021, and on 11 May 2025, when the PS took 53.3% of the vote and 83 of 140 seats). His tenure is defined by the 2016 justice reform — the constitutional overhaul, backed by the US and EU and passed with cross-party votes, that vetted every judge and prosecutor in the country and created the Special Structure Against Corruption and Organized Crime (SPAK, operational since December 2019) — and by Albania's advance to the front of the EU accession queue: by November 2026 all six negotiation clusters were open and four chapters provisionally closed. Graded: November 2026, on the documented public record. Method: Each test is graded only on the evidence listed under it. Both shelves are shown — the strongest evidence that he passes, then the strongest evidence that he fails. Allegations are labeled ALLEGATION and never stated as verdicts. Contested items are named as contested, with who disputes them. Seat-holders and public figures are named; people below the public eye appear by role only, unless the documented record itself establishes their wrongdoing. Grades: HOLDS / FAILS / MIXED / NOT ENOUGH EVIDENCE. This record is correctable: new evidence changes grades, in the open.

Test 1 — GIVES BY ONE LAW

Same rules for allies and opponents, insiders and outsiders — no favorites at the source.

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • The institution his reform built has struck his own camp at every level, repeatedly, and the strikes were allowed to land. Former Interior Minister Saimir Tahiri was convicted of abuse of office in February 2022 (3 years 4 months; acquitted of drug trafficking). Former Environment Minister Lefter Koka was convicted in the incinerators affair — 10 years in the Fier case and 6 years 8 months in the Elbasan case at first instance (September 2023; the Fier verdict was overturned on appeal in December 2024 and a retrial ordered — the Elbasan sentence stood as definitive). Socialist mayors were arrested and prosecuted, among them Vangjush Dako of Durrës and Safet Gjici of Kukës (2023), and Tirana municipal directors in the "5D" case (2024). In 2025 alone SPAK sent 34 corruption proceedings to court with 160 defendants and secured final convictions against 10 former senior officials (European Commission Albania Report, 4 November 2026; SPAK annual reporting to Parliament, 2026).
  • When SPAK sought the arrest of his own former deputy prime minister, Arben Ahmetaj, Rama's parliamentary majority voted to lift his immunity (14 July 2023, 117 votes in favour). Ahmetaj fled to Switzerland before the warrant could be executed and has been internationally wanted since July 2023. The exact record: he is charged with passive corruption, money laundering, and concealment of assets in the incinerators file, and is on trial in absentia in a separate abuse-of-office case (the Buka privatisation); no verdict against him appears in the sources reviewed as of November 2026, and an extradition request to Switzerland was reported in November 2025. He denies the charges and calls the case political. (The businessmen behind the incinerator concessions — Klodian Zoto, Mirel Mërtiri, Stela Gugallja — were convicted in absentia; the fugitive here is the minister, and he has not been convicted.)
  • The law also reached the two leaders of the opposition while he held the majority to stop it — and their cases proceeded through courts, not decrees. Sali Berisha, the Democratic Party leader, was placed under house arrest (December 2023 – 27 November 2024) and formally charged on 11 September 2024 in the Partizani-complex privatisation case — an ALLEGATION he denies, calling it a vendetta ordered by Rama; a court revoked the house arrest, and his trial is pending. Ilir Meta, former president and Freedom Party leader, was arrested on 21 October 2024 and formally charged on 27 May 2025 with corruption, money laundering, and asset concealment — ALLEGATIONs he denies; his trial began in August 2025. Berisha also carries foreign designations on the documented record: the US barred him in May 2021 under Section 7031(c) for "significant corruption," the UK followed in 2022, and in June 2026 the US State Department confirmed it had issued waivers lifting the entry bar on national-interest grounds — a waiver, not a revocation of the underlying designation.
  • In September 2026 SPAK moved against Rama's own intelligence chief: the Special Court suspended SHISH director Vlora Hyseni and ordered her house arrest on suspicion of aiding a suspect and disclosing classified information in the AKSHI state-IT tenders investigation (ALLEGATIONs; she surrendered on return from abroad). Rama, in New York at the time, had her dismissed by presidential decree the same evening and raised no institutional obstacle (SPAK statement and Reuters/OCCRP reporting, 21–22 September 2026).

Evidence against:

  • When the case reached his sitting deputy, the ceiling moved. SPAK indicted Deputy Prime Minister and Infrastructure Minister Belinda Balluku in November 2025 over alleged rigging of public tenders; the Special Court suspended her from duty on 20 November 2025. Rama challenged the suspension before the Constitutional Court (which on 6 February 2026 ended its proceedings without overturning it), publicly rejected her resignation three times by his own account, and removed her only in a cabinet reshuffle in late February 2026. SPAK's request of 16 December 2025 for authorization to arrest her was held by the Socialist-controlled Assembly for nearly three months and then rejected (12 March 2026). The European Commission's November 2026 report names that sequence a concern for "checks and balances" and "the principle of equality before the law," and records "strong negative reactions" and direct attacks on SPAK from the executive and legislature. Balluku was formally sent to trial on 16 November 2026 — charged with corruption, money laundering, and asset concealment across nine tender procedures, alongside 25 other defendants (ALLEGATIONs; she denies them) — but she faces that trial unarrested, an outcome no comparably charged figure in this file obtained. The contrast is internal to his own majority: the same Assembly voted within days to authorize proceedings touching Berisha (2023) and Ahmetaj (2023).
  • The protection was also proposed as a general rule, not only practiced in one case. On 16 February 2026 Rama submitted, over his own signature, amendments to Article 242 of the Criminal Procedure Code that would bar courts from suspending the prime minister, ministers, the president, and other senior officeholders during criminal proceedings. He presented it as a defence of executive authority unconnected to Balluku's case; the opposition and EU statements on record contest that reading, and the EU Delegation publicly restated that SPAK's independence is a cornerstone of the accession process (Reuters, 16 February 2026; EU Delegation statement to BIRN, February 2026). Adoption of the amendment is not confirmed in the sources reviewed; the proposal itself is the documented act.
  • The "Fiscal Peace" tax-settlement scheme adopted under his government drew a formal caution from the European Commission, which called for it to be brought in line with FATF anti-money-laundering standards — a settlement mechanism critics in the domestic debate describe as an amnesty weighted toward those who evaded (European Commission Albania Report, November 2026; the characterization is the critics', labeled as such).
  • A contested favoritism file sits at the edge of his office: the strategic-investor status granted in December 2024 to a development project on Sazan Island and at Zvërnec linked to Jared Kushner's Affinity Partners. SPAK has opened investigations into property-title transfers at Zvërnec (European Commission, November 2026). The opposition's claim that the grant was a political favour is an ALLEGATION, unadjudicated; the government presents the project as a lawful strategic investment. No charge names Rama.

Test 2 — HOLDS WITHOUT GRIPPING

Do the reformed courts and SPAK, the media, the central bank, and the municipalities keep their autonomy — or is independence punished and control centralized?

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • The vetting he championed was allowed to cut as deep as designed. The re-evaluation of all judges and prosecutors, concluded formally in March 2026 after nine years, dismissed or removed 456 magistrates and confirmed 348 in office (EU Rule of Law Report 2026, Albania chapter) — a purge of the bench conducted by independent vetting bodies, not by the executive, and one that at its height left the Constitutional Court and High Court without quorum for extended periods rather than packed by the government.
  • The reformed courts rule against the state and against his government's preferences, and the rulings stand. The Constitutional Court voided the legal acts transferring the National Theatre's ownership in July 2021 (after the building's demolition — see Test 3); on 22 April 2025 it ruled unanimously (8–0) that the search of journalist Elton Qyno and seizure of his equipment by SPAK had disproportionately violated freedom of expression and source protection, and ordered the extracted data destroyed; it declined to rescue Balluku's suspension challenge in February 2026; and a special court released Berisha from house arrest in November 2024 over the prosecution's position. The European Commission's 2026 report states that SPAK "has demonstrated independence" and is building a sustainable track record.
  • The succession at SPAK happened by the book: when Altin Dumani's three-year term ended in December 2025, the High Prosecutorial Council elected Klodian Braho as chief special prosecutor (11 December 2025, 7 of 11 votes) in a contested vote the government did not control; under Braho, SPAK escalated the Balluku case to trial and opened the AKSHI operation that reached the intelligence chief (SPAK and BIRN reporting, December 2025 – November 2026).
  • The central bank's autonomy shows no documented breach: Governor Gent Sejko was reappointed by Parliament in February 2022 for a second seven-year term, monetary policy has run without recorded government interference, and no governor, judge, prosecutor, or central banker is documented as removed for ruling or deciding against the government.

Evidence against:

  • The executive's pressure on the judiciary in 2025–26 was sustained, public, and from the top. After Veliaj's arrest (Test 3), Rama convened his parliamentary group for a set-piece attack on SPAK — calling the indictment a "400-page detective novel" with "no evidence," accusing prosecutors of arbitrary detention and of recreating the unchecked prosecutorial power of the communist era, and warning that the legislature and executive are "independent from the power of the judiciary" too (parliamentary group address, February 2025, and repeated statements through 2026, including calling Veliaj's continued detention "a unique calvary in the history of democratic Europe" after the High Court upheld it on 15 September 2026). The European Commission records this rhetoric — used, it notes, by majority and opposition alike — as discrediting the judiciary.
  • The pressure became material in the judges'-pay conflict of 2026: a dispute between the government and the judicial councils over implementing a Constitutional Court ruling on judicial salaries left judges' August 2026 salaries unpaid — a situation the European Commission calls "unprecedented" and a concern for judicial independence. Parliament approved a 2% increase and released the arrears on 18 September 2026; the judges' and prosecutors' associations have challenged the law in the Constitutional Court (European Commission Albania Report, November 2026).
  • The Article 242 proposal (Test 1) belongs here too: the response to a court suspending a minister was a bill, signed by the prime minister, to remove the courts' power to suspend ministers.
  • Media autonomy is squeezed by documented state action short of formal censorship. On 9 August 2025 state police surrounded the Focus Media Group headquarters (News24 television, BalkanWeb, Panorama, Gazeta Shqiptare), cut power, and took News24 off air in an eviction over a disputed lease on state property the government had transferred to a state arms company in March 2025; the group resumed broadcasting from other premises ten days later while its headquarters remained blocked. The government states the operation enforced a final court eviction ruling on an expired lease (CONTESTED: the group states it holds a lease on part of the complex to 2033 and that no final order covered the buildings seized; a Tirana court on 12 August 2025 partially upheld the group's request for interim relief). RSF, IPI, ARTICLE 19, and the SafeJournalists Network condemned the operation's manner and proportionality. Albania ranked 80th in RSF's 2025 World Press Freedom Index — improved from 99th, still in the "problematic" band. The longer record includes Rama's own rhetoric (journalists as "kazans" — rubbish bins — and "public enemies," condemned by the Council of Europe, which counted 35 defamation suits filed by Socialist officials in 2018–19) and the 2015 closure of Agon Channel after a tax seizure, whose owner later won an ICSID arbitration award against Albania — a state action against a critical broadcaster that an international tribunal found wrongful.
  • Municipal autonomy exists on paper but the map is one-party: the Socialists have held Tirana and nearly all municipalities since the opposition-boycotted 2019 local elections (Test 4), and Tirana has been run by acting officials since its elected mayor's arrest in February 2025 — with Rama refusing, on the record, to dismiss him (Test 3).

Test 3 — ANSWERS THE WOBBLE

When courts, voters, the EU, or events push back, does he adjust openly — or deny and double down?

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • The clearest full retreat in the file is the "anti-defamation" package. His majority passed the two media laws on 18 December 2019 over the objections of the Council of Europe, the OSCE, the EU, and every domestic journalists' organization. President Meta returned them; on 30 January 2020 the majority postponed the override vote to await the Venice Commission. The Commission's June 2020 opinion found the package would chill free debate and create censorship, and recommended self-regulation over administrative control. Rama publicly thanked the Commission and promised its suggestions would be reflected "without wasting time"; the package was never brought into force, and in October 2022 his parliamentary group chairman conceded on the record that the initiative "has been defeated" and remained only to be withdrawn procedurally. He pushed the law through, was checked by Europe's referees, and let it die rather than govern with it (Venice Commission opinion, 19 June 2020; Citizens.al and S&D reporting, 2020–2022).
  • Street pressure has moved him before: the December 2018 student protests were answered within weeks by the dismissal of eight ministers including the education minister and a "Pact for the University" conceding fee and governance demands in part (government announcements, December 2018 – January 2019).
  • Even in the Balluku confrontation, the end state was adjustment, not rupture: he litigated the suspension in the Constitutional Court and lost without defying the outcome, dismissed her in February 2026, and her prosecution proceeded to a trial request. And in the Hyseni case (September 2026) he accepted SPAK's move against his own intelligence chief the same day, dismissing her by decree rather than shielding her (Reuters, September 2026).
  • The EU accession process itself is a thirteen-year record of absorbing external correction: the justice reform's design, the vetting, and the cluster benchmarks were all accepted as conditions, and by November 2026 the Commission recorded Albania meeting the Cluster 1 interim benchmarks and provisionally closing four chapters (European Commission, November 2026).

Evidence against:

  • The National Theatre is the doubling-down archetype. After two years of daily protest by artists and activists, Europa Nostra's listing of the 1939 building among Europe's seven most endangered sites (March 2020), and condemnation by more than twenty MEPs, the theatre was demolished at about 4:30 a.m. on 17 May 2020, during COVID-19 gathering restrictions, by roughly 800 police who forcibly evacuated and detained protesters. The Constitutional Court ruled the enabling ownership transfer unconstitutional in July 2021 — after the building was rubble. The promised replacement theatre, pledged for construction start in 2021, had not been built five years later, and in March 2024 it emerged that the development partner intended a 23-storey tower on adjacent transferred land, contradicting public guarantees given by Rama and Veliaj (Europa Nostra; Constitutional Court decision, July 2021; Citizens.al five-year review, May 2025).
  • The Veliaj case shows the reflex at full length. SPAK arrested Tirana's mayor — Rama's closest municipal ally — on 10 February 2025 on nine counts of corruption and money laundering (ALLEGATIONs; he denies them; no verdict as of November 2026). Rama refused to dismiss him, attacked the indictment as evidence-free, accused prosecutors of Kafkaesque coercion of witnesses, and kept escalating through 2026 as court after court upheld the detention (Constitutional Court partially accepted Veliaj's appeal in May 2026 and ordered reconsideration; the High Court re-upheld detention on 15 September 2026). Veliaj has now been in pre-trial detention for over twenty months, and in November 2026 he publicly turned against Rama from prison — the alliance ending in the open, with the capital still run by stand-ins (SPAK statement, 10 February 2025; Reuters and Albanian Times reporting, 2025–2026).
  • The McGonigal affair drew denial without accounting. Charles McGonigal, the FBI's former New York counterintelligence chief, pleaded guilty in Washington in September 2023 to concealing his Albanian ties and was sentenced on 16 February 2024 to 28 months in the Albania-related case (a separate Russia-related sentence ran alongside). The US court record establishes: McGonigal received $225,000 from a former Albanian intelligence officer (Agron Neza, "Person A" in the filing), was facilitated by an informal adviser to Rama (Dorian Duçka, "Person B"), met Rama in September 2017, discussed Albanian oil-licensing interests, and passed the FBI information about a lobbyist for Albania's opposition. Stated exactly: no charge has been brought against Rama in the United States or Albania; Rama acknowledged the meetings and denied any wrongdoing. What he did not do is account publicly for what was discussed or for his office's channel to a serving FBI official who was secretly on an Albanian payroll — the opposition's demand for a parliamentary inquiry was not granted (US District Court for DC, Statement of Offense, Case 1:23-cr-21, September 2023; sentencing, February 2024).
  • Where the wobble came from voters rather than institutions, the answer has sometimes been to proceed without them — the 2019 local elections held on schedule against an opposition boycott (Test 4) — and where it came from judges on their own pay, the answer in 2026 was a standoff that left the bench unpaid for a month (Test 2).

Test 4 — COUNTS EVERYONE

Does the accounting include the whole flock — the opposition's voters, Kosovo Albanians, the poor, journalists, the diaspora — or are some counted out?

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • The diaspora was counted in, formally and for the first time: the 11 May 2025 election was the first in which Albanians living abroad could vote from abroad, a long-demanded inclusion his majority legislated and administered (ODIHR reporting, 2025).
  • The material floor rose over his tenure on the documented aggregates: the economy grew through most of the period, the minimum wage was raised repeatedly, pensions were increased, and measured poverty declined over the decade (World Bank and INSTAT series) — while the state he runs delivered the EU process to the point where membership negotiations cover every chapter, a good his voters and the opposition's voters share equally if it completes.
  • Kosovo Albanians were counted in at the level of state policy in his first years: Albania under Rama funded and politically backed Kosovo's international recognition campaign, and the two governments held joint cabinet meetings and signed dozens of cooperation agreements — the relationship's later friction (below) is a dispute over method between two elected governments, not an exclusion of Kosovo Albanians from Albania's own polity.

Evidence against:

  • The opposition's voters play on a tilted field, on the referees' documented findings. ODIHR's final report on the 11 May 2025 elections found them competitive and professionally administered but held in a highly polarized environment without a level playing field: "widespread misuse of public resources and institutional power by the ruling party," numerous reports of pressure on public-sector employees and other voters, reports of vote-buying, and self-censorship among journalists. The opposition rejected the results, alleging fraud — an ALLEGATION ODIHR's findings support only as to the uneven field, not as to the count itself (OSCE/ODIHR Final Report, 2025). The 2019 local elections stand behind this as the extreme case: after the opposition resigned its parliamentary mandates and boycotted, the elections of 30 June 2019 went ahead with Socialist candidates unopposed in most municipalities — lawful per the Constitutional Court's later ruling, and a map of local power drawn with a third of the electorate's representatives absent by their own choice and his refusal to postpone.
  • The flock is shrinking, and the shrinkage is the count's hardest number: the 2023 census recorded 2,402,113 residents, about 420,000 fewer than in 2011, with INSTAT attributing the decline mainly to emigration; the resident population fell further to about 2,363,000 by 1 January 2025 (INSTAT Census 2023; INSTAT population estimates, 2026). Whatever the economic aggregates do, roughly one in seven of the people counted in 2011 was no longer there to be counted in 2023 — the young and working-age above all. The government's position is that emigration is a free choice in an open Europe and is now partially offset by returns and immigration; the opposition counts the exodus itself as the verdict. Both characterizations are on the record; the census figure is not contested.
  • Kosovo Albanians are counted on his terms, not theirs. Rama co-founded the Open Balkan initiative with Serbia's President Vučić and North Macedonia (2019) over Kosovo's refusal to join what Pristina saw as a substitute for EU integration; in June 2023 he drafted, on his own initiative, a statute for the Association of Serb-majority Municipalities in Kosovo and sent it to Paris and Berlin without the Kosovo government's agreement — Prime Minister Albin Kurti objected publicly that drafting Kosovo's internal arrangements was not Tirana's to do, and Rama cancelled a joint government meeting in the ensuing row. He later declared Open Balkan's mission fulfilled (July 2023) and returned to the Berlin Process frame. The dispute is documented on both sides; what it shows for this test is that when the other Albanian government's count differed from his, he proceeded over it (Balkan Insight and RFE/RL reporting, 2019–2023).
  • Migrants are counted as someone else's problem on Albanian soil — by his signature. The November 2023 protocol with Italy placed Italian-run reception and detention centres at Shëngjin and Gjadër under exclusive Italian jurisdiction. The documented operational record: the centres opened in October 2024; three transfers totalling 73 people were made by January 2025, each returned to Italy within days after Italian courts refused to validate the detentions; the European Court of Justice ruled on 1 August 2025 (Cases C-758/24 and C-759/24) that Italy's "safe country" designations underpinning the scheme breached EU law as applied; the facilities were repurposed as a repatriation centre, and by July 2026 roughly 685 people had been transferred in total with 101 repatriations effected (ActionAid Italia figures as reported). Albania's Constitutional Court rejected the opposition's challenge to the protocol in early 2024. Rama defends the deal as Albania repaying Italy for 1991 and standing "on the right side"; human-rights organizations document it as externalization that counts the people inside the wire out of both countries' ordinary protections. Both positions are on the record.
  • Journalists are counted in the rhetoric as enemies when they criticize: the "kazan" label, the 35 official defamation suits of 2018–19, the News24 operation (Test 2), and the December 2023 episode in which SPAK itself searched journalist Elton Qyno's home and newsroom and seized his equipment to force source disclosure — an act of the prosecution, not the executive, but one the Constitutional Court had to reverse in April 2025, in a media environment ODIHR describes as marked by self-censorship (Council of Europe Platform alerts; Constitutional Court, 22 April 2025; ODIHR, 2025).

Test 5 — KEEPS HIS WORD OVER TIME

Campaign promises vs. the record since 2013, with dates.

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for (promises kept or credibly in motion):

  • The justice reform — kept, and it is the structural promise of his career. He pledged a root-and-branch judicial overhaul in opposition and delivered it: constitutional amendments adopted 22 July 2016, the vetting law, SPAK's creation (2019), and a decade of implementation that the EU credits with a functioning, independent special prosecution (Test 2). Whatever else moved, this word was kept at the price of building the institution that now prosecutes his own allies.
  • The European path — kept beyond any predecessor's record. Candidate status came in June 2014; negotiations opened in 2022 and the first cluster in October 2024; all six clusters were open by 17 November 2025, with four chapters provisionally closed by November 2026 and the Commission publishing a roadmap to conclude talks by end-2027. His 2025 campaign promise — close negotiations by 2027, membership by 2030 — is a target, not an EU commitment, and is so far on its procedural track (European Commission country fiche and Albania Report, November 2026).
  • Tax promises to the small — kept. Progressive income taxation replaced the flat tax in 2014 as pledged, and the zero-profit-tax regime for small businesses, promised and legislated mid-decade, has been maintained and extended.
  • "Tenders 100% corruption-free" was at least attempted as theatre with a mechanism: on 11 September 2025 he appointed "Diella," an AI system, as Minister of State for public procurement — a symbolic appointment (the constitution requires human ministers) presented as removing human discretion from tenders (government announcement, 11 September 2025).

Evidence against (promises not kept, or receding):

  • 300,000 jobs — not delivered as stated. The headline promise of the 2013 "Renaissance" programme was 300,000 new jobs in the first term. By the 2017 campaign the documented assessment — including from independent observers cited in the contemporaneous record — was that less than half that number had been created, with part of the increase being formalization of existing informal work; the government disputed the count and claimed employment gains on its own methodology. The promise was renewed in reduced form (220,000 jobs for the second term) rather than acknowledged as missed (Tirana Times campaign review, May 2017; INSTAT labour series).
  • Free healthcare — delivered in part, not as promised. The 2013 programme promised NHS-style free healthcare. What arrived was a free basic check-up programme (from 2015) and expanded insurance coverage, while co-payments, private-sector growth, and documented informal payments persisted — the promise's plain meaning was not met (2013 PS programme; World Bank health-financing reviews).
  • The VAT cut on the basic food basket — not delivered. The 2013 pledge to reduce VAT on staple foods was not enacted; the standard rate structure for food remained, a staple of the opposition's documented broken-promise lists across three campaigns (2013 PS programme; campaign records 2017–2025).
  • The tender promise met its own record within two months. "Public tenders will be 100% free of corruption" (September 2025) was followed in November 2025 by the indictment of his deputy prime minister over nine tender procedures, in December 2025 by SPAK's arrest-authorization request, and through 2026 by the AKSHI investigation into state IT tenders reaching his intelligence chief and a businessman described in reporting as close to his circle (Ergys Agasi — wanted from late 2025, arrested August 2026; ALLEGATIONs, denied or unanswered as the record stands). The charges are allegations, not verdicts; the gap between the word and the documented state of the tender system he staffed is the test's subject, and it is wide. Transparency International's index as reported in 2026 moved the wrong way with it: Albania's CPI score fell from 42 (2024) to 39 (2025).
  • The theatre promise (Test 3): construction of the new National Theatre was pledged to begin in 2021; five years on, the site record is a transferred plot and a reported tower plan, not a theatre.

Overall Summary

One law: MIXED · Without gripping: MIXED · The wobble: MIXED · Counts everyone: MIXED · His word: MIXED.

Edi Rama is the only subject in this series who built the instrument that grades him. The justice reform he forced through in 2016 vetted away more than half the bench, created a prosecution the European Commission certifies as independent, and has since convicted his interior minister and his environment minister, driven his deputy prime minister into fugitive exile, put his capital's mayor in a cell for twenty months, and sent his second deputy prime minister to trial — all without a single documented instance of him stopping a case. That is the strongest "holds" record of any long-serving ruler graded here, and it is real. The seams are equally specific, and they all sit at the same altitude: when the file reached Belinda Balluku, his majority refused the arrest, he litigated her suspension, praised her service, and proposed a law exempting ministers from suspension altogether; when it reached Erion Veliaj, he waged a two-year rhetorical campaign against the prosecutors while the courts held his mayor anyway. Around those peaks the pattern repeats at lower stakes — a theatre demolished at 4:30 in the morning before the court could save it, a news channel taken off air over a lease, a bench left unpaid for a month in a pay dispute, a census that counts 420,000 fewer people than the one before. The tests measure the seat, not the intentions: this seat gives by one law more often than any of its regional peers, grips by rhetoric and legislative threat rather than by capture, answers Europe's wobbles and defies its own citizens', counts the diaspora in and the departed out, and kept the two promises that restructured the state — the reform and the road to Brussels — while missing the ones that were only numbers. The record is correctable, in the open, and the largest open files — Balluku's trial, Veliaj's verdict, the Article 242 proposal, and the 2027 negotiation deadline — will move these grades in one direction or the other.
