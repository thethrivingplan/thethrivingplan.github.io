Everything

Your home base for the things you love — learning a thing, teaching what you know, playing, and just enjoying life, in rooms and on boards built around your interests, not around an advertiser.

The name is just Everything — not “The Everything.” Everything the way you mean it about a life: your things, your learning, your teaching, your fun, your peace.

The law of this place, in plain words

Everything is free, always free — and it cannot be bought.

Not “won’t be sold.” Literally cannot be bought, because there is no owner to buy it from and nothing to take over. Every page is copyleft — shared under the Creative Commons BY-SA 4.0 license, which means anyone may copy it, re-post it, and build on it, and every copy carries the same freedom with it, forever. Copies live everywhere. There is no company behind this page, no shares, no sale, no handle to grab.

So the machinery that eats every other platform was never installed:

  • No company
  • No ads
  • No accounts
  • No tracking
  • No feed
  • No like counts
  • No follower counts
  • No streaks
  • No rankings

Pages end. A page here has a bottom, and when you reach it, you’re done — nothing on this page is engineered to keep you scrolling.

You come in through your own interests. You walk through your own doors. What springs on you here is knowledge and freedom — the room is yours, and you can take a copy home.

And this place is for more than keeping up with society. It is for learning a thing you’ve always wanted to try, teaching what you know to whoever wants it, and the plain peaceful part — humans enjoying life: playing, making, wandering, sitting in the sun. No audience required, no score kept. A place that only mirrors the noise misses the best of being human, so this one makes room for it right on the face — see Learn & play, just below your things.

Learn & play

Not just society — humans enjoying life: learning a thing, teaching a thing, and plain peace.

Everything is more than a mirror of what everybody’s already doing. This is the part for curiosity and for joy: pick up something new, pass on something you know, and spend some time just being alive — no audience, no score, no one to impress.

Learn something new

  • Give one small thing ten minutes a day — small and daily beats big and someday.
  • Learn the names of the trees and birds on your own street.
  • Learn one song all the way through, not just the chorus.
  • Learn to juggle, to whistle with your fingers, to tie the knots that actually hold.
  • Start at the very beginning and be bad at it out loud — that’s the first lesson, for you and for anyone watching.

Teach something you know

  • Teaching is learning twice — you find out what you actually know the moment you try to say it plainly.
  • Teach a kid, a friend, a neighbor: one real skill, at their pace.
  • Show your rough drafts and your early tries. The bad parts teach too — sometimes they’re the whole lesson.
  • Write it down where others can find it: a fan page for your thing is a classroom with the door open.

Just enjoy being alive

  • Sit outside with nothing to do, and do it well.
  • Play a game badly on purpose, for the laughing.
  • Cook something slow, share it, and linger at the table.
  • Take the long way home because the light is good.
  • No score, no audience, no post about it afterwards — some things are just for the living of them.

The road so far — how it works

Every thing you add above carries its own road so far, right on its card: where you started, where you are now, and how it grew — dated steps, in the order you choose, oldest first or newest first.

The whole road — the good parts and the bad parts. The bad parts teach too. The failures, the rough early attempts, the hard stretches and what went wrong belong on the road with the same plain dignity as the wins — they’re half the teaching, for you and for anyone you’re showing the way.

Nothing on the road is ranked or scored: no streaks, no points, no levels. It’s a record, not a contest — and it lives on your device, like everything here.

Your scroll

Sometimes you just want to scroll. This one is yours.

Scrolling isn’t the problem — the trap is. This scroll is assembled on your device, from your own things: your interests, your boards, your pages. Nothing is ranked by anyone else, nothing autoplays next, and it has an end. When you reach the bottom, it says so, and you’re caught up.

Two lanes, your pick: Scroll & learn — knowledge first: the pages, the record, things worth learning. Just nonsense — the fun shelf, labeled honestly as nonsense, for when that’s what you want. Every interest above carries a lane tag — tap it on the card to move the thing between lanes.

Your scroll is empty because your Everything is. Add a thing, a board, or a page above and it shows up here — in your order.
That’s the bottom — you’re caught up. Nothing new will slide in underneath you, and nothing is queuing up a next one. That’s the whole fix: your scroll, your order, with an end.
Pictures & videos — how media works here open ▾

There has to be a way to share pictures and videos — so there is. It works the way everything here works: by door, not by hosting.

  • A fan page carries its media as doors. When you build a page for your thing, its pictures and videos are doors to where the files already live — your video shelf, your own pages, a public video door — each one labeled with where it lives.
  • Your boards carry media doors too. Save a picture or a video to a board by its address, and the board shows the door and its home shelf, not a copy of the file.
  • Your files stay on your own shelves. Everything points at media; it doesn’t hold the files, doesn’t re-host them, and can’t lose them, sell them, or bury them under ads.
  • Short and long video are both doors out, never a feed. A short spark (about a minute) or a long deep-dive — you tap the door, you watch it where it lives, you come back. Nothing autoplays a next one at you.

The Video Release Template (in the standing rooms, above) is the tool that goes with this: title, description, and tags in separate copy boxes, so the video you post on your own shelf goes out clean and findable.

What this page will not do: fake an upload button, pretend to host a file it can’t hold, or run a hosted video feed. Hosting video at scale is on the honest “can’t do yet” list below — a separate build, on our own roof, when it comes.

What we took, what we fixed, what we left out open ▾

We read the big platforms — TikTok, Facebook, Instagram, YouTube, X, Reddit, Discord, Snapchat, Pinterest — and graded every loved and hated thing about them: take it, fix it, or leave it. Here is the ledger in plain language. (A “server” below means a computer of ours that would have to stay awake holding everyone’s stuff — the thing that can be bought. Everything runs without one, on purpose.)

TikTok

Took

  • Short video as a unit — about a minute for a spark, longer where the thing needs it.
  • The ease of making something — here that’s the fan-page builder and the Video Release Template, not an app you rent your creativity from.

Fixed

  • Discovery. TikTok finds your thing for you — and keeps the method secret. Here, discovery is your interests as labeled doors, in plain sight. And scrolling itself? Scrolling is fine — your scroll (on the face of this page) is built from your own doors, in your order, and it ends. What’s fixed is the trap, not the scroll.
  • Replies in kind. Answer a post with your own post, visibly linked — conversation instead of a reaction strip.

Left out

  • The endless pull — a feed engineered so there is no bottom — and autoplay that starts the next one before you’ve decided anything.
  • The shop inside the feed, the secret ranking, and the data harvesting.

Facebook

Took

  • Groups — rooms built around a thing, where people who share the interest actually talk. This is Everything’s core shape: fan pages, the Poster Board, the tables.
  • Save-for-later — your boards and your shelf, saved on your device.
  • Gatherings as plain dated listings on a page — a fan page’s events, a table’s session time.

Fixed

  • Moderation with an appeal built in. Every moderation act is recorded — who, what, why, when — corrections ride beside the original, and nothing is silently dropped. Moderation that cannot be seen is not moderation.

Left out

  • The feed that buries your friends under ads and suggestions, pay-to-be-seen reach, and shadow profiles of people who never signed up.

Instagram

Took

  • Easy visual sharing — a post should take a picture or a video with one tap and show it plainly (here, by door — see the media layer).
  • The profile as a body of work — a fan page is exactly that: a shelf for its thing.

Fixed

  • Low-stakes posts without the fake disappearing act. Casual posts live in a casual corner, small and unpolished — but this page won’t promise a 24-hour deletion it can’t enforce for other people.
  • The comparison engine, removed. No public like counts, no follower counts as a score. Everything shows what people made, not how many people applauded.

Left out

  • The reach lottery, sponsored posts everywhere, and paid verification ticks.

YouTube

Took

  • Long-form video as a first-class citizen, and search that finds the exact thing — every page here is findable, and the doors are labeled with what’s behind them.
  • Honest signals over hidden ones: labels, dates, and “what’s missing” doors in the open.

Fixed

  • Following a maker becomes a door you keep on your own shelf and revisit — not a subscription a company can throttle. (A “new video” ping needs a server; it’s on the honest list below, not faked here.)
  • Earning. Ad revenue can’t exist on a place with no ads — so the maker keeps their own door: their tip jar, their shop, linked from their page. Everything takes nothing and ranks nothing by payment. A maker keeps 100% of what their own door earns; Everything never becomes the middleman.

Left out

  • Ad overload, copyright-strike machines with no recourse, and recommendations built to keep you watching rather than show you anything good.

X (Twitter)

Took

  • Short posts, fast — the Poster Board is this: short, dated, signed, no ceremony.
  • Public replies to anyone — conversation in the open, threaded.
  • Live rooms for talking — the tables do live voice while a page is open.

Fixed

  • “What’s happening now” becomes a dated, labeled “what’s new” shelf — newest first, dates printed. A live global trending list needs a server and a firehose; ours stays per-room and small.
  • Crowd fact-checking, without the crowd vote. A note rides beside a claim, with its source, its name, and its date — filed, not voted into visibility, so a majority can’t bury a true correction.
  • Bots gain nothing here: no ranking to game, claimed names that refuse impersonators at the door, and a public record where a flood shows as a flood.

Left out

  • Paid verification, pay-to-be-seen tiers, and an algorithm that rewards negativity and conflict. There is no algorithm here to reward anything.

Reddit

Took

  • Topic communities with their own rules, printed on the face — the best model in the whole survey, and Everything’s closest kin. A room per interest, made by the people in it.
  • Threaded discussion — real conversation, not a reaction strip.
  • A public, searchable, permanent record — the archive waits for you instead of disappearing down a feed.
  • The freedom principle: these pages are plain files under a copyleft license, so anyone may build a mirror, a client, or a fork without asking. There is no switch an owner can flip to take that away.

Fixed

  • Voting. A plain “this helped” mark — counted on the page, never a score on a person. No downvote that buries anyone: a dissenting view is never sorted out of sight.
  • Moderation without the burnout and the fog: recorded acts, the room’s own rules on its face, and the original always kept.

Left out

  • Karma — points, levels, and thresholds you must earn before you’re allowed to speak. A new voice posts on day one.

Discord

Took

  • A home per community, with rooms per topic — Everything’s shape, spread across pages.
  • Drop-in voice rooms — a room you walk into, not a call you place. The tables already work this way while a page is open.
  • Proof a big place can run with no ads and no algorithmic feed — and the principle that the free version is the whole version. Here there is no tier at all, because there is no payment.

Fixed

  • The vocabulary wall. Roles here are plain words on the face — host, guide, seat, guest — not a permission matrix to study before you can sit down.
  • Notifications. One plain nudge when it matters — never a badge avalanche that follows you around.

Left out

  • The one flaw Discord can’t fix about itself: it has an owner, so its free tier exists at the owner’s pleasure. Everything has no owner. That’s the whole point.

Snapchat

Took

  • No public popularity contest — no public friend lists, no public likes. The cheapest fix in the ledger, and it kills the contest at the root.
  • The low-stakes feeling: casual words shouldn’t carry the weight of the permanent record.

Fixed

  • The honest split. The record is permanent — and says so. Whispers at a table are for the session only — and say so. What Everything never does is promise “disappearing” it can’t enforce: a screenshot or a second phone defeats that promise on any platform, so we print the boundary instead of a timer.
  • Telling the sender the truth about copies: anything on another person’s screen can be kept. That’s printed, not hidden.

Left out

  • Streaks — a daily chore dressed as friendship. Friendship is not scorekeeping here.
  • Friendship rankings of any kind, and live location maps of real people.

Pinterest

Took

  • Boards — save ideas into named collections, with room to keep them tidy. Your boards are on the face of this page.
  • The low-pressure pace. No obligation to post, interact, or keep up. Reading quietly is a complete, respectable way to be here.
  • Long shelf life — saved things stay saved and findable for years, because nothing here dies when a feed moves on.

Fixed

  • “More like this” becomes a labeled door in the open — a page links its kin — never a taste-prediction engine looping you through the same thing forever.
  • Dead doors get fixed in the open, and everything made here is signed by the people who made it, with machine-made parts labeled as machine-made.

Left out

  • Full-screen ads every few pins, and ranking that changes without warning. No ranking, no changes to announce.
The fixes, all together: discovery without the feed (interests as doors; chronological or your order; no hidden ranking) · a plain “this helped” instead of karma, with no burying downvote · moderation where every act is recorded and corrections ride beside originals · the maker’s own door for tips and shop, with Everything taking nothing · the honest split — the record is permanent and says so, whispers are session-only and say so · and scrolling itself, kept and fixed: your scroll, your order, with an end.
Left out for good, all together: the endless pull and the autoplay trap · ads and boosted reach · secret algorithms and data harvesting · paid verification · karma points · streaks and friendship rankings · live location maps · public counts of any kind.
Built for small rooms, on purpose. The research behind this page says communities talk most in groups of about 11–25 people, and most successful communities have under 500 members — in small rooms a third of people join in, while in giant ones only about a tenth do. Quiet members aren’t a problem: most are getting exactly what they came for. So Everything isn’t built to feel alive at a million people. It’s built to be alive at small scale — small rooms, real interests, a warm reply within the hour, and one regular thing people can count on.

Why every big platform rots the same way has a name — researcher Cory Doctorow calls it “enshittification”: first a platform is good to its users, then it squeezes users for its business customers, then it squeezes everyone for its owners. The squeeze needs an owner and a lock on the door. Everything removes both: copyleft copies everywhere, no accounts, no ads — there is nothing to squeeze and no one positioned to squeeze it.

What this page can’t do yet, said plainly open ▾

This page is one file with no server behind it — no computer of ours stays awake holding everyone’s stuff. That’s what makes it unbuyable, and it has a price. Here it is, unhidden. Nothing below is faked anywhere on this page:

  • Nothing happens while nobody has a page open. No messages delivered offline, no inbox waiting for you, no push notifications, no email digests. If a thing must happen while every page is closed, it needs a server.
  • No global feed and no global trending — by design as much as by limit. A live ranking across all rooms needs a central collector, and a central collector is exactly the thing that gets bought. “What’s new” here stays per-room, dated, and small.
  • No roaming accounts. Your identity lives on your device, by law — your things, boards, and pages don’t follow you to another device, because following you would mean an account somewhere, and accounts are the handle owners grab. A name you claim at a table is yours by your own code, held by you.
  • Everything links video; it does not host it. Your pictures and videos stay on your own shelves and arrive by door. A video vault on our own roof is a separate build, not a promise hidden in this page.
  • No Everything-wide “who’s online.” Being present exists inside a room that’s open — a table, while it stands — not across all of Everything.
  • No in-page video editing, camera effects, or video-reply duets. Making is served by the builder and the Video Release Template; a full editing studio needs machinery this page doesn’t pretend to have.
  • No enforced disappearing. Nothing here can make words on someone else’s screen un-happen, and no server could guarantee it either — a second phone defeats it. The record says it’s the record; whispers say they’re for the session. That honesty is the feature.
  • Spam and bot control at giant scale is unsolved here — and said so. Everything’s answer at its intended scale is small rooms, claimed names, and a public record. It does not promise machinery it doesn’t have.

The test every future feature has to pass: does it need an owner, an ad, a ranking, or a server that could be bought? If yes, it gets rebuilt without that need — or it stays on this list until a free, unbuyable way exists. Everything grows by adding rooms and copies, never by adding a center.
