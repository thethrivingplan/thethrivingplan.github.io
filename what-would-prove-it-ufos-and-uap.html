UFOs & UAP — judge it yourself

What Would Prove It?

Nobody knows what it actually is.

Every claim on this subject currently routes through somebody's word — a government's, a whistleblower's (someone who says they saw secret things from inside), a content creator's, or ours. This page does not ask you to trust any of them.

Instead it separates what anyone can check themselves from what is still an unknown with a label hung on it — and it sets, in plain sight, the bar that real proof would have to clear. Then you can measure any claim against that bar yourself.

UFO means Unidentified Flying Object. UAP means Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena (the newer official term — “anomalous” just means it doesn't fit what was expected). “Unidentified” is not a claim about aliens. It is a true statement: we don't know what that was.

First — the bottom

The bottom: what you can check yourself

These are not opinions. Each one names where you can check it without trusting this page. If a source below doesn't say what we say it says, the grade falls — check us.

People genuinely see things they cannot identify VERIFIED

“Unidentified” is a true statement, not a dodge. Pilots, radar operators (people who watch aircraft-tracking screens for a living), and ordinary people report objects and lights they cannot name. That reporting itself is real, documented, and not in dispute — by believers or skeptics.

Go one layer down — what this does and doesn't mean ▾

It means the reports exist and many are sincere. It does not mean the thing reported was alien, secret, or even unusual once you have better data. Most reports shrink when a second look arrives — a distance, a second camera, a flight log. A small remainder stays unexplained mostly because the data is thin: one camera, no distance, no second sensor (any device that records — a camera, radar, a tracker).

Check it yourselfGovernment case files, pilot reports, and public sighting databases are published and searchable. The point you can verify is narrow and solid: the reports are real. What they are reports of is the open question — and this page never confuses the two.

The documents are public — read them, don't take summaries VERIFIED

The CIA's FOIA reading room collection “UFOs: Fact or Fiction?” is readable by anyone, free, right now. FOIA means the Freedom of Information Act — the U.S. law that lets any person request and read government records.

Go one layer down — the door itself ▾

The collection holds declassified (formerly secret, now officially released) CIA documents on UFOs from the 1940s onward. You do not need our reading of them. Open the documents and read the words the agency itself wrote at the time.

Check it yourselfCIA Reading Room — “UFOs: Fact or Fiction?” ↗ — the primary documents, on the CIA's own site. Primary means the original record, not someone's article about it.

The manipulation layer is documented — in the government's own paper VERIFIED

Governments have lied and stage-managed this subject, on the record. This layer is graded VERIFIED from the documents themselves — not from anyone's summary, including ours.

Go one layer down — the three documented pieces ▾

1. The Robertson Panel (1953). A CIA-convened panel of scientists recommended an official debunking campaign — using media and ridicule to make the public lose interest in UFO reports. The panel's own report says so. Why it matters: “the government mocked it” is not evidence it was false. Mockery was, at times, the policy.

2. Roswell and Project Mogul (1947). The famous “weather balloon” explanation covered Project Mogul — a classified (secret) balloon program listening for Soviet nuclear tests. The balloon story was a cover for a real secret program. It was not evidence about aliens in either direction — it was evidence that a cover story gets used when a secret program needs one.

3. U-2 and OXCART. The CIA later admitted its high-altitude spy planes — the U-2 and its successor OXCART (the A-12) — accounted for over half of all UFO reports in the late 1950s and 1960s, and that officials let the alien explanation run rather than expose the planes. A strange silver object at impossible height was, often, ours — and the alien story was useful cover.

Check it yourselfAll three are in declassified records: the Robertson Panel report, the Air Force's Roswell / Project Mogul reports, and the CIA's own published history of the U-2/OXCART programs (in the same CIA reading room linked above). Read the admissions in the agencies' own words.

The trap to avoid: a denial from a proven liar is worth nothing in either direction. “They said it's not aliens, therefore it is” still lets them steer you — just in reverse. The denial is not evidence. Only the record grades.

The honest wall: in 80 years, nothing testable has ever been produced VERIFIED

No craft, material, or body has ever been placed where independent examiners could test it and publish. That absence is itself a checkable fact — and it is the wall every claim runs into.

Go one layer down — what “placed where examiners could test it” means ▾

It means: an object anyone qualified can handle, in a lab that does not belong to the claimant, with methods written down so a rival lab can repeat the test and get the same result — or show it was wrong. That has happened for meteorites, for new species, for the Higgs boson (a particle physicists confirmed by publishing data other teams could re-check). It has never happened for an alien craft, material, or body.

Claims of secret warehouses, private collections, and “materials we can't show you” may or may not be true — but by definition they are not checkable, so on this page they sit at CAN'T VERIFY, at the same size as any other claim with the same evidence. Secrecy explains why proof is missing. It does not substitute for proof.

Second — the bar

The bar: what would count as proof

Set the bar before the next video arrives — not after, when excitement moves it. Here is the bar, tiered, so you can see exactly where any claim lands. A claim does not climb a tier because it is repeated, famous, or sincerely believed. It climbs when its evidence does.

Tier 1 — Settles itPROOF TIER

A physical thing, tested in the open

A physical object or material with an unbroken chain of custody (a written record of who held it, every step, with no gap where it could have been swapped), examined by multiple independent, rival labs — labs that would love to prove each other wrong — with methods and results published for anyone to re-check.

  • Why rival labs? One lab can be fooled, funded, or mistaken. Rivals checking each other is how error gets caught.
  • Why published? If only the claimant's team can see the data, you are back to taking somebody's word.
Tier 2 — Settles that something real & anomalous happenedPROOF TIER

One event, many independent eyes, raw data public

One event captured at the same time by multiple independent sensors owned by different parties — civilian cameras, commercial flight tracking, military radar, satellite — with the RAW data public. Raw means the unedited original recording, not a clip, a screenshot, or a summary.

  • Why different owners? No single owner controls the whole record, so no single owner can edit the story.
  • Why raw? Edited clips can mislead by accident or on purpose. Raw data lets strangers re-measure speed, distance, and path themselves.
Tier 3 — Strong, but not proofSTRONG

Witnesses plus an instrument, data available

Multiple trained witnesses — people whose job is watching the sky, like pilots and air-traffic controllers — plus one instrument record (one radar track, one sensor recording), with the data available to examine. This deserves serious attention and real investigation. It does not settle what the thing was, because one instrument can glitch and witnesses, however trained, can misjudge distance and speed with nothing to measure against.

Below the bar — never proof, however often repeated

  • Testimony alone — one person's account, however senior, sincere, or sworn. Testimony is where investigation starts. It is not where it ends.
  • A single video from a single source — one camera, one uploader, no second angle, no raw file.
  • Anonymous leaks — a claim with no name attached cannot be questioned, checked, or held to its story.
  • A government statement in either direction — “it's aliens” or “it's nothing” are both just statements. Section one shows why this source, on this subject, has spent its credibility.
  • A famous person's certainty — fame measures reach, not evidence.

Claims below the bar are graded CAN'T VERIFY. That grade is not an insult and not a dismissal — it is the honest label for “the evidence offered cannot carry the conclusion, yet.” Some CAN'T VERIFY claims will one day climb. They climb with evidence, not with repetition.

Fourth — worked example

The current wave, measured: November 13–14, 2026

A wave of sightings and clips swept feeds on November 13–14, 2026. Waves feel like proof because there's so much of it. Volume is not a tier. Here is each piece, graded against the same bar — the worked example of how to use this page.

The nationwide FAA ground stop — the anchor event VERIFIED

November 13, 2026 · FAA advisories

What happened is documented in the FAA's own advisories — the Federal Aviation Administration, the agency that runs U.S. air traffic — as a telecommunications outage: equipment and communications failures, Chicago first, then national, lasting 4 hours 25 minutes, departures only. That is bedrock paper — the operational record itself, signed and timestamped — not a press release and not a spokesman's summary.

Nothing in that record mentions UAP. The internet fused the outage and the night's clips into one story anyway. Two things happening on the same night is a coincidence to investigate, not a connection established. Grade: the outage is VERIFIED. The link to UFOs is CAN'T VERIFY — no evidence offered connects them.

The viral trailing-light clips from that night CAN'T VERIFY

November 13, 2026 · Social video, multiple cities

The night's most-shared clips show a bright object with a trailing, curved line of evenly spaced lights. Each clip is a single video from a single source — below the bar on its own. And there is an ordinary candidate the same night: SpaceX flew a Starship flight that day, and a rocket with a train of lights is exactly what that looks like from the ground.

An ordinary candidate does not prove the clips are the rocket — matching each clip to the flight path, time, and angle would. Until that check is done and published, the honest grade is CAN'T VERIFY — with a strong ordinary explanation waiting to be confirmed, not an anomaly confirmed.

The Anchorage “jellyfish” video CAN'T VERIFY

Filmed June 15, 2026 · Viral November 10, 2026

The clip of a dangling, drifting “jellyfish” near Anchorage was filmed on June 15, 2026 — five months before it went viral on November 10. The date matters: a June video cannot be part of a November wave, however it was packaged. Frame-by-frame analysis — examining the video one still image at a time — concluded it is almost certainly a tethered octopus-style kite: a kite shaped like an octopus, held by a line, its “tentacles” moving exactly as cloth in wind moves.

As a UFO claim it never had more than a single video — below the bar — and the best close look points to cloth and string. The lesson travels: check the filming date before the filming location impresses you.

The original “jellyfish” — Iraq, 2017 VERIFIED

2017 · Pentagon case resolution

The Anchorage clip borrowed its nickname from a famous 2017 military video over Iraq, long presented as deeply anomalous (not fitting any known explanation). The Pentagon's own resolution of that case: a cluster of balloons — ordinary objects that, filmed through a thermal camera (a camera that sees heat, not light — shapes look strange and detail washes out), read as a single dangling creature.

That resolution is checkable case paper, and it cuts both ways honestly: the system can resolve its famous cases to ordinary things, and it took years to say so. Neither fact upgrades the next single video.

The wave is real. Nothing in it clears Tier 3.

The reports happened, the outage happened, the clips are real clips. What none of them carries is evidence at Tier 3 or above — no physical material in open labs, no multi-sensor raw data, not even witnesses-plus-instrument with data available. That is a statement about the evidence, not about what is or isn't in the sky. The sky keeps its answer. This page only grades what has actually been handed to us — and so far, on this wave, the answer is: not proof, not close, and nothing here required anything extraordinary to happen.

Fifth — the open door

What are we missing?

The bar itself can be wrong. Tell us where.

Is a tier too strict — or too loose? Is there a kind of evidence this page doesn't weigh? A documented fact for “the bottom” we left out, with the paper to check it by? A wave item graded unfairly? The enemy here is unearned certainty, on every side — including ours. If you can spot a gap, you can improve the bar.
