The Long Shore

You wash up on an island that has never heard of you. Walk it, learn it, feed yourself, keep warm through the night — and when you are ready, build a raft and leave it behind. Surviving is the game. Escaping is the win.

Real scope, plainly: one survivor on one procedurally grown island, drawn top-down on a single page. Four needs to manage, six rungs of crafting, passive wildlife and one biting hazard, a full day-and-night cycle. A run takes roughly 20–40 minutes; you can leave mid-run and resume — the run is saved on this device. No multiplayer, no accounts, no network play, nothing leaves your device.

Keys: W A S D or arrow keys walk (a tap steps, a hold keeps walking) · E do the thing in front of you · F eat · Q drink · R rest · P pause. On touch: hold a direction to keep walking; every button says what a tap does. You can also tap the island itself to walk toward that spot.

The crafting ladder

Six rungs, in the order survivors have always climbed them: a tool, a fire, a roof, a way to carry water, better tools, a way off. Each rung makes the next one reachable. Costs are paid from your pack when you tap Craft.

Strategies — more than one honest way

There is no single right way to survive this island. These are the real forks, with what each side costs you.

Where to camp

Coastal campFire and shelter near the beach. The raft will be built here, so no long haul at the end, and the shore breeze keeps snakes scarce. Cost: the coast is the coldest ground at night and far from the inland pond — every drink is a walk.
Inland campCamp by the pond in the trees. Water at your door, wood all around, warmer nights. Cost: snakes live in that grass, and when the raft is ready you must carry the plan back across the island to the shore.

How to play the run

Escape fastAxe, fire, raft — skip the shelter and the fine tools. Fewest days exposed, and every day survived is a day something can go wrong. Cost: thin margins — one cold night or one bite can end a run that had no shelter and no stored food.
Settle strongBuild everything: shelter, waterskin, spear. Slower, but each night gets safer and each day easier. Cost: hunger and thirst keep their own schedule — a long build is a long time for the meters to grind you down.

Scope & basis

What this game is built on

The Long Shore is an original work in the survival game — a documented kind of game with a long public record: a lone figure in a wild place, needs that run down like clocks (hunger, thirst, cold), gathering by hand, and a ladder of made things — tool, fire, shelter, better tool, escape — each unlocking the next. Games in this shape have been played and written about for decades, in print, on boards, and on screens. This page takes that documented shape and nothing else: the name, the island, the art drawn on its canvas, and every word of writing are original to this page. No assets, names, art, or designs come from any existing game.

What it honestly is — and is not

It is a complete, playable survival run on one page: a procedurally grown island (different every run), four meters, wildlife that minds its own business and one hazard that does not, day and night, weather of seasons, crafting, and a raft that ends the story. It is not a boundless open world, has no other players, no voice or video, and no physics beyond walking and gathering. The real survival teaching in it — water before food, fire before dark, shelter against the cold — is the genuine order of priorities, simplified to fit a game; it is a game, not a survival manual.

How the record is shaped

Each run is one entry: game, run id, alias, started, days survived, crafts made, ending (escaped / still going / didn't make it), and the settings it was played under — the same shape for every run, so the record can be read, exported, and compared honestly, variant runs labelled as variants.
