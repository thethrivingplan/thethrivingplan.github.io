Sparring — practise against the pattern

An adaptive practice partner plays documented manipulative patterns. First name the pattern, then choose your response. Hold a boundary and it will escalate or switch tactics — exactly the way the real pattern does. That escalation is not you failing. It is the pattern showing itself.

How you're graded: Recognition (did you name it?) · Boundary (did you hold a clear line?) · Exit (did you disengage when arguing was pointless?). You get no points for a clever comeback.

Deep teaching: the responses that actually work

Grey rock: brief, flat, boring replies that give the pattern nothing to feed on. Broken record: repeat one calm boundary sentence, word for word, without adding new arguments. Boundary statement: name what you will do — “If the shouting continues, I'm leaving the room” — not what they must do. Walking away: the strongest move on this page. Exit is not losing. Exit is the win condition.

Trust Your Gut — Graded Honestly

Your gut is real data and it can be wrong. In each round, decide: is this person telling the truth or lying? Then look at the maths — honestly, against pure chance. No TV lie-detector magic here.

How to read the maths

Guessing truth/lie is a coin flip: chance = 50%. Over 10 rounds, pure luck expects about 5 right. Getting 7/10 feels skilled — but luck alone produces 7 or more about 17 times in 100. That is why this game shows the numbers every round: a good gut feeling is a prompt to check, never a verdict by itself.

The Science Shelf — what actually works

✅ Gut feelings are real data

Intuition is pattern recognition running below conscious awareness — your brain matching this moment against thousands of stored moments. A sudden unease around a person or offer is worth listening to. It is a signal to slow down and check, especially when something feels “off” but you can't yet say why.

⚠️ …and gut feelings are fallible

The same system fires on bias, tiredness, hunger, fear, and familiarity. Confidence is not accuracy: people can feel completely certain and be completely wrong. Treat the gut as a first alert, then do the checking work below.

📺 Body-language lie detection is mostly myth

Decades of research (including large reviews by Bond & DePaulo) put average human lie-detection accuracy at roughly 54% — barely above the 50% coin flip. Averted eyes, fidgeting, and “micro-expressions” as sold on TV do not reliably reveal lying. Nervous truth-tellers get misjudged; calm liars sail through.

🎯 What actually works

Consistency-checking: does the story stay the same over time and across tellings? Verifiable details: ask for specifics that can be checked, and check them. Baselines: how does this person normally behave — a change from their baseline means more than any universal “tell.” Documents over vibes: receipts, messages, dates.

The Pattern Shelf — names worth knowing

Naming a pattern is half the defence. A thing you can name is a thing you can point to, check, and refuse.

Love-bombing

Flooding you with affection, praise, gifts and “you're my soulmate” intensity very early — to bond you fast, before you've had time to see clearly.

Devaluation

The warmth switches to contempt, criticism and coldness — often right after the bond feels secure. The swing itself is the pattern.

Gaslighting

Denying things you saw and heard — “that never happened,” “you're too sensitive” — until you doubt your own memory and perception.

DARVO

Deny, Attack, Reverse Victim and Offender: confronted, they deny it, attack you, and present themselves as the real victim — so now you're comforting them.

Hoovering

After distance or a breakup, a sudden pull back in — apologies, nostalgia, emergencies, “I've changed” — like a vacuum (a Hoover) sucking you back.

Triangulation

Bringing in a third person — real, exaggerated, or invented — to make you jealous, insecure, or competing: “Everyone agrees with me, even your friend said so.”

False scarcity

“Only 2 left!” “Offer ends tonight!” — manufactured urgency so you decide before you can think.

Foot-in-the-door

A tiny yes first (a signature, a free sample, “just two minutes”) makes the much bigger yes that follows feel consistent.

Authority & social proof

Uniforms, titles, testimonials and “everyone's doing it” used as a shortcut past your own judgement.

Tap to open — a note on the word “narcissist”

This page teaches patterns of behaviour, not diagnoses. Only a clinician who has assessed someone can diagnose narcissistic personality disorder, and most manipulative behaviour comes from people with no diagnosis at all. You never need a diagnosis to set a boundary: “this behaviour is not okay with me” is complete on its own.

Safety, plainly

This is a practice game — not therapy

Practising here can sharpen your eye. It is not a substitute for real support, and no game score says anything about what you should do in a real relationship. If someone is hurting, controlling, or frightening you, that matters more than any pattern name.

Real-world help (US): National Domestic Violence Hotline — call 1-800-799-7233 or text START to 88788 · Crisis Text Line — text HOME to 741741 · If you are in immediate danger, call 911. Outside the US, findahelpline.org lists support by country. If talking isn't safe, many hotlines offer silent chat options through their websites.

You don't need to be sure it's “bad enough” to ask for help. Asking is allowed at any point.
