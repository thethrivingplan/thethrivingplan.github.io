The Ruler's Test — Faustin-Archange Touadéra, President of the Central African Republic

Subject: Faustin-Archange Touadéra — President of the Central African Republic since 30 March 2016. The seat graded is the presidency, which in the Central African Republic is the executive seat: head of state and head of the executive, with the prime minister (Félix Moloua, in post since 2022) serving as his appointee. Touadéra, a former prime minister (2008–2013) and university rector, won the presidency in the 14 February 2016 runoff against Anicet-Georges Dologuélé, was re-elected in the first round on 27 December 2020 with 53.16% in a vote held under armed attack, and — after the July 2023 constitutional referendum abolished term limits and lengthened the presidential term from five years to seven — won a third term on 28 December 2025. The figures for that third election must be stated in their two official versions and never blended: the National Elections Authority (ANE) announced provisional results on 5–6 January 2026 of 76.15% for Touadéra and 14.66% for Dologuélé on turnout of 52.42%; the Constitutional Council, in validating the results on 19 January 2026 and rejecting Dologuélé's fraud appeal for insufficient evidence, declared final figures of 77.9% for Touadéra and about 13.1–13.5% for Dologuélé. He was sworn in on 30 March 2026 for a seven-year term. Two facts about that mandate belong in the header, not the footnotes: the main opposition coalition, the Republican Bloc for the Defence of the Constitution (BRDC), boycotted the election, arguing that the 2023 constitutional change and the composition of the electoral bodies made free competition impossible; and the election was the first in the country's history to combine presidential, legislative, regional and municipal ballots on one day, the municipal vote being the first local election since 1988. He is graded on the full decade 2016–2026, weighted toward the recent record, with the state he inherited in 2016 — roughly 80% of the territory held by armed groups, a state present in little more than Bangui — stated as the baseline every test measures from. Graded: November 2026, on the documented public record. Method: "How you play the game matters. Play by the rules. Stay true to your word. And if another option becomes available, at least consider it." Each test is graded only on the evidence listed under it. Both shelves are shown — the strongest evidence that he passes, then the strongest evidence that he fails. Allegations are labeled ALLEGATION and never stated as verdicts. Contested items are named as contested, with who disputes them. Seat-holders and public figures are named; people below the public eye appear by role only, unless the documented record itself establishes their wrongdoing. Grades: HOLDS / FAILS / MIXED / NOT ENOUGH EVIDENCE. This record is correctable: new evidence changes grades, in the open. The mandate rule applies: Touadéra governed, and in 2025 was re-elected, on a platform of security, sovereignty and continuity; carrying out that platform is not itself graded as a failure. The Test grades the holding — whether one law applied to all, whether the referees stayed free, whether pushback was answered in the open, whether everyone was counted, and whether his word held — never the platform itself. The no-good-choice clause is applied where the record shows a moment with no clean door, most sharply in January 2021; its mechanics are worked in Test 3, and the origin of that trap is graded separately, in Tests 1, 4 and 5, as the clause requires.

Premise corrections to the brief that commissioned this dossier: (1) The brief's expected subject is confirmed — Touadéra holds the seat — but the December 2025 election is not adequately described as a win without its conditions: it was boycotted by the main opposition coalition, its two official results differ (76.15% provisional, 77.9% final), and the runner-up's fraud appeal was rejected by the validating body. (2) The brief dates the removal of the Constitutional Court's president "before" the 2023 referendum; the record is more precise and worse: the Court annulled Touadéra's constitution-drafting committee in September 2022, its president Danièle Darlan was dismissed by presidential decree in October 2022 — a dismissal the 2016 constitution did not empower him to make — and was forcibly retired in January 2023; the referendum followed on 30 July 2023, and the court that validated it was the reconstituted one. (3) The body that validated the 2025 election is the Constitutional Council created by the 2023 constitution in place of the Constitutional Court; some wire reporting still calls it the Constitutional Court, and this dossier uses the 2023 constitution's name.

Test 1 — GIVES BY ONE LAW

Same rules for allies and opponents, insiders and outsiders — no favorites at the source.

GRADE: FAILS

Evidence for:

  • Two accountability processes with genuine independence have run to verdicts on his watch, against men of the era before him and of the rebellion alike. The UN-backed Special Criminal Court (SCC) — a hybrid court of Central African and international judges — issued an international arrest warrant for former president François Bozizé in February 2024 and opened his trial in absentia on 16 June 2026 on crimes-against-humanity charges for 2009–2013, with Bozizé represented by counsel and three of his former senior officers in pre-trial detention (Special Criminal Court, February 2024 and June 2026; contemporaneous reporting). The International Criminal Court, to which CAR under Touadéra maintained its cooperation, convicted anti-balaka leaders Alfred Yekatom and Patrice-Edouard Ngaïssona in July 2025. These are courts he does not control, reaching verdicts his government did not write.
  • The mining law was reformed in the open and for everyone: a new mining code, developed with World Bank support over four years, was adopted in August 2024, presented as a single framework to reduce informality, raise public revenue and improve transparency across all operators (IPIS, 2026; World Bank programme reporting).
  • His government signed, and in 2025 finally executed, a peace framework that applied the same disarmament terms to the two strongest remaining armed groups as to the twelve that had gone before: under the N'Djamena agreements of 19 April and 19 November 2025, the UPC and 3R re-entered the 2019 Political Agreement's framework, their leaders dissolved both movements' political and military wings at a Bangui ceremony on 10 July 2025, and more than 1,900 combatants were demobilised (UN Secretary-General report S/2026/481, June 2026; MINUSCA, 2026).

Evidence against:

  • The law's hardest edge falls on his opponents, and it bends when the opponent becomes useful. In September 2023 a Bangui court sentenced Bozizé, his sons, and rebel leaders including UPC chief Ali Darassa to forced labour for life in absentia, on charges including conspiracy, rebellion and murder. In July 2025 the same Ali Darassa stood in Bangui dissolving his movement at a ceremony Touadéra presided over, his life sentence neither served nor formally lifted in any public act this record shows — the verdict simply stopped applying when the politics changed. A law that convicts a man for life and then seats him at the peace table without process is not one law; it is a law with a political override, and the override is his.
  • The nationality file against his main rival was run through the prosecution in a way only the presidency could complete or cure. The 2023 constitution — his constitution — bars dual nationals from the presidency. Dologuélé renounced his French citizenship in August 2025. The public prosecutor then referred his case to the Bangui High Court, which on 16 October 2025 revoked his nationality certificate and rendered him stateless, on the argument that his Central African citizenship, lost on acquiring French nationality in 1994, could be restored only by a presidential decree — a decree the president had not issued. Dologuélé's counter-argument, recorded as his ALLEGATION against the process, is that he had run in 2015 and 2020 on the same certificate. The Constitutional Council rescued the candidacy on 14 November 2025, ruling he had retained his citizenship (International IDEA democracy tracker, October–November 2025; Reuters factbox, 23 December 2025). That the referee corrected it is credited in Test 2; that the machinery of the state was set in motion to make the incumbent's chief rival stateless six weeks before the vote, on a defect only the incumbent could waive, is the Test 1 fact.
  • For the forces that keep him in power, the law effectively does not run. A decade of documentation — the UN Working Group on mercenaries' statement of 31 March 2021 reporting grave abuses by Russian private military personnel operating jointly with the national army (mass summary executions, arbitrary detention, torture, forced displacement among the reported categories); the UN Security Council experts' report of June 2021 finding excessive force, indiscriminate killings and large-scale looting in the 2021 counter-offensive; Human Rights Watch's 2022 documentation of killings and torture by Russian-linked forces; The Sentry's June 2023 investigation of a Wagner "blueprint for state capture" — has produced no documented prosecution of any Russian contractor, and no documented prosecution of national soldiers for the operations' civilian toll, in the record assembled here. The UN Independent Expert recorded 1,636 cases of violations and abuses affecting 2,480 victims in the first half of 2025 alone, with armed groups the main perpetrators and state forces among them (UN Independent Expert report A/HRC/60/89, August 2025). One law for the defeated; no law, in practice, for the guard.
  • The resource settlement follows the same two tracks. The US Treasury's June 2023 sanctions designation — a US government finding, recorded as such and not a judicial verdict — states that Midas Ressources, a Wagner-linked company, held preferential mining rights at the Ndassima gold mine, in ground estimated to hold more than USD 1 billion in gold, and that Midas and Wagner prevented Central African government officials from inspecting the mine (US Treasury designation, June 2023; CSIS analysis, 2023). A state whose own inspectors are barred from its richest mine by the company its president's security depends on has a second law at the source, whatever the new mining code says. In November 2024 CAR was suspended from the Extractive Industries Transparency Initiative after validation identified breaches of the EITI civil-society protocol and gaps in contract and beneficial-ownership disclosure (IPIS, 2026) — the transparency regime his government had joined, suspended on the participation and disclosure counts.

WHY the grade lands here: The for-shelf is real — two courts he does not control have convicted or are trying the powerful, and the 2025 peace execution applied one framework to the last holdouts. But the domestic pattern is consistent across ten years: prosecution is a weapon that fires at opponents (a statelessness case against the runner-up, life sentences in absentia for the rebellion's leaders) and is then switched off by politics (Darassa's sentence dissolving into a ceremony), while the armed patron on whom the seat depends operates outside the law's reach entirely, up to and including barring the state's own mine inspectors. That is the definition of a second law for one's own side.

Helped: the victims and plaintiffs in the SCC and ICC processes, who got real courts; compliant mining operators, who got a single modern code in 2024; the communities of the centre and northwest, where the UPC/3R disarmament applied the peace framework evenly at last. Hurt: Anicet-Georges Dologuélé, subjected to a state-driven statelessness proceeding on the eve of the election; the civilians in the UN, HRW and Sentry files, for whom no prosecution of the forces that harmed them is documented; Central African artisanal miners and the public treasury, displaced at Ndassima by a preferential concession the state could not inspect; civil-society organisations, whose documented exclusion from the extractives-transparency process contributed to CAR's EITI suspension.

Test 2 — HOLDS WITHOUT GRIPPING

Do the courts, the electoral commission, the media, the prosecution, and the central bank keep their autonomy — or is independence punished and control centralized?

GRADE: FAILS

Evidence for:

  • The Constitutional Council ruled against the presidency's interest in the most sensitive case of the 2025 cycle and the ruling stood: on 14 November 2025 it declared Dologuélé eligible, finding he had retained his Central African citizenship, against the prosecution's position and against complaints filed by Touadéra's supporters; it likewise cleared former prime minister Henri-Marie Dondra against a dual-citizenship challenge (his alleged Congolese nationality — an ALLEGATION the Council found unproven) (International IDEA, November 2025; Reuters, 23 December 2025). The president faced both of his principal rivals at the ballot rather than having them struck off.
  • The legislature passed, and he promulgated, a law protecting human-rights defenders — adopted 27 December 2024, providing security mechanisms, legal support and guarantees against reprisals — and the UN Independent Expert welcomed it while urging its enactment (CIVICUS Monitor, 2025; UN Independent Expert, 2025).
  • The one referee structurally beyond his reach held, and he complied: the regional central bank (BEAC) and the CEMAC authorities declared the 2022 bitcoin legal-tender law incompatible with the monetary union's treaties, and his government amended the law in March 2023 rather than defying the institution (IMF Country Report 23/156, May 2023). The referee he cannot grip is the one whose ruling he obeyed in full — the contrast is the evidence, in both directions.
  • He has not gripped the UN mission: CAR's government supported MINUSCA's renewal, co-chaired the joint transition committee through Prime Minister Moloua, and accepted the Security Council's conditions-based drawdown framework — resolution 2800 of 13 November 2025 extending the mandate to 15 November 2026, and resolution 2836 of 12 November 2026 extending it to 15 November 2027 at a reduced ceiling of 12,000 military personnel (UN Security Council, November 2025 and November 2026). The mission that reports on his government's abuses remains in place, by a renewal his delegation publicly welcomed.

Evidence against:

  • The defining act of the decade: when the Constitutional Court ruled against him, he removed the Court's president. In September 2022 the Court annulled the 55-member committee he had appointed to draft constitutional changes, ruling the process unconstitutional because the Senate envisaged by the 2016 constitution had never been established. Touadéra responded by dismissing Court president Danièle Darlan by decree in October 2022 — a dismissal the 2015/2016 constitution did not empower him to make, described by opposition parties as a self-coup — and Darlan was forcibly retired in January 2023. Darlan further stated that in March 2022 the Russian embassy's chargé d'affaires had approached her to ask how Touadéra could be kept in power — her account, recorded as her statement (2023 constitutional referendum record; CIVICUS, 2023; Darlan's public statements). The reconstituted Court then permitted the referendum, and the constitution it produced replaced the Court with a Constitutional Council of 11 members, three appointed by the president (up from one of nine). Independence was not argued with; it was dismissed, then redesigned.
  • The electoral referee is contested at its root and starved in its operation. The BRDC's stated conditions for ending its boycott included restructuring the ANE and the Constitutional Council, which it regards as captured — a characterisation recorded as the opposition's position, contested by the government. What is not contested: in April 2026, ANE staff struck over two months of unpaid salaries, delaying results in 70 legislative constituencies (International IDEA, 2026). An electoral commission that cannot pay its own staff on time is not being held at arm's length; it is being held short.
  • The prosecution moves against the government's critics on a documented rhythm. In March 2025, two brothers of declared presidential contender Henri-Marie Dondra were arrested at night for alleged acts against state institutions (charges recorded as ALLEGATIONs). Journalist Landry Nguéma Ngokpélé was arrested on 8 May 2025 after publishing an article about former president Bozizé's supposed return, charged with disseminating information intended to disturb public order, and provisionally released in August. Lecturer and regime opponent Dominique Désiré Erenon was arrested on arrival from exile on 3 October 2025 and sent to Ngaragba prison on 17 December, accused among other things of a coup plot — again, ALLEGATIONs, untried (Africa Yearbook 2025, Brill; CIVICUS Monitor, 2025). A new press law adopted 26 May 2025 reintroduced criminal penalties for defamation and insult, flagged by the UN Independent Expert as harmful to press freedom; the UN Independent Expert also documented bans and suppression of peaceful demonstrations and the arrest of human-rights defenders during a march on 26 June 2025 (UN Independent Expert report, August 2025). In October 2024 the National Assembly considered a "foreign agents" law modelled on Russia's — not adopted in the record assembled here, but tabled in his majority's chamber (CIVICUS Monitor, 2025).
  • The presidency's grip reaches into the count itself: the 2023 referendum's official figures — 95.03% yes on 57.23% turnout — were contested by the boycotting opposition, which estimated turnout below 15%; the two counts are printed here separately and never blended (Constitutional Court validation, 20 August 2023; opposition estimates via CIVICUS, 2023). A referendum that abolished the limit on his own tenure, run after the referee's president was removed, validated by the reconstituted court, on a turnout the two camps measure threefold apart, is the grip made visible.

WHY the grade lands here: Test 2 does not ask whether every institution is a puppet — the Council's Dologuélé ruling and the BEAC compliance show referees that still bite, and both are credited in full. It asks what happens to independence when it rules against the seat. The documented answer, at the one moment it mattered most, is that the referee was dismissed by decree, in excess of the constitution's grant, and the institution was then rebuilt with a larger presidential share of appointments. Every later act of forbearance happens inside a house whose master has already shown he can remove the judge. That demonstration, once made, is the grip — and it was made by him.

Helped: the presidency itself and the MCU majority, whose constitutional project survived its judicial veto; appointees to the reconstituted Council. Hurt: Danièle Darlan, dismissed and forcibly retired for a ruling; the Constitutional Court as an institution, abolished and replaced on altered terms; ANE staff, unpaid for two months in the middle of an electoral cycle; journalists and defenders — Landry Nguéma Ngokpélé, the defenders arrested on 26 June 2025, Dominique Désiré Erenon in Ngaragba prison on untried charges; Henri-Marie Dondra's brothers, arrested in the night in March 2025; and every future litigant whose judge has read the Darlan file.

Test 3 — ANSWERS THE WOBBLE

When courts, voters, parliament, the street, foreign partners, or events push back, does he adjust openly — or deny and double down?

GRADE: MIXED

The no-good-choice file, stated first because it frames the tenure. In December 2020 the Coalition of Patriots for Change (CPC) — armed groups that had signed the 2019 peace agreement, politically fronted by Bozizé after the Constitutional Court rejected his candidacy — launched an offensive to overturn the election and take Bangui, reaching the capital's outskirts on 13 January 2021. The options actually open to Touadéra were three, and each carried real harm. (1) Rely on MINUSCA alone: a mission with a defensive protection mandate that had not stopped the CPC's advance across the country in December. (2) Negotiate with the CPC: a coalition formed to annul a completed vote by force, led politically by a former president whose own record the SCC would later charge as crimes against humanity. (3) Accept the help that was offered at speed — Rwandan bilateral troops and Russian contractors already in-country — knowing the Russian contingent's documented record and that its price would be paid in mining concessions and dependence. He chose the third door, bundled with the first two's instruments (MINUSCA held ground; Rwanda's deployment was the cleanest component). Under the clause, the choice itself is recorded as the best available in the moment: the elected government stood, the capital did not fall, and no hindsight option — a national army that did not exist, a regional force that was not offered — counts as an option refused. What remains gradable, and is graded in Tests 1, 4 and 5, is the trap's origin and the honesty inside it: the trap was built in part on his side of the ledger — the 2019 agreement's disarmament stalled on his government's watch, the national army was left unreformed and unpaid into crisis, and the 2018 decision to build the state's security on a foreign contractor rather than a national force was his — and inside the trap, his government for years described combat troops as "instructors," which was not an honest account of the arrangement the country was living under.

Evidence for (adjusts openly):

  • The clearest policy reversal of the decade was done in the open, by law. When the IMF, the BEAC and CEMAC partners objected that the April 2022 bitcoin legal-tender law violated the monetary union's treaties and endangered the financial system, the National Assembly amended the law in March 2023, removing legal-tender status and the obligation to accept crypto — a public, legislative climbdown from his own flagship announcement, taken to keep the IMF programme and the regional currency (IMF, May 2023; Law No. 22.004 as amended, March 2023). If another option became available — here, the regional framework's terms — he considered it and took it, in the open.
  • Faced in 2025 with the two armed groups his army had failed to defeat, he took the negotiated door his 2019 self had signed and his 2021 self had abandoned: the N'Djamena track, mediated by Chad, produced the April and November 2025 agreements, the July 2025 dissolution ceremony, and demobilisation of more than 1,900 fighters — an adjustment from the military-only answer of 2021–2024, made publicly and with the enemy's leaders received in Bangui rather than only targeted (UN reporting, 2025–2026).
  • The street, eventually, was answered rather than only banned. On 30 March 2025, with the BRDC mobilising against his third-term candidacy, Touadéra publicly announced his readiness for dialogue with the coalition; the 4 April 2025 opposition demonstration in Bangui — initially banned — proceeded under MINUSCA protection with more than 1,000 participants (Africa Yearbook 2025). In November 2026 the pattern repeated in miniature and in the open: the government spokesman declared the BRDC's 13 November fuel-price march illegal on 7 November; the march was then authorised and policed, took place on 13 November, and the spokesman's 14 November statement — warning the coalition while conceding the march had been authorised "out of respect for public freedoms" — is the sound of a government adjusting under pressure while saving face (Radio Ndeke Luka reporting via Afrikactus, November 2026; government spokesman statements, 7 and 14 November 2026).
  • With Moscow, he has visibly weighed the demand rather than simply obeying or refusing: when Russia pressed from 2025 to replace Wagner with the state-run Africa Corps and to be paid in cash — billions of CFA francs — CAR officials told the Associated Press the government was reluctant, judging Wagner more effective and preferring to pay in minerals; Bangui delayed for roughly a year before Touadéra's 4–5 March 2026 Moscow visit pledged deeper security cooperation with President Putin (Associated Press, August 2025; Reuters, 5 March 2026). The end state of that negotiation is contested in the reporting — residual Wagner structures still operating, consolidation deadlines reported for late 2026 — and is recorded here as unresolved, not as either obedience or defiance.

Evidence against (denies, downgrades, or removes the source of the pushback):

  • The foundational wobble of the tenure was answered by removing the referee (Test 2): the Constitutional Court's September 2022 annulment of his drafting committee was not adjusted to, appealed, or publicly reasoned against — its president was dismissed within weeks. Every later adjustment happens in the shadow of that first answer.
  • The March 2025 dialogue offer was downgraded by his own camp within days: his special adviser clarified that Touadéra would not participate personally, reducing the exchange to a technical level, and the government refused the opposition's request for a high-level African mediator (Africa Yearbook 2025). An offer of dialogue that withdraws the offerer is a half-adjustment, and the opposition read it as such.
  • The street's other answers were bans and cells: the UN Independent Expert documented bans and suppression of peaceful demonstrations through 2025, the arrest of defenders at the 26 June 2025 march, and arrests of media professionals (UN Independent Expert, August 2025); civil-society leaders were detained after the "dead city" protest day of 12 October 2026 and remained a live grievance in the November 2026 march demands (Radio Ndeke Luka reporting, November 2026 — the detentions as reported by the march's organisers and local media; the prosecutor's office announced an investigation into the call's initiators). The November 2026 authorisation is credited above; the October detentions are the same government's other hand.
  • On the referendum's legitimacy, the answer was denial without inspection: official turnout of 57.23% against opposition estimates below 15% was never independently reconciled, and the fraud appeals against the 2025 results — Dologuélé's and Dondra's ALLEGATIONs of malpractice and widespread fraud — were rejected by the Council for insufficient evidence, with no audit or recount mechanism engaged (Constitutional Council, 19 January 2026). Rejection by the referee is a lawful answer; it is not an adjustment, and on the count that seated him for seven more years it is the only answer the record shows.

WHY the grade lands here: The wobble record is genuinely two-handed. This is a ruler who reversed his own signature law in public when the region pushed back, who chose negotiation with undefeated enemies over another four years of war, and who twice in 2025–2026 let banned marches proceed. It is also the ruler whose first answer to the highest court's pushback was to dismiss its president, whose dialogue offers shrink when his own presence is requested, and whose security services detain the organisers of protest days. The adjustments cluster where the pressure carries money or guns — the IMF, CEMAC, Moscow, armed groups; the refusals cluster where the pressure carries only votes and voices. That distribution, not any single act, is why the grade is MIXED and not higher.

Helped: the treasury and the CFA arrangement, protected by the 2023 bitcoin reversal; UPC and 3R fighters and their home communities, reached by negotiation rather than only by offensive; the BRDC's marchers of April 2025 and November 2026, who marched. Hurt: the Constitutional Court's dismissed president (cross-referenced from Test 2, where the act is graded); the defenders and organisers detained in June 2025 and October 2026; the dialogue's intended participants, offered a technical table when they asked for a political one; voters whose fraud appeals were answered with rejection and no recount.

Test 4 — COUNTS EVERYONE

Does the accounting include the regions, the minorities, the displaced, the protesters, and the poor — or are some counted out?

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • The counting machinery itself reached further in 2025 than at any point in the country's history: four ballots on one day, 28 December 2025, including the first municipal elections since 1988; voting held across most of the territory; turnout of 52.42% of 2.4 million registered voters, up from 35.3% in the 2020 presidential vote; women 47% of registered voters (International IDEA, 2026; ANE, January 2026). A state that in 2016 administered little beyond Bangui ran a nationwide count.
  • The peace settlement's benefits were routed to the constituencies most often counted out: the UPC and 3R draw heavily on Fulani communities of the centre and northwest, and their 2025 return brought demobilisation, cantonment and reintegration processing to those communities — more than 1,900 ex-combatants processed since July 2025, with tangible security gains recorded by the UN in the centre and northwest (UN Secretary-General reporting, 2025–2026). Eleven of the fourteen 2019 signatory groups have now formally dissolved (MINUSCA, 2026).
  • The displaced are coming back into the count: 75,800 internally displaced persons returned spontaneously amid improving security on main roads and in main cities, and 75,031 Central African refugees have been voluntarily repatriated since 2017, 21,445 of them in 2025 (UNHCR operational update, June 2026). CAR also counts others in: it hosts some 62,000 refugees and asylum seekers, mostly Sudanese fleeing a worse war (UNHCR, June 2026).
  • State authority — the precondition of being counted by the state at all — has been extended prefecture by prefecture with MINUSCA's support, and the government co-owns the transition plan that sequences the handover rather than demanding an exit that would abandon the periphery (UN Security Council resolutions 2800 and 2836).

Evidence against:

  • After a decade of his rule, the humanitarian ledger counts nearly half the country as in need: OCHA's 2026 plan counts 2.3 million people — 43% of the population — in need of humanitarian assistance, 1.3 million targeted, with only about 36% of the targeted reached by mid-2026 (OCHA Humanitarian Response Plan 2026; OCHA dashboard, January–July 2026). The displacement counts are kept separate, as they must be: 428,227 internally displaced as of March 2026 (OCHA), and 736,000 Central African refugees in neighbouring countries as of October 2025 (UNHCR) — together roughly one in five Central Africans still outside their homes. The food-security count is its own: some 2.29 million people projected in IPC Phase 3 or above (crisis or emergency) in the April–August 2026 lean season (IPC via EU humanitarian reporting, 2026). CAR stands 191st of 193 on the Human Development Index (UNDP). A ruler is not charged with every consequence of a war he inherited; he is charged with the trajectory, and the trajectory's honest summary is that the counted-in share grew while the absolute number in need stayed at nearly half the nation.
  • In the southeast, a population is being counted out by force, with the state's partners implicated. In Haut-Mbomou prefecture, the Azande Ani Kpi Gbe militia committed killings, gang rapes, torture, expropriations and kidnappings particularly targeting Muslims, Fulani and Sudanese asylum seekers around Zémio, Dembia and Rafaï (UN Independent Expert report, August 2025). Investigative and UN reporting describe the group as having been trained and equipped with Russian and national-army support and operating alongside state forces — a characterisation the dossier records as documented reporting, contested by no public government accounting in the record assembled here. The victims are precisely the communities — Muslim, Fulani, refugee — that CAR's wars have repeatedly written out of the national count.
  • The 2025 count, for all its reach, was boycotted by the main opposition coalition and ran over rolls the opposition contests; the BRDC's absence means the 77.9% measures, as one assessment put it, what remains when abstention is organised (CES Intelligence analysis, January 2026, citing Al Jazeera) — a characterisation, recorded as such. Displaced persons and refugees abroad were registered only partially, on observer reporting; the count was the widest in the country's history and still not the whole country.
  • The southeast and the Sudan border remain outside the peace: fighting and displacement escalated in Haut-Mbomou and Vakaga through 2026 — over 42,000 people preventively displaced in two months in the southeast, 16,000 displaced Central Africans gathered near the MINUSCA base at Am-Dafock, humanitarian workers hit by 36 security incidents in January–May 2026 (OCHA humanitarian bulletin, 2026; UNHCR, June 2026). The state counts these people as a protection caseload for the UN mission more than as citizens reached by their own government.

WHY the grade lands here: The direction of the decade is toward counting more people in — territory, ballots, demobilised enemies, returning displaced, hosted refugees — and the 2025 elections were the broadest act of national counting CAR has ever performed. But the test is not direction alone; it is whether anyone is counted out, and the record names them: the Muslim and Fulani communities of the southeast, preyed on by a militia the state's own partners trained; the 736,000 refugees and 428,000 displaced who remain outside the count's full reach; the opposition's voters, whose organised abstention the official result simply steps over. MIXED, with the counted-out named rather than averaged away.

Helped: voters in the recovered prefectures, counted in four ballots for the first time in a generation; Fulani communities of the centre and northwest, whose armed wings were demobilised into the settlement; returning displaced persons and repatriated refugees; Sudanese refugees, hosted and registered. Hurt: the Muslim, Fulani and Sudanese-asylum-seeker communities of Haut-Mbomou, targeted by the Azande Ani Kpi Gbe; the still-displaced fifth of the nation; the hungry — 2.29 million in crisis-level food insecurity in the 2026 lean season; the opposition electorate, whose boycott converted their absence into the incumbent's margin.

Test 5 — KEEPS HIS WORD OVER TIME

Program pledges and public statements vs. the record since 30 March 2016, with dates.

GRADE: FAILS

Evidence for (promises kept or credibly delivered):

  • The 2025 elections — promised, dated, and held. Through 2025 his government committed to general elections on 28 December 2025, and held them on that date — presidential, legislative, regional and the first municipal ballots since 1988 — peacefully across most of the territory (ANE; International IDEA, 2026). Whatever the contest over their fairness (Tests 1–2), the word about holding them was kept, on the day named.
  • The 2019 Political Agreement — signed by him, and its machinery finally run. The Khartoum agreement of 6 February 2019 committed his government to an inclusive peace: disarmament, mixed units, armed-group leaders in government. Its first life was a failure both sides own (Test 3's origin file) — but in 2025 he returned to its framework and executed its core transaction with the last two major holdouts, dissolving eleven of fourteen signatory groups in total (MINUSCA, 2026). A promise delivered six years late, through a second negotiation, is recorded as delivered late — not as never made.
  • Security restored in the capital and the main axes — the core 2016 and 2021 pledge, delivered by the means graded elsewhere. Bangui has not faced a serious armed assault since January 2021; state authority and army deployment extend over most prefectural capitals; the 2025 vote could be held nationwide. The delivery is real. Its price and its instruments are graded in Tests 1 and 4; under the two-axes rule, the keeping is recorded here and the hurt is not laundered by it.

Evidence against (promises not kept, or receding):

  • The founding word — the constitution he swore to, and the limit it set on him. Touadéra took office on 30 March 2016 under a constitution that limited the president to two five-year terms, and was re-elected in 2020 under the same text. The path to his third candidacy ran: his appointed drafting committee (annulled by the Constitutional Court, September 2022); the Court president's dismissal (October 2022); a referendum (30 July 2023) organised by his camp, boycotted by the opposition, on contested turnout; and a new constitution with no term limits. His own adviser's public framing of the transaction was that there would be no third term because "the count will be set back to zero" (Fidèle Gouandjika, presidential adviser, 2023) — which is the sound of a limit being redefined rather than honoured. Under the mandate rule, seeking a third term is not itself the failure; a platform may be anything. The failure graded here is narrower and documented: the limit he twice swore to uphold was removed by a process he controlled, after the referee who blocked the first attempt was removed by his decree. The word about his own limits is the word that did not hold.
  • The crypto word — announced as transformation, delivered as a retraction and a collapsed coin. In April 2022 he announced bitcoin as legal tender and the Sango project as the country's digital future — tokenised resources, a crypto island, citizenship and land by investment. The Constitutional Court struck down the citizenship-and-land sale in August 2022 as unconstitutional; the legal-tender law itself was repealed in substance by the March 2023 amendment (Test 3, where the reversal is credited as an adjustment); Sango Coin sold less than 10% of its target supply (Global Initiative Against Transnational Organised Crime report, 2025, via Cointelegraph); a July 2023 law opened land and natural resources to tokenisation; and in February 2025 his account announced a national memecoin, $CAR, which spiked and collapsed within days. The sequence is recorded whole: a promise of sovereign digital transformation, delivered as a repealed law, an undersold coin, a tokenisation statute critics — GI-TOC — assess as deepening elite control, and a memecoin. The word did not survive contact with its own delivery.
  • The dialogue word — offered at the top, withdrawn at the door. On 30 March 2025 he announced readiness for dialogue with the BRDC; his adviser's clarification that he would not attend personally, and the refusal of a mediator, followed within days (Africa Yearbook 2025). The 2022 "republican dialogue" had already run the same shape: convened by him, boycotted by much of the opposition, excluding the armed groups whose absence defined it.
  • The March 2026 word — recorded, not yet gradable. At his third inauguration on 30 March 2026 he pledged to "consolidate peace, security, reconciliation, and national unity" and to "build a sovereign economy and ensure transparent management of our natural resources" (inaugural address, 30 March 2026). The transparency clause is noted against the standing facts of Test 1 — the uninspectable Ndassima concession and the EITI suspension — and the seven-year term will grade it. It is recorded here so the record can.

The four crossings, named: 1. Kept his word and it helped: the 28 December 2025 elections, held on the date promised, with the first local ballots since 1988 — a kept word that extended the national count (Test 4). 2. Kept his word and it still hurt: the security partnership. He said from 2018, and again at his 2021 inauguration, that he would restore the state's authority with his partners' help and without apology for the choice of partners; he kept that course for eight years, and its cost was borne by the civilians in the UN, HRW and Sentry files and by the treasury in concessions (Tests 1 and 4 carry the hurt in full; the keeping earns its Test 5 credit here and launders nothing). 3. Went against his word and it helped: the bitcoin repeal. His own flagship 2022 law was reversed by his own assembly in March 2023 under regional and IMF pressure — a broken announcement that protected the currency union, the IMF programme and the treasury from a documented systemic risk (IMF, May 2023). The break is recorded; so is the help. 4. Broke his word and it hurt: the term limit — the two-term constitution under which he was twice elected and twice sworn, removed through a process he controlled after its guardian was dismissed; the hurt falls on every voter and party for whom the limit was the agreed rule of succession, and on the credibility of every future constitutional promise in CAR, including his own of March 2026.

WHY the grade lands here: His delivered words are not nothing — an election held on its date, a peace framework executed at last, a capital kept. But Test 5 weights the words a ruler gives about the rules that bind the ruler himself, because those are the words the whole system leans on. On that word — the limit in the constitution he swore to uphold — the record is a sequence of controlled steps ending in the limit's abolition and an adviser's "reset to zero." Add the crypto arc, in which a transformation was announced and a retraction delivered, and the dialogue offers that withdrew the offerer, and the shelf against outweighs the shelf for.

Helped: voters and local communities who received the promised December 2025 ballots; the beneficiaries of the kept security course in the capital and recovered prefectures; the treasury, protected by the bitcoin reversal. Hurt: the constitutional order's believers — the BRDC's constituency and every citizen who treated the two-term limit as settled; investors and citizens who bought the Sango and $CAR announcements at their word; the opposition, offered dialogues the offerer would not attend.

Overall Summary

One law: FAILS · Without gripping: FAILS · The wobble: MIXED · Counts everyone: MIXED · His word: FAILS.

Faustin-Archange Touadéra inherited in 2016 a state that existed mainly in its capital, and a decade on he holds more of his country than any Central African president since the war began: the capital secure since 2021, eleven of fourteen armed groups dissolved, a nationwide four-ballot election held, a UN mission negotiating its own conditions-based departure. The Test does not grade the map; it grades the holding, and the holding's pattern is consistent. On one law, the pattern is a legal system that fires at opponents — a statelessness prosecution against his runner-up, life sentences in absentia for rebel leaders — and switches off for allies: a life-sentenced rebel chief seated at the peace table with his sentence unlifted and unserved, a security patron whose forces face no documented prosecution and whose mining company barred the state's own inspectors from the country's richest gold mine. On gripping, the tenure's defining act came in October 2022, when the Constitutional Court's president was dismissed by decree for ruling against his constitutional project; the court was then abolished and rebuilt with a larger presidential share, and everything since — including the successor Council's genuine ruling for Dologuélé in November 2025 — happens in that shadow. On the wobble, the record is honestly two-sided: a flagship law reversed in public under regional pressure, undefeated enemies negotiated into dissolution, banned marches twice allowed to proceed — set against dialogue offers that withdrew the offerer and protest organisers in detention. On counting, the machinery reached everyone it ever has, and the southeast's Muslims, Fulani and refugees, the 428,000 displaced and the 736,000 refugees abroad mark the count's edge. On his word, the crossings tell the story in one line: he kept the words that extended his power and broke the one that limited it — and, to the record's credit and his, also broke one that helped (the bitcoin repeal) and kept one that hurt (the security partnership, whose price Tests 1 and 4 carry). The January 2021 trap was real, and this dossier grades its choice as the best available in the moment; the trap's origin — a stalled disarmament, an unreformed army, a security built on a contractor — was substantially of his making, and the decade since has consolidated the seat faster than it has rebuilt the referees. The open files that will move these grades are named: whether the Africa Corps negotiation ends on terms his government chose or Moscow's; whether any prosecution ever reaches the forces documented by the UN; whether the March 2026 transparency pledge survives contact with Ndassima; and whether the Constitutional Council's November 2025 independence was a precedent or an exception.
