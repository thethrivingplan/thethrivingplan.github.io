Why this door exists — the honest problem ▾

Here is the honest problem with building pages like ours: we can only work on what we can see. There are whole fields — whole kinds of work, whole systems people live inside every day — that have nothing to do with our lives. Things we haven't even come close to thinking about, and never will on our own, because they never cross our path.

The only way those fields get worked on is if the person living in them says so. You are the expert on your field. Nobody knows where it breaks like the person standing in the break. What we bring is the treatment — the same one, for every field. This page is the door. Walk through it.

The treatment, step by step ▾

Ask why, down to bedrock

Why is it broken? Why is it done that way? Who decided that? We keep asking why — this, that, who — until the chain reaches bedrock: the law, the money, or a person deciding. A chain that stops early gets labeled stopped, never dressed up as the bottom.

Follow the money

Somebody is usually getting paid for the breakage — or saving money by leaving it broken. We trace who collects, who pays, and who profits from nothing changing.

Call out the red tape

Red tape means paperwork, waiting, and runaround that stands between a person and what they're owed. Where a rule protects nobody and only exhausts people, we say so, by name, on the record.

Why? → How? → Act. — at bedrock, the how reveals itself. You don't invent the fix; you find it waiting.

What's welcome here — and how the mic works, honestly ▾

Just talk. You do not have to phrase anything “appropriately.” There is no grammar to get right, no polish expected, no right way to say it. Say what you like and what you don't like, however it comes out — fragments, rambles, half a thought, a correction in the middle of a sentence. All of it is read for what it means, never judged on how it was said. The door takes it from there.

Tap the mic once and start talking. It stays on — scroll up, read the pages, check what's already here, come back — your words keep landing in the box the whole time, live, as you say them. Tap it again when you're done. Then read it over, fix any word the mic heard wrong, and send it exactly like typed text — the check below runs on it the same way.

How it works, honestly: one tap starts it — your browser will ask permission for the mic once. It uses your browser's own speech service to turn your voice into text. This page records nothing and sends nothing — your words become text in the box here, and only what you choose to copy and post at the bottom travels from this page, exactly as with typing. One honest limit, stated plainly: some browsers — Chrome is the big one — run their speech service on their maker's servers, so your voice audio goes to your browser maker's speech service to be turned into text; some other browsers and devices do it on the device itself. This page has no say in which yours does. If you'd rather your voice go nowhere at all, typing works exactly the same. Some browsers and in-app viewers can't do live dictation at all; if yours can't, the button will say so plainly. A regular browser is the reliable door for voice.
