How to read these profiles

Results and mechanism are graded separately. A documented result — a record set, a study published — stands on its own shelf. Why it happened is a second, separate question, and it gets its own grade.

Skeptical claims get graded too. “It was just cold reading” is a claim, and it has to fit the specific case — a blinded phone protocol is not a stage show. Each skeptical explanation below is marked supported, unsupported, or unverifiable against that person's actual record.

Fit notes are analysis, not their claims. Where a profile says what someone could contribute to a community plan, that is our reading of the record, labelled as such — not something they said or promised.

Results layer — what the record actually shows

The Windbridge certification, honestly described. Before joining Windbridge research, each medium is screened over several months through 8 steps: (1) written questionnaire, (2) personality/psychological tests, (3) phone interview with an existing certified medium, (4) phone interview with a Windbridge investigator, (5) two blinded phone readings, (6) mediumship research training, (7) human-research-subjects training, (8) grief training. Only Step 5 is a performance test — the other seven are screening, interviews, and training. Beischel's own account says certification cost the Institute $7,000–$10,000 per hopeful, one in four washed out, and about 20 survived. Jackson's publisher states she “underwent and passed” the process in 2011. Sources: the 8 steps · Psi Encyclopedia · Penguin NZ

No individual score exists in public. No source found publishes Jackson's own accuracy scores from any of this. cannot-verify any Jackson-specific score — what exists is the group's result, below.

The 2015 quintuple-blind study (Beischel, Boccuzzi, Biuso & Rock, EXPLORE 11(2):136–142): 20 Windbridge Certified Research Mediums performed 86 readings by phone under blinded conditions — mediums, raters, and experimenters all blinded. Published results: estimated accuracy of target vs decoy readings p=.05, d=0.49 (n=27); calculated accuracy p=.002, d=0.75 (n=31); hits vs misses p<.0001 and p=.002; global scores p=.001, d=0.57 (n=58); sitters selecting their target reading over a decoy p=.01 (n=58). The authors concluded that because normal sensory sources were eliminated, “a non-local source (however controversial) remains the most likely explanation.” Source: PubMed abstract

Inside the 2015 study: specific-question accuracy by topic

Target readings vs decoy readings, four scored sections (n=31). Overall: targets 52.8% ±3.9% vs decoys 36.6% ±3.8% (p=.002, d=.75). Per-topic decoy figures were not published in sources read, so the decoy line shown is the overall decoy average. This is a group result for 20 mediums — it does not isolate Jackson.

Mechanism layer — her explanation, and the criticism at full size

Her explanation: psychic ability reads a living person's energy field/aura (clairvoyance, clairaudience, clairsentience); mediumship connects with the consciousness of the deceased, which she distinguishes from psychic reading; the dead communicate through “signs” and a “Team of Light” — loved ones, guides, “God energy.” Consciousness, in her account, is non-local and survives bodily death. Windbridge's own framing is the narrower term “anomalous information reception.” Sources: Jay Shetty episode summary · Beischel on AIR

The strongest criticism, printed whole:

  • Scientific consensus: “There is no reliable evidence that mediums can communicate with the dead; reported successes are generally attributed to cold reading, the Barnum effect, selective reporting, sensory leakage or fraud.”
  • Certification has no mainstream standing: Windbridge and Forever Family Foundation screening procedures “are not recognised by mainstream scientific or professional bodies.”
  • Named critics — Richard Wiseman, Ray Hyman, and skeptical organisations — argue high-profile studies suffered inadequate blinding, optional stopping, biased scoring, and insufficient guards against sensory leakage and fraud; positive results concentrate in specialist parapsychology journals (EXPLORE published both key Windbridge papers) rather than general scientific literature.
  • Even the proponent-side meta-analysis concedes weakness: Tressoldi et al. (2021) reported “a small but statistically significant effect, while noting heterogeneity and the possibility of publication bias.”
  • Survival vs “super-psi” is undecidable even on proponents' terms: in earlier triple-blind work, a high-level psychic ability in the living “masquerading as discarnate communications could not be ruled out” — so even a real anomaly would not prove the dead are the source, which is Jackson's specific claim.

Sources: Mediumship studies overview · Psi Encyclopedia — Beischel · Hyman's critique

Mechanism layer — a diagnosis, plus training

In his own words (ABC News 20/20, Mar 2015): “I can dislocate both arms, both legs, turn my torso 180 degrees and all kinds of crazy stuff” — and the secret is Ehlers-Danlos syndrome: “It's a collagen disorder, and it makes me very, very flexible.” The segment included physician commentator Dr Michael Holick (Boston Medical Center / Boston University), estimating “probably one in 1,000 people have it.” Hypermobile EDS is one of 13 EDS types; affected people “often have extremely mobile joints which are vulnerable to dislocation and skin which is easily bruised,” many with severe pain (Tinkle et al. 2017).

Grade the diagnosis honestly: no primary medical record, imaging study, or case report on Smith himself was found. The hEDS diagnosis is documented as his consistent, long-standing self-report, amplified by news coverage — medically coherent and uncontradicted, but clinically cannot-verify from public sources.

Training is the other half — and a corrective to “born rubber.” hEDS is relatively common among contortionists due to tendon and ligament laxity, but extreme flexibility “often necessitates extensive conditioning and strength training to ensure stability and reduce the risk of long-term injury.” Smith's years under Master Lu Yi are the documented training component. The cost: he “experienced only moderate pain for most of his contortion career, but eventually had to give up performing as his condition progressed” — sourced to his own Facebook post, so treat it as single-chain, low-to-moderate confidence.

Profile 3 · Mental calculator

Scott Flansburg

“The Human Calculator” — a name given by Regis Philbin · born 28 Dec 1963, Herkimer, New York · U.S. Air Force veteran · author of Math Magic (1993) and Math Magic for Your Kids (1998)

His story

The discovery, in his own repeated telling, happened at age nine: not paying attention in math class, asked to add four numbers on the blackboard, he added the columns left to right instead of right to left as taught — and solved it. He then kept a running tally of his family's groceries so his father could hand the cashier an exact check before the total was rung up, and began noticing that “the shapes and numbers of angles in numbers were clues to their values,” counting 0–9 on his fingers instead of 1–10. A fifth-grade teacher, Mr Mike Potter, later let him share his own carry-free multiplication method with the class — “it was screaming at me from off the board — I can't not see it.” Sources: Wikipedia biography · his own telling, Instagram

The chapter older bios omit: he “served over six years with the United States Air Force Office of Special Investigations working in the Tech Services Division supporting high-level investigative and intelligence operations” (2026 veterans-podcast description). Secondary bios add enlistment in 1982 after West Canada Valley High School, service to 1988 including Yokota, Japan — single-source-chain detail, moderate confidence. From ~1990 he used the ability in entertainment and education; in 1991 he co-created “The Human Calculator System” (study guide + cassettes) and appeared on the infomercial Amazing Discoveries. In 1999 he invented a 13-month “Human Calculator Calendar” using zero as a day, month, and year. TV peak: episode 1 of Stan Lee's Superhumans (History, 5 Aug 2010) and hosting The Human Calculator on H2 (Jan 2016). In 2019, with his father dying, he returned to Herkimer — which led to the basketball-history campaign below. Source: The Recalibrated podcast

Results layer

The Guinness record, best-documented form: “Scott Flansburg of Phoenix, Arizona, USA, correctly added a randomly selected two-digit number (38) to itself 36 times in 15 seconds without the use of a calculator on 27 April 2000 on the set of Guinness World Records in Wembley, UK.” The Guinness Book of World Records listed him as “Fastest Human Calculator” in 2001 and 2003 — the feat being to add the number more times in 15 seconds than someone could with a calculator. Sources: Guinness-derived wording reproduced in secondary material; restated by 2025 profiles and Wikipedia
  • Date conflict, flagged: one 2026 blog claims the record was set in 1990. 1990 is when his entertainment/education career began; the 27 Apr 2000 date is supported by every other source including the Guinness-derived wording. Treat the 1990 record date as erroneous.
  • Televised self-beating (not shown as Guinness-listed): 26 Apr 2009, on Japanese primetime Asahi's Otona no Sonata, he broke his own record with 37 answers in 15 seconds.
  • Breadth, televised and checkable in real time: he can mentally add, subtract, multiply, divide, and find square and cube roots almost instantly, with calculator accuracy — demonstrated across Oprah, Ellen, The Tonight Show, Larry King Live, Today, and Good Morning America. His own site adds that “his mental calculations are faster than his ability to speak the answers” — in the 15-second format, the bottleneck is speech, by his account.
  • Independent context: the Mental Calculation World Cup — a specialist, non-promotional body — hosted him as a presenter and described him as “more an auditory than a visual calculator.” But no peer-reviewed laboratory verification of his speed was found: cannot-verify any lab-grade independent replication. The verification chain is Guinness adjudication plus decades of live TV demonstrations with audience-supplied numbers.

Mechanism layer — a method, not a mystery

His explanation: numbers should be learned 0–9, not 1–10; everything “goes back to the number nine” (Day 1 of his course is “The Power of 9's,” with digit-pattern and 9-check techniques); addition is done left to right; and with practice “everyone has the ability to do mental math.” His stated mission: “Why has it become so socially acceptable to be bad at math?… We have to change the attitude” — students should learn calculation methods rather than relying on table memorization. Sources: his course page · about page

The TV brain-scan claim stays in this layer, not the results layer. Stan Lee's Superhumans Ep. 101 reported an fMRI scan while he calculated: activity in Brodmann area 44 was said to be absent, with activity instead higher and closer to the motor cortex. This was a television segment, not a peer-reviewed study — no paper was found. It is also internally inconsistent in retelling: in a Jul 2026 interview clip, Flansburg himself recounts researchers finding that “rather than one ‘math center’ lighting up, his whole brain activated, particularly an area called Broca's area (Area 44)… 12 other brain areas came online” — roughly the opposite emphasis. Grade: unverifiable as neuroscience; usable only as “a TV show scanned him and reported atypical activation.”

The ordinary explanation, and why it doesn't debunk him: the record task is narrow and highly practicable — repeatedly adding 38 produces the sequence of multiples of 38, so the feat is partly rapid production of a memorised-pattern sequence at ~2.4 answers/sec, limited by speech. Forum-level critics make the same point about his other feats (cube roots via memorised cubes 1–9; division by 7 via the recurring 142857 cycle): “more down to good memory and lots of practice than any particular insight.” That reframes the mechanism as trained technique + memory — which is compatible with Flansburg's own teachability claim, and is exactly why his method works as a course. No fraud allegation against Flansburg was found.
