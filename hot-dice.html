🎲 Hot Dice

A press-your-luck dice game for points only — no money anywhere. Roll six dice, set scoring dice aside, then choose: bank what you have, or roll the rest and risk it all.

Homage, in plain words: Hot Dice is our own version of a folk dice shape played under many names at kitchen tables for generations. The shape is old and shared; the name Hot Dice, the words, the dice art, and the code on this page are our own.

How a turn works: roll all unbanked dice, tap dice to set aside a selection that scores with every selected die used — the page scores your selection by searching for its best exact breakdown, so four 1s read 1,100 (three 1s + a single 1), not 1,000. Then roll the rest or bank. A roll with no scoring combination at all busts: the unbanked turn total is lost. If all six dice score across a turn, that is hot dice — roll all six fresh and keep accumulating. Your first bank of a game must reach the entry threshold (a setting, standard 500). When a player crosses the target score, every other player gets one final turn; then the highest score wins.

What press-your-luck really is

Every “roll again?” is the same trade: a sure thing in hand (your unbanked turn total) against a chance to grow it and a chance to lose it all. There is no luck skill — there is only knowing the price of the next roll before you pay it. That price is in the bust table above, counted from these exact rules.

Why the last two dice are the dangerous ones

Read the bust table from six dice down to one. With six dice a bust is rare; with two dice it is close to a coin flip; with one die, two times in three the roll scores nothing, because only a 1 or a 5 saves you. The fewer dice you roll, the more each roll is a gamble — exactly when a big turn total tempts you to keep going.

Why a threshold to open exists

The entry threshold (standard 500 for your first bank) stops a game from being won by tiny, safe banks. It forces every player to press their luck at least once before they are on the board, which is where the game — and the lesson — lives.

Strategies — pick your own, none is the one true way

Bank early (around 300): small, steady banks; few busts; slow climb. You trade big turns for safety.

Steady middle (around 500): bank a solid turn, but ride a hot start a little. A balance of growth and busts.

Ride it (750+): chase big turns and hot dice. You will bust more — the table tells you exactly how much more — and sometimes land the turn that wins the game.

Read the table (the Pro way): weigh what you stand to lose — turn total × bust chance for the dice you would roll — against the likely gain. When the likely loss outweighs the likely gain, bank. This is expected value, the same math casinos and insurers use, taught here by playing.

Each bot's rule, in plain words. The bots in real games use these exact rules, and the checker below uses the same rules and the same game engine:

  • Learning: banks as soon as its turn total is 300 or more. It takes its points early and often.
  • Steady: banks at 500 or more — but banks at 300 or more when only 1 or 2 dice are left to roll, because those rolls are the dangerous ones.
  • Sharp: banks at 750 or more; banks at 500 or more with 1–2 dice left; banks at 350 or more with only 1 die left.
  • Pro: decides from the bust table itself. It estimates the likely gain of rolling again (the average set-aside score for that many dice, sampled from real rolls) and the likely loss (turn total × bust chance). When the likely loss outweighs the likely gain, it banks; otherwise it rolls.

All bots set aside the best-scoring selection they can find — the same exact search the page uses for you — and respect the entry threshold for their first bank.
