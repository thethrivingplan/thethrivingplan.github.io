The Examiner will see you again.

A polygraph does not detect lies. It records three things: heart rate, breathing, and sweat — the body’s arousal. Fear, embarrassment, anger, and a sudden noise all move the same needles. There is no bodily response unique to deception.

The National Research Council reviewed the evidence in 2003. Its finding, in its own words: for specific-incident tests, polygraphs sort people

“well above chance, though well below perfection”

— and for screening (testing many people to find a few), its accuracy is

“insufficient to justify reliance.”

The American Psychological Association: most psychologists agree there is little evidence polygraph tests can accurately detect lies.

Courts generally exclude polygraph results — yet the same results are still used in investigations, in plea negotiations, and as supervision conditions. The front door is closed; the side doors are open.

The wire-free version — voice-stress analysis, run over an ordinary phone call — performs at chance level in controlled studies. No wires, same confidence, no better accuracy.

Two words from the machine’s own vocabulary, opened up: inconclusive · the baseline · deception indicated

Sources: National Research Council, The Polygraph and Lie Detection (2003); American Psychological Association, “The Truth About Lie Detectors (aka Polygraph Tests).” The quotes above are the sources’ own words.

The needle

The chart is simulated — there are no sensors and nothing is measured. The needles obey dread and the scare, never your answers. The microphone, if you enabled it, is the one real input: a loud sound from you — a gasp — drives the needles during a scare. One guard on it, and it is a standard tool with an honest name: a noise gate, keyed to the machine’s own voice. The microphone’s startle detection sits behind a gate sidechained to the page’s own sound — while the page is making any sound, the gate is closed and nothing registers as you; it opens on a short hold and a release tail (the release is the allowance for speaker bleed and room echo). The page’s own scare sounds can never be mistaken for your gasp. Only a new loud sound from you, heard after the gate opens, counts. That is the entire trick, shown plainly here and nowhere on the game’s surface.
