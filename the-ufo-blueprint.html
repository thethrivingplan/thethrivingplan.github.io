The UFO — Blueprint

A design exercise, drawn honestly: take the characteristics that official reports describe, and design — on paper — a craft that would show them. Every gap between the reports and a buildable machine is labeled, at full size, as an open how.

The stance

“An unknown is not a no.”

An unknown stays on the shelf, labeled as an unknown — it is not a refusal, and it is not an impossibility. The work proceeds on what is known.

Read this first — the honesty frame

“UFO” names a class of reports, not a proven craft.

What is documented: official reports exist. Government UAP reports and military pilot reports describe objects with characteristics our public craft don't show. Those reports are graded here as reports — real documents, real testimony, describing things not yet explained.

This page never claims such a craft exists, is ours, or is anyone else's. It designs one that would match the reports, and it says plainly — in the biggest type on the page, in the Open Hows — what nobody here knows how to build yet.

Why?Because the reports are on the record and unexplained — and an unexplained report is a design brief waiting to be drawn.
How?Point the house toolkit at each reported characteristic, one at a time, and label every place the toolkit runs out.
Act.Measure first. The build order starts with instruments — a blueprint for knowing before a blueprint for flying.

Read it in three layers

Layer 1 — What it is

A paper design for a craft that would behave the way the reports describe: no visible propulsion, instant-looking maneuvers, no sonic boom, hover and high speed in one object, air-to-water without slowing.

Layer 2 — What it means

The reports and the design are two different shelves. The reports are evidence of something unexplained being seen. The design is evidence of how we would answer it. Neither shelf borrows the other's authority.

Layer 3 — What it could mean for you

The transferable part is not the craft — it's the method. Grade reports as reports. Design against the hardest version of the problem. Put the unknowns on a shelf at full size instead of burying them. That works on any problem.

The Standing Laws of This Design

Two house laws govern every sheet in this family. This section states what they honestly mean for this craft — no more, and no less.

The Three Modes — set down November 21, 2026, under copyleft (CC BY-SA 4.0) — rule every spec that distributes, spaces, proportions, or flows. Golden = spread, never align: each new element into the biggest gap, at the golden angle 137.5077641°. Platinum = align exactly: equal, on the line, 1:1 — the right mode where alignment is the function. Diamond = the weave: align locally, spread globally. The full treatment lives on the Super Suit sheet (System 9) and on Sheet № 0 (§14). Here is this craft's own grading:

Golden — the absorption field

The one system on this sheet whose whole job is distribution. The field absorbs, spreads, and sheds energy — maneuver energy in a turn, crash energy in an impact, the air–water slam at the interface — across the whole envelope, never borne at one point. Its envelope also meets the air first and grades the pressure change gradually, which is the same spread at work. Where this craft distributes, it runs Golden.

Platinum — the instruments

Nothing in the craft itself runs on exact equality — its honest Platinum lives in the build order's first step. The observation kits are calibrated and time-synced: optical, infrared, radar, and acoustic records aligned exactly, 1:1 in time, because grading is impossible if the records don't line up. Where this design aligns, alignment is the function — and it aligns its evidence before it aligns anything else.

Diamond — the craft as a whole

Each system is fitted exactly where it belongs — the crew at the hull's center, the most field-protected point; sensing through the skin of a sealed hull — and one field spreads over all of it at once, doing three jobs. Local exactness woven under a global spread: the whole design runs Diamond.

And one refusal, printed at the same size as the gradings: propulsion carries no mode. Formed-energy propulsion is a shaped unknown — the box is drawn, the inside of the box is not — and a mode cannot be graded on a mechanism that does not exist yet. Characteristics 01 and 04 ride on that drive, so they inherit the refusal: no distribution, spacing, or flow can be stated for them until the how falls. Nothing else on this sheet carries a mode either, and that is printed on purpose. The report shelf is graded evidence, not an arrangement. The characteristics are a target list, not a spacing. The hull's smooth shape is geometry, not an arrangement. The build order is a sequence, not a spacing. The modes' own law governs the modes too: use it when it works — don't force it.

Adapt and overcome — the second standing law, and a top law: learn your lessons and grow. This design is built to take in new knowledge and get better, and the sheet is already shaped for it: the build order starts with instruments because everything they capture makes the next version of this sheet truer; negative results are published at the same size as positive ones, and they revise the sheet too; and anyone who closes an open how — from the Missing door or anywhere else — improves the blueprint for everybody. The law names the whole of it: today's sheet is the floor, not the ceiling. When better knowledge arrives, the design is revised to fit it in: fitted when verified better, never forced, and never a from-scratch rebuild of what already works.

§ 01

The Report Shelf

What is actually documented, graded as what it is. A report being official makes the report real. It does not, by itself, make the reported object understood — most cases on this shelf end at “unexplained,” and unexplained is where this blueprint starts, not where it cheats.

The ODNI Preliminary Assessment

Office of the Director of National Intelligence · June 2021 · Unclassified

The U.S. government's public UAP assessment: 144 reports from U.S. government sources, mostly military aviators, covering 2004–2021. Of those, one was explained with high confidence (a large balloon). The rest were not explained — the report says the data were too limited to conclude, and that some objects appeared to show unusual flight characteristics.

Documented The document exists and says this. Graded as report It grades sightings, not craft.

The Nimitz Encounter

USS Nimitz carrier group · November 2004 · Pacific, off San Diego

Navy radar operators tracked objects descending from high altitude and holding position; F/A-18 pilot Commander David Fravor visually encountered a smooth, wingless white object — the “Tic Tac” — that mirrored his movements and departed at extreme speed. A second crew recorded it on infrared camera. The Navy later confirmed the released videos are genuine Navy footage of unidentified aerial phenomena.

Documented Testimony, radar accounts, and Navy-confirmed video. Graded as report What the object was remains officially unexplained.

The Roosevelt Encounters — “Gimbal” & “GoFast”

USS Theodore Roosevelt carrier group · 2014–2015 · U.S. East Coast

After a radar upgrade, Navy aircrews reported near-daily encounters with objects showing no visible wings or exhaust, some appearing to rotate or move against the wind. The infrared videos known as Gimbal and GoFast come from these patrols and were officially released by the Department of Defense in 2020.

Documented DoD-released footage and pilot testimony. Graded as report Later analyses argue over sensor effects — that argument itself is part of the record, and this shelf keeps it.

AARO & the NASA Study

All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (est. 2022) · NASA Independent Study, September 2023

The Pentagon's standing office for these reports has reviewed hundreds of cases; its historical review found no verifiable evidence of recovered off-world technology — a finding this page prints at full size, because the shelf holds both directions. NASA's independent study reached a matching practical conclusion: the data are too poor to settle the question, and the fix is better instruments and better data.

Documented Both reports are public. Not claimed Neither report — and nothing on this shelf — proves a craft, a builder, or an origin.

How to grade everything on this shelf: Documented = the report, video, or testimony verifiably exists. Reported = someone credible reported it; the report is the fact, the object is not yet. Open how = the gap between the report and anything we can build. If a sentence on this page ever sounds stronger than its tag, the tag wins.

§ 02

The Characteristics

Five characteristics recur across the report record. Each one gets the same four-part treatment: what was reported, what it would take, this design's candidate answer, and the open how that remains.

01

No visible means of propulsion or lift

No wings, no rotors, no exhaust plume, no engine sound reported at close range.

What was reported

Smooth objects — the Tic Tac, the Gimbal object — with no wings, no tail, no visible engine, no exhaust trail, maneuvering in ways wings and jets explain poorly. Pilots at close range reported no propulsion signature at all.

What it would take

Thrust that doesn't come from pushing air or exhaust backward in any visible way — no intake, no nozzle, no downwash a witness would feel. Every propulsion system we fly today announces itself. A silent one is a different kind of machine.

The design's candidate answer

Formed-energy propulsion: the craft shapes and directs energy fields around its hull, pushing against the field geometry rather than against air or expelled mass. On paper, that gives thrust with nothing to see, hear, or photograph — which is exactly why it's the candidate.

The open how  Open how — the headline

Nobody here knows how the reported craft are propelled — and this blueprint says so at full size. Formed-energy propulsion is a labeled candidate, not a mechanism. No working version exists in this design or, publicly, anywhere. This is the biggest open how on the page, and it is not blurred, shrunk, or footnoted.

02

Sudden acceleration & instant direction change

Reported maneuvers that would destroy a human body — and a normal airframe.

What was reported

Objects reported to stop, reverse, and depart at accelerations radar operators and pilots describe as effectively instant. Run the numbers on the reported versions (try it below) and you get hundreds of g. A trained pilot blacks out near 9 g; airframes are built to single-digit g limits.

What it would take

Two separate survivals. The airframe must not tear itself apart — a materials and structure problem. And anything alive inside must not experience the maneuver as acceleration at all — because no body, suit, or seat survives hundreds of g applied to the body.

The design's candidate answer

The absorption field — the same field as the Suit, the Ship, the cars, and the bikes. The field that takes crash energy takes maneuver energy. The crew rides inside the field, not inside the g-forces: the field absorbs and spreads the maneuver's energy the way it absorbs an impact, so the cabin's occupants feel the turn as a ride, not as a wreck.

The open how  Open how

Capacity. How much energy can the field take, how fast can it take it, and does that scale to a full craft at full maneuver? The same open how the whole fleet carries — stated here at the same size, because a field that works for a bike crash and fails at 300 g is a partial answer, and partial answers get labeled partial.

Try the numbers yourself

Acceleration in g is just speed change ÷ time ÷ 9.81. Set the maneuver the reports describe and see what a body — and this design's field — would have to handle. The math is plain physics; no numbers here are invented.

≈ 102 g

A fighter pilot in a g-suit blacks out around 9 g. An airliner is built to about 2.5 g. At 102 g, no unprotected body survives — which is why this design never asks the body to take it. The field takes it, or the maneuver doesn't happen.

03

No sonic boom at reported speeds

Fast, by every account — and quiet in a way fast things are not.

What was reported

Objects tracked or seen moving at high speed with no sonic boom reported by the witnesses and crews in a position to hear one. Honest label: absence of a boom in a report is weaker evidence than a measurement — nobody had a microphone array waiting. It is graded as reported, not as measured.

What it would take

A sonic boom is the air's receipt for being shoved aside faster than it can move. Avoiding it means not shoving: the craft's passage has to be negotiated with the air continuously, not split by a wedge moving faster than sound.

The design's candidate answer

The same field that propels and protects does a third job: the hull's field envelope meets the air first, shaping the pressure change gradually ahead of the craft instead of letting the hull slam a shock front into existence. No shock front, no boom.

The open how  Open how

Whether a field envelope can grade a pressure change smoothly at every speed and altitude in the reports — and what it costs in energy to do it continuously — is unworked. It rides on the same propulsion and energy unknowns below.

04

Hover and high speed in the same object

Two flight regimes that, in our craft, take two different machines.

What was reported

Objects reported hovering motionless — against the wind, in the Gimbal account — and also departing or transiting at high speed, with no configuration change: no rotors tilting, no wings unfolding, no transition a witness could see.

What it would take

Our answers to this are compromises: helicopters hover and crawl; jets run fast and can't hover; tilt-rotors and jump jets do both by carrying two systems and paying for both in weight and complexity. One object doing both without visible machinery means the lift and the thrust come from the same source.

The design's candidate answer

That's the natural shape of formed-energy propulsion if it works: a field drive doesn't care which way is thrust. Point it up, you hover. Point it forward, you run. No transition because there is nothing to transition — one system, every direction.

The open how  Open how

This answer is only as real as Characteristic 01's. If the propulsion how stays open, this one stays open with it — the blueprint links them instead of pretending each characteristic gets its own separate miracle.

05

Transmedium — air to water without slowing

The characteristic that walks off this sheet and onto the USO blueprint.

What was reported

Reports — including accounts discussed in congressional UAP hearings — describe objects moving between air and water without slowing, splashing, or breaking up. Air and water differ in density by a factor of about 800; hitting water at air speed is, for any craft we build, hitting a wall.

What it would take

The interface moment is the killer: in one instant the medium around the hull gets ~800× denser. The hull must survive the slam, the propulsion must work in both media, and the transition energy has to go somewhere that isn't the crew.

The design's candidate answer

The absorption field takes the interface slam the way it takes a crash — it is, again, the same answer, because it is the same problem wearing a different medium. The field envelope also meets the water before the hull does, grading the density change the way it grades air pressure in Characteristic 03.

The open how  Open how

Field behavior across the interface is untested even on paper beyond this paragraph. The full treatment — hull, depth, pressure, the underwater half of the problem — lives on the companion sheet: The USO — Blueprint.

§ 03

Systems

Six systems. Two of them — the field and the drive — carry the whole design, and both carry open hows at full size. The other four are drawn conservatively, from technology whose principles are known, because a blueprint that invents everything teaches nothing.

1 · The Absorption Field Open how: capacity

The house system, shared across the whole fleet — Suit, Ship, cars, bikes, and this craft. It absorbs, spreads, and sheds incoming energy: crash energy on the ground, maneuver energy here, the air–water slam at the interface.

On this craft it wraps the hull as an envelope and fills the crew volume's boundary, so acceleration is something the field experiences and the crew doesn't. The field runs Golden — every load it takes is spread across the whole envelope, never borne at one point. (Standing Laws, above.)

2 · Formed-Energy Propulsion Open how: the mechanism itself

The candidate drive: energy formed and directed by the craft, thrust without expelled mass, exhaust, or moving aerodynamic surfaces. Silent and invisible in operation — matching the reports by construction, which is a reason to hold it as a candidate, not a conclusion.

Stated plainly: this system is a shaped unknown. The blueprint draws the box it would occupy and the jobs it would do. It does not draw the inside of the box, because nobody here can. No mode is graded for this system — a mode cannot be graded on a mechanism that does not exist yet. (Standing Laws, above.)

3 · Hull & Structure Open how: materials

A smooth, closed hull with no apertures the mission doesn't force — no intake, no nozzle, no canopy seam if sensing allows a sealed shell. Structure is drawn as a pressure-and-load shell first and an aerodynamic body second, because the field, not the shape, does the flying.

The reported maneuvers imply structural loads past our best public materials if the field doesn't carry them — so the hull is designed assuming the field does, and the materials question stays open either way.

4 · Energy Source Open how: at reported performance

The reported performance — hover indefinitely, dash at high speed, maneuver hard, cross media — implies an energy store far denser than batteries or fuel as we know them, or a way of drawing energy this design doesn't specify.

The blueprint sizes the bay and the cooling for “far beyond current stores” and writes the requirement honestly instead of naming a fantasy source.

5 · Sensing & Navigation Known principles

Drawn from what exists: multi-spectrum cameras, radar, lidar, inertial navigation, and sensor fusion of the kind modern aircraft already fly — extended, not invented. A sealed hull senses through its skin; the crew sees a fused picture, not a windshield.

This is also the system the build order starts with — because before this craft, we need instruments that can settle what the reports actually show.

6 · Crew & Life Support Known principles

A small crew volume at the hull's center — the most field-protected point in the craft — with closed-loop air and water on submarine principles. The crew is optional to the design: the same sheet flies uncrewed, and the build order crews it last, after everything else is proven.

Nobody rides inside hundreds of g. They ride inside the field, or they don't ride.

§ 04

Build Order

Instruments and measurement first. This is a blueprint for knowing before it is a blueprint for flying — because the honest state of the subject is that the reports outrun the data, and better data is buildable today, with today's technology, by anybody.

  1. Build the instruments

    Calibrated, multi-sensor observation kits — optical, infrared, radar, and acoustic recording together, time-synced, with the raw data kept. Deployed where reports recur. This step needs no new physics and no permission. It is the step NASA's study pointed at, and it is first because every later step depends on knowing what's actually up there. Calibration and time-sync are this sheet's Platinum: records aligned exactly, 1:1, because data that doesn't line up can't be graded. (Standing Laws, above.)

  2. Grade the data, publish the grading

    Every capture gets the shelf treatment: documented, reported, explained, unexplained — with sensor artifacts (the honest explanation for many famous videos) hunted first and hardest. What survives that grading, at instrument quality, is the real design brief for everything after.

  3. Prove the field small

    The absorption field's first flying job is not a craft — it's a test article. Small, uncrewed, instrumented: can a field envelope measurably take maneuver and impact energy, and how much, and how fast? The capacity question gets a number or the design stops here, honestly.

  4. Attack the propulsion how in the open

    Formed-energy propulsion is researched as what it is — an open problem, worked in public, negative results published at the same size as positive ones. If a mechanism emerges, it gets tested at bench scale before it gets drawn at craft scale.

  5. Fly it uncrewed, then — and only then — crew it

    The full characteristic set, demonstrated by an uncrewed article against instruments, is the gate. A crewed version is the last line of the build order, not the first sketch — the same discipline as the Ship blueprint: prove it small, crew it last.

Why instruments before airframes?Tap to open

Because the field's founding problem is data quality. The famous cases rest on a handful of sensors, partial tracks, and testimony — enough to justify looking, nowhere near enough to specify a machine. A blueprint that skips the measurement step and starts with the craft is building on the weakest part of the record.

Instruments reverse that. They are cheap next to a craft, they work whether the answer turns out exotic or mundane, and everything they capture makes the next version of this sheet truer.

Is this blueprint a claim that the reported craft are real?Tap to open

No — and the page is built so it can't be read as one. The reports are real documents. The characteristics are reported characteristics. The craft on this sheet is a design: ours, on paper, dated, with its unknowns labeled.

If the instrument step someday explains every report with ordinary causes, this blueprint still stands as what it always said it was — a design exercise against a hard set of requirements. Nothing on the sheet needs the reports to be craft for the sheet to be honest.

§ 05

The Open Hows Shelf

Every gap between the reports and a buildable machine, on one shelf, at full size. Per the stance on the face of this sheet: these are unknowns, not noes. They stay here, labeled, until work — anybody's work — takes them down.

Open how № 1 — the headline unknown

Propulsion

Nobody here knows how the reported craft are propelled, and this blueprint says so. Formed-energy propulsion is the design's candidate — a named direction, not a mechanism. Until this one falls, the craft on this sheet does not fly, and the sheet does not pretend otherwise.

Open how № 2

Energy source at the reported performance

Hover, dash, hard maneuver, and media-crossing in one sortie imply energy storage or generation beyond anything public. The requirement is written; the source is not invented.

Open how № 3

Materials under the reported stresses

If the field doesn't carry the maneuver loads, no public material survives them. How much the field carries — and what the hull must therefore be — is an unmeasured pair of questions.

Open how № 4

Whether the reports are even one kind of object

The deepest unknown on the shelf: the five characteristics are gathered from different decades, sensors, and witnesses. They may describe one class of craft — or several different things, some mundane, wearing one label. This design answers the combined set because that's the brief; the record hasn't proven the set belongs together.

Open how № 5

Field capacity, measured

The fleet-wide question, carried here too: how much energy the absorption field can take, how fast, and how it scales from a rider to a craft. Build Order step 3 exists to put a number on it.
