The Tellings

How a thing travels between people, what doesn’t travel at all, how sure we can honestly be, and why the words themselves bend. Four thoughts. One choice standing in the middle of them.

5 — Discernment

The choice, standing in the completed four. Weigh each telling against the facts. Don’t dismiss a story for bending. Don’t swallow it whole either.

Thought 1

The Tellings

A telling is how a thing travels from one person to another. There are three currents — and every one of them bends a little, even the honest ones.

1 — Tell it straight

The whole thing, as best you know it. The aim is accuracy. Even here, choosing what to include is already a small bend.

2 — Play along, even exaggerate

Entering the bit. When everybody knows it’s a bit, playing along is its own honesty — the exaggeration is the point, not a lie.

3 — Tell it how you remember it

Memory is a mixture, and it settles differently in everybody. “How I remember it” is not an exaggeration and not a claim on the whole record. It’s the shape the thing has in you.

Humoring is not the enemy of checking

Enter the telling first — that’s the humoring. The record settles what’s settleable later, in its own time, without making the teller wrong for remembering it the way it landed.

The standing example: the griffin

The old tellings say: lion’s body, eagle’s beak, out in the gold country, guarding the gold. Out in that same Central Asian gold country, the ground is full of Protoceratops — lion-sized, four-legged, with a great hooked beak, fossilizing in clusters with eggs and nests. Miners pulling gold from that ground would have kept meeting those beaked skeletons.

So “what they saw really was a giant griffin” can be true as told. They weren’t lying, and they weren’t even exaggerating. The bones were real, the gold was real, the beak was real. Only the assembly was theirs. A witness can be honest and still need the record to finish the sentence.

Two old words that started it

Euhemerism — the idea that the gods were mortals remembered upward: real people whose stories grew in the telling until they became myths.

Humorism — the old medicine of the body’s four humors (blood, phlegm, yellow bile, black bile). A completeness-is-four doctrine, two thousand years early: health was the balance of the four.

“Humor me” carries the whole journey in two words: humor meant fluid, then temperament, then this — flow with the person’s mixture, don’t grip against it, then tell them. Humoring is going with the flow of how a thing lives in someone, long enough to actually hear it.

Thought 2

The Facts

The fourth part — and the only part that doesn’t travel at all.

The bones are in the ground or they aren’t. The date adds up or it doesn’t. The facts don’t care how they were told, and they don’t change when the telling does. They are what’s still standing when every version of the story has finished talking.

That doesn’t mean we always have the facts. Often we don’t. Where the facts run out, we don’t stop — and we don’t pretend. We fill the blank honestly:

An educated guess — labeled as a guess

A guess standing on the four: built from the tellings we have, the facts we can check, and the percent we can honestly claim. It wears its label out loud. An honest label is not a weakness in the guess — it’s what makes the guess usable.

Honest labels, always

Say which shelf a thing sits on: fact (checked against the record), telling (how someone carries it), or guess (our best fill where the record runs out). Never blend shelves and call the blend a fact. Never make a claim bigger than its evidence — a simulation says it’s a simulation, a “would” stays a “would.” The claim is exactly as big as the evidence, no bigger. That is the whole trick, and it works everywhere.

Thought 3

The Percent

Until it is 100% we cannot be sure. “Sure” is a hundred-percent word — and the facts rarely hand out a hundred.

1%Still a door

One percent is a statement about how often — across a hundred tries, you’d expect it once. It says nothing about this try. The one time it lands, it lands at a hundred percent for the person standing there.

Improbable is not impossible

A low percent is a reason to weigh a guess lightly. It is never a reason to strike it from the list. Until the facts close a door, even the one-percent door is a door. An unknown is not a no.

Sure can change

Even a held hundred is a hundred on the information we have now. New information comes in, and sure is allowed to move — not because the old sure was a lie, but because it was standing on a smaller record. A sure that can’t change isn’t knowledge. It’s a grip.

Why we use grades, not verdicts

Nearly everything we actually work with lives below a hundred: strong, likely, possible, one percent. All real positions. None of them is sure. So honest instruments are built from grades and labels instead of verdicts — holds, mixed, fails, not enough evidence — stating the percent you’re standing on, and keeping open the door that percent leaves open. When the record grows, the position updates, and the correction goes on the record in the open. Nothing about that weakens the system. That is the system working.

Standing by your actions: the only thing anybody can use against a person is their actions. Stand by them in the open, and there is no gap for leverage to hold — nothing hidden, nothing denied, no difference between the story and the record.

Thought 4

The Words Themselves

Meaning lives in the mixture, not in the letters. The same sound can carry opposite charges — tone, timing, and who is saying it to whom decide.

Would
The model’s word

What a voice would draw. A claim about a thing that didn’t physically happen — honest because it says so.

Could
The open door

What could this mean for me? A possibility that stays a possibility, and leaves the choosing with you.

Wood
The stuff itself

The material that actually stands in the weather and remembers every year in a ring.

Woods make models, and models are made of wood. A model ship carved from wood. A model house built from wood. A model of a tree, made of the tree. The model made out of the thing it models is the most honest model there is — the material won’t let it lie about the material. The grain argues back.

Counterparts inside a single word

Fuck and thank sit almost in the same place in the mouth — same rhythm, same “you” at the end, a couple of sounds twisted at the front — and one is gratitude and the other is war. Between the right people, “fuck you” said laughing means thank you, and “thank you” said cold and flat can mean the opposite. The phrases trade places depending on the mixture they’re delivered in.

Fuck and love can be the same act with opposite charges. Nothing about the mechanics tells you which one it is. The difference is entirely in what’s inside it — the intent, the care, whether the other person is a someone or a something. The act is identical; the mixture decides. Everything has a counter — even, maybe especially, the four-letter ones.

The shorter the word, the more meanings it can carry

The short words are the old, busy ones — set, run, get carry hundreds of meanings each because they’ve been carrying freight for a thousand years. A long word like photosynthesis can only mean one thing; there’s no room in it for a second job. Abbreviations are where that road ends: squeeze a whole phrase into a few letters and the letters mean almost nothing on their own — the context has to carry everything.

All the way down to a number turned on its side

7 → ∧
Seven on its side is a caret

The mark for AND — in logic, ∧ literally means and. “There’s always an and, always more coming.” We are seven.

8 → ∞
Eight on its side is infinity

The and that never stops: this and this and this, the count never closing, every point joined to every other point.

&
Once the 27th letter

Children once recited “…X, Y, Z, and per se and.” Slurred over years, “and per se and” became the word ampersand. One mark whose whole job is joining.

Seven says there’s always another and coming. Infinity says the ands never run out.

For everybody now

The Decoding Method

Tonight’s decodes were test runs. The method itself is for everybody — the same instrument, in anyone’s hands. One carrier can hold several messages at once; grade each one separately.

  1. Grade every message by its typesName what kind of message it is before you judge what it says.
  2. Find the patternsLook for what repeats, what pairs, and what has a counter.
  3. Answer the four doorsSound — what it sounds like. Sight — what it looks like. Touch — what it feels / does. Language — what the words themselves carry.
  4. Test every candidate until one checks all the boxesDon’t stop at the first fit. Keep testing until a reading passes every door.
  5. Ties go to discernmentWhen two readings both check out, the fifth decides — weigh them against the facts, state your percent, and label your guess.

That is the Tellings instrument pointed at a message: tellings in, facts checked, percent stated, words weighed — and discernment making the call.
