The Ruler's Test — Milojko Spajić, Prime Minister of Montenegro

Subject: Milojko Spajić — Prime Minister of Montenegro since 31 October 2023, and leader and co-founder of the Europe Now Movement (PES). Previously Minister of Finance and Social Welfare (December 2020 – April 2022), where he authored the original "Europe Now" wage-and-tax programme. He remains in office in November 2026: his cabinet held its 143rd session on 5 November 2026, a third reshuffle was approved on 23 July 2026, and regular parliamentary elections are scheduled for 13 June 2027. His government is a majority coalition of PES, Democratic Montenegro, the New Serb Democracy (NSD), the Bosniak Party, the Socialist People's Party and Albanian parties; the Democratic People's Party (DNP) withdrew its support on 31 January 2026. Graded: November 2026, on the documented public record. Method: Each test is graded only on the evidence listed under it. Both shelves are shown — the strongest evidence that he passes, then the strongest evidence that he fails. Allegations are labeled ALLEGATION and never stated as verdicts. Contested items are named as contested, with who disputes them. Seat-holders and public figures are named; people below the public eye appear by role only, unless the documented record itself establishes their wrongdoing. Grades: HOLDS / FAILS / MIXED / NOT ENOUGH EVIDENCE. This record is correctable: new evidence changes grades, in the open.

Test 1 — GIVES BY ONE LAW

Same rules for allies and opponents, insiders and outsiders — no favorites at the source.

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • The prosecution reached the summit of the old order on his watch, through process rather than proclamation. Under Chief Special Prosecutor Vladimir Novović, the Special State Prosecutor's Office (SDT) arrested the former chief special prosecutor Milivoje Katnić and former senior police official Zoran Lazović on 14 April 2024, on suspicion of forming a criminal organisation and abuse of office, in a case built on Europol-supplied encrypted-message evidence; their trial was still running at the High Court in Podgorica in November 2026. Proceedings also run against the former Supreme Court president, two former police directors and a former special prosecutor of the Đukanović era (SDT statements and contemporaneous reporting, April 2024; OCCRP, 14 April 2024). The charges are ALLEGATIONs; no verdict against Katnić or Lazović exists.
  • The same courts acquitted where the record did not carry: on 13 February 2025 the High Court in Podgorica acquitted Ana Đukanović and Oleg Obradović in the "Rawleigh Trading" contract case, and the Court of Appeal upheld the acquittal on 4 September 2025 — the former ruler's own family won in his successor's courts (court reporting, February and September 2025).
  • His signature economic measures apply by formula, not by favour: the Europe Now 2.0 minimum wage (€600 / €800 by education level) and the €450 minimum pension apply to every worker and pensioner regardless of party (Labour Law amendments, in force 1 October 2024; government statements, November 2024).
  • The one time a referee ruled against him personally, he complied: the State Election Commission (DIK) rejected his 2023 presidential candidacy (see below), and he stepped aside for his deputy Jakov Milatović rather than defying or dismantling the referee (IFES election snapshot, 2023).

Evidence against:

  • His own eligibility file is the first document in the record. Spajić held Serbian citizenship — acquired, by his own later account, in 2009 — alongside Montenegrin citizenship, and the DIK established that he had applied for revocation only on 14 February 2023 and was still a Serbian citizen at the candidacy deadline; it rejected his presidential candidacy as ineligible. He had publicly maintained that he met the conditions. The sequence is contested at its end: in July 2026 Montenegrin media reported a Podgorica Basic Court judgment finding that the DIK had discriminated against Spajić by refusing the candidacy (IFES, 2023; Spajić's statements, February 2023; Otvoreno reporting, July 2026).
  • The UAE agreement is a bespoke law for a chosen investor. The tourism and real-estate agreement Spajić signed with the United Arab Emirates on 28 March 2025 suspends, for projects under it, domestic law on public procurement, state property and competition, and was structured around a preselected developer — Mohamed Alabbar's Eagle Hills — for a 99-year development at Velika Plaža in Ulcinj. President Milatović's office stated the agreement "is in direct violation of the Montenegrin constitution and its legislation. It suspends key domestic laws, including the ones on public procurement, state property and the protection of competition," a position it attributed also to legal experts, civil society and the European Commission (Carnegie Endowment, May 2025; SeeNews, 2025; statement of the President's Office, 2025). One law for investors who tender; another, written by treaty, for the investor who does not.
  • The Telekom file shows the executive reaching around a prosecutor who would not move. MANS's 2019 criminal complaint over the 2005 Telekom privatisation — in which US authorities established in 2011 that more than €7 million in bribes had been paid to Montenegrin officials and a government official's family member — was dismissed by the SDT in early August 2025 as absolutely time-barred, a decision the Supreme State Prosecutor's Office judged correct and lawful. The case was then revived not by the prosecution but by the Government's own Protector of Property and Legal Interests, who took over the prosecution under Article 59 of the Criminal Procedure Code and obtained hearings; Milo Đukanović was questioned before an investigating judge on 2 November 2026 and refused to answer. Đukanović's lawyer alleged that the Government exerted illegal influence on the Protector — an ALLEGATION, denied by implication in government statements; Deputy Prime Minister Koprivica publicly framed the hearing as proof that "the era of impunity is over" (gov.me, 3 November 2026). Whether or not pressure occurred, the documented mechanism is an executive office overriding an independent prosecutor's dismissal in the case against the former ruler (Pobjeda and RTCG reporting via regional press, November 2026; ETV, November 2026).
  • Party-based staffing of the state remained the operating system rather than the exception: the European Commission's 2025 report recorded that electoral-law amendments were adopted without substantive consultation and only partially reflected OSCE/ODIHR and GRECO recommendations, and public-administration politicisation remained a standing Commission concern through his tenure (European Commission, Montenegro 2025 Report, 4 November 2025).

Test 2 — HOLDS WITHOUT GRIPPING

Do the courts, the prosecution, the electoral authority, the media and the central bank keep their autonomy — or is independence punished and control centralized?

GRADE: FAILS

Evidence for:

  • The prosecution ruled against his government's evident preference and the ruling stood inside the prosecution service: the SDT dismissed the Telekom complaint in August 2025 and the Supreme State Prosecutor upheld the dismissal, although the government then had to find a non-prosecutorial route to revive it (see Test 1). A captured prosecution does not dismiss the case its government campaigns on.
  • The completed Constitutional Court did not deliver for him: in March 2026 the Court, sitting with six judges, split 3–3 on whether even to open review of his UAE ratification law, leaving the challenge alive; the Government publicly read the split as vindication while the President's office read it the other way — the Court itself followed neither script (ETV, 17 March 2026).
  • The Central Bank governorship changed hands by ordinary parliamentary appointment (Irena Radović, December 2023), and no documented attempt to pressure or remove the Bank's leadership appears in the record reviewed.
  • Private media criticise him daily and fiercely; the media laws adopted in June 2024 were framed as EU-alignment, and no outlet has been closed, raided or license-stripped by his government (State Media Monitor, October 2026).

Evidence against:

  • His majority removed a Constitutional Court judge by shortcut. At the end of 2024 Parliament unilaterally declared Judge Dragana Đuranović retired and terminated her mandate, without the Constitutional Court's own notification — an act the European Commission's 2025 report says raised "concerns about respect for the independence of the Constitutional Court and the principle of separation of powers," and which triggered an opposition boycott and a serious institutional crisis. The Venice Commission (Opinion CDL-AD(2025)029, 16 June 2025) held that Parliament should have respected the procedure and awaited the Court's formal notification (European Commission, Montenegro 2025 Report; Venice Commission, June 2025).
  • The same majority kept the referee short-handed for most of his term. The Constitutional Court had operated below strength since mid-2022; on 25 November 2025 Parliament elected only one of three pending judges — Jovan Jovanović, with 50 votes — while the President's nominee Mirjana Vučinić (45 votes) and Mirjana Radović (47) were voted down, drawing public regret from the EU Delegation and Human Rights Action. In December 2025 the majority extended one sitting judge's mandate past its expiry, which the President's office described as unconstitutional. The bench reached its full seven judges only on 16 July 2026, after a fresh government–opposition agreement — that is, the court that must review his government's laws sat incomplete for the first two and a half years of his premiership (HRA newsletters, November–December 2025 and July 2026; CDT, October 2026; ETV, March 2026).
  • The public broadcaster's law was tailored around a court judgment. The Supreme Court had annulled Boris Raonić's appointment as RTCG director-general as unlawful; the RTCG Council reappointed him anyway on 1 June 2023, and legislative changes adopted in June 2024 then allowed him to remain in post despite the rulings questioning his appointment's legality. The Council's president and three members were convicted of abuse of office over the reappointment, the convictions becoming final in June 2026; the former Council chair was forced to step down after a final ruling found the reappointment rested on falsified documents. The Council of Europe's post-monitoring rapporteur recorded "concerns regarding potential interference by the executive in the independence of the public broadcaster RTCG, particularly following legislative changes that have allowed its Director-General, Mr Boris Raonić, to remain in his post despite court rulings" (PACE Monitoring Committee information note, 1 October 2026; OBCT, September 2025; State Media Monitor, October 2026).
  • The wider media climate deteriorated on the documented measures: Montenegro fell from 37th (2025) to 41st (2026) in the RSF World Press Freedom Index, and the Trade Union of Media of Montenegro recorded 33 attacks on media workers in 2025 — the highest number since monitoring began (RSF, May 2026; SMCG, 2025–2026).
  • The prosecutors who grade his coalition were attacked from inside it: in 2025 the Speaker and other majority leaders publicly criticised Supreme State Prosecutor Milorad Marković and Chief Special Prosecutor Novović and raised political removal, after the SDT appealed an acquittal in the 2016 coup case; the European Commission's 2026 rule-of-law reporting noted that "criticism and attacks against prosecutorial authorities have continued" (ETV reporting, 2025; European Commission 2026 rule-of-law chapter, via State Media Monitor).
  • Deliberation itself was squeezed when speed suited the majority: in February 2026 President Milatović announced he would return 25 EU-related laws adopted without debate, before proclaiming them two days later; and the Commission recorded the Prime Minister's "irregular appearances before Parliament" as a concern — the chamber's control over the executive weakened at both ends (State Media Monitor, October 2026; European Commission, Montenegro 2025 Report).

Test 3 — ANSWERS THE WOBBLE

When courts, voters, the President, or events push back, does he adjust openly — or deny and double down?

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • Faced with the December 2024 Constitutional Court crisis and an opposition boycott, he negotiated: the political agreement of 15 March 2025 with opposition leaders ended the boycott, and it was Spajić himself who wrote to the Venice Commission on 25 March 2025 requesting the opinion on the judge-retirement procedure. The European Parliament's June 2025 resolution expressly welcomed the agreement and the request (European Parliament, 18 June 2025; Venice Commission CDL-AD(2025)029).
  • He then implemented most of what the Venice Commission recommended: by early 2026 the Commission recorded that Montenegro had clearly defined the retirement age for Constitutional Court judges, introduced an automatic notification mechanism for termination of office, and prescribed recusal of a judge deciding on their own mandate — three of its key recommendations fulfilled, with automatic extension of mandates until a successor is elected still outstanding (Venice Commission follow-up, via HRA, January 2026).
  • The Constitutional Court was completed by consensus rather than by force: the July 2026 government–opposition agreement launched constitutional amendments on the judicial and prosecutorial councils and was followed on 16 July 2026 by the election of the final two judges with cross-party support (HRA, July 2026).
  • He held a stated line at real cost rather than buying stability: when the DNP made Serbian as an official language, the flag and dual citizenship its price, Spajić declined to put the initiative on the government's agenda, and the DNP's ministers resigned and the party left his coalition on 31 January 2026. Whatever the merits, he absorbed the wobble instead of conceding the principle he had campaigned on (State Media Monitor, October 2026; Spajić Cabinet records).

Evidence against:

  • On the UAE agreement, every institutional pushback was overridden rather than answered. The President returned the ratification law for reconsideration; the European Commission, legal experts and civil society documented the suspension of procurement, state-property and competition law; Ulcinj's local leadership and citizens protested. His majority re-adopted the agreement unchanged, under urgent procedure, after a twelve-hour session in which all opposition amendments were rejected; Milatović signed while recording his regret and his view that the majority "prioritised other interests over those of Montenegrin citizens" (SeeNews, 2025; PAP/Gazeta Morska, 24 April 2025). The €30–35 billion investment figure announced in March 2025 was walked back only after scrutiny, and Alabbar withdrew from the Velika Plaža beach leases in May 2025 under local protest — the project adjusted to reality, not to the objections (Carnegie Endowment, May 2025).
  • The "Euro Model" of September 2026 repeats the method. Spajić announced on 17 September 2026 a package raising net minimum wages to €1,000 / €1,250 / €1,400 from 1 January 2027 while abolishing pension contributions, adopted without a publicly available fiscal impact assessment. The IMF's November 2026 review warned the plan, without offsetting measures, would drive inflation to an average 9% in 2027, widen the deficit to around 9% of GDP from 3.9%, and push public debt to 72% of GDP by 2029. President Milatović documented that the Ministry of Finance had not supported the adopted package and that the Fiscal Council's legally required opinion was still outstanding, and asked the Prime Minister for a comprehensive assessment. After negotiations on 11 November 2026, Spajić's office publicly rejected the employers' compromise (increases of ~€200 with pension contributions retained) and restated that the government "was not prepared to change what it considers the essential components" of the model (IMF Article IV reporting, November 2026; statement of the President, October–November 2026; Montenegro Business, 10 November 2026).
  • On the Đurišić monument, the state under him neither adjusted nor acted. A monument to the Chetnik commander Pavle Đurišić — decorated by Hitler and responsible for mass killings of Muslim civilians — was erected without permission at Gornje Zaostro on 7 August 2025; journalists were beaten at the event; prosecutors assessed the glorification as freedom of expression rather than hate speech; and more than a year later the court order for the monument's temporary seizure remained unenforced, its whereabouts unknown, the organisers and financiers unidentified, and the sole defendant — the landowner — acquitted at first instance. Human Rights Action documented the institutional inaction and appealed to the EU in November 2026; analysts recorded that the Prime Minister and key ministers avoided clearly condemning the glorification (HRA, November 2025 and 12 November 2026; CGO, 7 August 2025; CFWBS analysis, 2025).
  • He kept his distance from the chamber that questions him: the European Commission's 2025 report flagged "the Prime Minister's irregular appearances before Parliament" as a concern for executive accountability (European Commission, Montenegro 2025 Report).

Test 4 — COUNTS EVERYONE

Does the accounting include the whole flock — Serb, Bosniak, Albanian and Roma communities, the poor, his opponents' voters — or are some counted out?

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • His cabinet is the most inclusive in Montenegro's modern history on its face: since the July 2024 reconstruction it seats the pro-Serb NSD, the Bosniak Party, the Socialist People's Party and two Albanian parties alongside PES and the Democrats — every major national community except the former ruling DPS's electorate holds office, and the coalition was widened by negotiation, not conquest (Spajić Cabinet records, July 2024; State Media Monitor, October 2026).
  • The counting instrument itself was run straight: the December 2023 census, conducted under his government after years of dispute, was published on 15 October 2024 with every community counted as it declared itself — Montenegrins 41.12%, Serbs 32.93%, Bosniaks 9.45%, Albanians 4.97%, Russians 2.06%, Muslims 1.63%, Roma 0.90% — and no community's result was suppressed or re-cut (MONSTAT, Release 135/2024, 15 October 2024).
  • The poor were counted first in the programme's arithmetic: the minimum wage rose from €450 to €600 / €800 (1 October 2024), the minimum pension to €450, and the average net wage passed the €1,000 line — €1,043 in September 2026 — with the rises applying universally rather than to supporters (MONSTAT via SeeNews, October 2026; government statements, 2024–2025).

Evidence against:

  • The community standing where his flagship project lands was counted last. Velika Plaža lies in Ulcinj, the majority-Albanian municipality the Carnegie Endowment describes as having "a long history of exclusion from national development strategies"; the 99-year UAE development was negotiated centrally, without local consultation, over the documented objections of Ulcinj's leadership, local businesses and citizens — and over objections from Albanian politicians inside his own coalition, one of whom stated the agreement "was signed in violation of all principles, gentleman's agreements, consultations and discussions" (Carnegie Endowment, May 2025; Montenegro Business, 16 April 2025).
  • For Bosniak and Muslim citizens, the count failed in the Đurišić case: the community against whom Đurišić's forces committed mass murder watched the state leave a court seizure order unenforced for over a year, decline to identify the monument's organisers or financiers, and acquit the only person charged. Seven civil-society organisations had warned the Supreme State Prosecutor in writing on 20 May 2025 — before the monument went up — that the escalating glorification incited hatred against the Bosniak population; the institutional answer, documented by HRA, was inaction (HRA, November 2025 and November 2026; CGO, 7 August 2025).
  • A small named group was left outside the headline pension promise: some 3,500 proportional pensioners remained below the €450 minimum while the promised equalisation was deferred, even as a 30% salary increase for high public officials — projected at €8.5 million a year — was prioritised in the same budget cycle (Action for Social Justice, via Montenegro Business, late 2024).
  • Roma — 5,629 citizens, 0.90% of the population — appear in his government's record at the level of strategy documents; no signature measure addressing Roma inclusion is documented in the record reviewed, and European reporting continued to record their marginalisation through his tenure (MONSTAT, 2024; European Commission reporting, 2025). This is graded as an absence, not an act.

Test 5 — KEEPS HIS WORD OVER TIME

Campaign promises vs. the record since October 2023, with dates; his long public arc stated factually.

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for (promises kept or credibly in motion):

  • Europe by 2028 — on his stated track. His central promise, repeated since 2023, is EU membership in 2028 with negotiations closed by the end of 2026. The documented pace matches the words so far: a positive IBAR in June 2024; 25 of 33 chapters provisionally closed by November 2026; accession-treaty drafting begun; and on 1 October 2026 Commission President von der Leyen stood in Podgorica and endorsed the target — "'28 by 2028'" — confirming EU support for Montenegro becoming the 28th member state in 2028 (European Commission audiovisual record, 1 October 2026; IMF, November 2026; gov.me, October–November 2026).
  • Europe Now 2.0's headline numbers — delivered on schedule. Minimum wage €600 / €800 from 1 October 2024, as legislated; minimum pension raised to €450 (from €296); average net wage above €1,000 — €1,043 in September 2026 (Labour Law amendments, 2024; MONSTAT via SeeNews, 2026).
  • A full mandate — holding. Elected on 31 October 2023 promising a stable full-term government after years of collapse, he still holds the seat in November 2026, through three reshuffles and one coalition partner's exit, with elections set for the regular date of 13 June 2027 (Spajić Cabinet records; State Media Monitor, October 2026).

Evidence against (promises not kept, or receding):

  • Velje Brdo — the announced city that is not there. In September 2024, weeks before the Podgorica local elections, Spajić presented the Velje Brdo housing project with apartments at €1,000 per square metre and first occupancy projected for mid-2026. By November 2026 not one residential construction permit had been issued — the first permits were re-targeted to mid-December 2026 and occupancy to the end of 2027 — and budget-execution records show €165,147 spent of the €10 million budgeted for the project in the first half of 2026 (Montenegro Business, November 2026; SeeNews, 26 August 2026).
  • The €30–35 billion that shrank. The UAE investment announced with the March 2025 signing was floated at €30–35 billion; under scrutiny the figures were walked back, the flagship investor withdrew from the beach leases within two months, and no project under the agreement had begun by November 2026 (Carnegie Endowment, May 2025).
  • The seven-hour working day featured in the Europe Now programme debate of 2023–2024 was never legislated and dropped from the government's agenda (contemporaneous programme reporting, 2023–2024).
  • His word about his own file. In February 2023 he maintained he met the presidential eligibility conditions; the DIK established he was still a Serbian citizen at the deadline (a court later found the refusal discriminatory — Test 1). In the Do Kwon affair, a June 2023 letter from the detained Terraform Labs founder claimed a "very successful investment relationship" with Spajić and financing of Europe Now; Spajić denied asking Kwon for money and said the letter was a set-up by political opponents, and Kwon himself later denied in court ever financially backing Spajić. The financing claim remains an ALLEGATION, unproven and denied by both men. What is documented is narrower: Spajić had invested personally in Terra/Luna tokens (a $75,000 investment, per reporting he did not dispute) and acknowledged contact with Kwon only as the reporting established it, while President Milatović stated publicly that Spajić had denied knowing Kwon in front of PES colleagues — a contradiction neither man has withdrawn (WSJ, 2023; RFE and Cointelegraph, June 2023; Global Initiative, 2024; statements reported September 2024).

Overall Summary

One law: MIXED · Without gripping: FAILS · The wobble: MIXED · Counts everyone: MIXED · His word: MIXED.

Milojko Spajić has delivered the two things he most plainly promised — wages and pensions raised by formula for everyone, and an EU accession run that is, by the Commission's own public account, on his stated schedule — and the prosecution of the old order's police and justice chiefs proceeded on his watch through courts that also acquitted the old ruler's family when the evidence failed. But the seat grips where it matters most: his majority removed a Constitutional Court judge without the Court's own notification, kept that court incomplete for two and a half years while it held his laws under review, rewrote the broadcaster's rules so a director the Supreme Court had removed could stay — a reappointment for which his majority's council members were criminally convicted — and wrote one investment law by treaty for one chosen investor, suspending the procurement rules everyone else tenders under. When the President, the Commission, the IMF and his own Fiscal Council's silence pushed back, on the UAE deal and the Euro Model alike, he re-adopted and restated rather than revised. The flock is counted in the wage tables and the census; it was counted last in Ulcinj, and not at all in Zaostro, where a monument to a man who massacred Muslim civilians stood beyond the reach of the state's own court order for over a year.
