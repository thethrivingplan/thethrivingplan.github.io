The Ruler's Test — Rashad al-Alimi, Chairman of the Presidential Leadership Council of Yemen

Subject: Rashad Mohammed al-Alimi — Chairman of the Presidential Leadership Council (PLC) since 7 April 2022. The seat graded is the chairmanship of the PLC, and the reason is stated plainly: this dossier grades the recognized government's seat. The PLC is the executive body of Yemen's internationally recognized government, formed on 7 April 2022 when President Abdrabbuh Mansur Hadi, in a televised declaration, dismissed his vice president, "irrevocably" delegated his full powers "in accordance with the constitution and the Gulf Initiative and its executive mechanism" to a new eight-member council, and named al-Alimi — a former interior minister under President Ali Abdullah Saleh and later a Hadi adviser — as its chairman (Reuters, 7 April 2022; Security Council Report, April 2022). The declaration vested the chairman personally with sweeping powers, including unilateral command of the military and the power to appoint governors and other key officials, and it tasked the Council with managing Yemen's political, military and security affairs and with "negotiating with Ansar Allah (the Houthis) for a permanent ceasefire… and a final and comprehensive political solution" (Security Council Report, April 2022). The mandate is therefore a transfer, not an election: no voter chose al-Alimi, the constitution does not explicitly establish such a council — Article 124 permits delegation of certain functions to a vice president, and Articles 115–116 prescribe resignation and succession procedures — and multiple Saudi and Yemeni officials alleged Hadi was pressed by Riyadh to cede power, an ALLEGATION recorded here as contested, not as a finding (PLC record summaries, 2022–2026). The chairmanship has NOT changed hands: al-Alimi was still chairman in November 2026, receiving ambassadors' credentials, swearing in ministers on 4 November 2026, and chairing PLC meetings that month (SABA via Yemen Ministry of Foreign Affairs, November 2026). He is graded on the full tenure, April 2022 to November 2026 — four years and seven months, the longest record in this batch — not on a fragment of it. The parallel rule of the Sana'a authorities (the Houthi movement, Ansar Allah, whose Supreme Political Council is chaired by Mahdi al-Mashat and is recognized only by Iran) is NOT graded as the seat; it is noted where it bears on the tests, above all Test 4, because the majority of Yemenis live under Sana'a's rule and a seat that claims the whole country is graded on whether it counts them. Three prime ministers have served under his chairmanship in the graded period's second half alone — Ahmed Awad bin Mubarak (5 February 2024 – 3 May 2025), Salem Saleh bin Breik (3 May 2025 – 15 January 2026), and Shaya Mohsen al-Zindani (appointed 15 January 2026, cabinet announced 6 February 2026) — and their records are the seat's record where the chairman appointed, blocked, or removed them (Reuters, 15 January 2026; SABA via Kuwait Government Online, 16 January 2026). Graded: November 2026, on the documented public record. Method: The ethic of the Test, printed verbatim: "How you play the game matters. Play by the rules. Stay true to your word. And if another option becomes available, at least consider it." Each test is graded only on the evidence listed under it. Both shelves are shown — the strongest evidence that he passes, then the strongest evidence that he fails. Allegations are labeled ALLEGATION and never stated as verdicts. Contested items are named as contested, with who disputes them. Conflicting counts are printed as separate sourced figures, never blended. The mandate rule applies: governing on the platform a ruler was seated to carry out is not itself a failure; the Test grades the holding, never the platform, and carrying out the seated mandate is evidence on the FOR shelf of Test 5. Seat-holders and public figures are named; people below the public eye appear by role only, unless the documented record itself establishes their wrongdoing. Grades: HOLDS / FAILS / MIXED / NOT ENOUGH EVIDENCE. This record is correctable: new evidence changes grades, in the open.

Test 1 — GIVES BY ONE LAW

Same rules for allies and opponents, insiders and outsiders — no favorites at the source.

GRADE: FAILS

Evidence for:

  • The single clearest one-law act of the tenure was aimed at an insider, not an enemy. On 7 January 2026, after Southern Transitional Council (STC) president Aidarous al-Zubaidi — himself a PLC member — failed to board the plane to Riyadh for talks and, on the Saudi-led coalition's account, fled after distributing weapons in Aden, the PLC under al-Alimi revoked al-Zubaidi's council membership and referred him to the attorney general on charges including high treason, harming Yemen's political and economic standing, obstructing state efforts against the coup, and inciting internal strife; the same meeting dismissed Transport Minister Abdulsalam Humaid and Planning Minister Waed Badhib and referred them for investigation (PLC emergency-meeting record via Yemeni and regional reporting, 7 January 2026; International Crisis Group CrisisWatch, January 2026). The guilt of al-Zubaidi and of the dismissed ministers is an ALLEGATION until a court rules; what is documented is that the referral machinery was used against the chairman's own council colleague. Four PLC members had already resolved in December 2025 to suspend any public official who supported southern secession — a rule stated generally, before it was applied (cabinet record summaries, December 2025).
  • The 2025 economic reform program was, in its design, a one-law instrument: a national committee to regulate and finance imports, tighter supervision of exchange companies, closure of non-compliant exchange outlets, relocation of commercial banks' headquarters to Aden so the Central Bank could supervise them, and directives requiring governorates and agencies to transfer revenues to the government's account at the Central Bank in Aden rather than holding them locally (Central Bank of Yemen–Aden measures via FAO and Yemen & Gulf Center for Studies, August 2025; Studies and Economic Media Center bulletin, November 2025). Applied across areas, the package coincided with the rial's recovery from roughly 2,837 per US dollar in July 2025 to about 1,632 in August 2025 (ACAPS data cited in South24 Center study, 2026).
  • Al-Alimi's own public account claims the anti-corruption and oversight machinery was reactivated for all: in his Eid al-Fitr address of 29 March 2025 he stated the PLC had "reactivated state institutions, particularly the judiciary, law enforcement agencies, and anti-corruption bodies" in the liberated governorates (presidentialalimi.net, 29 March 2025). The claim is recorded as his claim; the shelf below tests it.

Evidence against:

  • The seat itself was built as a quota, not as one law. The PLC's eight chairs were allocated to faction leaders — the STC president, the Giants Brigades commander, the National Resistance leader, the governor of Marib, the governor of Hadramawt, and others — each retaining his own armed force and his own external patron, which is why analysts across the spectrum describe the body it produced as paralyzed by the divisions it was meant to solve (International Crisis Group and Sana'a Center assessments, 2022–2026; Yemen International Forum III report, Sana'a Center, 2025: three years after formation the PLC "is widely regarded as having failed to meet the expectations of the Yemeni people," with "competing agendas" and "widespread corruption that has severely undermined public and donor confidence"). A council in which each member commands a separate militia is, structurally, eight laws.
  • Revenue law did not run evenly across the government's own territory. The November 2025 economic bulletin records that directives to close unofficial ports and remit revenues to the Central Bank in Aden were still being issued as correctives — because agencies and governorates had not been remitting — and that government revenues covered less than 30 percent of the wage bill (Studies and Economic Media Center, November 2025). The Sana'a Center's reform assessment records the same fragmentation from inside: the head of the State Land Authority, Salem Thabet al-Awlaki, resigned in autumn 2025 protesting obstruction of his efforts to register state property and dismiss officials accused of corruption, which the Center reads as showing "the persistent issue of quota-based politics and the absence of political will to support governance and anti-corruption policies" (Sana'a Center, The Yemen Review, July–September 2025).
  • The prime minister's attempt to apply one cabinet law was blocked by the chairman's council. Bin Mubarak resigned on 3 May 2025 saying he had been unable to exercise his constitutional authority to overhaul the cabinet; the Sana'a Center records that he clashed with al-Alimi over appointing 12 new ministers, that on 7 March 2025 only three ministers attended a cabinet meeting he called while 21 boycotted, that only 16 regular cabinet meetings were held in his entire tenure, and that PLC members representing armed factions "refused to abide by the prime minister's instructions, as the PLC had not codified its bylaws" (Sana'a Center, "A New Prime Minister is Not the Answer," 2025; The Yemen Review, April–June 2025). His predecessor Maeen Abdelmalek Saeed had likewise been prevented from appointing ministers of his choice — the pattern predates and postdates any one premier.
  • The outcome measure sits at the bottom of the world table for the whole tenure: Yemen scored 13 of 100 on Transparency International's Corruption Perceptions Index 2025 (177th of 182, released February 2026), unchanged at 13 from 2024 and down from 16 in 2022–2023, the year the PLC was seated (Transparency International, CPI 2022–2025).

Why: The grade lands on FAILS because the exception is documented and the rule is not. One insider — al-Zubaidi — was referred to a prosecutor, and one reform package was written generally; but the operating system of the seat for four years was allocation by faction: separate militias, separate revenues, ministers a prime minister could not appoint or remove, and an anti-corruption official resigning because his own side obstructed him. That is a second law for insiders as the standing arrangement, not as an episode.

Helped / Hurt ledger:

  • Helped: Importers and consumers in government areas who benefited briefly from uniform import-financing and exchange rules in mid-2025 (food-basket cost fell from about 199,000 rials in July to about 129,000 in September 2025, South24-cited figures); the public record itself, by the January 2026 demonstration that a council member could be referred for prosecution.
  • Hurt: Prime Minister Bin Mubarak, whose constitutional appointment power was nullified in practice; state-land and anti-corruption officials whose work was obstructed (role-named in the record; the resigning authority head, Salem Thabet al-Awlaki, is named as a public office-holder); and ordinary salary-earners in government areas, who bore a revenue system in which what reached the central bank covered under 30 percent of the wage bill (SEMC, November 2025).

Test 2 — HOLDS WITHOUT GRIPPING

Do the courts, the electoral commission, the media, the prosecution, and the central bank keep their autonomy — or is independence punished and control centralized?

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • The Central Bank in Aden was publicly defended as independent by the chairman himself, and acted in its own voice. Returning to Aden in September 2025, al-Alimi praised the reform results as the product of institutional harmony and committed the PLC "to enhance the independence of the central bank in managing monetary policy" (SABA via Arabian Sea, September 2025). The bank's 2025 measures — closing non-compliant exchangers, running the import-financing committee that covered nearly USD 2 billion of imports over about three months through non-inflationary sources — were central-bank operations, publicly attributed to the bank and its governor (SEMC, November 2025; FAO reporting, 2025).
  • Critical monitors publish against his government, from inside its territory, and are answered rather than silenced on the documented record: Mwatana for Human Rights, based in Yemen, published in 2026 a detailed indictment of detention practice in PLC areas addressed directly to the PLC (Mwatana, 2026), and the Sana'a Center publishes running assessments calling the PLC a failure without documented reprisal against the Center. A draft package of laws was reported in November 2026 as prepared with Parliament Speaker Sultan al-Barkani and the judiciary — parliament and courts working as separate institutions on legislation, on the record as reported (social-media report of the draft-law package, 16 November 2026 — a thin source, recorded as such).
  • No election has fallen due under the PLC's transitional mandate, so no electoral commission has been gripped or spared on this record; the absence is stated rather than filled.

Evidence against:

  • The chairman's personal grip is written into the seat's founding instrument: the 7 April 2022 declaration vests the chairman with the power to unilaterally command the military and appoint governors and key officials (PLC record summaries of the declaration text). He has used appointment power at the top of every referee institution's neighborhood: he appoints and accepts the resignations of prime ministers (Bin Breik, January 2026, by his signed decree), appoints governors who swear their oaths before him personally (the governors of Lahj, Abyan and al-Dhale in March 2026; the governor of al-Jawf on 1 November 2026), and reshuffles cabinets by republican decree (Decree 74, 2 November 2026) (SABA records, 2026).
  • The executive reached into the security referees directly in January 2026: on 5 January 2026 al-Alimi announced two decisions merging the Political Security and National Security agencies and various intelligence departments into a new Central State Security Agency and creating a Counter-Terrorism Agency — a restructuring of the detention apparatus by presidential decision, whose promised safeguard (bringing facilities under the Public Prosecution and the judiciary) Mwatana documents, six months on, as not delivered: enforced disappearance, arbitrary detention and torture "continue to occur on a large scale in Aden and other areas under the control of factions aligned with the Presidential Leadership Council," with no significant improvement in the rule of law (Mwatana for Human Rights, July 2026). Mwatana's cumulative documented count for areas under the internationally recognized government, Islah-loyal authorities in Marib and Taiz, and the STC is over 1,225 cases of arbitrary detention, 553 of enforced disappearance, and 357 of torture or other cruel treatment over the conflict years (Mwatana, 2026) — a count that spans forces the chairman commands on paper and does not control in fact, which is itself the finding.
  • Media and civic space narrowed in his areas on independent monitors' records. Human Rights Watch's World Report 2026 finds that in 2025 "the Houthis, the UAE-backed Southern Transitional Council (STC), and the Yemeni government arbitrarily detained and forcibly disappeared people in areas they controlled, including journalists and human rights defenders," and its August 2026 briefing repeats that the Houthis, the STC and the Yemeni government "continue to detain journalists and restrict civic space in the areas under their control, including suppressing peaceful protest" (Human Rights Watch, World Report 2026; HRW briefing, 26 August 2026). Journalist Mohammed Aydah was killed in Mukalla on 24 June 2026 by an explosive device in his car; no perpetrator is established in the record assembled here and the killing is recorded unattributed (HRW, August 2026).
  • The referees he does not grip are gripped by others, from inside his own council. Until January 2026 the STC ran a parallel security authority across the south; Saudi Arabia then moved, 11–14 January 2026, "to impose direct military oversight over southern Yemen," with Saudi officials working to integrate all military and security units into the PLC command structure (International Crisis Group CrisisWatch, January 2026). A chairman whose referees are held by a factional colleague on one side and a foreign patron on the other is not holding them loosely; he is not holding them.

Why: MIXED, because both movements are documented at once. The central bank was strengthened and publicly committed to independence, and Yemen's own monitors still publish against him by name — those are real. Against them stand a founding grant of unilateral military and appointment power, a security merger decreed from the chair whose judicial safeguards did not arrive, and HRW's finding that his government is one of the three authorities detaining journalists in its areas. The grip is partial because the state is fragmented, not because the hand is open.

Helped / Hurt ledger:

  • Helped: The Central Bank of Yemen–Aden as an institution, given public political cover for unpopular enforcement in 2025; Yemeni human-rights and research organizations (Mwatana, the Sana'a Center), which continue to operate and publish.
  • Hurt: Detainees and the disappeared in PLC-aligned areas and their families — Mwatana's documented 1,225 arbitrary-detention and 553 disappearance cases in government/STC/Islah areas over the conflict (not all within this tenure, and the dossier does not claim otherwise); journalists working in government areas, on HRW's 2025–2026 findings; and Prime Minister Bin Mubarak again, whose March 2025 cabinet meeting was boycotted into paralysis without documented consequence for the boycotters.

Test 3 — ANSWERS THE WOBBLE

When courts, voters, parliament, the street, foreign partners, or events push back, does he adjust openly — or deny and double down?

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • When the street pushed, in the largest protest wave in his territory, the first response included adjustment. From May 2025, thousands of women — the "Women's Revolution" — protested across Aden, Lahj, Abyan and Taiz demanding electricity, water, salaries, healthcare and prosecution of the corrupt, against power cuts exceeding 20 hours a day (Peace Track Initiative / Women Solidarity Network statement to the UN Human Rights Council, 25 May 2025). In Aden and Lahj the authorities increased fuel supply to power stations to provide extra hours of electricity (International Viewpoint report, June 2025), and the National Women's Committee's appeal to al-Alimi to answer the demands was carried publicly by Yemeni media (Yemen Monitor, May 2025). The adjustment was partial and temporary; it was also real, and it is recorded before the shelf against.
  • When his own coalition broke apart, he took the dialogue door that was open. After the STC's December 2025 seizure of Hadramawt and al-Mahra, the PLC on 3 January 2026 requested that Saudi Arabia host an "inclusive southern conference," and southern leaders including senior STC figures were invited to Riyadh (International Crisis Group CrisisWatch, January 2026). Al-Zubaidi refused the invitation and fled; the conference route was offered to him first, which matters for this test: the option of talks was extended to the man who had just seized two governorates, and the refusal was his.
  • When another option became available in the detainee file, he took it even mid-escalation: on 14 May 2026, after 14 weeks of UN-observed negotiations in Amman building on December 2025 consultations in Muscat, the government and the Houthis agreed to release more than 1,600 conflict-related detainees — the largest such deal of the war — with the government's delegation stating the releases included politicians and media professionals held for years (UN Special Envoy Hans Grundberg's announcement, 14 May 2026; Associated Press, 14 May 2026). The figures are printed separately because the sources give them separately: "more than 1,600" in total (UN/AP); 1,100 Houthi-affiliated detainees to be released by the government side and 580 by the Houthis, including seven Saudis and 20 Sudanese, on the Houthi negotiator's account; and "nearly 1,728" on the government delegation head's account (AP, 14 May 2026). Implementation was ICRC-facilitated and its completion is not documented in the record assembled here.
  • The founding mandate's peace track was pursued while it existed: the PLC welcomed renewal of the UN-brokered truce in 2022 and conditioned extension on opening roads and lifting the siege of Taiz (Xinhua reporting, September 2022), and the parties committed in December 2023 to the UN roadmap — nationwide ceasefire, payment of public salaries, resumed oil exports, reopened roads, Sana'a airport and Hodeidah port — before it stalled amid the Red Sea escalation (UN Special Envoy's roadmap announcement, 23 December 2023).

Evidence against:

  • The same protest wave that drew extra fuel also drew force. During the 24 May 2025 demonstration in Aden, militiamen blocked the roads to the rally site and female militia members were deployed who "violently assaulted" demonstrators; roadblocks remained the next day and bus drivers were barred from carrying female passengers (International Viewpoint report, June 2025). The Peace Track Initiative statement records that political actors including the STC sought "to suppress or instrumentalize women-led protests" (UN HRC statement, 25 May 2025). No documented order from al-Alimi either directed or punished the Aden response; the shelf records what happened in his government's capital to citizens answering his government's failures.
  • The largest wobble of the tenure — the Houthi offensive of August–September 2026, which took Mokha and Dhubab, reached the Bab el-Mandeb, seized al-Turbah on the Taiz–Aden road on 4 October and by 2 November had advanced to within roughly 80 kilometres of Aden — was answered with a maximal claim rather than an adjusted one. On 4 October 2026 al-Alimi announced an all-out campaign; on 5 October Operation Dawn of Yemen began, with the stated objective of reclaiming all Houthi-held territory "including the capital Sanaa" (International Crisis Group Q&A, October 2026; FDD analysis, October 2026). Weeks later the documented position was not Sanaa but a Houthi advance toward Aden, and the Horn Review's assessment records that the offensive "unfolded alongside continued Houthi pressure on multiple fronts" and exposed the limits of translating battlefield activity into state authority (Horn Review, 7 November 2026). The PLC's own tallies — 40,761 strikes and about 20,000 Houthis "neutralised" in the month from 5 October, per its 5 November statement — are printed as the PLC's claims, explicitly not independently verified (PLC statement via Geopolitiki, 5 November 2026). A separate PLC figure, 12,000 Houthi militants killed or wounded, appears in the operation's summary record; the two PLC figures conflict with each other and are printed separately, not blended.
  • The UN's warning at the escalation was not taken up on the documented record: on 9 October 2026 Special Envoy Grundberg warned Yemen had returned to full-scale war and that military developments could not replace negotiations (Horn Review, 7 November 2026). No documented PLC return to the roadmap track followed in October–November 2026; instead the Council "reaffirmed commitment to continue Fajr al-Yemen [Dawn of Yemen] until all objectives are achieved" (SABA, November 2026).
  • The no-good-choice clause, applied by its mechanics: the October 2026 decision itself is NOT held against him under this test, and the dossier says why in the clause's own terms. The options that actually existed after the Houthi seizure of the Bab el-Mandeb coast and al-Turbah were: (1) no counteroffensive — accepting the loss of the strait coast, the encirclement of Taiz and a probable advance on Aden, the seat's own capital; or (2) a counteroffensive dependent on Saudi air power (about 100 Saudi aircraft on the opening day, FDD, October 2026), with the civilian harm that renewed full-scale war predictably carried (Test 4). Each carried real harm; no third door was open in that moment — the roadmap had been stalled since 2024 and the Houthis were advancing, not negotiating. He did not build that trap in October 2026: the offensive that created it was the Houthis', launched 6 August 2026. What remains gradable, and is graded above, is the honesty inside the trap — announcing Sanaa as the objective and publishing unverified tallies — and whether the least-harm door was taken in how the campaign was framed and claimed, not that a campaign was launched. The trap's deeper origin — a unified national army never built in 2022–2025, leaving the seat dependent on factional forces and Saudi aircraft — IS part of his record and is graded under Tests 1, 2 and 5.

Why: MIXED. He has repeatedly taken the open door when one existed — fuel for the protesters' cities, a conference invitation for the secessionist who had just seized two governorates, a record detainee deal signed in the middle of a war year. He has also answered the street with roadblocks in his own capital and answered strategic failure with maximal objectives and unverifiable numbers. Considering another option when it became available is documented; so is doubling down on the claim when the facts on the ground moved the other way.

Helped / Hurt ledger:

  • Helped: The more than 1,600 detainees named in the May 2026 agreement and their families, if and as the ICRC-facilitated releases proceeded (implementation not fully documented here); residents of Aden and Lahj who received additional electricity hours in May–June 2025; southern leaders who took the Riyadh conference route in January 2026 and kept a political rather than military file.
  • Hurt: Women demonstrators assaulted and obstructed in Aden on 24–25 May 2025 (role-only; no individual victim is named in the sources); civilians on all fronts after October 2026, whose exposure followed from both the Houthi offensive and the maximal framing of the response (figures in Test 4); and the public's ability to judge the war, hurt by official tallies that conflict with each other (20,000 "neutralised" vs. 12,000 killed or wounded, both PLC-sourced).

Test 4 — COUNTS EVERYONE

Does the accounting include the north and the south, the displaced, the marginalized, women, and the people who live under the other authority — or are some counted out?

GRADE: FAILS

Evidence for:

  • The seat's claim, and some of its practice, runs to the whole country. The PLC's November 2026 statements pledge to reclaim "their decision-making" for "the Yemeni people" as a whole and to assert sovereignty "over the entire national territory" (SABA, 5 November 2026). The May 2026 detainee agreement counted detainees on both sides of the line, including men held by his own government (UN, May 2026). In September 2026 al-Alimi launched a package of relief projects through the King Salman Center directed at Taiz's resilience and "those affected across Yemen" (SABA, 27 September 2026).
  • Southerners were counted back in after January 2026 rather than collectively punished: the response to the STC's collapse was an inclusive southern conference, replacement PLC appointments to fill the vacated seats (Mahmoud al-Subaihi and Salem al-Khanbashi, appointed by decree 15 January 2026), and the reintegration of formerly STC-aligned elite units that pledged allegiance to the PLC (PLC record, January 2026; Arab Press reporting, 2026).
  • Women reached the cabinet's top table within the graded period's end: by November 2026 Yemen's foreign minister delivering the chairman's messages to European heads of government was Dr. Afrah al-Zouba (SABA via Yemen MFA, November 2026) — a documented change from the all-male cabinets recorded through 2025 (see against shelf).

Evidence against:

  • The whole-country numbers, printed as separate sourced figures and never blended: the UN's 2026 Humanitarian Needs and Response Plan (OCHA, March 2026) counts more than 22 million people needing humanitarian assistance — 22.3 million in the plan's summary figure used by UN agencies — including 10.95 million women and girls and 5.2 million internally displaced people; 18.3 million people acutely food insecure; over 2.2 million children under five acutely malnourished, including 516,157 with severe acute malnutrition, and 1.3 million pregnant and breastfeeding women malnourished; only 59.3 percent of health facilities fully functional (OCHA HNRP 2026). UNFPA's count for early 2026 puts 19.3 million people in urgent need of healthcare, with two of five facilities non-functional (UNFPA, January–March 2026). These figures cover both authorities' territories; they are the population the seat claims, and after four years of its tenure this is the counted condition of that population.
  • The majority who live under Sana'a were counted out in practice at the war's sharpest point. In July 2026, Yemeni government forces in coalition with Saudi Arabia attacked Sana'a airport — "the only airport providing international flights to and from Houthi-controlled territory, where the majority of Yemen's population lives, and a critical entry point for humanitarian personnel and aid" (Human Rights Watch briefing, 26 August 2026, which states the July 2026 attacks by parties "may be in violation of international humanitarian law" — a monitor's legal assessment, recorded as such). The Sana'a authorities' own conduct bears on this test from the other side and is noted, not graded: as of 2026 the Houthis arbitrarily detained at least 73 UN personnel and dozens of civil-society staff, some since 2021, with one WFP worker dying in Houthi custody on 11 February 2025 (HRW, 2026; UN reporting, 2026) — a fact that worsens the counted-out condition of the northern population without transferring its authorship to the graded seat.
  • The 2026 escalation's displacement fell on the counted and uncounted alike, in separate sourced counts: IOM figures reported by UNICEF record roughly 350,000 people uprooted between 7 June and 16 November 2026, including about 174,000 children (UNICEF via Global Issues, 20 November 2026); a UN News account of the western-coast escalation records more than 112,000 displaced in two weeks across Aden, Hudaydah, Abyan and Taiz, against a pre-existing displaced population of nearly 4.8 million on UNHCR's figure (UN News, September 2026) — UNHCR's 4.8 million and OCHA's 5.2 million IDP figures are printed separately, as sourced; a further IOM/UNHCR-portal count records about 278,443 newly displaced between 1 August and 25 October 2026 (UNHO summary of IOM DTM data, October 2026). UNICEF recorded at least 64 children killed and 64 injured since 3 September 2026, damage to at least 7 schools and 28 health facilities, and an attack on 2 November 2026 on the UNICEF-supported al-Suqyah Health Center in Lahj that killed five people including three health personnel — the attacker is not attributed in the UNICEF account and none is assigned here (UNICEF via Global Issues, 20 November 2026).
  • In his own territory, the accounting failed the people with the least leverage. Civil servants' salaries went unpaid from April 2025, with tens of thousands of public employees more than three months unpaid by September 2025 (Sana'a Center, April–June 2025; Xinhua reporting, September 2025) — in a country where the state salary is the welfare system. OCHA names the Muhamasheen among the groups "disproportionately affected," alongside women and girls, persons with disabilities, IDPs, migrants and refugees (OCHA HNRP 2026). WFP reported on 2 October 2026 that 74 percent of households in areas its teams could reach could not meet minimum food needs, the highest level recorded that year, and that in government-controlled areas one in two people faced high levels of hunger on IPC figures (WFP, 2 October 2026; UN News, September 2026). Women were excluded from the cabinet entirely from 2021 through the cabinets of most of the tenure (Peace Track Initiative statement, May 2025), a fact only partly answered by the foreign-ministry appointment noted above.

Why: FAILS — with the constraint named, not used as a blanket excuse. The seat did not create the whole catastrophe: the Houthi attacks of October–November 2022 on the al-Dhabba, al-Nushaima and Qena oil terminals halted oil exports and with them the government's primary revenue (Sana'a Center and Middle East Institute records, 2022), and no chairman could have paid salaries from an empty treasury or fed Sana'a's population across a front line. That is the no-good-choice mechanics of the fiscal trap, and it is recorded. What the grade rests on is what remained within the seat's reach: in its own areas, salaries stopped while factional revenues stayed local (Test 1); the marginalized — the Muhamasheen, the displaced, women shut out of the cabinet for years — appear in the record as the disproportionately hurt, not as the prioritized; and in July 2026 the coalition his seat leads struck the one international airport serving the majority population his seat claims to represent. A chairman who counts everyone does not have to succeed in feeding everyone; he has to be seen spending his scarcest resources — money, access, restraint — as if the uncounted were his people too. On this record, that ordering is not what happened.

Helped / Hurt ledger:

  • Helped: Taiz residents targeted by the September 2026 King Salman Center relief package; southern factions and fighters reintegrated after January 2026 rather than proscribed wholesale; detainees on both sides named in the May 2026 agreement.
  • Hurt: The 5.2 million internally displaced (OCHA) / nearly 4.8 million displaced (UNHCR) — separate figures, both printed; the 18.3 million acutely food insecure; unpaid civil servants, teachers and health workers in government areas from April 2025; the Muhamasheen and other marginalized groups named by OCHA as disproportionately affected; residents of Houthi-controlled territory dependent on Sana'a airport, struck in July 2026; and the roughly 350,000 newly displaced in June–November 2026 (IOM via UNICEF), including about 174,000 children.

Test 5 — KEEPS HIS WORD OVER TIME

The founding declaration's pledges and his public statements vs. the record since 7 April 2022, with dates.

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for (promises kept or credibly in motion):

  • The economic reform word — kept, and it helped (crossing 1: kept his word AND it helped people). Through 2025 the PLC pledged a comprehensive reform program — central-bank independence, import financing, revenue centralization — and in mid-2025 it was visibly executed: cabinet convening regularly in Aden under Bin Breik, PLC members increasing their presence inside the country, the import committee established, banks relocated to Aden, and the rial regaining about 50 percent of its value in July–August 2025 (Sana'a Center, July–September 2025; South24/ACAPS figures). Food-basket costs fell by roughly a third into September 2025. The keeping is credited; its fragility (Test 1, and food costs rising again after November 2025) is recorded on the other shelves, not used to erase the keeping.
  • The detainee word — kept in motion. The Stockholm-framework commitment to exchange detainees produced the war's largest agreed release on 14 May 2026 (UN, May 2026). Implementation is not fully documented; the agreement itself, after 14 weeks of negotiation his government sustained, is.
