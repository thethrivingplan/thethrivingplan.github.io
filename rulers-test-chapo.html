The Ruler's Test — Daniel Chapo, President of Mozambique

Subject: Daniel Francisco Chapo — President of Mozambique since 15 January 2025. The seat graded is the presidency, and the reason is stated plainly: Mozambique's president is the executive — head of state and government, commander-in-chief of the defence and security forces, the appointer of the prime minister (Maria Benvinda Levi), the provincial governors' overseer, and the holder of the pardon power. Chapo, born 6 January 1977 in Inhaminga, Sofala province, is a lawyer and former provincial governor of Inhambane (2016–2024) who became secretary-general of FRELIMO — the party that has governed Mozambique since independence in 1975 — and its presidential candidate in 2024. He is the first president born after independence. The holder named in the original brief is verified as still holding the seat in November 2026 (he met Russia's president at the Russia–Africa Summit in Moscow on 27–28 October 2026 and attended COP31 in Antalya on 11–12 November 2026). He is graded on his tenure since inauguration — roughly twenty-two months — and the file's centre of gravity is the crisis that seated him. The general election of 9 October 2024 was announced by the National Elections Commission (CNE) on 24 October with Chapo at 70.67%; the Constitutional Council, in its ruling of 22–23 December 2024 (Acórdão 24/CC/2024), found that vote totals had been inflated at district-level tabulation, partially annulled the CNE's results, and re-tabulated from polling-station sheets: Chapo 65.17% (4,416,306 votes), Venâncio Mondlane 24.19% (1,639,333), Ossufo Momade (RENAMO) 6.62%, Lutero Simango (MDM) 4.02%, on 42.2% turnout, with FRELIMO taking 171 of 250 Assembly seats. The EU Election Observation Mission reported fraudulent alteration of polling results, deliberate invalidation of opposition votes, and results manipulation at district level; Mozambique's Catholic bishops called the election a fraud; Mondlane, an independent backed by the PODEMOS party, rejected the count and claimed victory on his own parallel tally — a claim recorded here as his claim, not a finding. The protests he called from late October 2024 ran in waves to March 2025 and were met with the deadliest crackdown of Mozambique's post-civil-war era. The tolls are kept in their separate sourced counts throughout this dossier and never blended: the civil-society monitor Plataforma DECIDE counted 315 people killed between 21 October 2024 and 16 January 2025, and a final toll of 411 killed, more than 3,700 injured, and more than 7,200 detained across October 2024–March 2025; the police put the deaths at 96, including 17 police officers. One boundary is stated before any grading: the crackdown's first and worst phase (October–December 2024) was conducted under the outgoing president, Filipe Nyusi; Chapo was then the candidate and his party's secretary-general, not the commander-in-chief. The killings of his inauguration period and the roughly ten weeks of repression that followed fall inside his tenure, and everything from 15 January 2025 — the investigation promised and not delivered, the pardons, the dialogue, the prosecution of Mondlane — is his. Graded: November 2026, on the documented public record. Method: Each test is graded only on the evidence listed under it. Both shelves are shown — the strongest evidence that he passes, then the strongest evidence that he fails. Allegations are labeled ALLEGATION and never stated as verdicts. Contested items are named as contested, with who disputes them. Seat-holders and public figures are named; people below the public eye appear by role only, unless the documented record itself establishes their wrongdoing. Grades: HOLDS / FAILS / MIXED / NOT ENOUGH EVIDENCE. This record is correctable: new evidence changes grades, in the open. The ethic this Test applies is printed here in full: "How you play the game matters. Play by the rules. Stay true to your word. And if another option becomes available, at least consider it." The mandate rule applies throughout: governing on the platform the voters elected is not inherently a failure and is never graded as one — Chapo's 2024 platform was continuity with reform: dialogue, a leaner state, anti-corruption, cheaper basics, and delivering it is credited where it is due, above all on Test 5. What is graded is the holding: whether the law is one law, whether the referees are gripped, whether everyone is counted, and whether the word survives contact with his own interest. Word-keeping and helped/hurt are graded as two independent axes; neither launders the other, and the crossings are named where they occur.

Test 1 — GIVES BY ONE LAW

Same rules for allies and opponents, insiders and outsiders — no favorites at the source.

GRADE: FAILS

Evidence for:

  • His government's anti-corruption machinery has moved, in public, against senior figures of the state sector his own party runs. In February 2026 the Central Office for Combating Corruption (GCCC) announced five criminal investigations at the state airline LAM — aircraft sales and leases, catering, fuel, payments without contracts — and on 26 February 2026 five former LAM senior managers, including a former general director, were arrested; by mid-2026 two LAM cases had been referred to court, one with nine defendants charged with offences including embezzlement, fraud, and abuse of office (GCCC statements, February and August 2026; ch-aviation, 27 February 2026). The GCCC and prosecutors handled 1,646 corruption-related cases in the first half of 2026 and seized some 582 million meticais (GCCC, August 2026). The guilt of every LAM defendant is an ALLEGATION until a court rules; that the process reached a flagship state company at all is the fact on this shelf.
  • The pardon power was exercised across the post-election detainee population, not only for the connected: 751 detainees were pardoned in 2025, 578 of them on Family Day expressly as part of implementing the Political Commitment for an Inclusive National Dialogue (presidency statements, 2025; Lusa reporting). The pardons' limits are on the against shelf; their existence and breadth are recorded here.
  • The written law he inherited and promulgates is even on its face: no statute in the record assembled here assigns rights or penalties by party, region, or proximity to the palace. The second law documented below operates in incidence, not in text — which is why this test grades incidence.

Evidence against:

  • The seat itself was allocated through a count in which one law for all ballots did not operate. The EU Election Observation Mission reported instances of fraudulent alteration of polling results, deliberate invalidation of opposition votes, and results manipulation conducted at district level; the Constitutional Council's own ruling found the announced totals inflated at district tabulation and partially annulled the electoral commission's results (EU EOM reporting, 2024; Acórdão 24/CC/2024). Whatever the true margin — and this dossier does not adjudicate who won — the documented record is that opposition ballots and ruling-party ballots were not processed by the same rules at the count's decisive layer. Test 1 grades the source, and the source was uneven.
  • The killings of the crackdown have met no law at all. Amnesty International's April 2025 report Protest under attack, built on 105 verified videos and photographs and 28 interviews, documented security forces firing AK-pattern rifles and handguns at protesters and bystanders, including a blogger shot while livestreaming on 12 December 2024 and three people killed on 9 January 2025 as a crowd waited for Mondlane. On 22 January 2025 Chapo said in a media interview that his government would investigate the deaths, and at his March 2025 meeting with Mondlane he promised an investigation into protest deaths (Amnesty International, April 2025). As of this grading, no commission's findings have been published, and no member of the security forces is documented as prosecuted for any of the killings — in DECIDE's count 411 dead, in the police's own count 96. The suspected perpetrators, in Amnesty's words, have enjoyed "complete impunity."
  • The political killings run on the same track. Human Rights Watch documented the killings of at least 10 key opposition officials between October 2024 and March 2025 — some by gunmen wearing security-force uniforms — and found, in April 2025, no arrests, no identified suspects, and no public updates, with families told nothing (Human Rights Watch, 13 April 2025). DECIDE documented at least 18 killings of prominent opposition members by 25 March 2025. In the founding case — the 19 October 2024 ambush killing of Elvino Dias, Mondlane's lawyer, and Paulo Guambe, a PODEMOS election official, in central Maputo — the Attorney-General, Américo Letela, told parliament in April 2026 that the case remained in preparatory investigation nineteen months on, that three suspects had been heard and two detained, that no charge had been brought, and that one line of inquiry ran through a convicted criminal who had died in prison in March 2025 (Attorney-General's annual report to parliament, April 2026; Carta de Moçambique, 23 April 2026). DECIDE criticised the reliance on a deceased suspect as limiting accountability; Africa Risk Consulting assessed that the Attorney-General had steered the investigation away from state security agencies — an analyst's assessment, recorded as such (ARC Mozambique Briefing, May 2026).
  • Against that shelf of non-enforcement stands the one prosecution the state has pursued to the country's highest court: the runner-up himself. Prosecutors charged Mondlane in July 2025 with five offences — public advocacy of crime, incitement to collective disobedience, public instigation to a crime, instigation to terrorism, and incitement to terrorism — all arising from the speeches and broadcasts with which he contested the result; he denies them, and they are ALLEGATIONs until the Supreme Court rules. His trial opened on 17 November 2026 (see Test 2). The asymmetry is the finding: the law moved, at full strength, against the man who called the protests, and has not moved at all against the men who shot into them.
  • Access to the seat itself is reported to run through intermediaries. The weekly Canal de Moçambique profiled two businessmen whom investors described as brokers able to arrange deals with President Chapo and his office; as of Zitamar's reporting, neither the men nor the presidency had denied the reporting (Zitamar News, 2026). This is recorded as an ALLEGATION-level item — reported, unanswered, unproven — and it is the perception environment in which the outcome measure fell: Mozambique's Corruption Perceptions Index score dropped to 21 of 100 in the 2025 index (161st of 180), its lowest ever, from 25 in 2024 (Transparency International, CPI 2025).

Why: The grade lands on FAILS because the law's incidence tracks alignment at every decisive point. At the source, the count that seated him was manipulated at district level on the documented findings of the EU mission and, in part, of the Constitutional Council itself. In enforcement, the state's energy reached a fallen administration's airline managers and, at maximum strength, the opposition leader — and reached no one at all for more than 300 killings by its own forces or for the assassination of the opposition's lawyer. A law that prosecutes the speech and not the shooting is two laws.

Helped / Hurt: Helped — the ruling network: FRELIMO's incumbency, confirmed in office by a count its own referee partially annulled; the security forces, whose members face no documented prosecution for the crackdown; and, on the reported allegation, brokers selling proximity to the presidency. Hurt — the families of the dead of October 2024–March 2025 (411 in DECIDE's count, 96 in the police's), who have no findings, no names from the state, and no one charged; Elvino Dias's and Paulo Guambe's families, nineteen months without a charge; the opposition officials killed without investigation; and Venâncio Mondlane, facing up to 24 years for words, in a state that has charged no one for bullets.

Test 2 — HOLDS WITHOUT GRIPPING

Do the courts, the electoral commission, the media, the prosecution, and the central bank keep their autonomy — or is independence punished and control centralized?

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • The Constitutional Council has twice ruled against the executive's own instruments, in the open, and been obeyed. In December 2024 it partially annulled the electoral commission's announced results and re-tabulated from polling-station sheets, cutting the ruling party's presidential figure from 70.67% to 65.17% and its Assembly seats from 195 to 171, while raising every opposition party's allocation (Acórdão 24/CC/2024). On 29–31 July 2026, on a review requested by the Ombudsman, the Council unanimously struck down 18 provisions of the government's Decree No. 48/2025 — the Telecommunications Traffic Control Regulation adopted on 16 December 2025, which would have empowered the regulator to monitor communications, collect user data, and suspend internet and telecommunications services — holding that restrictions on expression, privacy, and the inviolability of communications could be imposed only by parliament, not by executive decree. The communications minister publicly accepted the ruling on 3 August 2026 (Constitutional Council judgment, July 2026; Columbia Global Freedom of Expression case analysis; International IDEA GSoD tracker, July 2026).
  • Parliament has legislated in the open on the crisis files, including against pure executive preference for speed: it enacted the Political Commitment for an Inclusive National Dialogue into law (Law No. 1/2025, promulgated April 2025), and on 24 April 2026 it unanimously approved decentralization amendments abolishing the provincial State Representation Services and transferring their functions to the elected provincial governors (International IDEA GSoD tracker, April 2026).
  • The referees of public argument still operate. DECIDE, the Centre for Democracy and Development, Zitamar, Canal de Moçambique, and the Mozambique Bar Association publish detailed, named criticism of his government — including a lawyers' march in Maputo in October 2026 demanding answers on the Dias case — without documented closure or prosecution of the organisations (Bar Association statements, October 2026). The Mondlane trial itself is being heard in open court: the Supreme Court admitted Mondlane's chosen defender over the prosecution's objection on the trial's first day, admitted 20 defence witnesses alongside 12 prosecution witnesses, and received a 223-page defence response (Plataforma DECIDE trial log, 17 November 2026).

Evidence against:

  • The electoral referees remain structurally his party's. The CNE and its technical secretariat conducted the 2024 count that the EU mission found manipulated at district level and the Constitutional Council partially annulled; no electoral official is documented as sanctioned or prosecuted for the inflation the Council itself found, and the Council — whose members are appointed predominantly through the FRELIMO-majority Assembly — corrected the arithmetic and then validated the outcome the arithmetic produced. The referee fixed the score sheet and ratified the match.
  • The executive reached for a communications kill-switch of its own. Decree No. 48/2025 (16 December 2025) was his government's instrument, adopted after the 2024 protests — during which internet access had already been restricted for more than ten days under his predecessor — and would have let the regulator monitor traffic and suspend services on administrative findings of risk to public or state security (CDD analysis, January 2026). The Council struck its core down; and the day after the minister accepted the ruling, the Council of Ministers' spokesman said the executive would pursue the same end by a route that does not contradict the Council — no bill or timetable has been published as of grading (Data Landscapers Mozambique status report, August 2026). The grip was refused by the referee; the intention to grip was restated.
  • The media framework tightened on his watch. The Press and Media Law (Law 3/2026, gazetted 26 May 2026) brings online-only publications inside the state media framework and caps foreign capital in media organisations at 35%; cyber-security and cybercrime laws followed on 1 July 2026 (Laws 13/2026 and 14/2026). Press-freedom organisations warned the framework could become a barrier to independent journalism (Data Landscapers Mozambique progress report, 2026).
  • In the conflict province, the state's grip on witnesses is physical. Journalist and editor Arlindo Chissale was taken from a minibus in Cabo Delgado on 7 January 2025 by men, some in military uniform; his family later told media he had been tortured and killed, and as of October 2025 his body had not been recovered — the abduction predates Chapo's inauguration by eight days, and the failure to account for it spans his whole tenure (Human Rights Watch World Report 2026; CPJ, February 2025). In June 2025 soldiers in Macomia temporarily detained 16 journalists and seized their equipment; in June 2026 criminal-investigation officers seized the work equipment of investigative journalist Estácio Valoi in Pemba in proceedings linked to his environmental reporting (HRW World Report 2026; Amnesty International Urgent Action, 3 July 2026).
  • The route to the Mondlane trial ran through a body the president chairs. Mondlane holds a Council of State seat by constitutional right of the runner-up (assumed 1 September 2025), and Council members carry immunity liftable only by the Council — a body chaired by the President of the Republic. The Council's deliberation of 10 June 2026 suspended his immunity and cleared case No. 52/2025-P to proceed; the decision was made public only in August 2026, and Mondlane contests it as unlawful, arguing the Council lacks authority to suspend its own members' immunity — a challenge recorded here as his legal position, unresolved at grading (Torch Media Africa analysis, 2026; Supreme Court trial reporting, November 2026). A referee presided over by one party to the dispute cleared the prosecution of the other.

Why: The grade lands on MIXED because the file genuinely splits. The Constitutional Council's two rulings — re-tabulating against the electoral commission in 2024 and striking the internet decree unanimously in 2026, with the executive's public acceptance — are referee independence of a kind this series rarely records, and they happened under his presidency without documented retaliation. Against them stand the captured electoral machinery that seated him, his own government's decree reaching for the communications switch and its stated intention to reach again by another route, and a prosecution cleared through a council he chairs. The referees he inherited are gripped; the referee that ruled against him was, on the record, allowed to stand.

Helped / Hurt: Helped — every Mozambican who uses a phone or the internet, whose communications the July 2026 judgment kept under parliamentary rather than executive control; the opposition parties whose seat allocations the 2024 re-tabulation raised; and the dialogue process, which a functioning parliament could carry into law. Hurt — journalists in Cabo Delgado (Chissale's family without a body or an account; the 16 Macomia journalists; Estácio Valoi without his equipment); online media facing a new state framework and a foreign-capital cap; and Mondlane, whose immunity was lifted by a body his opponent chairs.

Test 3 — ANSWERS THE WOBBLE

When courts, voters, parliament, the street, foreign partners, or events push back, does he adjust openly — or deny and double down?

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • The street's pushback was ultimately answered with the largest political adjustment available to him: he sat down with the man who had proclaimed himself president. Chapo and Mondlane met in Maputo on 23 March 2025 and again on 20 May 2025; the day after the first meeting Mondlane called for an end to the violence, and no further social unrest tied to the electoral dispute has been reported since (Torch Media Africa, 2026; University of Antwerp policy brief on the Inclusive National Dialogue, 2026). This followed the 5 March 2025 Political Commitment for an Inclusive National Dialogue, signed with the parties represented in parliament, which Chapo promulgated as law in April 2025, formally launched on 10–11 September 2025, and opened to nationwide public consultation from 6 October 2025; by June 2026 its technical commission (COTE) was publicly debating three scenarios for the organisation of the state, from limited adjustments to far-reaching constitutional and electoral reform (Africa Risk Consulting, October 2025; International IDEA GSoD tracker, June 2026; UNDP, 2026).
  • The other-option rule is satisfied on the central file, and the contrast is documented. In December 2024, when another option became available — direct political talks with Mondlane — the outgoing president argued for a legal rather than a political solution and the attempt failed, with Mondlane refusing to attend without safety guarantees (International Crisis Group CrisisWatch, January 2025; Antwerp policy brief, 2026). Chapo, who had himself called for dialogue on 23 December 2024, took the option his predecessor had declined: he met Mondlane directly, twice, and the killing stopped. Considering the option is what decided it; the record shows the adjustment working.
  • He removed the face of the crackdown. On 23 January 2025, eight days into his term, Chapo dismissed police commander-general Bernardino Rafael, who had been criticised for his role in the post-election violence (International Crisis Group CrisisWatch, January 2025).
  • A referee's pushback was accepted in public. When the Constitutional Council struck down his government's telecommunications decree in July 2026, his communications minister accepted the ruling on the record within days (Data Landscapers Mozambique status report, August 2026). The spokesman's statement that the executive would seek the same end by another route is recorded on Test 2's against shelf; the acceptance itself, without defiance or purge, belongs here.

Evidence against:

  • The first answer to the wobble, on his watch, was still force. Protests around his 15 January 2025 inauguration were met with live fire: at least eight people were reported killed on inauguration day itself, and Amnesty documented killings and mass arrests continuing into late January 2025 — three people shot dead on 9 January as a crowd waited for Mondlane's return, and the crackdown running, in waves, to March 2025 (Amnesty International, April 2025; International Crisis Group, January 2025). The bulk of the killing predates his presidency; its final ten weeks do not.
  • The adjustment excluded the man who had mobilised the street. The 5 March 2025 Commitment was signed with the parties in parliament; Mondlane — then without a party — was not a signatory, and after his ANAMOLA party registered in August 2025 it remained outside the dialogue's signatory circle: the nine parties working in the process in mid-2026 were FRELIMO, RENAMO, PODEMOS, MDM, RD, PAHUMO, PARENA, PARESO, and ND — not ANAMOLA (UNDP, 2026). His bilateral channel to Mondlane, having produced the peace, was allowed to lapse into a prosecution: the charges were filed in July 2025, four months after the handshake, and the trial opened in November 2026 with the dialogue still unfinished. The wobble was answered; the answerer was then charged with terrorism offences for the wobbling.
  • On the accountability question he refused to look. An investigation into the protest deaths was the option repeatedly placed in front of him — by Amnesty International, by Human Rights Watch, and as a stated condition of Mondlane's January 2025 protest suspension (release of detainees and compensation for the families of the killed). He promised the investigation in January and again in March 2025, and then, in twenty months, produced no commission, no findings, and no prosecutions. Under the other-option rule, declining with stated reasons is not against-evidence; refusing to look is. No reasons for abandoning the inquiry were ever stated.
  • The no-good-choice clause is applied here, by its mechanics, to the inauguration window — and bounded. The options that actually existed on 15 January 2025 were: refuse the proclaimed result and re-open the count, voiding the only referee ruling in force and returning the dispute to a street already burying its dead; take office and rule by repression alone, the outgoing government's path; or take office, replace the police command, and open a political door. He took the third, and the constraint is recorded, not held against him. Two bounds are stated with it. First, the trap's origin is graded elsewhere in this dossier: the disputed count and the crackdown that preceded him were his own party's work, and he was its candidate and secretary-general (Tests 1 and 5). Second, the clause covers the choice of January–March 2025; it does not cover the accountability failure of the twenty calm months that followed, which was a free choice, nor the December 2025 internet decree, which was an initiative, not a dilemma.

Why: The grade lands on MIXED because both movements are documented at full strength. He did the thing his predecessor would not — met the rival, signed the commitment, built the dialogue, and the violence ended; that is answering the wobble in its hardest form. And the answer came after ten weeks of continued shooting on his own watch, excluded the street's own leader from the table it built, and was paired with a refusal to look at the one question — who killed the protesters — that the street had asked loudest. Adjustment on the politics, doubling down on the accountability: the file holds both.

Helped / Hurt: Helped — the whole country's daily life: protesters and police alike stopped dying after March 2025 (17 police officers were among the dead in the police's own count); businesses that received the government's credit line of more than 150 million dollars for firms damaged by the unrest; and the nine signatory parties, given a legal road into constitutional reform. Hurt — the demonstrators killed and arrested in January–March 2025, inside his tenure; Mondlane and ANAMOLA's supporters, whose movement was answered with a table they are not seated at and a trial for its leader; and the victims' families, whose stated condition — an accounting for the dead — was the one part of the settlement never taken up.

Test 4 — COUNTS EVERYONE

Does the accounting include the north, the displaced, the protesters, the young, and the poor — or are some counted out?

GRADE: FAILS

Evidence for:

  • The dialogue's consultation machinery was built to reach beyond the capital: COTE formed local teams and ran public consultations across the country from 6 October 2025, and in June 2026 put three competing models of the state — including electoral arrangements — to public hearings scheduled through July and August 2026 (Africa Risk Consulting, October 2025; International IDEA GSoD tracker, June 2026). Whatever its exclusions at the signatory table (Test 3), the consultation itself was national in design.
  • Early economic measures were aimed at the cost of living of the poor majority: a VAT exemption on sugar, edible oils, and soaps through the end of 2025, a reduction in fuel prices, and a credit line of more than 150 million dollars for businesses damaged by the unrest, all inside the first 100 days (360 Mozambique review of the first 50 days, March 2025).
  • Power was formally devolved toward the provinces: the April 2026 decentralization amendments abolished the appointed provincial State Representation Services and transferred their functions to the elected provincial executives (International IDEA GSoD tracker, April 2026).
  • The displaced of Cabo Delgado are, in large part, hosted and assisted inside a state-run and internationally supported system: the IOM recorded 449,876 internally displaced people and 673,517 returnees in the province in June 2026 — the returnees a real measure of ground recovered since the insurgency's 2021 peak (IOM via ACLED reporting, 2026).

Evidence against:

  • The state's own accounting of its dead citizens was never produced. The police count of the crackdown is 96 dead, including 17 officers; the civil-society count is 411. Twenty-two months after his inauguration promise to investigate, the government has published no list, no findings, and no reconciliation of the two numbers. The dead of the crisis that seated him are, in his state's books, uncounted — and a count is the first form counting takes.
  • The detained were counted as a security problem long after the streets emptied. DECIDE recorded more than 7,200 detentions across the unrest; months into the peace, it counted some 2,740 people still detained, of whom only 22 were reached by the first pardon tranche it monitored, with reports that some detainees were extorted for release and others held without legal detention orders or trial (DECIDE via Lusa, 2025). The 751 pardons (Test 1) are on this file's for shelf in Test 1; their arithmetic is against-evidence here: the pardon reached a fraction, and the fraction's selection was not explained.
  • The north remains outside the count where it matters most. The Cabo Delgado insurgency, begun in October 2017 and rooted — on the Institute for Security Studies' assessment — in local exclusion: young people without work, land and resources taken, a distant government accused of shutting the north out of the country's wealth. Under Chapo the war entered its ninth and tenth years: violence touched all 17 of the province's districts for the first time; more than 95,000 people fled in 2025, some 100,000 more were displaced in November 2025 alone as attacks spread into Nampula province, and 28,163 were displaced in January–July 2026; the IOM recorded a further 5,794 displaced in three weeks of September–October 2026. ACLED counts at least 6,632 deaths from political violence in the province from October 2017 to 14 June 2026, including 2,772 civilians — a toll for the whole conflict, kept separate from the post-election counts as this dossier keeps all its tolls (ACLED, 2026; IOM, 2026; HRW World Report 2026). Humanitarian aid reached about 619,000 of the 1.1 million people targeted in 2026, and the Norwegian Refugee Council has named the crisis among the world's most neglected two years running (UN humanitarian plan reporting via ACLED, 2026; NRC, June 2025 and June 2026). Children were abducted by the insurgents in rising numbers through 2025 (HRW World Report 2026).
  • The gas restarted; the province around it did not. The TotalEnergies-led consortium lifted force majeure on the 20-billion-dollar Mozambique LNG project in November 2025, and on 29 January 2026 Chapo and TotalEnergies' chief executive jointly announced the full restart at Afungi — a fenced, fly-in compound, some 40% built, targeting first LNG in 2029, guarded with Rwandan support while insurgent attacks rose in the districts outside the fence (TotalEnergies and government announcements, November 2025–January 2026; ISS analysis, 2026). A 200-million-dollar foundation for local farmers and fishermen accompanies the project; the structure it accompanies is an enclave: the counted economy resumes behind wire while the surrounding districts supply the displacement figures above.
  • The general condition he governs has worsened on the measures that count everyone. The economy contracted in 2025 (−0.2% on the World Bank's figure; −0.5% on another of its releases — the Bank's releases differ, and both are recorded) after the unrest, and is projected to grow 0.6% in 2026, below population growth, so income per person keeps falling. The World Bank's March 2026 Economic Update ranks Mozambique the second-poorest country in the world, with about 81–82% of the population below the 3-dollar-a-day line and the national poverty rate at 62.9% (2022), up from 48.4% in 2014/15; public debt is classified in distress and unsustainable (World Bank, March–September 2026). Poverty on this scale is substantially his inheritance — a decade of hidden-debt crisis, insurgency, and shocks — and is recorded as context, not as his act. What is his act is the distribution of his government's attention: the restart, the dialogue, and the trial all proceeded on schedule; the investigation, the detainee accounting, and the north's aid gap did not.

Why: The grade lands on FAILS on the accounting itself, not on the poverty he inherited. A government counts everyone first by counting them: his state never counted its own crackdown dead, left thousands of detainees unaccounted months into the peace it had signed, and presides over a northern province whose people flee in five figures a year while the province's wealth restarts behind a fence. The for-shelf items — consultations, VAT relief, devolution, returns — are real, and they are the smaller half of the ledger.

Helped / Hurt: Helped — consumers of basic goods (VAT exemption, cheaper fuel); unrest-damaged businesses (the credit line); the 673,517 returnees of Cabo Delgado; the gas consortium and the future treasury, if the 2029 target holds; and provincial governments, formally strengthened. Hurt — the families of the uncounted dead; the roughly 2,740 detainees DECIDE counted still held, and those who paid extortion for release; the displaced of Cabo Delgado and Nampula (449,876 still displaced in June 2026, aid reaching little more than half the target); the children abducted by the insurgency; and the poor majority — four-fifths of the country below the 3-dollar line — whose per-person income fell again in both of his first two years.

Test 5 — KEEPS HIS WORD OVER TIME

Campaign and inauguration pledges and public statements vs. the record since 15 January 2025, with dates.

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for (promises kept or credibly in motion):

  • The leaner state — delivered on day one. His inauguration speech on 15 January 2025 promised to reduce the number of ministries, abolish the deputy-minister post, and review leaders' perks. His first presidential decree, the next day, abolished and re-merged ten ministries into a smaller structure and eliminated deputy ministers (inauguration speech and first decree, 15–16 January 2025; Africa-Press reporting).
  • Dialogue — promised, then built. As president-elect he called for dialogue on 23 December 2024; the Political Commitment was signed on 5 March 2025, enacted as Law No. 1/2025 in April, launched in September 2025, and was running nationwide consultations and debating constitutional scenarios through 2026 (see Test 3). The process has not concluded — its own timeline runs toward the 2028–2029 electoral cycle — and the word is graded as kept in motion, not completed.
  • Cheaper basics — delivered in the first 100 days. The 100 Days of Governance Plan, approved in February 2025, was followed by the VAT exemption on sugar, edible oils, and soaps, a fuel-price reduction, and the credit line for unrest-damaged businesses (Council of Ministers, February 2025; 360 Mozambique, March 2025).
  • The LAM cleanup — promised and visibly pursued. He publicly condemned corruption at the state airline and promised the era was over; the GCCC's five LAM investigations, the February 2026 arrests of former senior managers, and the referral of cases to court followed (see Test 1). The airline files concern the previous administration's management; the pursuit of them was his word, and it was kept in the open.
  • The gas restart — delivered. Through 2025 his government committed to bringing the Mozambique LNG project back; force majeure was lifted in November 2025 and he announced the full restart in person on 29 January 2026 (see Test 4). Under the mandate rule, carrying out what he ran on — continuity of the development program — is credited here, not graded as a fault.

Evidence against (promises not kept, or receding):

  • The investigation — his heaviest word, unkept. On 22 January 2025 he said his government would investigate the deaths of the crackdown; at the 23 March 2025 meeting he promised Mondlane an investigation into protest deaths; Mondlane's January 2025 suspension of protests had been conditioned on exactly this, together with detainee releases and compensation for the families of the killed. Twenty months on: no commission, no findings, no prosecutions, no published list of the dead, and no documented compensation program for victims' families (Amnesty International, April 2025; the record assembled here through November 2026). The promise was not formally withdrawn; it receded, which this Test grades the same way when the receding is the whole of the record.
  • The detainees — half a word. The March 2025 settlement carried an amnesty-and-release logic, and 751 pardons were granted; DECIDE's monitoring found some 2,740 protest detainees still held months later, most of the pardoned being ordinary prisoners rather than protest detainees, with extortion reported in the release process (DECIDE via Lusa, 2025). A word kept for a fraction of the people it was given to is recorded as such.
  • The victory claim — the word underneath the others. As candidate and secretary-general he stood on the claim that the October 2024 election was a clean win to be defended, not renegotiated. In office he signed a commitment whose premise is that the constitutional and electoral framework itself must be renegotiated — the concession that ended the killing. The departure from his campaign word is recorded on this shelf as a break; its consequences are recorded in the crossings below, where this series keeps the two axes apart.

Why: The grade lands on MIXED because the kept shelf is unusually concrete — ministries cut by decree the day after the promise, tax relief inside a hundred days, a dialogue law inside three months, an airline investigation reaching arrests, a gas restart announced in person — and the broken shelf holds the single promise that mattered most to the people who had paid most: the investigation into the dead. His word is reliable on the state he runs and unreliable on the reckoning he owes; both halves are documented, and neither launders the other.

Helped / Hurt: Helped — taxpayers and consumers (leaner ministries, VAT relief, cheaper fuel); the signatory parties and the public, who got the dialogue he promised; investors and the future treasury (the restart). Hurt — the families of the killed, promised an investigation and compensation and given neither in documented form; the detainees beyond the pardon's reach; and Mondlane, who suspended his protests on stated conditions — releases, compensation, investigation — of which only the first was even partially performed, and who now stands trial for the protests he called off.

Overall Summary

One law: FAILS · Without gripping: MIXED · The wobble: MIXED · Counts everyone: FAILS · His word: MIXED.

Daniel Chapo took office on 15 January 2025 as the first Mozambican president born after independence, seated by a count his own constitutional referee partially annulled, over a country burying — in DECIDE's count — 411 dead from the election's aftermath. One law FAILS because the law's incidence tracks alignment at every decisive point: a district-level count manipulated against opposition ballots, total impunity for the security forces who killed protesters and for the assassins of the opposition's lawyer, and the state's one maximal prosecution reserved for the runner-up's speeches. Without gripping lands MIXED because the Constitutional Council ruled against his party's count in 2024 and unanimously against his government's internet decree in 2026 and was obeyed — while the electoral machinery stays captured, the decree showed the executive's instinct, and Mondlane's immunity was lifted by a council the president chairs. The wobble lands MIXED because he answered the street with the thing his predecessor refused — direct talks, a signed commitment, a dialogue law, nationwide consultations — and the violence ended; but the answer came after ten more weeks of shooting on his watch, excludes the street's own party from the table, and refuses to look at accountability. Counts everyone FAILS on the accounting itself: the dead of the crackdown never officially counted, thousands of detainees unaccounted months into the peace, and a northern province fleeing in five figures a year while its gas restarts behind a fence — against real but smaller entries on the other side: consultations, tax relief, devolution, and 673,517 returnees. His word lands MIXED: ministries cut on day one, basics relieved inside a hundred days, the dialogue built, LAM pursued, the gas restarted — and the investigation into the dead, promised twice in the settlement that made him governable, receding for twenty months without a word of explanation.

The two axes cross in his file in all the ways this Test names. Kept his word and it helped: the dialogue promise — signed, enacted, launched — and the peace that followed the March 2025 meetings; the VAT and fuel relief delivered inside the promised hundred days. Kept his word and it still hurt: his word to defend the proclaimed result and restore order was kept at full weight through January–March 2025, and the keeping cost the lives of protesters shot in those weeks — the promise's content, not its keeping, is what Tests 3 and 4 grade. Went against his word and it helped: the candidate who stood on a clean, closed victory signed, as president, a commitment premised on renegotiating the electoral framework itself — a break with his campaign word that bought the end of the killing; the break is recorded on Test 5 and the help is recorded here in full. Broke his word and it hurt: the investigation promised to the nation on 22 January 2025 and to Mondlane on 23 March 2025, on which the protest suspension was conditioned — unperformed for twenty months, leaving 411 families (DECIDE's count) without findings, names, or compensation. The open files that will move these grades are named in the dossier: the Supreme Court's verdict in case No. 52/2025-P and whether Mondlane can stand in 2029; whether any inquiry into the crackdown dead is ever opened; whether the Dias case reaches a charge; whether the dialogue's scenarios become law before the 2028–2029 cycle, and whether ANAMOLA is ever seated at it; and whether the north's districts, and not only its gas field, come back inside the count.
