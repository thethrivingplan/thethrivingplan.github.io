Budget — last month, every line named

No month has run yet. Press play or step a month and every income and upkeep line will be listed here with its amount.

How money works, in plain words: each resident pays tax of $3 a month at a 10% tax rate (it scales with the rate you set). An open shop pays $20 a month at 10%, an open workplace $30. Upkeep is charged per building at the prices printed on the tools above. If the treasury falls below $0 the town does not instantly end — but the Coach will warn you, and a town that stays broke cannot build.

Teaching

Settings — rules are settings

Standard play is: standard map, standard funds, disasters on. Anything else is a variant, and the game says so with a banner and labels the record as a variant. Changing a setting starts a new town.

How fires work, printed plainly: each month, every building with no fire station within 5 tiles has a small chance (3 in 100) to catch fire. A fire destroys that building, and each neighbour without fire cover has a 40 in 100 chance to catch too. Rubble must be bulldozed ($5) before the tile can be reused.

Rules & prices — everything printed, nothing hidden

What a home needs

  • A road next to it. No road, nobody can reach it.
  • Power: a power plant within 6 tiles. Water: a water pump within 6 tiles.
  • Jobs within reach: a shop or workplace within 5 tiles, with jobs free. A shop gives 4 jobs, a workplace 8.
  • Decent happiness (40 or more, town-wide). Parks, school, clinic and fire cover raise it; high taxes and joblessness lower it.

A home that has everything gains up to 2 people a month, to a full house of 6. A home missing power, water or a road loses 2 people a month. A home with no jobs nearby loses 1 a month. A home empty for 3 straight months is abandoned. Each home shows its state in plain words in the Inspector: no power, no water, no road, no jobs nearby, happy, unhappy, or abandoned.

Shops need customers

A shop only opens — and only pays tax and offers jobs — if it has power, water, a road beside it, and homes with people in them within 4 tiles. A shop with no homes nearby fails, however nice the building is. A workplace needs power, water and a road, but not nearby customers.

About this game. The city-building game is a documented form: in 1989 a landmark city simulator defined the genre — a grid, zones and buildings, power and water, taxes against upkeep, and citizens who arrive when a city works and leave when it doesn't. New Town is our own original work in that form: our own name, art, text and building designs, written from scratch. Nothing here copies that classic's name, art, or text.
