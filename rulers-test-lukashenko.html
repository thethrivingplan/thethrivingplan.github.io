The Ruler's Test — Alexander Lukashenko, President of Belarus

Subject: Alexander Grigoryevich Lukashenko (Alyaksandr Lukashenka) — President of Belarus since 20 July 1994, now in a seventh five-year term. The seat graded is the presidency, and the reason is stated plainly: in Belarus executive power sits with the president, who appoints and dismisses the prime minister, the judges, the prosecutor general, and the leadership of the security services, and who chairs the All-Belarusian People's Assembly. The prime minister is subordinate: Alexander Turchin, appointed prime minister by Lukashenko's decree on 10 March 2025 after the January 2025 election, replaced Roman Golovchenko, whom Lukashenko moved the same day to chair the National Bank (Presidential press service, 10 March 2025; Interfax, 10 March 2025). Turchin administers the government; he does not hold the seat of power, and where his role bears on a test it is stated as the president's appointment acting, not as an independent centre. Premise verified: the seat has not changed hands. Lukashenko was declared re-elected in the presidential election of 26 January 2025 and sworn in on 25 March 2025 (BelTA via belarus.by, 25 March 2025). The documented record of that election is printed in full because the mandate is itself evidence: the Central Election Commission (CEC) certified on 3 February 2025 that 5,916,195 voters took part (85.69% of 6,903,994 registered voters) and that 5,136,293 votes (86.82%) were cast for Lukashenko, with Sergei Syrankov at 3.21%, Oleg Gaidukevich at 2.02%, Anna Kanopatskaya at 1.86%, Alexander Khizhnyak at 1.74%, and 3.60% against all candidates (CEC announcement, 3 February 2025). Some secondary summaries round or restate these as 87.48% and 85.70% turnout; the CEC's own certified figures above are the ones used here, and the discrepancy is recorded rather than blended. No independent count exists to set against them: Belarus did not invite the OSCE's ODIHR to observe — the third election since 2020 without a timely invitation, in breach, ODIHR said, of Belarus's 1990 Copenhagen Document commitments — no polling stations were provided abroad, and the four rival candidates were all leaders or members of the only four registered, pro-government parties, one of whom, Syrankov, had endorsed Lukashenko before the vote (OSCE/ODIHR and OSCE PA statement, 9 January 2025; International IDEA democracy tracker, January 2025). The European Parliament resolved not to recognise the result (European Parliament resolution, January 2025, as reported in the Belarus Network bimonthly review, February 2025). On succession: there is movement of words, not of power. In a TIME interview published 8 August 2025 Lukashenko said he was "not planning" to seek another term and denied grooming his youngest son Nikolai — "No, he is not a successor" — while naming no successor, setting no mechanism, and retaining every lever described in Test 2 (Reuters reporting of the TIME interview, 8 August 2025). Analysts mapping the elite note the system remains hyper-personalist, with no designated heir (Chatham House analysis in Belarus Analytical Digest No. 007, December 2025). He is therefore graded as a sitting holder on a continuous tenure of more than 32 years, not as a completed tenure; the heaviest evidence is the post-2020 record, because that is the record the 2025 mandate claims to renew. Graded: November 2026, on the documented public record. Method: The ethic of the Test, printed verbatim: "How you play the game matters. Play by the rules. Stay true to your word. And if another option becomes available, at least consider it." Each test is graded only on the evidence listed under it. Both shelves are shown — the strongest evidence that he passes, then the strongest evidence that he fails. Allegations are labeled ALLEGATION and never stated as verdicts. Contested items are named as contested, with who disputes them. Conflicting counts — votes, turnout, political-prisoner figures — are printed as separate sourced figures, never blended. Governing on the platform voters elected is not inherently a failure and is never graded as one; the Test grades the holding, never the platform — and in this case the prior question, whether a verifiable platform-and-mandate exists at all, is itself part of the documented record above. Seat-holders and public figures are named; people below the public eye appear by role only, unless the documented record itself establishes their wrongdoing. Grades: HOLDS / FAILS / MIXED / NOT ENOUGH EVIDENCE. This record is correctable: new evidence changes grades, in the open.

Test 1 — GIVES BY ONE LAW

Same rules for allies and opponents, insiders and outsiders — no favorites at the source.

GRADE: FAILS

Evidence for:

  • The criminal law is, in form, applied to his own officials as well as to outsiders. At a government conference reported 14 June 2024, Lukashenko publicly named corruption investigations against senior insiders, including First Deputy Head of Belarusian Railways Piotr Dulub, in whose properties investigators reported seizing more than USD 3.2 million in cash, and warned ministers at the table that he had told them all, "It is better to be poor but free than rich but in prison" (BelTA via belarus.by, 14 June 2024). Anti-corruption prosecution of serving officials is a recurring, documented feature of his tenure, not a one-off.
  • The pardon power, whatever its terms (Tests 3 and 5), has been exercised at scale and across categories in 2025–2026 — opposition figures, journalists, foreign nationals, and ordinary convicts alike: 14 prisoners in June 2025, 52 in September 2025, 123 on 13 December 2025, 250 on 19 March 2026, and further groups through September 2026 (Congressional Research Service, Belarus: An Overview, updated 2026; International Accountability Platform for Belarus, 11th Progress Report, April–September 2026). The releases were not confined to one favoured group.
  • The formal rules of the state — the 2022 Constitution, the electoral code, the party law — are written in general terms and apply on their face to everyone, including the provision, new in 2022, limiting a president to two terms (2022 constitutional text, as analysed by the Venice Commission, Opinion of 24 October 2022).

Evidence against:

  • The two-term limit itself carries a built-in second law for one man: it applies only prospectively, so the terms Lukashenko had already served do not count against it, leaving him eligible to remain president until 2035 — a design noted at the time by the Venice Commission, which found the 2022 amendments provided "no reasonable limitations" on his tenure, and by RFE/RL and the Atlantic Council in January–February 2022 (Venice Commission Opinion, 24 October 2022, as cited by the Parliamentary Assembly of the Council of Europe, Doc. 16105; RFE/RL, 21 January 2022). A rule that binds every future holder and exempts the present one is the definition of a favourite at the source.
  • The party law was rewritten so that only one side can exist legally. The February 2023 law raised the membership threshold fivefold and required structures in every region; in the 2023 re-registration that followed, 11 of 15 parties — mostly opposition parties — were liquidated at the Ministry of Justice's request, leaving exactly four registered parties, all pro-government: Belaya Rus, the Communist Party of Belarus, the Republican Party of Labour and Justice, and the Liberal Democratic Party (2024 parliamentary election record; CSO Meter update, 2023). The February 2024 parliamentary election consequently had no opposition candidates at all.
  • "Extremism" law operates as a separate legal universe for one side. By 2025 the authorities' lists held more than 300 "extremist formations" and courts had declared more than 2,000 information materials "extremist" in 2025 alone; the Belarusian Association of Journalists and most independent media are designated extremist, and subscribing to, donating to, or interacting with designated content is prosecuted (Freedom House, Freedom in the World 2026, key developments for 2025; BAJ, Mass Media in Belarus 2025). No pro-government outlet or organisation carries any equivalent designation in the record assembled here.
  • The source itself — the count — is one law for him and no law that anyone else may check. In 2020 the CEC declared Lukashenko the winner with 80.10% and Sviatlana Tsikhanouskaya second with 10.12% (CEC figures, as recorded in the European Parliament recommendation of 21 October 2020). The independent Golos/Zubr/Honest People count, built from photographed protocols of 1,310 of 5,767 polling stations covering 32.2% of voters, found Tsikhanouskaya's officially reported votes at those stations irreconcilable with the CEC totals — at the stations whose protocols were obtained she had received over 81% of the total the CEC credited to her nationwide (Golos final report, 20 August 2020). The two counts are printed separately, as they must be: the Golos count is partial and does not itself prove a nationwide alternative total, and the CEC count was produced with no independent observation, in 2020 or in 2025. What is not contested by any party is that checking the count was treated as an offence: citizen observers were excluded, photographing a ballot was later banned outright (2023 electoral-code amendments), and ODIHR was not invited in 2020, 2024, or 2025 (OSCE/ODIHR, 9 January 2025).
  • Impunity runs in one direction. The UN Human Rights Office documented at least five unlawful deaths in the 2020 protest context and widespread, systematic torture, and recorded that of some 5,000 ill-treatment complaints filed with the Investigative Committee, no investigation establishing official responsibility followed; the authorities denied responsibility for the deaths (OHCHR reports A/HRC/49/71 and A/HRC/52/68, March 2022 and March 2023). No security official is documented as convicted for the 2020 violence; thousands of the people they beat were convicted (Viasna figures in Test 4).

Why this grade: One law means the same rule at the source — who may organise, who may publish, who may be counted, who may be prosecuted. On the documented record the source itself is differentiated: a term limit that exempts its author, a party register with one side on it, an extremism regime that covers only critics, and an impunity record that covers only the state's own forces. The anti-corruption cases against insiders are real, and they are recorded above; but they run through the same discretionary machinery — announced by the president personally, at his table, as his warning — rather than through a law that also binds him. That is enforcement by favour and disfavour, not one law.

Helped / Hurt ledger: Helped — the four registered pro-government parties and their candidates, who face no lawful competition; serving officials who stay in favour, for whom the anti-corruption sword stays sheathed; the president himself, exempted from his own term limit until 2035. Hurt — the 11 liquidated parties and their members; independent media and the Belarusian Association of Journalists, designated extremist for journalism; citizen election observers, whose checking activity was criminalised; and the complainants in some 5,000 torture complaints that produced no official accountability (OHCHR, 2022–2023).

Test 2 — HOLDS WITHOUT GRIPPING

Do the courts, the electoral commission, the media, the prosecution, and the central bank keep their autonomy — or is independence punished and control centralized?

GRADE: FAILS

Evidence for:

  • The 2022 Constitution formally removed one grip: presidential decrees no longer have the force of law, and a prime minister's role in budget and government formation was formally strengthened (2022 constitutional amendments, as summarised in the referendum record). Prime Minister Turchin's government publicly reports economic results, including misses against target, in its own briefings (Belarusian state and regional press reporting of Turchin's January 2026 economic briefing).
  • The presidency has shown it can loosen a specific grip when it chooses: the 2025–2026 prisoner releases (Test 1) were executed by presidential pardon, and the September 2026 pardon of 15 prisoners followed published criteria as his office stated them — petitions for clemency and expressed remorse (Reuters, 24 September 2026). The referees' outcomes, in those cases, changed.
  • No documented case appears in the record assembled here of Lukashenko dismissing a central bank chairman mid-term for refusing an instruction; the National Bank's chairmanship changed in March 2025 as part of the post-election reshuffle, with the outgoing prime minister, Roman Golovchenko, moved into the post by presidential appointment (Presidential press service, 10 March 2025).

Evidence against:

  • The courts are his by design. Under Article 84(10) of the Constitution, judges of the general courts are appointed by the president; under the Code on the Judiciary they are appointed for five years and may then be reappointed indefinitely — or not — with no appeal against the president's decision and no published objective criteria for reappointment (UN Working Group on Arbitrary Detention, Opinion No. 5/2024, citing the Human Rights Committee's concluding observations). A submission by the Belarusian Association of Human Rights Lawyers to the UN Special Rapporteur on the Independence of Judges and Lawyers (16 January 2026) states the position plainly: Article 6 declares separation of powers, but "in practice, virtually all branches of power in Belarus are controlled by the President," and the courts "legitimize state repression through judicial proceedings" as a result. Since 2022, Constitutional Court judges are elected and dismissed by the All-Belarusian People's Assembly on the president's proposal — and the Assembly is chaired by Lukashenko himself, elected its chairman on 24 April 2024 (Assembly record, 24 April 2024; IBAHRI submission to the Special Rapporteur, 2026).
  • The electoral commission answers upward, not outward. The CEC's members are selected through the same presidential-and-Assembly machinery the 2022 amendments created — the Assembly may select CEC members and top judges and may dispute election results — and the commission ran the 2025 election with no ODIHR observation, no overseas polling stations, and no independent domestic observation, which the Belarusian Helsinki Committee and Viasna's joint expert mission described as public control "reduced to zero" and treated as a persecuted activity (OSCE/ODIHR, 9 January 2025; Human Rights Defenders for Free Elections, preliminary analytical report on the 2025 election).
  • The prosecution and the security services are instruments, on the UN's documented finding. OHCHR found the 2020 crackdown's beatings were "approved at a high level within Government," that torture practices were replicated identically across locations "suggesting widespread, systematic practices," and that the justice system was used to silence dissent (OHCHR, A/HRC/52/68, March 2023). The UN Group of Independent Experts on Belarus has since found further evidence of the crimes against humanity of imprisonment and persecution against a segment of the population defined by its real or perceived political views — a finding the Belarusian government disputes, characterising 2020 as an attempted overthrow backed from abroad; both positions are recorded here, and the Group's finding is a finding, not an allegation (UN Group of Independent Experts reporting, 2025, as summarised by Forum 2000, March 2025; OHCHR interactive dialogue record, March 2023).
  • The media referee was not gripped but abolished as an independent institution. Belarus ranked 166th of 180 in RSF's 2025 World Press Freedom Index and 165th of 180 in 2026 (score 27.72) — the small numerical improvement expressly not, RSF says, evidence of structural liberalisation; 478 attacks or threats against media workers were recorded in 2025, 95% of them through judicial or economic mechanisms; at least 10 journalists were convicted in 2025; 39 media organisations had been designated "extremist formations" by August 2025; and the KGB designated PEN Belarus extremist on 27 February 2026 (RSF Indexes 2025 and 2026; BAJ and Justice for Journalists Foundation, Attacks on media representatives in Belarus in 2025; State Media Monitor summary, September 2026). Most independent outlets now publish from exile or have closed.
  • The central bank and the government are staffed by rotation from his own hand: in a single day, 10 March 2025, he appointed the prime minister, moved the previous prime minister to the central bank, and reshuffled the government — described even in the sympathetic Belarusian Yearbook 2026 as "a formal government renewal, while the existing management framework remained intact" (Presidential press service, 10 March 2025; Belarusian Yearbook 2026, KAS).

Why this grade: Test 2 does not ask whether a referee ever rules against the ruler's wishes; it asks whether the referees keep their autonomy. In Belarus the appointment, reappointment, and dismissal of judges, the composition of the election commission, the leadership of the prosecution, the central bank, and the government, and the chairmanship of the body that can override them all run through one office — his. The formal 2022 loosening (decrees, a strengthened premiership on paper) left every appointment lever in place, and the one genuinely independent referee that existed in 2020 — a press and a citizenry able to watch the count — has been designated extremist, exiled, or banned. Independence is not merely punished; it is structurally unavailable.

Helped / Hurt ledger: Helped — the presidency itself, which faces no institution able to say no and make it stick; the All-Belarusian People's Assembly's presidium, elevated into a parallel seat of power under his chairmanship. Hurt — judges on five-year renewable appointments, whose tenure depends on the man whose state prosecutes before them; journalists (at least 10 convicted in 2025, hundreds in exile per BAJ); voters, whose commission cannot be checked; and defendants in political cases, tried by courts the UN Working Group has found do not meet the independence requirement of Article 14 of the ICCPR (Opinion No. 5/2024).

Test 3 — ANSWERS THE WOBBLE

When courts, voters, parliament, the street, foreign partners, or events push back, does he adjust openly — or deny and double down?

GRADE: FAILS

Evidence for:

  • Under sustained United States pressure in 2025–2026 he adjusted, repeatedly and in the open, on the prisoner file. The releases were negotiated face-to-face with US envoys (Keith Kellogg in June 2025; John Coale thereafter), announced publicly, and sequenced with stated American consideration: 14 prisoners in June 2025 as sanctions talks opened; 52 on 11 September 2025 as the US lifted sanctions on the national airline Belavia; 123 on 13 December 2025 as Washington lifted sanctions on the potash sector; 250 pardoned on 19 March 2026 as the US lifted sanctions on two state banks, the Finance Ministry, and the main potash producers; Andrzej Poczobut freed on 28 April 2026 in a five-for-five border exchange; and smaller groups through September–October 2026, with the US delisting Belaruskali's general director on 21 October 2026 (Congressional Research Service, 2026; AP reporting, 11 September 2025 and 19 March 2026; IAPB 11th Progress Report, 2026; Kyiv Independent Belarus Weekly, 24 October 2026). Whatever the motive, the pushback — sanctions and isolation — produced a visible change of conduct, openly transacted.
  • On the war, he has adjusted his rhetoric toward Kyiv at the margins: Belarusian troops have not been sent to fight in Ukraine, he has repeated that they will not be ("Nobody will send you to this slaughter," to military graduates, July 2025, via Radio Liberty reporting), and in a 2025 interview he apologised to President Zelenskyy for earlier personal attacks — "If my words offended Volodymyr Zelenskyy, I apologize" (Al Arabiya interview, as reported by The Gaze, 2025).
  • On succession talk, he engaged the question rather than banning it: the August 2025 TIME interview answered the term and successor questions directly, if non-committally (Reuters, 8 August 2025).

Evidence against:

  • The defining wobble of his tenure — the street in 2020 — was answered with denial and force, and the denial has never been revised. The official result was declared final; the protests were characterised as a foreign-backed attempted overthrow; and the response, on OHCHR's documented record, was at least 13,500 arrests in five days (9–14 August 2020), including some 700 children, with beatings, torture, and sexual violence documented across all six regions (OHCHR, A/HRC/49/71, March 2022). No recount, no investigation, no concession followed — then or since. He was sworn in for the sixth term in an unpublicised ceremony in September 2020 while the protests continued (Britannica).
  • When the courts of world opinion pushed back, he gripped harder: ODIHR uninvited in 2024 and 2025, overseas voting abolished, ballot photography banned, opposition parties liquidated, and in-absentia "special proceedings" created to convict exiles and confiscate their property (2023 electoral and party legislation; BAJ, 2025). Each external criticism of the count has been answered by making the next count less checkable.
  • The prisoner releases, his strongest for-shelf item, are structured as transactions, not as an answer to the underlying pushback — and the door revolves. The released were, in the documented cases, forcibly deported: the September 2025 group to Lithuania (Nikolai Statkevich refused to cross and his whereabouts were then unknown), the December 2025 group mostly by bus to Ukraine, many without identity documents (CIVICUS Monitor, 2025; Freedom House, Freedom in the World 2026). Convictions were left in place. And new political prisoners were recognised throughout the same period — Viasna recognised 191 more between April and mid-September 2026 alone (IAPB, 11th Progress Report, 2026) — a pattern the Kyiv Independent's Belarus Weekly summarised in May 2026 as Belarus "taking new political prisoners faster than Washington can secure the release of ones imprisoned earlier." He also continues to deny the category exists: he "denies there are any political prisoners in the country" while pardoning them by the hundred (Reuters, 8 August 2025 and 24 September 2026).
  • On the question this test's other-option rule names — when another option becomes available, did he at least consider it openly? — the record shows refusal to look. In 2020 the option of a checked recount or a negotiated transition was available and was not considered in public; it was criminalised. In 2025–2026 the option of releasing prisoners unconditionally, as the UN and the exiled democratic movement proposed, was available at every release point; what was considered, openly, was only the priced version — prisoners against sanctions lines — with the unpriced version never entertained on the record. Considering an option and declining it with stated reasons would not be against-evidence; there is no documented instance of the unpriced option being weighed at all.

Why this grade: A wobble answered means the pushback changes the conduct it was pushing on. The street pushed on the count and got batons and a less checkable count. The courts of foreign opinion pushed on repression and got deportation-priced pardons alongside fresh arrests. The one genuine adjustment — the releases themselves — is real and is credited on the for shelf; people are free because of it. But it answers the sanction, not the objection: the objection was that the prisoners should not be prisoners, and that proposition he still denies outright while selling its exceptions. Deny, double down, and transact the symptoms is the documented pattern across six years of pushback.

Helped / Hurt ledger: Helped — the roughly 500-plus prisoners released in the US-brokered sequence from June 2025 onward and their families (Viasna's running total of those freed, including sentence completions, was 569 by 23 December 2025, per the Congressional Research Service); the potash sector and state banks, relieved of US sanctions; Lukashenko himself, whose isolation measurably eased, up to discussion of a US visit (Reuters, March–April 2026). Hurt — the released prisoners who were deported rather than freed at home, some stateless in practice without documents; the newly arrested who refilled the cells (191 recognised in under six months of 2026); and the 2020 protesters, whose pushback was never answered at all.

Test 4 — COUNTS EVERYONE

Does the accounting include the opposition voters, the exiles, the Polish and Lithuanian minorities, Belarusian-language speakers, political prisoners, and the poor — or are some counted out?

GRADE: FAILS

Evidence for:

  • The releases of 2025–2026 counted some of the counted-out back in, including the most excluded names in the country: Nobel Peace Prize laureate Ales Bialiatski, Maria Kolesnikova, and former presidential candidate Viktar Babaryka were freed on 13 December 2025, and Siarhei Tsikhanouski in June 2025 (AP and Moscow Times reporting, 13 December 2025; US Senate statement, December 2025). Foreign nationals were included in the releases rather than held as a separate class — citizens of the US, UK, Lithuania, Ukraine, Latvia, Australia, and Japan among the December 2025 group (Belarusian state media, as reported December 2025), and Polish activist Andrzej Poczobut in April 2026 (IAPB, 2026).
  • The state's social accounting, on its face, is universal: pensions, public healthcare, and subsidised utilities are not formally conditioned on political loyalty, and his 2025 programme promised social policy principles "unchanged" (Pozirk summary of candidates' programmes, 17 January 2025). No documented policy in the record assembled here formally excludes a named ethnic or religious group from state services.
  • Belarus took in and did not expel Ukrainian refugees in significant numbers after 2022; no mass refoulement of Ukrainians is documented in the record assembled here. (Recorded as an absence of a documented contrary policy, not as a positive programme.)

Evidence against:

  • The opposition half of the country was counted out of the count itself. In 2025, citizens abroad — several hundred thousand of whom left after 2020; OHCHR put departures at at least 100,000 by March 2023, and exile-community estimates run far higher — had no polling stations and therefore no vote (OSCE/ODIHR, 9 January 2025). Domestically, the only candidates permitted were the four pro-government party figures (Test 1). A voter who wanted anyone else had "against all" (3.60%) as the sole lawful expression.
  • The political-prisoner ledger, printed as separate sourced figures and never blended: the Belarusian authorities' position is that the number is zero — Lukashenko denies any political prisoners exist (Reuters, 2025–2026). Viasna's recognised count stood at 1,146 on 16 January 2026 (Belarusian Association of Human Rights Lawyers submission to the UN Special Rapporteur, 16 January 2026); at more than 1,100 in March 2026, after the 250-person release (Viasna via IDEA tracker, March 2026); at 881 on 26 September 2026 (IAPB, 11th Progress Report); and at 887 identified on 19 November 2026, with the whereabouts of 139 of them — 15.7% — unknown to rights defenders (Viasna via Pozirk, 20 November 2026). Since May 2020 Viasna has recognised 4,815 people as political prisoners in total and documented 8,773 convictions in politically motivated cases (IAPB, 2026). Nine political prisoners had died in custody by January 2026 (BAHRL submission, 2026); a ninth death in custody was already being recorded in September 2025 reporting (CIVICUS Monitor, 2025). The Congressional Research Service's cumulative figures — more than 50,000 arrests or detentions and some 4,370 people recognised as political prisoners at some point since 2020 — are a different measure (cumulative, not current) and are printed as such (CRS, 2026).
  • National minorities were counted out of their own schools. The revised Education Code, in force from 1 September 2022, ended instruction in minority languages: the two Polish-medium schools and two Lithuanian-medium schools in the Hrodna region were switched to Russian or Belarusian instruction, with minority languages reduced to subject classes by request and approval (Institutiones Administrationis journal study, 2025; UN Special Rapporteur reporting via the Baltic Journal of Law and Politics, 2025). Polish minority organisations were closed and their activist Andrzej Poczobut imprisoned from 2021 until the April 2026 exchange. PEN's 2025 UPR submission documents mother-tongue education bans for Polish and Lithuanian schools and the labelling of minority-support materials as "extremist" (PEN Belarus/PEN International/PEN America UPR submission, April 2025).
  • The titular nation's own language is counted second in its own state. PEN's submission records more than 5,800 violations of cultural rights since August 2020, widespread discrimination against the Belarusian language, and Belarusian-language books under particular state scrutiny, while cultural integration with Russia is officially advanced (PEN UPR submission, 2025). Belarusian speakers who made the language a civic identity after 2020 were treated as a suspect class; the language's decline in schooling and state use continued on his watch.
  • The death penalty counts Belarusians out of the European settlement entirely: Belarus remains the only country in Europe applying the death penalty, its scope expanded on his signature in March 2023 to high treason by officials and servicemen, with at least one execution confirmed and one death sentence issued in 2024, and the statistics themselves a state secret (Council of Europe/EU joint statement, 9 October 2026; capital punishment record, 2025). LGBT Belarusians are counted out by law and by platform: restrictions on LGBT expression were tightened in 2024–2025, and a registered 2025 presidential candidate campaigned on a "crackdown on LGBT" with no rebuke from the incumbent (Freedom House, 2026; Pozirk, 17 January 2025).

Why this grade: Counting everyone fails at the first step here: whole categories — voters abroad, opposition voters at home, minorities in their schools, speakers of the state language who dissent — are excluded by design before any counting begins, and the largest single ledger in the file, the political prisoners, is officially declared to be zero while the state pardons its members by the hundred. The releases and the universal social floor are real and are recorded; they are exceptions granted by the counter, not a count that includes everyone.

Helped / Hurt ledger: Helped — state-sector and pensioner constituencies whose universal benefits were protected; the freed prisoners named above; Russian-language state institutions, favoured in education and culture policy. Hurt — the exiled electorate, disenfranchised in 2025; the 881–887 people still recognised as political prisoners in autumn 2026 (and the 139 whose whereabouts are unknown); Polish and Lithuanian minority schoolchildren in the Hrodna region, who lost mother-tongue instruction in 2022; Belarusian-language cultural figures (5,800 documented cultural-rights violations since 2020, PEN); and those under sentence of death in Europe's last executing state.

Test 5 — KEEPS HIS WORD OVER TIME

Program pledges and public statements vs. the record since 1994, with the heaviest weight on the current term; the two axes — word-keeping and helped/hurt — graded independently, with all four crossings named.

GRADE: FAILS

Evidence for (promises kept or credibly in motion):

  • The alliance with Russia — kept, in full. His 2025 programme promised a strategic partnership with Russia and delivered it in the most concrete forms available: the Zapad-2025 joint exercises of 12–16 September 2025, in which Belarusian and Russian forces practised the planning and use of non-strategic nuclear weapons and the deployment of the Oreshnik missile system (Arms Control Association, October 2025; BelTA, 16 September 2025); and the Oreshnik system's entry onto combat duty in Belarus on 17 December 2025, announced by Lukashenko himself to the All-Belarusian People's Assembly (AP, 18 December 2025). Russian tactical nuclear weapons were already stationed in Belarus under the earlier security arrangements he had announced.
  • No Belarusian troops in Ukraine — kept, in its narrow letter. He has said repeatedly that Belarusian soldiers will not be sent to fight in Ukraine, and no documented deployment of Belarusian combat units into Ukraine appears in the record through November 2026 (OSW analysis, March 2022; Radio Liberty reporting, July 2025; Reuters, April 2026). Belarusian families have not been sent casualty lists; this is a kept word with real beneficiaries, and it is recorded as such.
  • Dialogue with the West — partially performed. His 2025 programme promised to improve ties with the West while blaming the West for the tension (Pozirk, 17 January 2025); the US track of 2025–2026 (Test 3) is that promise being acted on, on transactional terms.

Evidence against (promises not kept, or receding):

  • The constitution promise — his clearest word, broken in the open. On 27 November 2020, facing the protest movement, he declared: "I'm not making a constitution for myself. I will not be working with you as president with the new constitution" — reported by state media and summarised by Reuters, UPI, and the International Crisis Group as a promise to step down once a new constitution was adopted. The amended constitution was adopted by referendum on 27 February 2022. He did not step down. He chaired the new Assembly it created, stood again in January 2025, and holds the presidency under that constitution today (BelTA via Reuters/UPI, 27–28 November 2020; Crisis Group CrisisWatch, November 2020; referendum record, 27 February 2022).
  • "No threat from Belarus" — broken by geography. In September 2019 he told Ukrainian media that participation in combat operations against Ukraine "will not happen as long as I am the president of Belarus" and that Ukraine should expect no "nasty things" from Belarus (BelTA, 26 September 2019). In February 2022 Russian forces used Belarusian territory as a staging ground for the assault on Kyiv and launched missiles from it — a fact he has acknowledged while presenting it as a necessity imposed by Kyiv's policy (OSW, March 2022). The narrow troop promise (above) was kept; the broader assurance of no threat from Belarusian soil was not. Ukraine's dead from the northern axis are part of this ledger, though the primary responsibility for the invasion is Russia's and is recorded as such.
  • "There are no political prisoners" — a standing statement contradicted by his own signature. He denies the category exists (Reuters, August 2025; September 2026) while signing pardons for hundreds of people Viasna recognises under exactly that description, most recently 15 on 24 September 2026 (Reuters, 24 September 2026). A ruler's word that his own decrees refute monthly is not a word kept.
  • The nuclear-free word — reversed. Belarus's post-independence constitutions declared the aim of a nuclear-free zone and neutrality; the 2022 amendments he championed deleted the nuclear-free provision, and by December 2025 Russian nuclear-capable systems were on combat duty on Belarusian soil (2022 referendum record; AP, 18 December 2025). The change was made openly and by his own process — but it is the opposite of the standing commitment it replaced, and it was made in the same referendum package as the constitution he had promised to leave under.
  • The 2030 word — on the clock, not yet gradeable. His August 2025 statement that he is "not planning" another term, and that Nikolai is not a successor, is recorded here so the record can grade it later (Reuters, 8 August 2025). It is weaker than a pledge — "not planning" is not "will not" — and his November 2020 precedent (above) governs how much weight it can carry now: none, until performed.

The four crossings, named (word × outcome are independent axes): 1. Kept his word AND it helped people: the no-troops commitment. Belarusian servicemen and their families were spared a war he had the formal power to join; the help is real even though the same policy's other half (territory for Russia) hurt others — that hurt is recorded under crossing 4's file and in Test 3, not netted off here. 2. Kept his word AND it still hurt people: the Russia partnership. The strategic-partnership pledge of his 2025 programme was kept to the letter — exercises, nuclear sharing, Oreshnik — and the keeping deepened Belarus's subordination (the Oreshnik battalion in Belarus is subordinate to Russian Strategic Missile Forces command, leaving Minsk, on the Jamestown Foundation's assessment, with liability but without launch control) and made Belarusian soil a documented launch platform against Ukraine (Jamestown Foundation, 2026; OSW, 2022). The keeping earns the Test 5 credit recorded on the for shelf; the hurt stands in full beside it. 3. Went against his word AND it helped people: the pardons. His standing word was that there were no political prisoners and that those convicted of "extremism" were criminals serving lawful sentences; the 2025–2026 pardons went against that word in practice — hundreds walked free, including Bialiatski, Kolesnikova, and Babaryka. The break is recorded here; the help is recorded in full in Tests 3 and 4. (The help was partial in form — most were deported, convictions intact — and that limit is part of the record, not a reason to erase the crossing.) 4. Broke his word AND it hurt people: the constitution promise and the no-threat assurance. The first kept a country under a ruler who had said, on the record, that he would not serve under the new basic law; the second left Ukraine's northern border, and Belarus's own claim to neutrality, undefended by the word that had guaranteed them.

The no-good-choice clause, considered: The clause was examined for February 2022 — the decision to admit Russian forces. The options as they appeared in the moment were: refuse Moscow and risk the regime's survival against the patron on whom, after 2020, its survival depended; or comply and become, in OSW's phrase, a co-aggressor without sending troops. Each carried real harm, and the least-harm element of the choice actually taken — holding Belarusian units out of combat — is credited above. But the clause's own mechanics deny it full shelter here, for two documented reasons: the trap was substantially of his building (the dependence on Moscow was the price he chose, in 2020–2021, for surviving the street — the situation's origin is part of the record); and he was not honest inside the trap, presenting the launch-platform role publicly as no involvement at all ("declarations about Belarus's non-involvement… are not in line with reality," OSW, March 2022). The clause therefore mitigates the troop decision; it does not cover the territorial one or the denial that wrapped it.

Why this grade: A 32-year word-record reduces, on the documented evidence, to a pattern: words given to the powerful (Moscow) are kept at any cost; words given to the powerless (protesters in 2020, Ukrainians in 2019, voters reading a constitution promise) are kept only where they cost nothing, and the one that cost the seat itself was abandoned the moment the danger passed. The August 2025 "not planning" statement about 2030 will be graded when it is performed or broken; the November 2020 statement has already been both made and broken, and it is the single most on-point word a ruler can give — the word about when he will stop ruling.

Helped / Hurt ledger: Helped — Belarusian servicemen and their families (crossing 1); the pardoned and released (crossing 3); the Russian state, whose partnership pledges were all honoured. Hurt — Ukrainians attacked from Belarusian territory in 2022 (crossing 4, with primary responsibility Russia's); Belarusians who acted on the constitution promise and stood down in 2020–2022 expecting a transition that never came; and every future Belarusian officeholder and citizen who must now price a presidential assurance by this record.

Overall Summary

One law: FAILS · Without gripping: FAILS · The wobble: FAILS · Counts everyone: FAILS · His word: FAILS.

Alexander Lukashenko still holds the presidency of Belarus in November 2026 — that premise was verified, not assumed: declared re-elected on the CEC's certified figures of 86.82% on 85.69% turnout in an election no independent body was permitted to observe, sworn in on 25 March 2025, with a subordinate prime minister, Alexander Turchin, appointed by his decree a fortnight earlier, and with succession existing only as an August 2025 remark that he is "not planning" another term and is grooming no one. The grades are all FAILS, and each is earned on its own page above. One law fails because the source rules are differentiated by design — a term limit exempting its author until 2035, a party register with only his side on it, an extremism regime covering only critics, and impunity for the forces that beat the protesters while the beaten were convicted by the thousand; it helped the four registered parties and hurt the liquidated eleven, the designated media, and 5,000 unanswered torture complainants. Without gripping fails because every referee — judges on his five-year renewable appointments, the election commission, the prosecution, the central bank, the government — is staffed from his hand, and the Assembly created in 2022 sits above them all under his own chairmanship; it helped the presidency and hurt judges, journalists, voters, and defendants. The wobble fails because the great pushbacks of the tenure were met with denial and force — 13,500 arrests in five days in August 2020 and a count made steadily less checkable thereafter — while the one real adjustment, the 2025–2026 prisoner releases, answered the sanctions rather than the objection, deported the freed, and ran alongside 191 new political-prisoner recognitions in under six months of 2026; it helped the 500-plus released and the potash sector, and hurt the deported, the newly arrested, and the never-answered protesters of 2020. Counts everyone fails because the counting excludes by design — no ballots for the exiled electorate, no mother-tongue schools for the Polish and Lithuanian minorities after 2022, a suspect status for Belarusian-language civic culture, Europe's last death penalty expanded in 2023, and a political-prisoner ledger (887 recognised on 19 November 2026 by Viasna's count; zero by the president's) whose very existence he denies. His word fails on the four crossings printed in Test 5: he kept his word where it helped (no Belarusian troops in Ukraine), kept it where it hurt (the Russian partnership, to the point of Russian-commanded missiles on Belarusian soil), broke it where breaking helped (the pardons that refute his own "no political prisoners"), and broke it where breaking hurt — above all the November 2020 promise that he would not be president under the new constitution, abandoned after the constitution was adopted in February 2022, and the 2019 assurance that Ukraine had nothing to fear from Belarus, abandoned in February 2022. The no-good-choice clause was applied to the 2022 territorial decision and mitigates only its least-harm element, because the dependence that created the trap was his chosen price for surviving 2020 and the denial inside it was his own. The open files that could move these grades are named plainly: whether the remaining recognised political prisoners are released at home, unconditionally, with convictions vacated; whether any institution — a court, a commission, a parliament — is ever documented ruling against his interest and making it stick; and whether the 2030 word is performed. On the record through November 2026, none of the three has happened.
