The Ruler's Test — José Antonio Kast, President of Chile

Subject: José Antonio Kast — President of Chile since 11 March 2026; founder and longtime leader of the Republican Party, which he left on inauguration day. A lawyer and former deputy, he ran for president three times: 2017 (about 8% of the vote), 2021 (won the first round, lost the runoff to Gabriel Boric, 44.13% to 55.87%), and 2025, when he won the 14 December runoff against Jeannette Jara with 58.16% of the vote — roughly 7.26 million votes, the highest total in Chilean history — carrying all 16 regions on turnout of about 85% (Servel official count; Tricel proclamation, 5 January 2026). He governs without a majority: the combined right holds 76 of 155 seats in the Chamber of Deputies and the Senate is tied 25–25. His in-office record at grading is about eight months — thin next to a completed tenure, and this dossier says so where the thinness matters. His prior public record — three campaigns and a documented history reaching back to the 1988 plebiscite — is graded where it bears on the tests. Graded: November 2026, on the documented public record. Method: Each test is graded only on the evidence listed under it. Both shelves are shown — the strongest evidence that he passes, then the strongest evidence that he fails. Allegations are labeled ALLEGATION and never stated as verdicts. Contested items are named as contested, with who disputes them. Seat-holders and public figures are named; people below the public eye appear by role only, unless the documented record itself establishes their wrongdoing. Grades: HOLDS / FAILS / MIXED / NOT ENOUGH EVIDENCE. This record is correctable: new evidence changes grades, in the open.

Test 1 — GIVES BY ONE LAW

Same rules for allies and opponents, insiders and outsiders — no favorites at the source.

GRADE: FAILS

Evidence for:

  • His founding commitment was stated in exactly this test's terms. On victory night, 14 December 2025, he promised: "Vamos a restablecer la ley, vamos a restablecer el respeto a la ley en todas las regiones, sin excepciones, sin privilegios políticos, sin privilegios administrativos, sin privilegios judiciales" — the law restored "without exceptions, without political privileges, without administrative privileges, without judicial privileges" — and "Yo voy a ser el presidente de todos los chilenos, sin exclusión" (victory speech, 14 December 2025).
  • No documented case in the record reviewed shows Kast shielding a personal ally or donor from prosecution, and Chile's prosecutorial and comptroller institutions have operated against his own government's interests in public during his eight months (Tests 2 and 3).
  • When the sentence-reduction scandal broke (below), he did order a case-by-case review of the decrees, a suspension of pending procedures, and an urgent bill to close the statutory gap prospectively (presidential address, November 2026; La Tercera reporting via The Boston Report, 9 November 2026).

Evidence against:

  • The anchor fact of his first year is a two-track law administered by his own Justice Ministry. An investigation by the outlet Interferencia, revealed in October 2026, documented 56 sentence-reduction decrees signed by Justice Minister Fernando Rabat's ministry between March and September 2026: 27 benefiting men convicted of sexual abuse of minors and 29 convicted of rape, with three benefits tied to crimes against humanity committed by dictatorship-era agents. On 23 November 2026 the ministry delivered the full file to the congressional review commission: 1,886 sentence-reduction decrees signed since Kast took office, of which Gendarmería data show 1,408 took effect and released people — 564 convicted of robbery, 504 of drug offenses, and 95 of sexual offenses; the beneficiaries carried 1,911 convictions in total (Interferencia investigation, October 2026; Poder y Liderazgo; NPR, November 2026; The Clinic, November 2026).
  • The ministry's defense — that the reductions were legally required where applicants met the requirements, because Supreme Court jurisprudence barred applying the later statutory exclusion for sex crimes to offenses committed before that reform — is contested on the record by the two authorities best placed to know: former Justice Minister Jaime Gajardo stated that the justice ministers of both the Piñera and Boric governments rejected such reductions, and that the questioned reductions were granted administratively, with no individual court orders obliging them; and Supreme Court president Gloria Ana Chevesich publicly contradicted Rabat's claim to have acted in accordance with the Court's jurisprudence (Gajardo and Chevesich statements, November 2026).
  • The beneficiaries were not chosen by a rule applied to everyone; they were chosen by discretion, and the discretion ran one way — toward convicted sex offenders and dictatorship-era agents, categories with no lobby but the minister's own signature. A law that releases a child rapist by administrative grace while the president promises "no exceptions" on stage is not one law; it is two.
  • Context the record supplies, stated as context and not as wrongdoing: Reuters reported on the cabinet's announcement (21 January 2026) that it included two lawyers who had defended Augusto Pinochet, among them Rabat. Defending a client is a lawyer's work and is not graded against anyone; it is recorded here because the ministry those appointments staffed is the one that signed the decrees above.

Test 2 — HOLDS WITHOUT GRIPPING

Do the courts, Congress, the comptroller, the central bank, and the media keep their autonomy — or is independence punished and control centralized?

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • The courts have ruled on his program through the normal process and in public: the Constitutional Tribunal upheld his over-65 property-tax law, including its municipal compensation mechanism, on 2 October 2026 — a ruling for his side, reached by the institution's own process rather than by pressure anyone has documented.
  • The Comptroller General investigated his own government: a social-media video published by the government on 24 March 2026 claiming "the state is bankrupt" was deleted within hours and prompted a Comptroller investigation (presidency record, 2026). No retaliation against the Comptroller is documented.
  • The judiciary corrected his minister to his face and kept its standing: Supreme Court president Gloria Ana Chevesich publicly contradicted Justice Minister Rabat's account of the Court's jurisprudence in the sentence-reduction scandal (November 2026). She remains in post; no punishment, transfer, or institutional reprisal is documented.
  • Congress has repeatedly made him negotiate rather than obeyed him. In the 2027 budget fight, the Chamber of Deputies rejected Article 3, the authorization to issue up to US$25.6 billion in debt; Finance Minister Jorge Quiroz had to reinstate it in the Senate with concessions, including US$160 million to the Autonomous Pension Protection Fund, winning 31 votes including Communist senators (budget reporting, November 2026). The constitutional accusation against Rabat, filed by opposition deputies on 12 November 2026, is proceeding through Congress's own machinery.
  • The Central Bank's autonomy is intact on the record reviewed: it held its policy rate at 4.5% through 2026 on its own reasoning, and on 18 November 2026 it published third-quarter figures deeply damaging to the government — GDP down 1.1% year-on-year, investment down 6.7% — without interference documented by anyone (Central Bank of Chile, 18 November 2026). No documented pressure on the Bank was found in this research; that absence is recorded as a finding, not assumed as a permanent condition.
  • The press that damaged him most — Interferencia's decree investigation, The Clinic's Gendarmería figures, El País's migration reporting — published freely and set the national agenda for weeks. No documented case of state pressure on media was found in this research pass; the file on this point is thin and is stated as thin.

Evidence against:

  • Much of the autonomy above is structural rather than chosen: he holds 76 of 155 Chamber seats and a tied Senate, so Congress can resist him whether he wishes it or not. The test credits what he did with the room he had; the room itself was not his gift.
  • Where his government did control the process, it narrowed it. Rabat did not appear before the constitutional-accusation commission, sending a lawyer with a written defense (filed 20 November 2026), and the ministry delivered the 1,886 decree files anonymized and under reserve; commission president Raúl Soto (PPD) alleged the delivery obstructed Congress's oversight function (commission record, November 2026). That allegation is the commission president's; the non-appearance and the anonymized delivery are documented facts.
  • The government's first instinct in the scandal was to frame scrutiny as conspiracy: government spokesman Gonzalo Arenas called Rabat's legal work "impecable" and suggested a political operation by the opposition, hours before the president's own address conceded the state's failure (November 2026).

Test 3 — ANSWERS THE WOBBLE

When courts, voters, Congress, or events push back, does he adjust openly — or deny and double down?

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • His prior record contains a model concession: on 19 December 2021, about an hour after polls closed in the runoff he had lost to Gabriel Boric, Kast conceded publicly — "I have just talked to Gabriel Boric and have congratulated him for his big triumph. From now on he is the president-elect of Chile and deserves all of our respect and constructive collaboration. Chile is always first" — and visited Boric's headquarters the same day (concession statement and contemporaneous reporting, 19 December 2021).
  • On fuel, the adjustment was real. After his government let fuel prices pass through (period increases reported at +32% for gasoline and +62% for diesel amid the 2026 oil shock) and the Fuerza Norte truckers' confederation began an indefinite strike on 10 November 2026, blockading two highways, the government backed down within days: a bill reversing the 42-peso diesel and 39-peso gasoline increases and freezing prices to 3 February 2027, with a further 100-peso-per-liter diesel cut reported by Lusa; the strike ended (NPR, 14 November 2026; Lusa, November 2026).
  • On the sentence-reduction scandal, the president himself took the correction onto national television: "El Estado les falló" — "The State failed you" — addressed to victims, promising responsibility "beyond the Executive" and choosing, in his words, "el camino más difícil," the harder path. Rabat suspended pending procedures and ordered a case-by-case review, and the government sent an urgent bill to close the pre-2022 loophole and asked Congress to fast-track a repeal bill and a "Nunca más" reform (presidential address, November 2026; La Tercera via The Boston Report, 9 November 2026).
  • On the migration data-sharing amendment (Test 4), when health and education institutions objected, the migration service director publicly promised a redraft "para darle tranquilidad a todo el mundo" — to give everyone peace of mind (SERMIG director Frank Sauerbaum, May 2026).
  • He adjusted his cabinet early rather than riding out failure: the 19 May 2026 reshuffle — the fastest of any government since 1990 — replaced the Security Minister and the government spokesperson (MercoPress, May 2026).

Evidence against:

  • The fuel adjustment came only after an indefinite strike and highway blockades, and after a documented refusal: in July 2026 the government had chosen not to use an available anti-inflation tool to ease fuel prices (NPR, 14 November 2026). The correction was priced by the street, not volunteered by the palace.
  • The Rabat correction stopped exactly where it would have cost him his minister. On 10 November 2026, after the "El Estado les falló" address, Kast met coalition leaders and ratified his support for Rabat; spokesman Arenas ruled out any cabinet change ("No hay cambios de gabinete"). The public had already ruled: Cadem (1 November 2026) found 70% of Chileans opposed keeping Rabat in the post and 78% disapproved of him. The decrees were reviewed; the man who signed them was kept (coalition meeting reporting, 10 November 2026; Cadem, November 2026).
  • The first response to the decree revelations was denial, not adjustment — "impecable" work, an opposition "political operation" (Test 2) — and the presidential correction arrived only after the story had run for weeks and his approval had fallen to the mid-20s (Cadem: 26% on 1 November, 29% on 8 November; Criteria, 15 November 2026: 18% approval, 72% disapproval).
  • His May 2026 handling of his signature promise was a wobble answered by redefinition: confronted with the gap between the promised expulsion of 300,000 and the record, he called the promise "una metáfora" — a metaphor — and the next day corrected himself to "quizás la palabra era hipérbole, no metáfora" (El País, 15 May 2026). That episode is graded again under Test 5, where it belongs; here it counts as the pattern: adjust the words before adjusting the policy.

Test 4 — COUNTS EVERYONE

Does the accounting include the whole flock — migrants, Mapuche communities, the poor, his opponents' voters — or are some counted out?

GRADE: FAILS

Evidence for:

  • Toward his opponents' voters, the opening record is inclusionary in form and partly in substance: the victory-night promise to be "presidente de todos los chilenos, sin exclusión"; his public request, in the same speech, for respect toward the defeated Jara ("Alguien puede ser de una ideología distinta, pero es una persona igual que nosotros" — someone can hold a different ideology, but they are a person just like us); a cabinet announced 20 January 2026 mixing his own coalition with figures from the former Concertación, whom he publicly defended; and his resignation from the Republican Party on 11 March 2026, hours before taking office, fulfilling his debate promise to govern as president of all Chileans rather than as a party leader (victory speech, 14 December 2025; cabinet reporting, January 2026; party resignation, 11 March 2026).
  • The state's ordinary counting continued for everyone in at least some domains: a Cancer Health Alert decree (23 March 2026) under which more than 20,000 pending cases were attended in the first official report, and a crime record that improved on the government's own figures — homicide victims down 16.9% year-on-year to 1 November 2026 (922 to 766), with the caveat, noted by fact-checkers, that the downward trend in homicides began in 2023, before his government (Subsecretaría de Prevención del Delito balance, November 2026; FactChecking UC, June 2026).

Evidence against:

  • His migration program counts a class of residents out by design, in the program's own documents. The day-one package (Border Shield Plan / Plan Escudo Fronterizo) combined a 3,000-strong joint military-police border task force with powers to detain, redirect, and immediately deport; a 10-kilometer border exclusion zone in which any undocumented person may be automatically detained; new detention facilities; exclusion of undocumented migrants from public services and benefits; and suspension of the previous government's regularization program, which covered up to 182,000 people, 91% of them Venezuelan (program documents as compiled in the Mixed Migration Centre's 2026 annual review).
  • The implementation to 20 November 2026 (official Interior/SERMIG balance): 13 expulsion charter flights; 1,972 people expelled since 11 March 2026 (+55.7% against all of 2025); 12,488 voluntary departures between January and 19 November, of whom 10,116 (80.9%) were Venezuelan. No Venezuelans were on the deportation flights — Chile has no diplomatic relations with Caracas, so the largest undocumented group, roughly 65% of the total, cannot be returned by air at all; the humanitarian corridor Kast proposed as president-elect (raised with Argentina's Milei and Ecuador's Noboa in December 2025) was publicly declined by Peru's acting president José Jerí on 13 January 2026 and never materialized. The counted-out are thus counted out twice: excluded from services at home, and unreturnable abroad.
  • The counting-out reaches into the clinic and the kindergarten. A government amendment presented in the Senate in May 2026 would oblige state bodies, public and private health institutions, pension bodies, and educational establishments including kindergartens to hand migration authorities the address, phone, and email of foreigners subject to migration proceedings. Interior Undersecretary Máximo Pavez presented it ("nadie va a perseguir a niños" — nobody is going to persecute children; the target is responsible adults' data); public-health academics warned it would deter care and vaccination and breach the firewall between health and enforcement; Kast defended the measure personally: "La única manera de ordenar [las prestaciones sociales] es saber quiénes utilizan los distintos servicios públicos" — the only way to order social benefits is to know who uses public services (El País, 15 May 2026; Radio Universidad de Chile, 21 May 2026). He also floated reinstalling police presence in health centers to verify identities (per academic Andrea Álvarez, Radio Universidad de Chile, May 2026). A parallel congressional bill his side supports (Boletín 17.474-06, approved by the Chamber on 24 March 2026, 95–48, and pending in the Senate) would restrict irregular migrants' access to state-funded benefits, prioritize nationals in health and education, and bar the issuing of ID numbers to those who entered at unauthorized crossings; a civil-society declaration of April 2026 with 160 signatories warned it would exclude migrant children from health and education (Chamber record, 24 March 2026; civil-society declaration, April 2026).
  • The Mapuche south remains under a military emergency regime on his watch, continued by his signature: the state of emergency in La Araucanía and Arauco/Biobío, first decreed in May 2022, has been extended under Kast in successive 30-day congressional approvals — his first on 24 March 2026, the most recent approved by the Senate on 12 November 2026 by 32–3 — reaching roughly 1,410 days of continuous emergency rule by March 2026 (National Congress Library report, April 2026; Senate record, 12 November 2026). Each extension passed Congress and senators recorded that violence has decreased; the regime is lawful and voted. It is also, in its fourth year, the standing condition of one named people in the republic, and in November 2026 his government advanced a change to Article 13 of the Indigenous Law allowing indigenous land to be leased to non-indigenous persons or companies for up to 10 years, and up to 25 with CONADI consultation — a proposal originating in the previous government's Peace Commission, now carried by his, and opposed by Mapuche organizations who warn it weakens the legal protection of indigenous land (Mapuche Diario, 20 November 2026; the criticism is the organizations', recorded as theirs).

Test 5 — KEEPS HIS WORD OVER TIME

Campaign promises vs. the record since March 2026, with dates; his long public arc stated exactly as documented.

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for (promises kept or credibly in motion):

  • Party resignation — kept, exactly. Promised in the Anatel debate and the victory speech; done on 11 March 2026, hours before the handover (Test 4).
  • Corporate tax cut — legislated on the promised path. The campaign promised 27% → 23%. The Ley de Reconstrucción Nacional (Law 21.850, promulgated 9 October 2026, published 17 October) phases the rate 27% → 25.5% (2027 income) → 24% (2028) → 23% (from 2029) — the promise converted into law on its stated schedule, not yet in effect (Law 21.850; KPMG GMS Alert, 5 November 2026).
  • Over-65 property-tax exemption and housing VAT exemption — legislated. Both campaign commitments are in the same law: exemption on a first home for those over 65 from 1 January 2027 with municipalities compensated (upheld by the Constitutional Tribunal, 2 October 2026), and a one-year VAT exemption on first sales of new homes from December 2026 (Law 21.850).
  • The "emergency government" framing — kept as a description of his own conduct. His first address (11 March 2026) framed an emergency government for a country "riddled with organized crime and weak finances" (Reuters, 11 March 2026), and the security record he promised to move did move on the government's figures (Test 4), alongside 21 security bills of which two were law and three dispatched by November 2026 (Libertad y Desarrollo assessment, 5 November 2026).

Evidence against (promises not kept, or receding):

  • The 300,000 expulsions — withdrawn by redefinition. The campaign promised to expel more than 300,000 undocumented migrants, with a public countdown demanding self-deportation by 11 March 2026. In May 2026 Kast said the day-one expulsion promise had been "una metáfora," then "hipérbole" (El País, 15 May 2026). The record at 20 November 2026: 1,972 expulsions and about 14,757 total departures against an undocumented population estimated at roughly 330,000 (Interior/SERMIG balance, November 2026). A promise its author renames a figure of speech has not been kept; it has been retired.
  • The border trenches — not kept on their own terms. On 16 March 2026 the government stated Army border works would grow from about 3,000 meters to more than 30,000 meters within 90 days. By July 2026 roughly 11 kilometers had been built, against a commitment by then reduced to 13.35 km (Chacalluta's 6.75 km finished; Colchane at 4.18 of 6.6 km); the 90-day window closed in mid-June with no source reporting 30 km (government statements, March 2026; Votum promise tracker, checked 16 November 2026: "Not kept").
  • The US$6 billion spending cut — receding. The campaign promised a US$6 billion cut within 18 months. March 2026 brought a first stage (3% across ministries; the 2026 cut begun at about US$3.8 billion per Ex-Ante); by 22 April 2026 the Budget Director put the cut actually achieved at about US$1.98 billion (Budget Directorate statements, April 2026). The inherited position is documented and is not his making — the previous government missed its structural target for a third consecutive year (2025 structural deficit 3.6% of GDP against a −1.6% target; El Mercurio, 27 March 2026), and the Autonomous Fiscal Council warned the inherited 2026 revenue projections were overestimated. But his own fiscal-policy decree (published 22 September 2026) set structural targets looser than the path he inherited on paper (−2.6% for 2026 easing to −1.5% by 2030), and the CFA's November 2026 report warned its base scenario puts debt above 50% of GDP in 2029 and near 55% in 2030. The austerity promise and the arithmetic are moving apart.
  • The outcome at grading: Central Bank figures of 18 November 2026 show third-quarter GDP down 1.1% year-on-year, unemployment at 9.8% with more than a million jobless for the first time since 2020, and copper production at two-decade lows despite record prices earlier in the year (Central Bank of Chile; Cochilco projections, November 2026). Eight months is early, and the inheritance was real; the figures are recorded because his campaign promised the opposite direction.

The long arc, stated exactly as documented. This subsection belongs to this test because the test is his word over time, and his longest-held stated positions concern the dictatorship era.

  • 1988: As a law student, Kast appeared in the televised campaign for the "Sí" — the vote to keep Augusto Pinochet in power in the 1988 plebiscite — in his own words: "quiero contarte tres puntos de por qué mi Sí en el plebiscito" (campaign broadcast, 1988).
  • 2017: In his first presidential campaign he said of Pinochet: "Si Pinochet estuviera vivo, votaría por mí" — "If Pinochet were alive, he would vote for me" — adding that they would have had tea together in La Moneda and that Pinochet's government, "for the development of the country, was better than Piñera's," "separating the human rights issue" (statements compiled by AP, December 2021). On CNN's Aquí está Chile (2017) he said "en el gobierno militar se hicieron muchas cosas por los derechos humanos" — many things were done for human rights under the military government. He defended Miguel Krassnoff, the former DINA brigadier convicted to more than 1,000 years in prison for crimes against humanity: "Es una buena persona y los jueces probablemente se equivocaron" — "He is a good person and the judges probably made mistakes" (T13 Radio, 2017; Radio Universidad de Chile, 18 September 2025).
  • 2025: He avoided the dictatorship topic entirely — analysts described a "campaign of silence"; he did not renounce his earlier views (EFE reporting, 2025). As president-elect he let it be understood he could pardon those convicted of crimes against humanity on grounds of advanced age or health (post-election commentary summarized by La Base; recorded here as reported intention, not as a formal announcement). In office, three of his Justice Ministry's sentence-reduction benefits reached crimes-against-humanity convicts (Test 1).
  • His father, as documented and as disputed: AP reported on 9 December 2021 that a German Federal Archive identity card shows an 18-year-old named Michael Kast joining the Nazi Party (NSDAP) on 1 September 1942, membership number 9271831; the archive could not itself confirm the card was his father's, but the birth date and place on the card match Michael Kast's (journalist Mauricio Weibel first published the card on 1 December 2021). José Antonio Kast's responses are on the record: his father was a forced conscript in the German army; in a 2018 television appearance he angrily rejected the adjective "Nazi," said he was proud of his father, and argued that "when there is a war and (military) enrollment is mandatory, a 17 or 18 year old doesn't have the option to say, 'I'm not going,' because they will be court martialed" (AP, 9 December 2021). The card's existence and data are documented; its attribution to his father is contested — by Kast, on the conscription account above — and is graded here as contested, not as established. A further claim, that the Kast family collaborated with the regime in the repression of farmers at Paine, is an ALLEGATION (summarized by the Rosa-Luxemburg-Stiftung), denied by Kast, and recorded as allegation only. His brother Miguel Kast was Pinochet's minister and Central Bank president — a documented fact of family history, recorded as context and as nothing more.

Overall Summary

One law: FAILS · Without gripping: MIXED · The wobble: MIXED · Counts everyone: FAILS · His word: MIXED.

José Antonio Kast won the largest vote in Chilean history on a promise stated in this test's own language — the law restored "without exceptions, without privileges" — and in eight months his Justice Ministry signed 1,886 sentence-reduction decrees, among them 56 benefiting men convicted of raping children and women and three benefiting men convicted of crimes against humanity, under a legal theory the president of the Supreme Court publicly contradicted and that his predecessors in the same ministry had refused to use. He addressed the victims on national television, said "the State failed you," ordered every case reviewed — and kept the minister who signed them, against the stated view of 70% of the country. That single file carries both of his failing grades' weight and most of his wobble grade's: the correction was real, and it stopped at the door of his own cabinet. Around it, the institutional record is genuinely mixed rather than captured — a tied Senate that made him buy his budget back with concessions, a comptroller who investigated his government, a central bank publishing his recession, courts ruling in the open — though how much of that openness is his choice and how much is the arithmetic of 76 seats in 155, eight months cannot yet separate. His word splits cleanly in two: the economic promises were legislated on schedule almost to the letter, while the signature promise of the campaign — 300,000 expulsions — was renamed a metaphor by its own author, with 1,972 expulsions on the official count. And the flock is counted with a named exception written into the program itself: the undocumented, most of them Venezuelan, placed outside the services of the state by design, reported on by their own schools and clinics under his amendment, and unreturnable to a country Chile does not recognize. The in-office file is eight months old; every grade here is correctable in the open, and several — above all the grip — will mean more at eighteen months than they can at eight.
