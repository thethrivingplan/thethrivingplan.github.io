The Ruler's Test — Hun Manet, Prime Minister of Cambodia

Subject: Hun Manet — Prime Minister of Cambodia since 22 August 2023. The seat graded is the premiership, and it is graded as it is actually held, which requires the family structure around it to be stated plainly. Hun Manet, born 1977, a four-star general and former commander of the Royal Cambodian Army (West Point; PhD in economics, University of Bristol), succeeded his father Hun Sen, who had held the premiership since 1985. The succession followed the 23 July 2023 general election, in which the National Election Committee had disqualified the main opposition Candlelight Party and the ruling Cambodian People's Party (CPP) took 120 of 125 National Assembly seats (FUNCINPEC took five); the Assembly then approved Hun Manet and he was sworn in on 22 August 2023. Hun Sen did not leave the board: he remains president of the CPP — a post he has said he intends to hold for life — became president of the Senate on 3 April 2024 following the February 2024 Senate election, and chairs the Supreme Privy Council to the King, a position ranked equal to the prime minister. As Senate president he serves as acting head of state when King Norodom Sihamoni is abroad, and in that capacity it was Hun Sen, not the King, who signed the May 2026 royal pardon of opposition leader Kem Sokha. Hun Manet is a CPP vice-president (since December 2023); his brother Hun Many is minister of civil service and his brother Hun Manith heads military intelligence. Hun Sen has publicly chided his son's government on occasion and announced at least one major policy reversal (the September 2024 CLV withdrawal) in his own name alongside the prime minister's. None of this makes the premiership ceremonial — Hun Manet chairs the cabinet, directs the security and economic apparatus, and personally fronted the two defining files of his tenure, the 2025 border war with Thailand and the cyberscam crackdown — but the grades below grade the seat inside that structure, and where the decisive hand on an item was the father's, the record says so. He is graded on roughly three years and three months in office. Graded: November 2026, on the documented public record. Method: Each test is graded only on the evidence listed under it. Both shelves are shown — the strongest evidence that he passes, then the strongest evidence that he fails. Allegations are labeled ALLEGATION and never stated as verdicts. Contested items are named as contested, with who disputes them. Seat-holders and public figures are named; people below the public eye appear by role only, unless the documented record itself establishes their wrongdoing. Grades: HOLDS / FAILS / MIXED / NOT ENOUGH EVIDENCE. This record is correctable: new evidence changes grades, in the open.

Test 1 — GIVES BY ONE LAW

Same rules for allies and opponents, insiders and outsiders — no favorites at the source.

GRADE: FAILS

Evidence for:

  • The cyberscam crackdown eventually reached the single most connected insider in the country. Chen Zhi, chairman of the Prince Group, had been an official adviser to Hun Sen and then, from 2023, to Hun Manet himself. After the US Department of Justice indicted him on 14 October 2025 (wire fraud and money-laundering conspiracy, with a forfeiture action over some 127,271 bitcoin valued at roughly USD 15 billion) and the US and UK sanctioned more than 140 Prince-linked entities and individuals, Cambodia revoked his Cambodian citizenship by royal decree in December 2025, arrested him on 6 January 2026, and deported him to China to face charges there (US Department of Justice, October 2025; Cambodian Ministry of Foreign Affairs statements, January 2026; Mekong Independent, January 2026). The enforcement continued through 2026 on the record of the government's own anti-scam commission: by October 2026 Cambodian courts had frozen nearly USD 1 billion in Prince-linked assets, investigators had mapped 198 connected companies (98 registered in Cambodia), identified 28 casino locations as implicated, obtained arrest warrants for 32 people, and — per the state news agency — 18 casino licences had been revoked and nine suspended as of 31 August 2026 (Commission for Combating Technology-enabled Scams disclosure via Agence Kampuchea Presse, October 2026). Six alleged network leaders were named as arrested, including a former director of the Jin Bei Group who had himself been US- and UK-sanctioned in 2025 (AKP, October 2026). The underlying conduct of every person named remains an ALLEGATION until a court rules; what is documented is that the state's machinery moved, at scale, against the prime minister's own former adviser and the largest conglomerate in the country.
  • The wider crackdown was not confined to one network. After Hun Manet's July 2025 directive, police detained more than 1,000 foreign workers within a day (CamboJA News, July 2025); officials later announced 5,106 online-scam suspects arrested over seven months across 23 nationalities, 4,534 of them deported, and the Immigration Department reported more than 13,500 foreigners deported in 2025, up 57 percent on the year before, largely from scam-compound raids (Cambodian officials via Asia Sentinel, 2026; Immigration Department via Mekong Independent, January 2026).

Evidence against:

  • For speech, the law runs on a separate, faster track. The criminal code's incitement and "plotting" provisions are the standard instruments against critics: ten Mother Nature Cambodia environmental activists were sentenced on 2 July 2024 to six to eight years for "plotting" over peaceful activism; award-winning journalist Mech Dara — known for exposing the scam compounds — was arrested by military police on 30 September 2024 on an incitement charge over social media posts and released on bail three weeks later only after a filmed apology to Hun Sen and Hun Manet, after which he quit journalism (Human Rights Watch; CamboJA; RFA, October 2024). A domestic worker deported from Malaysia at Cambodia's request in late 2024 was convicted in April 2025 in two cases — incitement and insulting the king — over Facebook posts criticizing Hun Sen, receiving two 18-month sentences (CIVICUS Monitor, 2025). Human Rights Watch counted 88 political prisoners as of November 2025, and records that detainees are offered release in exchange for a filmed apology and a request to join the CPP, with more than 90 such confessions published since 2018 (Human Rights Watch, World Report 2026 and country page).
  • The scam industry's protection years are documented by the governments now prosecuting it. The US State Department's 2025 Trafficking in Persons Report placed Cambodia among the countries with a government pattern of trafficking in online scam operations, recording that senior government officials reportedly benefited from the operations and that officials intimidated victims and witnesses and impeded investigations (US Department of State, TIP Report 2025, via OSAC country security report, March 2026). UNODC estimates at least 100,000 people worked in the industry inside Cambodia while it operated openly (UNODC via Asia Sentinel, 2026). When the US sanctioned sitting CPP senator and tycoon Ly Yong Phat in September 2024 over forced labor in scam operations, no Cambodian proceeding against him appears in the documented record assembled here.
  • The most serious claim reaches the seat itself, and is recorded at its proper weight. A June 2025 report by Jacob Sims, a visiting fellow at Harvard's Asia Center, directly implicated Hun Manet, a deputy prime minister, and 28 other senior government and business figures in the scam economy. This is an ALLEGATION — a researcher's report, not a court finding; the Cambodian government has dismissed such reporting, and no judicial body has ruled on it. It is recorded here because Test 1 grades the source of the law, and the allegation exists in the documented record; it is not treated as established fact (Asia Sentinel, 2026).
  • The one time the system's chief opponent was freed, both the conviction and the mercy ran through the family. Kem Sokha's 27-year treason sentence (March 2023, imposed before Hun Manet took office) was upheld by the Phnom Penh Court of Appeal on 30 April 2026; weeks later he received a royal pardon — signed by Hun Sen as acting head of state — which freed him from house arrest but left his conviction standing and a five-year ban on political activity and foreign travel in place. The UN human rights office welcomed the release while urging that the conviction be quashed entirely (International IDEA democracy tracker, May 2026; OHCHR statement, May 2026).
  • The outcome measure has not moved: Cambodia's Corruption Perceptions Index score fell from 22 in 2023 to 21 in 2024 (158th of 180) and 20 in the 2025 index (Transparency International, CPI 2024 and CPI 2025).

Test 2 — HOLDS WITHOUT GRIPPING

Do the courts, the electoral commission, the media, the prosecution, and the central bank keep their autonomy — or is independence punished and control centralized?

GRADE: FAILS

Evidence for:

  • The honest shelf here is thin, and its thinness is itself recorded. No documented instance appears in the record assembled here of Hun Manet dismissing a judge, a central bank governor, or an electoral official mid-term in retaliation for a specific adverse ruling: the subordination of Cambodia's institutions is structural and inherited — built over his father's 38 years — rather than seized by him in office. The qualification matters and cuts both ways: in three years no major institution ruled against the government in a major case, so the absence of punishment is untested rather than proven restraint.
  • The May 2026 pardon of Kem Sokha (Test 1) is the tenure's one documented release of pressure at the top of the system: the country's most prominent opposition figure walked out of house arrest. Its mechanism — conviction upheld by the appeal court weeks earlier, pardon signed by the prime minister's father as acting head of state, political ban retained — is recorded on the other shelf.
  • The courts did process the largest insider case in the country's history through judicial instruments: the roughly USD 1 billion in Prince Group asset freezes and the casino-licence actions of 2026 were executed as court orders and regulatory decisions, publicly itemized by the anti-scam commission (AKP, October 2026). The referees acted; the target was the executive's.

Evidence against:

  • The electoral referee barred the opposition from the election that seated him, and has kept barring it. The National Election Committee disqualified the Candlelight Party from the July 2023 election on a documentation ground, the Constitutional Council upheld the disqualification, and the CPP took 120 of 125 seats. Under Hun Manet the pattern continued: Candlelight was barred from the February 2024 Senate election, in which the CPP took 55 of the 58 elected seats (the Senate is chosen by commune councillors); its vice-president Thach Setha is among more than 40 opposition figures detained or imprisoned (Human Rights Watch, 2025–2026). In September 2026 the Senate approved amendments to the commune administration law altering how commune council leadership seats are distributed — a change International IDEA and Human Rights Watch assess as substantially extending CPP control to the local level ahead of the June 2027 commune elections, whose councillors in turn elect the Senate (International IDEA, September 2026).
  • The judiciary's alignment is the monitors' central finding. Human Rights Watch's country assessment states that Cambodia is effectively a single-party state with "ruling party control of all state institutions including the judiciary," and CIVICUS records a judiciary that "continues to operate in a politicised and opaque manner," with bail routinely denied to activists and prolonged pretrial detention used to silence dissent (Human Rights Watch country page, 2026; CIVICUS Monitor, 2025). Freedom House rates Cambodia Not Free, 22 of 100, for 2026. The Mother Nature convictions (Test 1), the upheld Sokha conviction, and three further activists sentenced to seven years on 2 September 2026 all ran through these courts during his tenure (Human Rights Watch, 2026).
  • The media space narrowed further on his watch, by regulation rather than only by prosecution. In December 2025 the Ministry of Information tightened press accreditation for 2026, requiring a "letter of good conduct" from local authorities and that applicants face no pending legal actions or complaints — a condition press-freedom advocates note is easily manufactured against reporters through strategic lawsuits; officials increasingly refuse to speak to unaccredited journalists (International IDEA, December 2025; CamboJA via IFEX). Environmental journalist Chhoeung Chheng was shot on 4 December 2024 while investigating illegal logging and died three days later (Human Rights Watch, 2025).
  • The seat itself sits inside a family command structure that no referee checks. Hun Sen retains the CPP presidency (which he says he will hold for life), the Senate presidency, the Supreme Privy Council chairmanship ranked equal to the prime minister, and the acting head-of-state function; he announced the September 2024 CLV withdrawal in his own statement, has publicly corrected his son's government, and signed the Sokha pardon. The prime minister's brothers run the civil service ministry and military intelligence (RFA and analyst reporting, 2024). A premiership exercised inside that structure may be energetic — this one is — but the institutions that could say no to it have no documented instance of doing so.

Test 3 — ANSWERS THE WOBBLE

When courts, voters, parliament, the street, foreign partners, or events push back, does he adjust openly — or deny and double down?

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • The street's largest pushback of his tenure produced a genuine policy reversal. When criticism of the Cambodia–Laos–Vietnam Development Triangle Area (CLV-DTA) — a 25-year pact covering four northeastern provinces — erupted in August 2024 into overseas protests and planned demonstrations in Phnom Penh, the government withdrew from the agreement entirely: announced 20 September 2024, formalized by a unanimous National Assembly repeal that November, with Hun Manet stating the decision took "into account people's concerns about territory" and notifying Hanoi and Vientiane directly (RFA, September–November 2024; contemporaneous wire reporting). The reversal's other half is on the against shelf.
  • The border war was answered, three times, with signed de-escalation rather than doubling down. After clashes began on 24 July 2025, Hun Manet accepted an immediate, unconditional ceasefire at the Putrajaya meeting chaired by Malaysian Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim on 28 July 2025 — four days into the fighting — and Cambodia simultaneously pursued the legal channel, applying to the International Court of Justice in June 2025 over the disputed temple areas and requesting UN Security Council attention in July. On 26 October 2025 he signed the Kuala Lumpur Joint Declaration with Thai Prime Minister Anutin Charnvirakul, witnessed by Presidents Trump and Anwar, committing to heavy-weapons withdrawal, ASEAN observation, joint demining, and prisoner release (Malaysian PMO joint statement, 28 July 2025; Kuala Lumpur Joint Declaration, 26 October 2025). When fighting resumed in December, he accepted a second ceasefire on 27 December 2025 and defended it publicly on explicitly civilian grounds: his 29 December message stated the government had prioritized protecting life, citing more than half a million displaced and 32 civilians killed, and secured the release of the 18 Cambodian soldiers held by Thailand since July, who were repatriated on 31 December 2025 under ASEAN and ICRC observation (AKP, 27–31 December 2025).
  • Foreign economic pushback was negotiated, not denounced. Facing a threatened 49 percent US tariff in 2025, his government negotiated the rate down to 19 percent (effective August 2025) and he wrote to President Trump; after the July ceasefire he formally nominated Trump for the Nobel Peace Prize (Cambodian government letter, August 2025; contemporaneous reporting). In October 2025 Cambodia reopened defense cooperation with the United States at the Kuala Lumpur meetings (Asia Sentinel, 2025).
  • The scam file, after years of official dismissal of foreign reporting as exaggeration, was eventually answered with the largest enforcement campaign in the country's history (Tests 1 and 5) — an adjustment, made late and under combined US, UK, Chinese, and South Korean pressure, but made openly and chaired by Hun Manet personally from January 2026 (Khmer Times via commission reporting, January 2026).

Evidence against:

  • The CLV adjustment was paired with punishment of the people who raised the issue. Ahead of the planned 18 August 2024 protests the government deployed security forces nationwide and arrested nearly 100 people — LICADHO counts more than 100 arrested since late July 2024, more than 60 charged, including children — many on "plotting" charges; both Hun Sen and Hun Manet publicly labeled the critics "extremists." The withdrawal answered the policy demand; the detentions were not undone by it, and UN experts were still raising the CLV arrests as arbitrary detention in a formal communication to the government in May 2025 (LICADHO via RFA, 2024; CIVICUS Monitor, 2025). The pattern — concede the policy, prosecute the pressure — recurs across the file.
  • Criticism of the border handling was answered with a new counting-out instrument rather than engagement. In August 2025, mid-crisis, the Nationality Law was amended to allow courts to strip citizenship from people convicted of treason or collusion with foreign powers — a measure rights monitors assess as aimed at dissidents and exiled critics, and which the government defends as targeting only those who conspire against the nation (Human Rights Watch, 2025). Human Rights Watch records at least 16 people charged over online posts in 2025, including posts about the border (HRW country reporting, 2026).
  • The October 2025 peace accord's breakdown was not of his making alone, and the record is kept two-sided: Thailand's National Security Council suspended the Kuala Lumpur accord on 10–11 November 2025 after a landmine blast injured four Thai soldiers; Cambodia denied planting new mines, maintaining the devices were legacy ordnance — a contested attribution no neutral body has settled. Fighting resumed on 7–8 December 2025, with each side accusing the other of firing first — likewise contested. What is documented on his side of the ledger is that the December war ran for three weeks, killed at least 101 people on both sides per wire counts, and ended with Thai forces holding the contested temple areas of Ta Muen Thom, Ta Krabey, and Khnar, which they have held since (Wikipedia crisis summary citing Cambodian and Thai official sources; Al Jazeera, December 2025). The ceasefire he signed has held since 27 December 2025, with military representatives still meeting on its implementation as of late October 2026 (CSIS, October 2026) — held, on ground his government does not control.
  • Direct engagement produced no adjustment on the rights file. When the European Union raised human rights, civil liberties, and press freedom with Hun Manet in person in June 2025, the documented sequence that followed was tightening: the citizenship amendment (August 2025), the press accreditation rules (December 2025), and the commune law amendments (September 2026) (CIVICUS Monitor, 2025; International IDEA, 2025–2026).

Test 4 — COUNTS EVERYONE

Does the accounting include the opposition, the minorities, the displaced, the trafficked, and those who share the streets — or are some counted out?

GRADE: FAILS

Evidence for:

  • The border displaced were counted, at scale, and the count drove policy. The 2025 fighting displaced more than 650,000 Cambodian civilians across the two rounds (Cambodian and Thai official figures compiled in crisis reporting); Hun Manet's 29 December 2025 message put the displaced at more than half a million with 32 civilians killed and 93 injured, stated that protecting civilians was the reason for accepting the ceasefire-in-place, and directed ministries to facilitate returns and provide interim solutions where return remained unsafe (AKP, 29 December 2025). The 18 captured soldiers were treated as a first-order obligation: their release was written into both the Kuala Lumpur declaration and the 27 December joint statement, and completed on 31 December 2025 (AKP, 31 December 2025).
  • The garment workforce — roughly a million workers, mostly women, in the country's largest formal industry — has received an announced increase every year of his tenure through the tripartite wage council: USD 204 a month in 2024, 208 in 2025, 210 in 2026, with 212 announced for 2027 (Ministry of Labour Prakas No. 214/25, September 2025; USTR docket submission by Cambodia, 2026). Unions demanded at least 232 for 2026; the increases are real and below the demand, and both facts are recorded.
  • His government's founding program named the vulnerable as a stated priority: the Pentagonal Strategy–Phase I and his 22 August 2023 inaugural program list social protection for vulnerable groups and livelihood measures among the government's five core priorities, alongside the upper-middle-income-by-2030 target (Pentagonal Strategy remarks, 24 August 2023; program summaries, August 2023).

Evidence against:

  • Political opponents are counted as criminals, in numbers the monitors keep current: 88 political prisoners as of November 2025 (Human Rights Watch); more than 40 opposition activists detained or imprisoned, including the Candlelight vice-president; the Mother Nature ten serving six to eight years; three more activists sentenced to seven years on 2 September 2026; CLV critics — including children — arrested for planned peaceful protest (LICADHO via RFA, 2024). The filmed-apology-for-release practice documented by Human Rights Watch makes the count explicit: freedom is available at the price of a public defection to the ruling party.
  • The August 2025 nationality amendment created a legal mechanism for removing people from the count altogether: citizenship itself can now be revoked by a court from those convicted of treason or collusion — offenses the record above shows are charged over speech. The government states the law targets only those who conspire with foreign powers against Cambodia; exiled opposition figures and rights monitors state it will be used against dissidents abroad. Both positions are recorded; the mechanism's existence is not contested (Human Rights Watch, August 2025).
  • The exiled opposition's file includes a killing. Former CNRP lawmaker Lim Kimya was shot dead in Bangkok on 7 January 2025. A Thai court convicted the gunman; Thai investigators named a Cambodian adviser to Hun Sen as a suspected organizer — an ALLEGATION that Cambodian officials deny, and on which no Cambodian proceeding exists. It is recorded as an allegation with the denial beside it, and as the documented reason exiled opposition figures cite for believing return is unsafe (Human Rights Watch, 2025; Thai court reporting, 2025).
  • The displaced of development, as distinct from the displaced of war, wait years for their count. The Funan Techo Canal — the 180-kilometre, USD 1.7 billion project Hun Manet launched on 5 August 2024 declaring "we must build this canal at all costs" — affects an estimated 10,000 homes along its route (RFA reporting, 2024); the government promised "fair compensation," impact resolution began in February 2025, and two years after groundbreaking, families along the route remained in limbo with compensation delayed and livelihoods lost even as fresh Chinese financing was secured (Open Development Cambodia, August 2026). Around the new Techo International Airport in Kandal, some 500 families petitioned authorities to halt evictions and grant them land titles (CamboJA via Open Development Cambodia).
  • The trafficked were, for most of his tenure, counted as an industry rather than as victims. UNODC's estimate of at least 100,000 people working in Cambodia's scam compounds — many trafficked and held under forced labor, per the US TIP Report's finding of a government pattern of trafficking with officials intimidating victims and witnesses — describes a population the state profited alongside rather than protected, until the 2025–2026 crackdown; the crackdown's own method was mass deportation (13,500 foreigners in 2025), with victims and workers processed together (US Department of State TIP Report 2025; UNODC via Asia Sentinel, 2026).
  • The border count is not closed: more than 24,000 Cambodian civilians remained displaced into 2026, and Cambodian provincial officials report Thai forces fencing and placing containers on land affecting four villages in Kork Romiet commune — some 292 hectares including residential and farmland, affecting more than 1,300 people — which Cambodia states is its territory and Thailand states is its own; the territorial attribution is contested, the displacement is not (crisis reporting compiled from Cambodian district officials, 2026).

Test 5 — KEEPS HIS WORD OVER TIME

Program pledges and public statements vs. the record since 22 August 2023, with dates.

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for (promises kept or credibly in motion):

  • The scam eradication order — delivered at scale, on his own directive. In July 2025 Hun Manet ordered a nationwide campaign to root out online scam operations and warned provincial governors and officials they would be held responsible for compounds operating in their jurisdictions; he personally chaired the Commission for Combatting Online Scams (meeting of 22 January 2026). The documented delivery: raids in every province, more than 5,100 suspects arrested in seven months, more than 13,500 foreigners deported in 2025, the arrest and deportation of Chen Zhi, nearly USD 1 billion in Prince-linked assets frozen by October 2026, and 27 casino licences revoked or suspended by August 2026 (CamboJA, July 2025; Cambodian officials via Asia Sentinel and AKP, 2026). The lateness and the pressure that preceded it are on the other shelf; the order was given and the machinery moved.
  • The wage pledge — kept annually. His inaugural program promised improved livelihoods and minimum wages; the garment minimum wage has risen every year of his tenure — 204 (2024), 208 (2025), 210 (2026), 212 announced for 2027 — through the tripartite council (MLVT Prakas No. 214/25; USTR docket, 2026).
  • The peace-seeking word during the war — kept as stated. From July 2025 onward he stated Cambodia sought a ceasefire and a legal settlement, and the record matches the statements: ceasefire accepted 28 July 2025, ICJ application filed, Kuala Lumpur declaration signed 26 October 2025, second ceasefire accepted 27 December 2025 with the civilian-protection rationale stated in his own message of 29 December 2025, and the ceasefire held through this grading (Malaysian PMO; AKP; CSIS, October 2026).
  • The long targets are stated as direction, not yet as record: upper-middle-income status by 2030, high-income by 2050, growth averaging around 7 percent, poverty below 10 percent (Pentagonal Strategy, August 2023). None falls due inside the graded period; all are recorded here so the record can grade them later.

Evidence against (promises not kept, or receding):

  • The first promise — peace and territorial integrity — met a war. His inaugural address of 22 August 2023 listed safeguarding peace, stability, security, and territorial integrity as the government's first priority. In 2025 Cambodia fought its first interstate war in decades: five days in July, three weeks in December, at least 48 killed in the first round and 101 in the second by wire counts, more than 650,000 Cambodian civilians displaced. Who fired first in each round is contested by the two governments and is recorded as contested in Test 3; what is not contested is the outcome against the pledge's second half: since December 2025 Thai forces have held the contested temple areas of Ta Muen Thom, Ta Krabey, and Khnar, and Hun Manet's December ceasefire explicitly froze positions "without prejudice to border demarcation" — he chose his people's lives over the ground, said so openly, and the ground remains held by Thailand at grading (AKP, 29 December 2025; crisis reporting, 2025–2026).
  • The governance word — reform, equity, efficiency — against the index and the lawfare record. The Pentagonal Strategy's mottos are growth, equity, efficiency, and sustainability, and his speeches pledged strengthened governance and justice. The measured record: the Corruption Perceptions Index fell each year of his tenure (22 → 21 → 20); civic space is rated "repressed" by CIVICUS; and analysts across the spectrum describe the transition as a generational reset that preserved the patronage system while refining legal pressure on critics — the citizenship amendment, press accreditation rules, and commune law changes of 2025–2026 are the legislative record of that assessment (Transparency International; CIVICUS, 2025; 9DashLine analysis, 2026). No pledge he made in words promised political liberalization — observers projected that hope onto his Western education — and this shelf grades his actual words: on governance quality, the indicators moved the other way.
  • The scam word was kept three years late. The compounds operated openly from before his tenure through its first two years; the July 2025 directive followed a US indictment pipeline, UK and US sanctions, a Harvard report naming his own circle (ALLEGATION, Test 1), the death of a South Korean student in a compound in August 2025, and South Korean travel restrictions — and NGOs still mapped more than 300 scam centers in the country in the March 2026 OSAC assessment, after the crackdown began (OSAC, March 2026). The promise as finally made was substantially executed; the years in which the state's word was that the problem was exaggerated are part of the word's record.
  • The canal compensation word — open. "Fair compensation" was promised at the August 2024 groundbreaking; two years on, affected families report compensation delayed and livelihoods lost while construction financing closed (Open Development Cambodia, August 2026). The canal's 2028 completion target has not fallen due; the compensation promise has, and is unmet for many on the documented record.

Overall Summary

One law: FAILS · Without gripping: FAILS · The wobble: MIXED · Counts everyone: FAILS · His word: MIXED.

Hun Manet is the second holder of a seat his family has occupied since 1985, graded here on his own three years inside a structure his father still co-holds — CPP presidency, Senate presidency, acting head of state — and the dossier records the father's hand wherever the record shows it, from the CLV announcement to the Sokha pardon. Within that structure he is neither a figurehead nor a reformer. The two genuine achievements of the tenure are exercises of raw executive capacity: a scam crackdown that, once finally ordered in July 2025, reached his own former adviser, froze a billion dollars, and deported thirteen thousand people in a year; and a war he did not seek out in public, answered three times with signed ceasefires and a stated priority of civilian life over contested ground. Against those stand the continuities that decide the failing grades: a judiciary and electoral commission that have never once ruled against the house; speech prosecuted as plotting while insider protection held until foreign indictments made it untenable; 88 political prisoners; a citizenship-stripping law aimed at the exiled; canal families waiting two years for promised compensation; and a corruption index that fell every year of a premiership pledged to governance reform. His adjustments are real but follow a pattern the record names plainly — concede the policy under maximum pressure, prosecute the people who applied it, and let the referees stay held. The open files that will move these grades are named in the dossier: whether the December 2025 ceasefire converts into demarcation and the return of the last 24,000 displaced; whether the June 2027 commune elections admit any opposition at all; whether the frozen Prince billions end in Cambodian court verdicts or in quiet release; and whether any institution — court, commission, or newsroom — is ever documented ruling against this government and surviving it.
