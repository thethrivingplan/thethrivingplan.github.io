The Ruler's Test — William Ruto, President of Kenya

Subject: William Samoei Ruto — President of Kenya since 13 September 2022; previously Deputy President (2013–2022). He won the August 2022 election on the Kenya Kwanza "bottom-up" platform, promising to govern for the "hustlers" — small traders, riders, farmers and the unemployed — against a "dynasties" elite. He remains president at grading, in the final year of his first term, with a general election due in August 2027 and a re-election bid under way. His deputy since 1 November 2024 is Kithure Kindiki, following the impeachment of Rigathi Gachagua. Graded: November 2026, on the documented public record. Method: Each test is graded only on the evidence listed under it. Both shelves are shown — the strongest evidence that he passes, then the strongest evidence that he fails. Allegations are labeled ALLEGATION and never stated as verdicts. Contested items are named as contested, with who disputes them. Seat-holders and public figures are named; people below the public eye appear by role only, unless the documented record itself establishes their wrongdoing. Grades: HOLDS / FAILS / MIXED / NOT ENOUGH EVIDENCE. This record is correctable: new evidence changes grades, in the open.

Test 1 — GIVES BY ONE LAW

Same rules for allies and opponents, insiders and outsiders — no favorites at the source.

GRADE: FAILS

Evidence for:

  • The machinery of accountability has, in individual cases, reached people in uniform and in office. After blogger and teacher Albert Ojwang died in police custody on 8 June 2025, the Independent Policing Oversight Authority (IPOA) investigated, an autopsy by five pathologists contradicted the police claim of self-harm, and the Director of Public Prosecutions charged three police officers and three civilians with murder; the Deputy Inspector General whose complaint had prompted Ojwang's arrest, Eliud Lagat, stepped aside pending the investigation (IPOA and court records, June 2025). Two police officers were likewise arrested and charged after the shooting of mask vendor Boniface Kariuki, filmed at close range on 17 June 2025 (court records, June–July 2025).
  • The removal of his own deputy president ran through the constitutional process rather than around it: the National Assembly impeached Rigathi Gachagua on 8 October 2024, the Senate upheld five of eleven charges on 17 October 2024, and when the courts later found (8 June 2026) that the Senate had violated Gachagua's right to a fair hearing, the same ruling left the impeachment standing, confirmed Kindiki's appointment as valid, and ordered the Senate to pay Gachagua KSh 50 million in constitutional damages — an outcome the government side accepted (High Court judgment, 8 June 2026).
  • Anti-corruption enforcement has not been wholly inert against the connected: the Ethics and Anti-Corruption Commission has continued to arrest and charge county governors and senior officials during his term, and former Kiambu governor Ferdinand Waititu was convicted in February 2025 in a case that ran its course through his presidency (court records, February 2025).
  • A state compensation framework he established in August 2025 — a Panel of Experts chaired by Prof. Makau Mutua — had by November 2026 paid out over KSh 1.218 billion to 1,476 verified claimants, including families of 236 people killed in protests and riots, at KSh 3 million per fatality (Panel of Experts statements, November 2026). Compensation is not prosecution, and is graded under Tests 3–4 as well; it is recorded here because it was applied to victims of his own government's force, including a personal apology from Ruto delivered to the family of Rex Masai, the first protester shot dead in June 2024, on 29 October 2026.

Evidence against:

  • The pattern set in his first hundred days has never been answered. Beginning within weeks of his inauguration, the Director of Public Prosecutions, Noordin Haji, applied to withdraw a succession of high-profile corruption and criminal cases against Ruto's allies — cases worth close to KSh 30 billion in aggregate (Business Daily tally, December 2022). The largest was the KSh 7.3 billion corruption case against his own Deputy President, Rigathi Gachagua, withdrawn on 10 November 2022; others included Cabinet Secretary Aisha Jumwa (corruption charges, and a murder charge the DPP also moved to drop), Cabinet Secretary Mithika Linturi, and former Kenya Power managing director Ben Chumo. The DPP's stated reasons were incomplete investigations and failures by the Directorate of Criminal Investigations; in the Gachagua case the investigating officer told the court he had been under pressure from his superiors to charge — evidence that cuts toward the cases having been abusive under the previous government, and the government side has consistently argued exactly that. But the remedy applied was not a rule: it was withdrawal, extended to the president's circle in the president's first months, and the Law Society of Kenya formally demanded an explanation, warning that unexplained withdrawals would destroy confidence in the prosecution service (LSK statement, November 2022; Business Daily, December 2022). No equivalent wave of withdrawals benefited defendants outside the governing circle.
  • The accountability ledger for the protest killings shows the same asymmetry in the other direction. As of June 2026, IPOA had documented 62 deaths from the 2024 protests — with three cases before court (IPOA reporting, June 2026). The Daily Nation's 1,000-days review recorded that no police officer had been charged with murder or abduction arising from the 2024 protest crackdown as a whole (Daily Nation, 16 June 2025). Individual prosecutions (Ojwang, Kariuki) exist; a pattern of prosecution does not.
  • His own history at The Hague belongs on this shelf, stated exactly. Ruto was charged at the International Criminal Court with crimes against humanity arising from the 2007–08 post-election violence. On 5 April 2016, Trial Chamber V(A), by majority, vacated the charges and discharged him — not an acquittal: the majority found the prosecution's evidence insufficient for conviction, and Judge Eboe-Osuji declared a mistrial because "it cannot be discounted that the weaknesses in the Prosecution case might be explained by the demonstrated incidence of tainting of the trial process by way of witness interference and political meddling that was reasonably likely to intimidate witnesses." The Prosecutor stated that at least 16 of her original 42 witnesses had withdrawn, most citing threats, intimidation or fear (ICC Trial Chamber V(A) decision and ICC Prosecutor's statement, 5 April 2016). The vacation was expressly without prejudice to re-prosecution. No verdict of guilt or innocence exists; what is documented is that the case against him collapsed in an environment the court's own judge described as tainted by witness interference.

Test 2 — HOLDS WITHOUT GRIPPING

Do the courts, media, the central bank, county governments and the electoral commission keep their autonomy — or is independence punished and control centralized?

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • The courts have ruled against his government on its signature measures and the rulings stand. A High Court declared the original housing levy unconstitutional in November 2023; the Court of Appeal declared the entire Finance Act 2023 unconstitutional in July 2024 before the Supreme Court partly reversed that declaration on 29 October 2024; a three-judge bench upheld the re-enacted Affordable Housing Act on 22 October 2024. The government litigated and legislated its way through these rulings rather than defying them (court records, 2023–2024). When a High Court bench ruled on 10 July 2025 that his first gazettement of the new electoral commissioners was unlawful because it violated existing court orders, Ruto issued a fresh Gazette Notice the same day and the commissioners were sworn in on 11 July 2025 (High Court ruling and Gazette Notices, July 2025).
  • The judiciary's independence has been exercised against his interests in live political cases: courts issued orders temporarily blocking his deputy's replacement in October 2024 (discharged by a bench on 31 October 2024), found against the Senate on Gachagua's fair-hearing rights in June 2026, and dismissed the petition challenging his broad-based cabinet in July 2026 on the merits rather than under pressure (court records, 2024–2026).
  • County governments remain in the hands of governors of multiple parties, including opposition figures, and continue to litigate and campaign against the national government in the open. The Central Bank governorship changed hands in June 2023 by ordinary appointment at the end of a term. No judge, central banker or governor is documented as removed for a decision against him.
  • Critical media continue to publish and broadcast: the Daily Nation, The Standard and Citizen TV have run sustained investigations and editorials against his government throughout his term, and no outlet has been closed by the state.

Evidence against:

  • The electoral commission was left empty for two and a half years. The Independent Electoral and Boundaries Commission had no commissioners from early 2023 until July 2025 — a vacuum that blocked by-elections and boundary review and that his government was in no hurry to fill, with the selection process repeatedly stalling in 2023–2024. When appointments finally came, Ruto gazetted the new chair, Erastus Ethekon, and six commissioners on 10 June 2025 while a High Court conservatory order barring gazettement was in force — a step the three-judge bench subsequently held unlawful, requiring the fresh gazette he then issued (High Court ruling, 10 July 2025; contemporaneous reporting, June–July 2025). The commission now functions, and by November 2026 was publicly rebuking opposition leaders who walked out of its consultative meetings — evidence it is not simply his instrument (IEBC statements, November 2026).
  • Media autonomy has been squeezed by economic and administrative means rather than by closure. Reporters Without Borders records Kenya falling from 69th in the 2022 World Press Freedom Index to 102nd in 2024 and 117th in 2025, recovering only to 106th in 2026, and describes heads of major press groups and leading outlets being dismissed under political pressure after his election, and self-censorship driven by government advertising power (RSF country file, 2025–2026). The Reuters Institute records the government channelling its weekly publication contract to a single newspaper early in the term and later indicating it would withdraw advertising from independent media toward the state broadcaster (Reuters Institute Digital News Report, 2024). On 25 June 2025 the Communications Authority ordered broadcasters to stop live coverage of the protests; the order was suspended after courts intervened (contemporaneous reporting, June 2025). Police violence against journalists covering protests is documented by RSF and the Kenya Human Rights Commission, including the September 2026 abduction of a Standard Group associate editor, Alex Kiprotich, reportedly seized by police and later found abandoned in Machakos County (KHRC report, November 2026).
  • A cabinet member who broke ranks on the central rights question was removed. Public Service Cabinet Secretary Justin Muturi — whose own son had been abducted in the 2024 wave — publicly and repeatedly condemned the government's handling of abductions; Ruto dismissed him on 26 March 2025 (contemporaneous reporting, March 2025). The dismissal was within presidential power; its documented context is that the offence was public dissent on the abductions file.

Test 3 — ANSWERS THE WOBBLE

When courts, voters, Gen-Z protesters or events push back, does he adjust openly — or deny and double down?

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • The largest concession any Kenyan president has made to street pressure is his. After protesters stormed Parliament on 25 June 2024 and police killed demonstrators in the streets, Ruto addressed the nation on 26 June 2024: "Listening keenly to the people of Kenya who have said loudly that they want nothing to do with this Finance Bill 2024, I concede, and therefore I will not sign the 2024 Finance Bill and it shall subsequently be withdrawn." Parliament deleted every clause of the bill on 25 July 2024. On 11 July 2024 he dismissed his entire cabinet except the Prime Cabinet Secretary, and on 24 July 2024 announced a "broad-based" cabinet including four senior figures from the opposition ODM — John Mbadi (Treasury), Opiyo Wandayi (Energy), Hassan Joho (Mining) and Wycliffe Oparanya (Cooperatives) — an absorption of his chief rival's party into his government in response to the protests (presidential addresses and Gazette Notices, June–August 2024).
  • He cancelled the Adani deals under pressure. After months of public outcry, litigation and a workers' strike over a 30-year privately initiated deal for Jomo Kenyatta International Airport and a signed KSh 95.68 billion transmission contract, and one day after US prosecutors unsealed a bribery indictment against Gautam Adani (concerning Indian contracts, not Kenya), Ruto used his State of the Nation address on 21 November 2024 to direct both procurement processes cancelled, citing "new information provided by investigative agencies and partner nations." Both deals stand cancelled as of October 2026 (State of the Nation address, 21 November 2024; Kenya Airports Authority statements, 2026).
  • The adjustment machinery continued into 2025–2026: the compensation framework of August 2025, payments to 1,476 claimants, and a presidential apology delivered to Rex Masai's family in October 2026 (Panel of Experts records, 2025–2026). After the Ojwang killing, the responsible deputy police chief stepped aside and officers were charged rather than the file being closed (June 2025).
  • On his flagship health reform he has conceded failure while defending the goal: as private hospitals suspended services over unpaid claims in February 2025, Ruto acknowledged on 2 March 2025 that "it is the biggest programme we have in the country; it may take time for things to align, but believe you me, it is going to work" — an open admission of malfunction, paired with persistence (public address, 2 March 2025).

Evidence against:

  • The concessions came paired with force and with language that licensed more of it. On 25 June 2024, before conceding, Ruto described the protests as "infiltrated by people funded to cause havoc" and backed military deployment in support of the police; the High Court approved the deployment on 27 June 2024 while directing the government to publish its duration, which it failed to do (Amnesty International Kenya report, April 2025). In late June 2025 his Interior Cabinet Secretary, Kipchumba Murkomen, described the protests as "a coup attempt" and urged officers to shoot on sight anyone storming public buildings. On 9 July 2025, during the Saba Saba unrest, Ruto himself directed: "Anyone who picks up petrol to burn someone else's business or any other person's property, such a person should be shot in the leg, taken to hospital, and then presented in court." Asked about the order on 9 November 2025, he said: "I do not regret those comments at all because the law allows the police to use force when other people's lives are in danger" — while adding, in the same interview, that "there is nowhere in the law where I have the power to direct the police" (public address, 9 July 2025; televised interview, 9 November 2025).
  • On abductions the sequence was denial, then a claim the record contradicts. Through 2024 the government denied state involvement in the abduction wave. In Homa Bay in December 2024 Ruto pledged abductions would stop. On 12 May 2025, beside Finland's President Stubb, he stated: "All the people who disappeared or who were abducted… all of them have been brought back to their families and to their homes and I have given clarity and firm instructions that nothing of that kind of nature will happen again." The documented counter-record: the Kenya National Commission on Human Rights had recorded 82 abductions between June and December 2024 with some victims found dead and others still missing; the fisherman Brian Odhiambo, taken by Kenya Wildlife Service officers on 18 January 2025, remained unaccounted for as his family continued to seek him after the May 2025 statement; and the Missing Voices coalition documented 22 further enforced disappearances in 2026 (KNCHR statements, 2024–2025; Missing Voices data via Amnesty International, 2026). IPOA's chair responded to the May 2025 statement by calling for a public inquiry to establish who was behind the abductions — an inquiry not conceded (IPOA statement, May 2025).
  • The tax substance rejected in 2024 returned by other doors: measures from the withdrawn Finance Bill were re-enacted through subsequent legislation and administrative action, a fact critics across the spectrum noted and the government's own later finance acts confirm in part (Finance Act 2025 and contemporaneous analysis). And in November 2026, campaigning in Bungoma, Ruto told crowds "the 2024 Finance Bill was eventually passed, and there was no harm to our nation" — a revision of a record in which the bill was withdrawn unsigned and IPOA counts 62 protest deaths (public rally remarks, November 2026).

Test 4 — COUNTS EVERYONE

Does the accounting include the young protesters, the poor ("hustlers"), pastoralists and his opponents' voters — or are some counted out?

GRADE: FAILS

Evidence for:

  • The broad-based turn materially included his opponents' voters: from July 2024 the ODM of Raila Odinga — the man who ran against him in 2022 — held senior cabinet portfolios, and after Odinga's death in India on 15 October 2025 Ruto accorded him a state funeral and, through November 2026, sustained the arrangement with Odinga's successor, Oburu Oginga, in negotiation toward a possible 2027 coalition (State House records; contemporaneous reporting, 2024–2026). Whatever its motive, the largest opposition constituency in the country has been governed with, not governed against, for most of the term.
  • The flagship programs are universalist in design. The Social Health Authority, launched October 2024, registers all residents regardless of income, with a KSh 300 monthly premium for the non-salaried; Ruto reported 33.7 million registered in his 18 November 2026 State of the Nation address (a figure whose comparison base against the old NHIF is contested — the 2025 Economic Survey records over 30 million NHIF members in 2021/22 — but the expansion of registration is documented). The fertilizer subsidy cut a 50kg bag from KSh 7,500 in 2022 toward KSh 2,000–2,500, and the Hustler Fund, launched 30 November 2022, has lent small sums to millions of borrowers outside the formal banking system (State of the Nation address, 18 November 2026; programme records).
  • Victims of state force were eventually counted in money: 236 protest and riot fatalities compensated at KSh 3 million each within the 1,476-claimant, KSh 1.218 billion programme, whose mandate Ruto extended back to violations from 2013, including the River Yala killings of the previous administration (Panel of Experts statements, November 2026).

Evidence against:

  • The young are counted, in the first instance, as a body count. The documented protest toll of his term: IPOA records 62 deaths from the June–July 2024 protests (government said 42; KNCHR counted 50 by mid-July 2024 and 60 by year's reporting); Amnesty International's Kenya report records 60 killed and hundreds injured in 2024, more than 600 arrested, and at least 104 extrajudicial executions recorded in the year. In 2025 the killing resumed on the anniversaries: at least 16 dead and over 400 injured on 25 June 2025 (KNCHR/Amnesty figures), and on Saba Saba, 7 July 2025, KNCHR reported 31 killed — later 38 — across 17 counties, including a 12-year-old girl shot by a stray bullet while watching television at home in Kiambu; Human Rights Watch recorded 51 deaths across the June–July 2025 demonstrations (KNCHR, Amnesty International, HRW reporting, 2025). Rights organizations put the cumulative protest-related death toll since 2024 above 180.
  • Albert Ojwang's death is the count in miniature: a 31-year-old teacher and blogger arrested on a senior police officer's complaint about social-media posts, transported 350 km, and dead in a cell within a day, with the police's first public account — that he had fatally injured himself against a wall — contradicted by the official autopsy (IPOA and autopsy findings, June 2025). A state that arrests a citizen for insulting an officer and returns him dead has, for that citizen, stopped counting him.
  • The abducted are counted out by method. KNCHR documented 82 abductions or enforced disappearances between June and December 2024; the Law Society of Kenya counted at least 72 people forcibly disappeared in connection with the protests; Missing Voices documented 55 enforced disappearances in 2024 — the highest it has ever recorded — and 104 police killings that year, with fewer than 2 percent of police killings prosecuted (KNCHR; LSK; Missing Voices annual report, May 2025). The victims were disproportionately young online critics — among them cartoonists and social-media users seized in December 2024 for satirical posts. Some were released after weeks; some were found dead; some, as under Test 3, remain missing against the president's statement that all were returned.
  • The "hustler" constituency itself reports being counted in rhetoric and out in outcome. The Bertelsmann Transformation Index 2026 assessment records that after the removal of fuel and maize subsidies and successive new levies — the housing levy, the SHIF deduction of 2.75 percent of gross pay, raised NSSF contributions — prices of maize flour, fuel and kerosene reached record highs in the term's first half, and KNBS's Economic Survey 2026 contradicts several presidential production claims (maize output up 2.4 percent to 45.8 million bags in 2025 against the 67–71 million bags the president has cited). Salaried workers' take-home pay shrank under the new statutory deductions even as headline inflation fell (BTI 2026 Kenya report; KNBS Economic Survey, April 2026).

Test 5 — KEEPS HIS WORD OVER TIME

Campaign promises vs. the record since September 2022, with dates.

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for (promises kept or credibly in motion):

  • The Hustler Fund — delivered on schedule. Promised through the 2022 campaign as the centerpiece of bottom-up credit, it was launched on 30 November 2022, within his first hundred days, and has operated continuously since (launch records, November 2022).
  • Macroeconomic stabilization — substantially delivered. He promised to stabilize an economy he inherited with inflation at 9.2 percent and a weakening shilling. Inflation fell into the 3–5 percent band through 2025–2026, the shilling stabilized after its early-2024 recovery, and in his 18 November 2026 State of the Nation address he could concede the remaining pain while pointing to documented stabilization: "Our economic proposition was simple: reduce the cost for families and increase their income… Both were necessary then and remain necessary now" (State of the Nation address, 18 November 2026; KNBS data).
  • Fertilizer and food — partly delivered. The subsidized-fertilizer promise was implemented from the 2023 planting seasons; the price of a 2kg packet of maize flour fell materially from its 2022–2023 peaks, though the scale of the production increase is CONTESTED between the president's figures and KNBS's (see Test 4).
  • Affordable housing — built, below the promised pace. The 200,000-units-a-year promise has not been met on any count; the programme is nonetheless real — the government claims some 205,000–230,000 units delivered or in progress by late 2026, and the levy funding it survived constitutional challenge through re-enactment (government delivery scorecard, January 2026; KNBS Economic Survey, April 2026). Graded as a promise kept in kind and missed in number.

Evidence against (promises not kept, or receding):

  • "No abductions, no extrajudicial killings under my administration" — broken on the documented record. Ruto made the pledge repeatedly from the 2022 campaign through his presidency, most solemnly after the unresolved disappearance of two Indian members of his own campaign team: under his leadership, he said, the police would operate independently and Kenyans would not disappear and be found dead "in all manner of places." Tests 3 and 4 set out the record against it: 82 documented abductions in six months of 2024, 55 enforced disappearances that year on Missing Voices' count, continuing cases into 2026, and a presidential assurance in May 2025 that all had been returned which the documented record does not sustain. This is the single clearest word-versus-record gap of the term, because the promise was absolute and personally framed.
  • The cost-of-living promise — half-kept and re-litigated by his own numbers. The 2022 campaign promised cheaper fuel, cheaper unga and, in March 2023, a 6kg cooking-gas cylinder at KSh 300–500 by June 2023; in May 2023 Ruto conceded the target had been over-optimistic and redefined it as the price of the cylinder rather than the refill — a public walk-back, to his credit, of a promise not kept. By November 2026 the Nation's assessment of his State of the Nation defence records fuel prices trending upward again and take-home pay reduced by his own new levies (Daily Nation, 18 November 2026).
  • The independent count is unflattering. Mzalendo Trust's Promise Tracker, as of 30 August 2026, was tracking 254 Kenya Kwanza manifesto commitments and classified 25 completed, 41 broken, 150 ongoing, 19 not started, 15 stalled and 4 modified — the tracker's own evidence-based assessments, not official determinations (Mzalendo Trust Promise Tracker, August 2026). Among specific shortfalls documented by November 2026: the pledge to employ 20,000 additional health workers, which the Kenya Union of Clinical Officers states remains largely unfulfilled, and the Auditor-General's findings that the SHA's core system is neither owned nor controlled by the government despite the public investment in it (union and Auditor-General statements, November 2026).
  • The 2024 concession's spirit was not kept. Having withdrawn the Finance Bill "because the people have spoken," his government re-enacted parts of its revenue programme through later instruments (Test 3) and by November 2026 he was publicly claiming the bill "was eventually passed, and there was no harm to our nation" — a statement irreconcilable with the withdrawal he announced on 26 June 2024 and with IPOA's count of 62 dead. A kept word does not require later revision of what the word was.

Overall Summary

One law: FAILS · Without gripping: MIXED · The wobble: MIXED · Counts everyone: FAILS · His word: MIXED.

William Ruto's presidency is two records running in the same office. In one, a president who lost the street in June 2024 conceded more completely than any predecessor — withdrew his finance bill unsigned, sacked his cabinet, absorbed his rival's party into his government, cancelled the Adani contracts, compensated 236 dead protesters' families and apologized to one of them by name — and whose courts, counties and press remain alive enough to rule against him, rebuke him and investigate him in the open. In the other, a president whose prosecution service emptied the corruption dockets of his allies in his first hundred days, whose electoral commission stood vacant for two and a half years, whose police killed more than 60 protesters in 2024 and dozens more on the anniversaries in 2025, whose young critics were abducted by the score while he denied and then declared the file closed against the documented record, and who told police to shoot arsonists in the leg and, a year later, said he did not regret it. The tests measure the seat, not the speeches: on the evidence assembled here, the seat gives by favor before it gives by law, holds its institutions without quite closing its fist around them, adjusts when the wobble is undeniable and denies while it can — and counts the hustlers it promised to lift more reliably in its programmes than in its morgues. The record is correctable in the open: prosecutions for the protest killings, an accounting for the still-missing, and the August 2027 election itself are all capable of moving these grades.
