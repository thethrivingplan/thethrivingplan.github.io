The Wheel

A pottery game about the real sequence of throwing a pot: wedge the clay, centre it, open it, pull the walls up in passes, shape it to the order, wire it off, glaze it, fire it. Your grade comes from the same things a real pot is judged by — how well it was centred, how even the walls are, and how close the shape lands to the one that was asked for.

Built on the documented craft of wheel-thrown pottery, in our own words: the sequence above is the sequence potters have used for centuries, and every lesson the Coach gives is a lesson from that craft, applied to the piece actually on your wheel.

Honest limits, printed plainly: a game can teach the shape of this craft — the order of the steps, the reasons behind them, what goes wrong and why. It cannot teach the feel of clay in your hands: the pressure that is “enough,” the wobble you sense before you see it. That only comes from real clay on a real wheel. Treat this as a map, not the territory — then go find a wheel.

The craft, in plain words

Why centring is everything

Every later step inherits the centre. If the lump spins even slightly off-centre, the walls come up uneven no matter how skilled the pulls are — one side thick, one side thin, and the thin side is where the pot will fail. Potters say you do not decorate a pot into being good; you centre it into being possible.

Water discipline

Water is lubrication, not an ingredient. A wet surface lets hands glide; a soaked wall turns to soup, loses its strength, and slumps or tears. The rule at a real wheel: wet the surface, never flood the clay. In this game your water gauge is the whole lesson — pulls drag and score badly when the clay is dry, and the collapse risk climbs steeply when it is soaked.

Pull speed against wall height

A pull moves a small wave of clay upward. Move your hands faster than one wheel revolution per stretch of wall and the wave spirals, leaving ridges and thin spots. Slow, steady pulls that take a full turn of the wheel to travel are how walls come up even. Height is bought with patience, pass by pass — three honest pulls beat one heroic one.

What a collapse teaches

A collapsed wall is not a failed potter; it is information. It tells you exactly where the wall was thinnest, how wet the clay had become, and which pull asked for more than the clay had. Every collapse in this game is written into your record with its cause named, because at a real wheel that is what a collapse is for.
