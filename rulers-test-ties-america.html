THE TIES — AMERICA

A documented-connections dossier, companion to The Ruler's Test

As of November 2026. Every entry below is a documented fact from the public record — a court filing, an FEC record, a financial disclosure, a Senate questionnaire, an official biography, a congressional document, or named reporting by a reputable outlet, with the source given. Nothing unsourced is included.

How to read this page. A tie is not wrongdoing. A tie states exactly what the record shows — appointed, employed, donated, clerked, flew, met, invested — and nothing more. Where a person has denied a characterization, the denial is printed with the tie. Allegations are labeled ALLEGATION. Convictions and court findings are labeled as such. Seat-holders and public figures are named. People below the public eye appear by role only — unless the documented record itself establishes their wrongdoing (a conviction, a court finding, an official finding), in which case they are named with the evidence cited.

The seats covered: the President (Donald J. Trump), the Vice President (J.D. Vance), the Secretary of State (Marco Rubio), the Attorney General (Todd Blanche, in the seat since August 2026), the Chief Justice (John Roberts), the Chair of the Federal Reserve (Kevin Warsh, in the seat since May 22, 2026), and the congressional leaders for whom documented ties were assembled (Speaker Mike Johnson; House Democratic Leader Hakeem Jeffries).

I. THE APPOINTMENT WEB

Who appointed, hired, or formerly employed whom — including the President's former personal lawyers now in government, and family members in official roles.

1. The Vice President's wife clerked for the Chief Justice.

Usha Vance, wife of Vice President J.D. Vance, clerked for Chief Justice John Roberts on the Supreme Court in 2017–18, and earlier for then-Judge Brett Kavanaugh on the D.C. Circuit (2014–15). — Documentation: the Vice President's official White House biography.

2. The President's lead criminal-defense lawyer is now the Attorney General.

Todd Blanche was Donald Trump's lead personal criminal-defense counsel. He became Deputy Attorney General (confirmed March 2025), then acting Attorney General (April 2026), then Attorney General (August 2026). In his own Senate questionnaire, Blanche disclosed that his law firm was paid approximately $10.2 million by the President's Save America PAC for that defense work. — Documentation: Blanche's Senate Judiciary Committee questionnaire; Senate confirmation records.

3. Blanche's law partner, also the President's co-counsel, is now a federal appeals judge.

Emil Bove was Blanche's partner and served as Trump's co-counsel in the same defense. He became acting Deputy Attorney General / Principal Associate Deputy Attorney General, and was then confirmed to the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Third Circuit, assuming office September 2, 2025. — Documentation: Senate confirmation records; court records.

4. The President's first Attorney General of this term came from his impeachment defense team.

Pam Bondi served on Trump's first-impeachment defense team and later led the legal arm of the America First Policy Institute. She became Attorney General on February 5, 2025, and was fired on April 2, 2026. — Documentation: Senate confirmation records; Reuters and AP reporting, 2025–2026.

5. The lawyer who argued the President's immunity case is now the Solicitor General.

D. John Sauer argued Trump v. United States — the presidential-immunity case — for Trump personally. He became Solicitor General of the United States on April 4, 2025. Will Scharf, another lawyer on the immunity-case team, became White House Staff Secretary. — Documentation: Supreme Court docket in Trump v. United States; Senate confirmation records.

6. A Trump civil-case lawyer was installed as a U.S. Attorney; a court ruled the appointment unlawful.

Alina Habba represented Trump in civil matters (including the New York civil-fraud case and the E. Jean Carroll cases); her firm was paid $3.6 million by Trump PACs. She became Counselor to the President, then interim U.S. Attorney for New Jersey in March 2025. A U.S. appeals court ruled her appointment unlawful, and she resigned in December 2025. — Documentation: ABC News reporting on PAC payments; the appeals court's ruling, December 2025.

7. The President's son's father-in-law: convicted, pardoned by the President, then made an ambassador by him.

Charles Kushner is the father of Jared Kushner and therefore father-in-law to the President's daughter. Court finding: in 2004 he pleaded guilty to 18 federal counts, including tax evasion, illegal campaign contributions, and witness retaliation. President Trump pardoned him on December 23, 2020. Kushner gave $1 million to a pro-Trump super PAC. Trump then nominated him Ambassador to France and Monaco; he was confirmed May 19, 2025 (51–45) and sworn in July 11, 2025. — Documentation: the 2004 guilty plea (U.S. District Court, District of New Jersey); the presidential pardon of December 23, 2020; FEC records; Senate confirmation record.

8. The President's son-in-law negotiates for the United States alongside the President's envoy.

Jared Kushner, the President's son-in-law and a former senior White House adviser, serves as a special envoy for peace missions (2026–present) alongside Special Envoy Steve Witkoff. His fund's foreign financing is documented in Cluster II, entry 7. — Documentation: official envoy announcements; New York Times reporting, 2026.

9. The Special Envoy is a friend of nearly forty years with no prior government service.

Steve Witkoff met Donald Trump in 1986, when Witkoff's law firm represented Trump; the two remained friends and golf partners. Witkoff had no prior government or diplomatic experience when he became Special Envoy to the Middle East (May 6, 2025) and then Special Envoy for Peace Missions. — Documentation: Witkoff's official biography and Senate testimony; New York Times and AP profiles, 2025.

10. The Secretary of State's career patron employed him, employed his wife, and funded his salary.

Miami auto magnate Norman Braman has been Marco Rubio's largest political backer. The documented record includes: Braman employed Rubio as a lawyer; Braman's family foundation employed Rubio's wife; Braman helped fund Rubio's college-instructor salary; and Rubio had access to Braman's plane. Rubio has said Braman never asked anything for himself. — Documentation: New York Times, May 9, 2015.

11. The Speaker was a litigator for a legal-advocacy organization and served on the President's impeachment defense team.

Before Congress, Mike Johnson was a longtime litigator and spokesman for the Alliance Defending Freedom. He served on President Trump's first-impeachment defense team, and in December 2020 he led the amicus brief — signed by more than 125 House Republicans — backing Texas v. Pennsylvania, the suit that sought to overturn the 2020 election results. Johnson is the outgoing Speaker: Democrats retook the House in the 2026 midterms, and he serves until January 3, 2027. — Documentation: Johnson's congressional biography; the Texas v. Pennsylvania amicus filing, December 2020; 2026 election results.

12. The Trump campaign's former finance chair is now an ambassador — and was formerly engaged to the President's eldest son.

Kimberly Guilfoyle chaired the Trump campaign's finance operation in 2020 and was formerly engaged to Donald Trump Jr., a public figure. She was nominated Ambassador to Greece in December 2024 and confirmed in September 2025. — Documentation: the nomination announcement, December 2024; Senate confirmation record, September 2025.

13. Two cabinet secretaries passed through the same outside institute.

Before taking office, Attorney General Pam Bondi led the legal arm of the America First Policy Institute, and Education Secretary Linda McMahon chaired it. The institute functioned as a shared institutional home for future office-holders between administrations. — Documentation: America First Policy Institute records; Reuters and AP reporting, 2024–2025.

II. THE MONEY

Campaign donations and super-PAC funding from FEC records and reputable reporting; business partnerships and investments from disclosures and reputable reporting. A donation is a documented fact. It is not, by itself, evidence of any return.

1. The largest donor of the 2024 cycle then ran a government office.

Elon Musk spent approximately $277 million in the 2024 cycle — roughly $239 million of it through his America PAC — making him the cycle's largest donor, per FEC filings. He then led the Department of Government Efficiency (DOGE) as a special government employee until May 30, 2025. — Documentation: FEC filings, as tallied by the Washington Post and OpenSecrets; White House announcements, 2025.

2. The next tier of donors.

Timothy Mellon gave approximately $150–165 million to MAGA Inc. Miriam Adelson gave more than $100 million to the Preserve America PAC. Neither took a government post. — Documentation: FEC filings, 2024 cycle.

3. The Commerce Secretary gave $8.75 million and co-chaired the transition.

Howard Lutnick gave $8.75 million to MAGA Inc. in the 2024 cycle and co-chaired the presidential transition. He was confirmed as Secretary of Commerce on February 18, 2025. His Epstein-record entry appears in Cluster III. — Documentation: FEC filings; transition announcements; Senate confirmation record.

4. The Education Secretary gave roughly $20 million and co-chaired the transition.

Linda McMahon gave approximately $20 million to MAGA Inc., co-chaired the transition, and chaired the America First Policy Institute (see Cluster I, entry 13). She was confirmed as Secretary of Education on March 3, 2025. — Documentation: FEC filings; Senate confirmation record.

5. The Treasury Secretary hosted fundraisers totaling roughly $57 million.

Scott Bessent hosted 2024 Trump fundraisers totaling approximately $57 million before becoming Secretary of the Treasury. — Documentation: contemporaneous fundraising reporting, 2024; Senate confirmation record.

6. The Federal Reserve Chair's father-in-law is a decades-long Trump friend and donor — and, by reporting, the source of the Greenland idea.

Fed Chair Kevin Warsh is married to Jane Lauder of the Estée Lauder family. His father-in-law, Ronald Lauder, is a decades-long friend of Donald Trump who has given more than $1.6 million to pro-Trump organizations since 2016, including $5 million to MAGA Inc. in 2025. Lauder reportedly first proposed the Greenland purchase to Trump. Warsh himself came to the Fed from a partnership at Stanley Druckenmiller's Duquesne Family Office and disclosed more than $100 million in assets. — Documentation: Warsh's financial disclosure and Senate confirmation record (confirmed 54–45; sworn in May 22, 2026); FEC filings; New York Times reporting on the Greenland proposal.

7. The President's son-in-law's fund took $2 billion from the Saudi sovereign fund months after he left the White House.

Jared Kushner founded Affinity Partners in July 2021, roughly six months after leaving his senior White House adviser role. In April 2022, Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund committed $2 billion. The PIF's own internal screening panel had objected to the deal — citing, among other things, the firm's inexperience and fee structure — and the PIF board, chaired by Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman, overruled the panel. The House Oversight Committee opened a document inquiry in 2022. Per the firm's own regulatory disclosures as cited by the Senate Finance Committee, 99% of Affinity's assets under management come from non-U.S. sources, principally the sovereign funds of Saudi Arabia, the UAE, and Qatar — governments Kushner now deals with again as a special envoy (Cluster I, entry 8). Kushner and Affinity have denied any impropriety, stating the investments were made on commercial terms. — Documentation: New York Times, April 2022 (from internal PIF documents); Senate Finance Committee letter to A Fin Management LLC, June 12, 2024; House Oversight Committee inquiry, 2022. — ALLEGATION: a book published October 27, 2026 (Matthew Cole, Princes and Thieves, excerpted in Vanity Fair) alleges that Gulf governments placed funds within Kushner's reach while he was still in office. As of mid-November 2026 that claim rests on one reporter's anonymous sourcing, has no independent corroboration in the public record, and is printed here as an allegation only. Kushner denies impropriety.

8. The President's family and the Special Envoy's family are co-founders of the same crypto company.

World Liberty Financial's founding document lists President Trump as the firm's "Chief Crypto Advocate," his sons Eric, Donald Jr., and Barron as "Web3 Ambassadors," Special Envoy Steve Witkoff as a co-founder, and Witkoff's son Zach Witkoff as a co-founder of the company. In May 2025, the Abu Dhabi investment firm MGX used World Liberty's stablecoin, USD1, to settle a $2 billion investment in the crypto exchange Binance — a transaction the New York Times reported could generate tens of millions of dollars a year for World Liberty's partners. Binance's founder, Changpeng Zhao, was pardoned by President Trump in October 2025. Separately, the Wall Street Journal reported in February 2026 that a firm backed by Sheikh Tahnoon bin Zayed, the UAE's national security adviser, had agreed in January 2025 to buy 49% of World Liberty for $500 million, with payments flowing to both Trump-family and Witkoff-family entities. The Senate Permanent Subcommittee on Investigations has an open inquiry; its November 5, 2026 letter states the company has not produced responsive records. — Documentation: World Liberty Financial's founding document, as quoted in the Senate Permanent Subcommittee on Investigations letter to Zach Witkoff, November 5, 2026; Senate Banking Committee letter, May 9, 2025, citing the New York Times; Wall Street Journal, February 2026.

9. The Chief Justice's household: a legal recruiter's commissions from firms with business before the Court.

The Chief Justice's wife worked as a legal recruiter at Major, Lindsey & Africa from 2007 to 2014 — an employment shown on the Chief Justice's own annual financial disclosures, which list the source of spousal income (federal law does not require the amount). In December 2022, a former managing director of that firm filed a whistleblower complaint with the House and Senate Judiciary Committees and the Department of Justice, attaching internal company spreadsheets showing $10,323,842.70 in commissions over those seven years, including placements at major law firms that appear before the Supreme Court. The complaint's figures were reported by Business Insider and the New York Times in early 2023. A 2009 Judicial Conference advisory opinion holds that recusal is not automatically required merely because a spouse worked as a recruiter for a firm with business before the court. Snopes, reviewing a broader $20-million version of this claim in May 2026, declined to rate it, noting the amounts cannot be confirmed from public disclosures. — Documentation: the Chief Justice's financial disclosure forms, 2007–2020; the whistleblower complaint filed with Congress, December 2022; New York Times and Business Insider, 2023; Snopes, May 2026.

10. The House Democratic Leader's documented ties to a pro-Israel advocacy network.

Hakeem Jeffries led a congressional delegation to Israel in August 2023 organized by the American Israel Education Foundation, AIPAC's charitable affiliate; his own office announced the trip, and a House ethics disclosure shows the foundation sponsored it. In May 2026, AIPAC's PAC transmitted two earmarked payments totaling $149,300 to the Jeffries Battleground Protection Fund, per FEC records. — Documentation: Office of Representative Hakeem Jeffries, press release, August 6, 2023; House ethics disclosure; FEC records as reported by Sludge, 2026.

III. THE DOCUMENTED EPSTEIN-RECORD NAMES AMONG THE POWERFUL

Names appearing in the Epstein case court records, flight records entered in evidence, and released government files — stated as exactly what the documents show. Appearing in these records is not wrongdoing, and no entry in this cluster states or implies that it is. Denials are printed where issued.

1. The President appears in the flight records on multiple 1990s flights.

An internal email by an SDNY prosecutor (January 2020), released in the case records, counted at least eight flights by Donald Trump on Jeffrey Epstein's aircraft between 1993 and 1996, at least four of them with Ghislaine Maxwell aboard. Trump has said he banned Epstein from Mar-a-Lago and knew nothing of Epstein's crimes. Virginia Giuffre testified that she had no knowledge of misconduct by Trump. Trump has never been charged in connection with Epstein. — Documentation: the January 2020 SDNY prosecutor email, released in the Maxwell case records; Giuffre's deposition testimony; AP and New York Times reporting.

2. The Commerce Secretary testified that he had lunch on Epstein's island in 2012.

Howard Lutnick testified to a Senate subcommittee on February 10, 2026, that he and his family had lunch on Epstein's island in December 2012. Emails released by the Department of Justice show contact after that date. Lutnick has said: "I have done absolutely nothing wrong in any possible regard." — Documentation: Lutnick's Senate subcommittee testimony, February 10, 2026; DOJ-released emails.

3. The Health and Human Services Secretary has said, on the record, that he flew on Epstein's jet twice.

Robert F. Kennedy Jr. said on Fox News that he flew on Epstein's jet twice — a 1993 family trip to Palm Beach and a second family trip — facilitated by his late wife's acquaintance with Ghislaine Maxwell. He has called for the full release of the Epstein files. — Documentation: Kennedy's statements on Fox News (Jesse Watters Primetime).

4. The Attorney General, as Deputy AG, personally interviewed Ghislaine Maxwell; she was then moved to a lower-security prison.

Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche personally interviewed Ghislaine Maxwell over two days (July 24–25, 2025, roughly nine hours); the transcripts were later released. On August 1, 2025, Maxwell was transferred to Federal Prison Camp Bryan, a lower-security facility. Blanche is the same official whose path from the President's personal defense team to the Attorney General's office is documented in Cluster I, entry 2. No finding of wrongdoing attaches to either fact; both are printed because both are documented and both sit at the junction of two clusters of this map. — Documentation: DOJ interview transcripts, released 2025; Bureau of Prisons transfer records; AP and New York Times reporting.

5. The official record around the files.

A DOJ/FBI memorandum of July 7, 2025 stated that no further Epstein disclosure was warranted. Congress then passed, and the President signed on November 19, 2025, the Epstein Files Transparency Act, mandating further release. — Documentation: the DOJ/FBI memorandum, July 7, 2025; the Epstein Files Transparency Act, November 19, 2025.

IV. SHARED COUNSEL AND STAFF

The quietest ties on the map: the same lawyers, firms, and patrons recurring around different seats.

1. One defense team now occupies three seats of government.

The President's personal criminal-defense team supplied: the Attorney General (Todd Blanche, lead counsel), a federal appeals judge (Emil Bove, co-counsel and Blanche's partner), and — from the related civil and immunity matters — the Solicitor General (D. John Sauer), the White House Staff Secretary (Will Scharf), and an interim U.S. Attorney (Alina Habba). The defense itself was substantially paid for by the President's Save America PAC: approximately $10.2 million to Blanche's firm (per Blanche's Senate questionnaire) and $3.6 million to Habba's firm (per FEC-based reporting). — Documentation: Senate questionnaires and confirmation records; Supreme Court docket in Trump v. United States; ABC News reporting on PAC payments.

2. A single donor built the Vice President's career before the Vice President held any seat.

Peter Thiel hired J.D. Vance at his firm Mithril Capital in 2016–17, backed Vance's venture firm Narya Capital, and gave $15 million to the Protect Ohio Values PAC supporting Vance's 2022 Senate race — at the time the largest single-donor contribution to a Senate candidate on record. Thiel reportedly brought Vance to Mar-a-Lago in 2021, before Vance's Senate run. Vance later co-founded the Rockbridge Network, a donor circle, with publisher Chris Buskirk. — Documentation: FEC filings (Protect Ohio Values PAC, 2022); New York Times and Washington Post reporting, 2021–2025.

3. The institute that housed two future cabinet secretaries.

The America First Policy Institute employed or housed, between administrations, the future Attorney General (legal arm), the future Education Secretary (chair), and the future Ambassador to France's predecessor network of the President's first impeachment defense (Cluster I, entries 4 and 13). It is the single most repeated institutional address in the appointment web after the President's own defense team. — Documentation: America First Policy Institute records; Reuters and AP reporting.

4. The clerkship bridge between the Vice President's household and the Court.

The Vice President's wife clerked for the Chief Justice who now presides over cases the administration litigates, and for a sitting Justice when he was a circuit judge (Cluster I, entry 1). Clerkships are the ordinary currency of the legal profession; the tie is printed because it is documented and because it connects two seats on this map directly. — Documentation: the Vice President's official White House biography.

WHAT THIS MAP DOES NOT SHOW

This map shows documented connections only. It does not show — and cannot grade — undocumented influence: private conversations, unrecorded meetings, favors asked and granted off paper, or pressure applied through channels that leave no filing, disclosure, or transcript. The absence of a tie from this page means only that no qualifying document was found; it is not evidence that no connection exists. The presence of a tie means only that the connection is documented; it is not evidence that anything improper flowed through it. Some seats and some networks are documented more richly than others because some lives are lived on paper and others are not. What is not on the record cannot be graded — and this page grades only what the record holds.
