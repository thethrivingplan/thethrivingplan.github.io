The Ruler's Test — Luca Beccari, Secretary of State for Foreign and Political Affairs of San Marino

Subject: Luca Beccari — Secretary of State for Foreign and Political Affairs of the Republic of San Marino since 8 January 2020, retained in the same seat after the general election of 9 June 2024. The framing is stated plainly, as the brief requires: San Marino has no prime minister. Executive power sits with the Congress of State (the government), which is elected by the Grand and General Council, the 60-seat parliament. The heads of state are the two Captains Regent, who preside over the Council and the Congress but rotate every six months and cannot carry a continuing executive record. The seat that does carry that record — in practice the head-of-government seat, and the one graded here — is the Secretary of State for Foreign and Political Affairs, held by Beccari, of the Sammarinese Christian Democratic Party (PDCS). Beccari, born 29 October 1974, was first elected to the Council in 2012 and himself served one six-month term as Captain Regent in April–October 2014, alongside Valeria Ciavatta, before taking the foreign-affairs seat in January 2020 at the head of the 30th Legislature's coalition (Secretary of State office listing; Beccari biography). After the 9 June 2024 election — PDCS 22 seats on 34.14% of the vote, in a chamber elected by proportional representation on 50.73% turnout (Secretariat of State for Internal Affairs results, via the 2024 election record) — the two pre-election coalitions recombined into a governing majority of 44 of 60 seats (PDCS 22, Libera/PS 10, PSD 8, Reformist Alliance 4; opposition: Future Republic 8, Domani Motus Liberi 5, RETE 3), and the 31st Congress of State took office on 24 July 2024 with Beccari continuing in his seat (Grand and General Council composition record; list of current governments). The Captains Regent in office at grading are Gerardo Giovagnoli (PSD) and William Casali (PDCS), invested 1 October 2026 for the semester to 1 April 2027. Premise checks and corrections, stated explicitly: (1) The brief's premise holds — Beccari is still the holder in November 2026; no change of hands occurred. (2) The EU association agreement was not signed in December 2023: negotiations were concluded in December 2023; the agreement was signed on 19 October 2026, after the Council of the EU authorised signature and provisional application on 16 July 2026 (Council of the EU press release, 16 July 2026; European Commission signing record, 19 October 2026). (3) No government crisis and no reshuffle of Beccari's seat appears in the 2025–2026 record assembled here. The one senior personnel change — Massimo Andrea Ugolini becoming Secretary of State for Health in July 2026 — followed the death in office of Health Secretary Mariella Mularoni on 29 May 2026 after a long illness, marked by a national day of mourning on 1 June 2026; it was a succession after a death, not a political reshuffle (San Marino RTV reporting, July 2026; UK Consulate notice of Regential Decree No. 75, 29 May 2026). A coalition partner's two-year review in September 2026 reaffirmed its loyalty to the majority while pressing its own priorities (PSD leadership statement, 10 September 2026) — friction inside a standing majority, not a crisis. Graded: November 2026, on the documented public record. Beccari's record is graded across both legislatures he has served in this seat (2020–present), with the post-2024 record weighted as the current mandate; where an item belongs to the 2020–2024 government he also headed, it is dated as such. Method: The Ethic: "How you play the game matters. Play by the rules. Stay true to your word. And if another option becomes available, at least consider it." Each test is graded only on the evidence listed under it. Both shelves are shown — the strongest evidence that he passes, then the strongest evidence that he fails. Allegations are labeled ALLEGATION and never stated as verdicts. Contested items are named as contested, with who disputes them. Conflicting counts are printed as separate sourced figures, never blended. Seat-holders and public figures are named; people below the public eye appear by role only, unless the documented record itself establishes their wrongdoing. Governing on the platform the voters elected is not graded as a failure; carrying out that platform is evidence on the FOR shelf of Test 5, and marks are lost only for how it is done. Grades: HOLDS / FAILS / MIXED / NOT ENOUGH EVIDENCE. San Marino is a microstate of some 33,660 people (Freedom House, 2026) and its English-language record is thinner than a large state's; where the shelf is thin, the dossier says so rather than padding it. This record is correctable: new evidence changes grades, in the open.

Test 1 — GIVES BY ONE LAW

Same rules for allies and opponents, insiders and outsiders — no favorites at the source.

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • The republic's largest modern corruption case was carried through the courts against its own former governing class, under governments of the seat Beccari holds. In the Conto Mazzini case, several former officials were convicted in 2017 of bribery, corruption, money laundering and vote-buying; in 2022, after years of hearings, an appellate court upheld most of the convictions while reducing nearly all the penalties (Freedom House, Freedom in the World 2024, §C2). The case predates Beccari's appointment as the trial record, but its appellate conclusion fell inside his first term, and no documented intervention by his government to stop it appears in the record assembled here.
  • The formal anti-corruption architecture for the executive itself was built on his watch: a Code of Conduct for members of the Congress of State has been in force since 2022, supplemented by the 2014 Code of Conduct for Public Officials; in 2022 parliament also adopted a code of conduct for lawmakers covering conflicts of interest and asset declarations, and the Judicial Council adopted a code of ethics for judges (GRECO Fifth Round Evaluation Report on San Marino, adopted 22 November 2024; Freedom House, 2024, §C2). Freedom House scores San Marino's safeguards against official corruption at the maximum 4 of 4 (Freedom House, 2024, §C2).
  • The banking clean-up left by the 2010s crisis has been run as a rules-based recovery rather than a rescue of named insiders, on the IMF's record: the state Asset Management Company's recoveries exceeded expectations — €41.7 million of the €70 million government-guaranteed senior tranche repaid by the first half of 2025 (IMF Staff Concluding Statement, 2025 Article IV Mission, 26 September 2025) — and by November 2026 the IMF recorded the senior tranche as fully repaid, with the net non-performing-loan ratio down to 11.1% in 2025 from 16.9% at end-2024 and 21% in 2023 (IMF staff visit statement, 5 June 2026; Central Bank of San Marino summary of the 2026 Article IV mission, 5 November 2026). The same provisioning calendar and the same supervisor (the Central Bank) apply across the sector, state-owned bank included, on the IMF's account.

Evidence against:

  • The anti-corruption referee for the executive — Beccari's own tier — is, on the Council of Europe's anti-corruption body's documented assessment, still built with gaps exactly where insiders stand. GRECO's Fifth Round report (adopted 22 November 2024, published 2 December 2024) covers persons with top executive functions, expressly including members of the Congress of State, and calls for: a specific anti-corruption policy based on a comprehensive risk assessment, which does not yet exist; integrity checks on top executives as part of appointment, which are not performed; harmonised and enforced codes of conduct; rules on gifts, clarified and strengthened; post-employment restrictions with an effective oversight mechanism; rules governing contacts with lobbyists, which do not exist; and asset, interest and liability declarations from all top executives that are regularly checked and made public, which the current system does not deliver (GRECO Fifth Round Evaluation Report, 2024; GRECO press release, 2024). These are findings about the system Beccari heads, printed as findings, not as accusations against him personally.
  • The sharpest one-law failure on the record is a law that was not general in its effect. In Simoncini v. San Marino (no. 14396/24, 19 February 2026), the European Court of Human Rights held that the 2020 legislative intervention with retroactive effect — enacted under the first government Beccari headed, which annulled a sitting first-instance judge's appointment — violated Article 6 §1, amounting to "an unjustified arbitrary course of action aimed at circumventing the principle of the rule of law," and violated Article 8 for its cumulative effects on the judge's salary, reputation and professional life; the Court ordered €150,000 in damages and €10,000 in costs — €160,000 in total, a figure also carried in Repubblica Futura's parliamentary question of November 2026 (ECtHR judgment, 19 February 2026; EU Law Live summary, 20 February 2026). A retroactive law whose practical target was one officeholder is the opposite shape from one law for all; its full judicial-independence weight is graded at Test 2, and it is recorded here for its selectivity.
  • The fiscal adjustment of the current term is formally general but uneven in incidence, on the IMF's own description: the 2026 income-tax reform broadened the base and made the system more progressive (IMF, November 2026, via Central Bank summary) — the for-shelf direction — while the banking sector's remaining losses still sit partly on the public balance sheet through mezzanine and junior securitisation exposures, and the state-owned bank's recapitalisation bond remains to be converted on terms the IMF flags as a public-debt risk (IMF, September 2025; November 2026). The record shows the bill for the old banking model still being socialised while its clean-up proceeds; it does not show any individual depositor or banker exempted by name, and none is alleged here.

Why this grade: The ordinary machinery — courts that convicted former officials, codes adopted in 2022, a banking recovery run by rule and ahead of schedule — points one way; the documented gaps in the integrity system for the executive tier itself, and one retroactive law that Strasbourg found was aimed around a single judge, point the other. Neither shelf is thin enough to dismiss, so the grade is MIXED rather than HOLDS or NOT ENOUGH EVIDENCE.

Helped / Hurt ledger: Helped — taxpayers and the state budget, by AMC recoveries ahead of expectation and a falling NPL ratio (IMF, 2025–2026); ordinary bank customers, by a more stable sector under one supervisory calendar. Hurt — Massimiliano Simoncini, a named public officeholder whose appointment was annulled by retroactive law, on the ECtHR's findings (€160,000 awarded); the public, who carry the banking crisis's residual exposures on the state balance sheet (IMF, 2025–2026). On the two axes: no promise specific to Test 1 was traced to Beccari personally in the record assembled here, so no word-crossing is claimed for this test — recorded as not documented, not invented.

Test 2 — HOLDS WITHOUT GRIPPING

Do the courts, the electoral commission, the media, the prosecution, and the central bank keep their autonomy — or is independence punished and control centralized?

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • The head-of-state office is structurally un-grippable, and its rotation is the strongest single piece of evidence in this dossier on this test. The Captains Regent are two in number, elected by the Grand and General Council from among its members for a single six-month term, installed every 1 April and 1 October, barred from immediate re-election, and subject after their term to the traditional Sindacato della Reggenza review of their conduct (Council of Europe and investiture reporting, 2026). The rotation ran on schedule through the whole graded period — most recently Alice Mina and Vladimiro Selva (1 April–1 October 2026) handing over to Gerardo Giovagnoli and William Casali on 1 October 2026, the pair conventionally drawn from different coalition parties (investiture reporting, 1 October 2026). Beccari himself passed through this office in 2014 and left it on schedule. Whatever weight the executive seat carries in San Marino, it does not carry the headship of state with it, and no act by Beccari to slow, capture or extend the rotation appears in the record.
  • The referees around the seat operate in the open. The Grand and General Council is elected in free elections scored at the maximum by Freedom House, which rates San Marino 97 of 100, Free — political rights 39 of 40, civil liberties 58 of 60 (Freedom House, Freedom in the World 2026). The Central Bank acts as sole financial supervisor and speaks in its own voice, coordinating IMF missions and publishing their conclusions, including conclusions critical of the government's sector (Central Bank of San Marino, 5 November 2026). Opposition parties attack the government in parliament and in print — Repubblica Futura's sustained campaign over the Simoncini case, Domani Motus Liberi's over the hospital — and a parliamentary question can compel the Congress of State to answer in writing (Insider.sm reporting, September–November 2026).
  • The Judicial Council decision at the centre of the 2026 justice dispute was, on the government's documented account, taken unanimously by all seven voting members, including the member designated by the opposition party Repubblica Futura itself (Congress of State statement, 27 September 2026). That fact is contested in its meaning — Repubblica Futura replies that its designee has "complete freedom and autonomy" and owes the party no account, and that the Council's 23 September 2026 act merely took note of the 8 September preliminary report rather than approving it (Repubblica Futura statement, September–October 2026) — but the vote's unanimity is stated by the government and not denied as a count by the opposition.

Evidence against:

  • The judiciary's governing structure has carried a documented political-influence warning for the whole of Beccari's tenure. GRECO's Fourth Round evaluation (2020) recommended changing the Judicial Council's composition to limit political influence and called for structural reform of the judiciary; its Second Compliance Report (adopted 21 June 2024, published 5 July 2024) records that of the original 14 recommendations, one remained not implemented and three only partly implemented at that date (GRECO, 2020; GRECO Second Compliance Report, 2024). Freedom House likewise records GRECO's call for structural changes "to prevent unwarranted political influence" in the judicial sector (Freedom House, 2024, §C2).
  • The 2020 retroactive intervention in a judge's appointment (Test 1) is, on this test, the central documented act: a legislature and executive of Beccari's first government changed the law backwards in time in a way the European Court of Human Rights found circumvented the rule of law, and the judge — Massimiliano Simoncini, appointed February 2019 — lost the post, with substantial salary and reputational consequences (ECtHR, Simoncini v. San Marino, 19 February 2026). The 2020 justice reform was carried by the then Secretary of State for Justice, Massimo Andrea Ugolini — named here because he is a seat-holder and the reform is a matter of documented public record (Repubblica Futura statement reconstructing the reform, September 2026; the same Ugolini was appointed Health Secretary in July 2026).
  • The pattern is alleged — and partly documented — to have continued into 2026 litigation posture. Repubblica Futura's November 2026 parliamentary question states that in both the Simoncini case and the Casali case (ECtHR ruling of 22 October 2026) the government sought the recusal of the San Marino judge at Strasbourg, Gilberto Felici, and raised abuse-of-process objections that the Court rejected, quoting the Court's words that the government's submissions on political affiliation "reveal a strong degree of domestic political tension" and that "the Court is not a forum for the expression of such hostilities" (Repubblica Futura parliamentary question, November 2026, quoting the Casali ruling). The question — which also asks whether the recusal requests were decided collectively by the Congress of State or by an individual Secretary, and seeks an account of a post-judgment mission to Strasbourg by Secretaries Beccari and Stefano Canti — was unanswered in the record assembled here. The characterisation of these steps as institutional overreach is Repubblica Futura's, an opposition party and an interested disputant, and is labeled accordingly: the recusal attempts and the Court's quoted rebuke are documented; their motive is ALLEGATION. Repubblica Futura further alleges that the court director's reconfirmation at the start of this legislature was achieved through an "ad personam" law circumventing a term limit — an ALLEGATION by an opposition party, recorded as such, not a finding (Repubblica Futura statement, September 2026).
  • The media referee carries a documented chill: Freedom House's standing assessment is that while investigative journalists are active, "the risk of heavy fines for defamation can encourage self-censorship" (Freedom House country profile, 2026). No defamation action by Beccari personally appears in the record; the chill is structural and inherited, and is recorded as the environment his governments have not, on the documented record, reformed.

Why this grade: The system's oldest design — a headship of state that changes hands every six months, in pairs, under post-term review — is a standing refusal to grip, and it has held without interference from this seat. Against that stands the one referee that matters most in the recent record, the judiciary: a GRECO political-influence warning unclosed since 2020, a retroactive law Strasbourg condemned, and a 2026 litigation posture the opposition documents as reaching toward the Strasbourg bench itself. The seat does not grip the rotation, the elections or the central bank; the judicial file is heavy enough that HOLDS is not available. MIXED.

Helped / Hurt ledger: Helped — every citizen and every future officeholder, by the six-month paired regency continuing on schedule (2024–2026 investitures); the opposition, whose designee sits and votes on the Judicial Council and whose questions the government must answer. Hurt — Massimiliano Simoncini, on the ECtHR's findings; the judiciary's standing, on GRECO's unclosed recommendations; journalists, by the unreformed defamation-fine chill (Freedom House). On the two axes: the hurt on this test was not the product of a kept public promise traced in this record — not documented — and is recorded in the ledger in full regardless.

Test 3 — ANSWERS THE WOBBLE

When courts, voters, parliament, the street, foreign partners, or events push back, does he adjust openly — or deny and double down?

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • The largest wobble of the term came from foreign partners, and it was absorbed openly and by adjustment. The EU association agreement — negotiations concluded December 2023 — stalled through 2024–2025: the Commission's signing proposals of 26 April 2024 waited while the Council determined in December 2025 that the agreement was of mixed competence, requiring approval in every EU member state's parliament, and while a Bulgarian veto stood. The government did not denounce the process or walk away; the veto was withdrawn, the Council authorised signature and provisional application on 16 July 2026, and Beccari signed on 19 October 2026 (Council of the EU, 16 July 2026; Commission signing record, 19 October 2026). A two-year delay imposed from outside was answered with patience and continued technical work, not retaliation or denial.
  • Inside the wobble at home, Beccari argued in the open and conceded the hard parts on the record. Before the special committee on institutional reforms on 16 October 2026 he acknowledged that dynamic transposition of EU rules "undeniably affects the Council's legislative authority," stated that such a choice "is not necessary in order to launch the agreement," proposed an annual transposition law mixing ordinary debate for political measures with faster tracks for technical ones, and left the proposal open to discussion with the Council's groups (Insider.sm, 16 October 2026). On provisional application he put the choice to parliament in terms: "If the Council decides it doesn't want it, so be it" (Insider.sm, October 2026). In the Foreign Affairs Committee on 9 November 2026 he defended the agreement against referendum demands and bank-readiness criticism while acknowledging "there could be difficulties in the initial phase" (Insider.sm, 9 November 2026). That is the shape of considering, in public, what happens if another option becomes available — and of declining some options (a referendum before signature; waiting years to align unilaterally) with stated reasons, which the method expressly does not count as against-evidence.
  • The death of a cabinet colleague mid-term was handled as continuity, not crisis: after Health Secretary Mularoni's death on 29 May 2026 and the national day of mourning, the health portfolio passed in July 2026 to Ugolini, who within weeks was answering party and union criticism point by point in public — waiting lists, community medicine, the new hospital (San Marino RTV, July 2026; Insider.sm, August 2026). The seat's occupant did not change; the government absorbed the loss without a confidence fight in the record assembled here.

Evidence against:

  • The judicial wobble was answered by doubling down, on the documented sequence. After the ECtHR's Simoncini judgment of 19 February 2026, the Judicial Council's act of 23 September 2026 did not reopen the appointment question; the Congress of State's joint statement of 27 September 2026 defended the outcome — Strasbourg "never ordered the automatic reinstatement" of Simoncini, and later professional evaluations were "broadly negative" — and attacked the opposition's consistency rather than announcing any change of course (Congress of State statement, 27 September 2026). Repubblica Futura's reply — "you're trying to pretend the ECHR rulings don't exist" — is an opponent's characterisation and is labeled as such; but the underlying sequence (judgment, no reinstatement, defence of the position, and then, on RF's documented account, the same recusal-and-abuse objections repeated in the Casali case, rejected again on 22 October 2026) is a record of holding position under judicial pushback, not adjusting to it (Repubblica Futura statements and parliamentary question, September–November 2026). No documented step by the government to implement the Simoncini judgment beyond the payment question appears in the record assembled here; implementation is recorded as an open file, not as refused.
  • A partner inside his own majority pushed back on a programme commitment and the adjustment is, at grading, only promised. In September 2026 the PSD — a governing party — publicly insisted that the indirect-tax/VAT commitment in the government's programme "cannot simply be declared unworkable during this legislature" and that "the discussion must be reopened, and the decision must be a collective one" (PSD leadership statement, 10 September 2026). The statement establishes that the commitment had been treated as unworkable somewhere inside the government; no documented collective re-decision appears in the record assembled here.
  • On readiness for the agreement, the government's own side concedes the wobble is real: PSD councillors stated in November 2026 that "we are not as ready as we should be" and that the country "moved very little" for years (Insider.sm, 9 November 2026) — said in support of the agreement, but an admission that the preparation pushback from the opposition (no development plan, banks unready) rests on something.

Why this grade: Two different wobbles drew two different answers. The external one — a stalled signature, a foreign veto, a competence ruling — was met with open adjustment and public reasoning, including real concessions about parliament's authority. The judicial one — a Strasbourg judgment against his own government's founding justice reform — was met with a defence of the position and, on the documented opposition account, a repetition of the litigation tactics the Court had already rebuked. MIXED, with the split named.

Helped / Hurt ledger: Helped — citizens and businesses waiting on the agreement, by the signature finally delivered on 19 October 2026 after the delay was absorbed rather than escalated; parliament, by the transposition proposal that reserves political measures to ordinary debate. Hurt — Massimiliano Simoncini, again, by the absence of any documented adjustment after the judgment in his favour; the state's standing at Strasbourg, by the Casali sequence as documented by the questioners. No-good-choice note: the mixed-competence delay itself is not held against the holder — the options in 2025 were to accept the slower ratification path or to abandon a decade of negotiation; accepting it is recorded as the constraint it was, not a demerit. The judicial file does not qualify for that clause: a door of compliance and reinstatement review existed and was, on the record, not taken.

Test 4 — COUNTS EVERYONE

Does the accounting include the minorities, the non-citizens, the protesters, and the poor — or are some counted out?

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • The agreement Beccari signed is framed, by him, as an equivalence claim for the whole resident economy, not for a sector: "seeking a right of equivalence for Sammarinese citizens and businesses with their European counterparts" (Beccari, Foreign Affairs Committee, 9 November 2026). Its beneficiaries on paper are all citizens and all firms facing the Italian and European market, and its costs and benefits were debated in committee with the opposition present and quoted (Insider.sm, November 2026).
  • Women's representation moved up on this legislature's record, on two separate sourced counts that are printed separately and not blended: at the June 2024 election women won 18 of 60 seats, 30.0% (IPU Parline monthly ranking, July 2024); the current chamber count stands at 21 of 60, 35.0% (IPU Parline current data; International IDEA Gender Quotas Database). The candidate-list quota — no list may present more than two-thirds of one sex — is legislated (Electoral Law, art. 15). The Congress of State formed in July 2024 included women in senior portfolios, among them Health and Equal Opportunities under Mariella Mularoni until her death (Congress of State composition, 2024).
  • The current term's rights and welfare file, on a governing party's own two-year accounting, includes a citizenship reform and an end-of-life bill approved or launched, recognition of caregivers, a pilot means-tested benefit indicator (ICEE), and renewal of the public employment contract (PSD leadership statement, 10 September 2026). The fiscal side was made formally more equal in the IMF's assessment: the 2026 income-tax reform broadened the base, strengthened compliance and increased progressivity, while public debt fell to 59.8% of GDP in 2025 with a fourth consecutive primary surplus (excluding banking-sector support, 3.8% of GDP) — figures that protect the welfare state's funding rather than cutting it first (IMF, November 2026, via Central Bank summary and reporting).
  • No group is counted out by violence or exclusion from the process on this record: Freedom House scores political rights at 39 of 40 and records that citizens generally enjoy full political rights, with elections, opposition activity and protest unimpeded in the sources assembled here (Freedom House, 2026).

Evidence against:

  • The largest counted-out group in San Marino is counted out by law, and the law is old and strict: about 16% of the population consists of noncitizens — mostly Italian nationals — who hold no political rights, and a person without a citizen spouse, parent or grandparent generally must reside over 30 years to be eligible for naturalisation (Freedom House, 2024, §B4; CIA-derived country data recording a 30-year residency requirement and no recognition of dual citizenship). A citizenship reform is listed by the PSD among the term's measures (September 2026), but its text and its effect on the 30-year rule do not appear in the record assembled here; the reform is therefore recorded as claimed by a governing party, its substance as not documented, and the 30-year figure stands as the last documented rule.
  • Women remain underrepresented at the top of the very seat graded: Freedom House's standing line is that "women are underrepresented in politics" (Freedom House country profile, 2026), the chamber's 35% is a candidate-quota result that preferential voting dilutes (OSCE assessment noted by Freedom House, 2024), and the foreign-affairs seat itself has been held continuously by one man since 2020 — a fact about the seat's occupancy, stated without personalising what is a party appointment.
  • One named file is admittedly unfinished by the government's own side: parenthood for same-sex couples is listed by the PSD as "one still to be addressed" (PSD statement, 10 September 2026). And the agreement's transition costs will not fall evenly: the IMF flags pension reform still to come, bank capital still tight "in some banks," and a transposition workload peaking in the agreement's first three years (IMF, November 2026; Beccari to the institutional reforms committee, 16 October 2026) — burdens that land on pensioners, small depositors and small firms first if mishandled. At grading these are documented risks, not documented outcomes, and are recorded as such.

Why this grade: Nobody in San Marino is counted out by force, and the term's documented movement — representation up on both counts, a more progressive tax reform, an equivalence agreement for all citizens and firms — runs toward inclusion. But one resident in six cannot vote under a 30-year naturalisation rule that no documented reform in this record has yet changed, and the government's own party lists a named rights file as still unaddressed. That is a mixed count, honestly stated. MIXED.

Helped / Hurt ledger: Helped — women candidates and councillors, by the quota and the rise from 18 to 21 seats across the two sourced counts; lower- and middle-income taxpayers, on the IMF's progressivity finding for the 2026 reform; citizens and businesses as a whole, by the equivalence the signed agreement promises. Hurt — the roughly 16% of residents who are noncitizens, who remain without political rights under the documented 30-year rule; same-sex couples awaiting the parenthood file the governing side itself lists as open. On the two axes: the citizenship reform, if it matches the governing party's claim, would be a kept word that helps; its substance is not documented in this record, so the crossing is recorded as not documented rather than claimed.

Test 5 — KEEPS HIS WORD OVER TIME

Program pledges and public statements vs. the record since January 2020, with the post-2024 mandate weighted; the seat's word distinguished from the man's where the record allows.

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for (promises kept or credibly in motion):

  • The European agreement — the seat's central word, kept, late. The association agreement featured in the programmes of the governing majorities across the last four legislatures (Lorenzo Bugli, PDCS, Foreign Affairs Committee, 9 November 2026), and Beccari's own tenure was defined by delivering it: negotiations concluded under his ministry in December 2023, and on 19 October 2026 he signed the agreement in Brussels as San Marino's head-of-government signatory, alongside the amended Monetary Agreement (Commission audiovisual record, 19 October 2026). A PSD statement of November 2026 records the expectation that the Grand and General Council would ratify during November 2026; ratification's completion is not documented in the record assembled here at grading (24 November 2026) and is recorded as in motion, not as done. Crossing on the two axes: kept his word AND it helped people — citizens and businesses gain the equivalence claim of Test 4 — with the transition costs of Test 4 recorded in that ledger in full.
  • Fiscal consolidation — delivered on the numbers. The post-2024 government's programme of consolidation is matched by the IMF's November 2026 findings: public debt below 60% of GDP (59.8% in 2025), a fourth consecutive primary surplus, a new €350 million Eurobond that "significantly reduced refinancing risks," and a positive assessment of the 2026 income-tax reform (IMF 2026 Article IV concluding statement, 5 November 2026, via Central Bank summary and Insider.sm reporting). Crossing: kept his word AND it helped people — the state's solvency underwrites the welfare and health services Tests 4 counts on.
  • The banking word — the clean-up continued on schedule. Calendar provisioning, AMC recovery and NPL reduction proceeded through both terms: NPL ratio 21% (2023) → 16.9% (end-2024) → net 11.1% (2025), the guaranteed senior tranche fully repaid by November 2026 (IMF, September 2025; June 2026; November 2026). The promise here was the seat's standing programme rather than a single dated pledge by Beccari; it is recorded as the government's word, kept in motion.

Evidence against (promises not kept, or receding):

  • The agreement's own timeline — the word was kept, the dates were not. Negotiations concluded in December 2023; the Commission proposed signature in April 2024; signature came on 19 October 2026, after the December 2025 mixed-competence determination and a member-state veto. A governing-party councillor described the final year as "a terrible year, marked by every kind of setback," with repeated moments when "this process was being sabotaged" (Matteo Rossi, PSD, Foreign Affairs Committee, 10 September 2026). Much of the delay sat in Brussels and the member states, outside the seat's control — recorded under Test 3's no-good-choice note — but any expectation of a 2024 signature set by the process's own early announcements was not met, and the dossier records the slip rather than smoothing it.
  • The VAT / indirect-tax commitment — receding inside his own majority. The PSD's September 2026 statement treats the programme's indirect-tax reform (a San Marino-tailored VAT, phasing out the T2 regime) as a commitment that had been declared unworkable and demands the discussion be reopened and decided collectively (PSD leadership statement, 10 September 2026). At grading, no VAT law and no documented collective re-decision appears in the record. Crossing: a programme word not kept on schedule AND the hurt is prospective and contested — businesses face continued uncertainty; no completed harm is documented, and none is claimed.
  • The compliance word after Strasbourg — stated, not yet shown. On 27 September 2026 the Congress of State stated: "No one intends to shirk enforcement of the ruling issued by the European Court of Human Rights. International rulings demand the utmost respect and rigorous implementation" (Congress of State statement, 27 September 2026). Seven months after the Simoncini judgment, the documented record shows the defence of the Judicial Council's position and no documented implementing measure; Repubblica Futura's charge that the government pretends the rulings "don't exist" is an opponent's characterisation, labeled as such. The word as stated — rigorous implementation — is not yet matched by a documented act in this record.
