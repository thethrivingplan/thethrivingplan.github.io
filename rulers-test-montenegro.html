The Ruler's Test — Luís Montenegro, Prime Minister of Portugal

Subject: Luís Montenegro — Prime Minister of Portugal since 2 April 2024, leading the centre-right Democratic Alliance (AD, his Social Democratic Party PSD with CDS–PP). His first minority government (80 of 230 seats) fell on 11 March 2025 when it lost a confidence vote it had itself called during the Spinumviva affair; he won the resulting snap election of 18 May 2025 with a strengthened plurality (91 seats) and was sworn in again on 5 June 2025, still in minority. Graded: November 2026, on the documented public record. Method: Each test is graded only on the evidence listed under it. Both shelves are shown — the strongest evidence that he passes, then the strongest evidence that he fails. Allegations are labeled ALLEGATION and never stated as verdicts. Contested items are named as contested, with who disputes them. Seat-holders and public figures are named; people below the public eye appear by role only, unless the documented record itself establishes their wrongdoing. Grades: HOLDS / FAILS / MIXED / NOT ENOUGH EVIDENCE. This record is correctable: new evidence changes grades, in the open.

Test 1 — GIVES BY ONE LAW

Same rules for allies and opponents, insiders and outsiders — no favorites at the source.

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • The criminal process was allowed to run against the prime minister himself, and he submitted to it. After press revelations about his family company Spinumviva (below), the Public Prosecution Service opened a preventive inquiry (DCIAP, 12 March 2025), the European Public Prosecutor's Office examined a complaint alleging misuse of EU funds (filed by former MEP Ana Gomes), and the Bar Association investigated possible illicit legal practice by the company. All three were archived on stated grounds: the EPPO closed its probe on 27 November 2025 finding no evidence Spinumviva rigged any EU-financed project; the DCIAP closed the preventive inquiry on 1 December 2025 stating there was "no news of the commission of a criminal offence"; the Bar Association closed its file in November 2025 finding no sufficient evidence of illicit attorneyship (PGR/DCIAP note, 1 December 2025; EPPO, November 2025; Bar Association statement, November 2025). Montenegro states he cooperated fully, made himself available to testify, and opened his and his family's finances to investigators (prime-ministerial statement, December 2025 — his account, consistent with the closures as announced).
  • He did not use his office to stop the political process either: when the opposition moved toward a parliamentary inquiry, he called a confidence vote, lost it on 11 March 2025 (88 in favour, 142 against), left office, and faced the voters (Assembly of the Republic record, March 2025).
  • The rule did reach a member of his own government for a lesser version of the same fact pattern: in January 2025 his Secretary of State for Local Administration, Hernâni Dias, resigned after it emerged that he and his family had created real-estate companies while he oversaw the government's land-use legislation (press reporting, January 2025).
  • Under pressure, the underlying facts were eventually disclosed: after initially refusing, Spinumviva published its full client list and the services provided on 28 February 2025, and its largest client, the Solverde casino and hotel group, cancelled its contract on 4 March 2025 (Expresso, February–March 2025).

Evidence against:

  • The fact pattern itself sat at the source of power. Spinumviva, founded in January 2021 by Montenegro, his wife and their two sons — a consultancy whose registered objects included the purchase and sale of real estate — received a monthly payment of €4,500 from Solverde, a casino group holding state concessions, from 2021 onward, continuing while Montenegro was prime minister; he had previously acted as Solverde's legal representative (Expresso, 28 February 2025). Whether any favour was given or received is not established anywhere in the record; the documented point is narrower and sufficient for this test: the prime minister's household income included a standing retainer from a state-regulated business throughout his premiership.
  • The 2022 transfer meant to separate him from the company was legally defective. Montenegro said he had sold his shares to his wife in 2022 on becoming PSD leader; it was then established that share sales between spouses married under community property are not valid in this form, leaving him, on the prevailing legal reading, still a shareholder of the company while he governed (press legal reporting, February–March 2025). The point is CONTESTED as a matter of civil-versus-commercial law, but the ambiguity itself was of his making, and it was never resolved by full divestment outside the family — ownership passed to his wife and sons.
  • Disclosure came in the wrong order. In the 21 February 2025 censure debate he revealed invoicing figures but refused to name the company's clients; the client list was published by the company a week later, only after Expresso had already named Solverde (parliamentary record, 21 February 2025; Expresso, 28 February 2025). The opposition's charge — that transparency was conceded to journalism rather than given to parliament — is a characterization, but the sequence it rests on is documented.
  • The instrument used to investigate him was the softest available. The preventive inquiry is a non-coercive mechanism: as President Rebelo de Sousa publicly noted when the file was archived, it has no power to compel cooperation and depends on voluntary collaboration and the subjects' own deadlines (presidential statements, December 2025). Opposition parties (Livre, PCP) maintain that the archiving settles the criminal question but not the ethics and transparency judgment; the Liberal Initiative's position is that the May 2025 election was the political assessment (party statements, December 2025). Both readings are recorded; the structural fact — the sitting prime minister was examined under the least intrusive instrument in the code — is not disputed.
  • The asymmetry with the Hernâni Dias case is documented in his own government's practice: the secretary of state resigned within days over real-estate companies created during the land-law process, while the prime minister's defence of his own family's company — whose registered objects included real estate, at the moment his government was liberalising building on rural land (Decree-Law 117/2024) — was that no real-estate business had ever been done. Montenegro denied any conflict of interest throughout and denied that Spinumviva ever engaged in property business (Correio da Manhã, 15 February 2025, and subsequent statements). No evidence that the company conducted real-estate transactions has been produced; the grading point is the difference in consequence for the same appearance.

Test 2 — HOLDS WITHOUT GRIPPING

Do courts, the central bank, media, municipalities, and the opposition keep their autonomy — or is independence punished and control centralized?

GRADE: HOLDS

Evidence for:

  • The Constitutional Court struck down the core of his government's flagship immigration law — and the government obeyed, in advance and in fact. Before the ruling, Montenegro stated publicly that "the majority I represent will accept the decision and find a legal solution that respects the court's interpretation" (RTP Madeira, 7 August 2025). On 8 August 2025 the Court (Acórdão 785/2025) declared five provisions unconstitutional; the government redrafted the law to the Court's specifications rather than defying or circumventing it, and the revised law passed in late September 2025 (Constitutional Court, August 2025; Assembly of the Republic, September 2025).
  • The press that investigated him operates untouched. The Spinumviva affair was broken and sustained by Correio da Manhã and Expresso; no retaliation against either outlet — no advertising boycott, no regulatory proceeding, no ownership pressure — is documented anywhere in the record of his tenure.
  • The central bank's autonomy is visibly intact. The government declined to reappoint Mário Centeno when his term ended on 19 July 2025 and nominated Álvaro Santos Pereira, the OECD's chief economist — an appointment within the government's ordinary power, defended on the stated ground that Santos Pereira had been out of government for over a decade and belonged to no party (Reuters, 24 July 2025). Under the new governor the Bank of Portugal continued to publish analysis uncomfortable for the government, including warnings about housing-credit growth and macroprudential risk through 2025–26 (Bank of Portugal reporting, 2025–26). No central banker was removed mid-term or punished for a forecast.
  • The prosecution service investigated the prime minister for nine months and closed the file on its own stated reasoning; the President of the Republic described the archiving as "justice working" and declined to make it a political instrument either way (presidential statements, December 2025).
  • Parliament's autonomy is not theoretical under Montenegro: it defeated his government in a confidence vote, forced an election, and has repeatedly amended or blocked government measures; opposition parties run municipalities across the country and the October 2025 local elections proceeded without any documented interference.
  • When his own party's candidate for president, Luís Marques Mendes, took 11.3% in the first round of the January 2026 presidential election — the lowest result ever for a government-backed candidate — and the runoff was won by the Socialist-backed António José Seguro (66.8% against Chega's André Ventura, 8 February 2026), the result was accepted without dispute and the transfer at Belém proceeded on schedule (CNE official results, 2026).

Evidence against:

  • Rhetorical pressure on the Constitutional Court is documented. Days after the August 2025 ruling gutted his immigration law, Montenegro used his speech at the PSD's Pontal summer festival to publicly attack the Court — the headline finding of the next day's press ("Montenegro attacks the Constitutional Court," Correio da Manhã, 15 August 2025) — while formally complying with the ruling. Compliance with criticism is a lesser offence than defiance, and it is graded as such; but the sequence — accept the decision, then campaign against the referee — is on the record.
  • A route to influencing the Court's composition through the far right was floated from inside his party: in August 2025 senior PSD figure Miguel Relvas publicly called for an agreement with the right that would seat Chega-backed judges on the Constitutional Court (whose judges are elected by a two-thirds parliamentary majority), a prospect President Rebelo de Sousa publicly described as a concern (Expresso reporting, August 2025). No such agreement was concluded on the record; the proposal and the President's rebuke are both documented.
  • The government publicly contested the central bank's economic assessments while Centeno was still in office — Montenegro criticised the Bank's forecasts as overly pessimistic (reporting, 2025) — and then replaced him with a former PSD/CDS economy minister (2011–13). The appointment was lawful and the nominee's independence formally defended; the pattern of criticise-then-replace is nonetheless the documented sequence, and it is the strongest grip-adjacent item in his file.
  • His governments have legislated at speed by decree-law in areas parliament later had to correct — the December 2024 land law (Decree-Law 117/2024), promulgated by the President with publicly stated reservations and amended by parliament within weeks, is the documented example (presidential promulgation note, January 2025; Assembly of the Republic, February 2025).

Test 3 — ANSWERS THE WOBBLE

When courts, voters, parliament, unions, or events push back, does he adjust openly — or deny and double down?

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • The immigration ruling was absorbed, not fought: the law was rewritten to the Constitutional Court's text and re-passed within two months (August–September 2025).
  • The confidence-vote loss was absorbed completely: he resigned, campaigned, accepted the arithmetic the voters returned — another minority — and governed on it rather than contesting the result or the process (March–June 2025).
  • On youth taxation he adopted the other side's design: his proposed 15% flat income-tax cap for under-35s was dropped in the 2025 budget negotiations in favour of a progressive exemption scheme (100% exemption in the first working year, tapering over ten years) modelled on the Socialists' approach — a public, negotiated reversal that benefited up to ~400,000 young workers (2025 budget record, October 2024).
  • On the labour package he did move, partially and late: after calling the union federations in for talks in late November 2025 he amended some proposals before the strike (BBC reporting, December 2025).
  • On the wildfires, the correction, when it came, was operational: he cancelled his holidays, the government activated the EU Civil Protection Mechanism (15 August 2025) and accepted Moroccan and EU firefighting aircraft, and pledged support to the devastated regions (government statements, August 2025).

Evidence against:

  • The Spinumviva sequence was denial first, disclosure under duress, and exit over inquiry. For two weeks he refused to name the company's clients and framed the reporting as insinuation; when the PS moved to open a parliamentary inquiry committee, he chose instead to stake his government on a confidence vote — a step his office framed as dispelling uncertainty, and which the opposition framed, on the documented sequence, as cutting the inquiry off (parliamentary record, February–March 2025). After the inquiries were archived in December 2025, his national address attacked the "media and political amplification of anonymous, unfounded allegations" and warned of a "totalitarian temptation" in political, journalistic and judicial behaviour (prime-ministerial statement, December 2025) — the wobble answered, in the end, by indicting the wobblers.
  • The August 2025 wildfires are the clearest deny-then-adjust file of his tenure. On 31 July, with fires already burning, he stated Portugal had sufficient resources to handle the situation (government statement, 31 July 2025). As the burned area climbed past 200,000 hectares — 278,438 hectares by season's end, the worst year since 2017, with four deaths (ICNF/EFFIS figures) — he attended his party's Pontal summer festival in the Algarve while the north burned, cancelled his holidays only afterwards, activated the EU mechanism on 15 August after days of criticism over the delay, and said he would assess the fires only after "winning the war" (Público and international reporting, August 2025). The mayor of Vila Real publicly criticised the earlier "prepared" assurances; former leaders of parties including his own coalition partner CDS joined the criticism of the festival attendance. He had not visited the hard-hit north as of 19 August (Insurance Journal/AP reporting, 19 August 2025). Adjustment came; it came after the photographs.
  • On the labour reform he doubled down through the largest mobilisation of his tenure. The Work XXI package (100+ labour-code amendments: easier dismissal in SMEs, longer rollable temporary contracts, outsourcing after dismissal, expanded minimum services during strikes) was presented in July 2025; both union confederations — CGTP and the moderate UGT, acting together for the first time since 2013 — called a general strike for 11 December 2025 that halted trains, cancelled hundreds of flights and closed schools. Montenegro refused to withdraw the package: "it is a government with a reformist spirit and will not give up on being reformist and transformative" (Reuters, 11 December 2025). The bill then stalled in parliament through 2026, its support shifting — by June 2026 his own coalition partner CDS–PP was publicly complaining that Chega had voted with the left against the package (CDS–PP statement, June 2026). Refusal to concede, followed by loss of control of the outcome, is the documented pattern.

Test 4 — COUNTS EVERYONE

Does the accounting include the whole flock — immigrants, the young priced out of housing, pensioners, rural communities, his opponents' voters — or are some counted out?

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • The young were given real, used instruments, not only rhetoric. From August 2024, buyers up to 35 pay no IMT transfer tax or stamp duty on a first home; a state guarantee of up to 15% lets banks finance 100% of purchases up to €450,000. Uptake is documented at scale: under-35s signed 58% of mortgage contracts for permanent homes in 2025, the guarantee backed 42% of young buyers' mortgages that year, and by end-March 2026 it had covered 32,338 contracts and €6.5 billion of lending (Bank of Portugal data, 2025–26). The IRS Jovem scheme gives a decade of tapering income-tax exemptions to young workers (2025 budget).
  • Pensioners were counted in cash: his governments paid an extraordinary supplement (€100–€200 by pension level) in September 2024 and repeated it in 2025, on top of formula increases, and followed the social-concertation wage path that put the minimum wage ahead of the government's own programme promise (see Test 5).
  • His opponents' voters were governed with, not against: a minority prime minister by arithmetic, he passed his budgets with Socialist abstention rather than by emergency procedure, consulted the PS on the youth-tax redesign, and left opposition municipalities and a Socialist-backed presidency to function (budget votes, 2024–25; presidential election, 2026).
  • Immigrants already inside the system were processed at greater speed: the inherited AIMA backlog of hundreds of thousands of pending cases was attacked through a dedicated mission structure, and automatic permit extensions were replaced by a functioning renewal system from October 2025 (AIMA reporting, 2025).

Evidence against:

  • New and recent immigrants were counted down by design, repeatedly, and partly beyond what the constitution allows. In June 2024 — weeks into his first government — the "expression of interest" regularisation route was abolished by decree with immediate effect, leaving tens of thousands already working in Portugal without the pathway they had entered under (Decree-Law 37-A/2024). In May 2025, during the election period, AIMA began notifying some 18,000 rejected applicants to leave the country — a timing the opposition criticised as electoral, which the government defended as routine administration (CONTESTED as to motive; the notifications are documented). The July 2025 Foreigners Law then imposed a two-year wait for family reunification and restricted recourse to the courts; the Constitutional Court struck those provisions down as violations of family unity and access to justice, while upholding the law's preferential treatment for golden-visa holders and the highly qualified — a two-tier design, by wealth and skill, that the government kept in the revised Law 61/2025 (two-year family-reunification wait in softened form; job-seeker visa restricted to the highly qualified). The 2026 nationality law doubled the residence requirement for most applicants (five years to ten; seven for Portuguese-speaking countries) and added civic testing; a companion provision allowing courts to strip naturalised citizens of citizenship was blocked by the Constitutional Court for a second time in July 2026; and in September 2026 President Seguro sent a further tightened immigration package to the Constitutional Court for review of eleven rules on asylum, detention and expulsion (Constitutional Court decisions, 2025–26; Assembly of the Republic; presidential referral, September 2026). Foreign residents numbered ~1.5 million, about 15% of the population, in 2024 (AIMA figures).
  • The young were counted in as buyers and counted out by the price. On the statistics institute's own index, house prices rose 17.6% in 2025 — the largest annual increase since the series began in 2009 — and roughly 27% cumulatively since his first housing package of August 2024; prices were still rising 16.5% year-on-year in mid-2026, with the IMF estimating overvaluation of 20–40% (INE House Price Index, 2025–26; IMF assessment). The government's demand-side subsidies are themselves identified by the central bank's data as fuel inside the segment they target: state-guaranteed loans made up about a quarter of new mortgage value in 2025 while the affordable segment shrank (Bank of Portugal; market reporting, 2026). Completions (30,425 homes in 2025) lag far behind need, and the structural backdrop is unchanged: about 850,000 Portuguese aged 15–39 — some 30% of that generation — live abroad (Emigration Observatory). His supply answers (a 6% VAT rate for moderately priced construction; a €1.34 billion EIB facility for ~12,000 affordable homes; €2 billion for housing in the 2027 budget) are documented and mostly land after 2026.
  • The rural interior carried the fire year. The 278,438 hectares burned in 2025 fell overwhelmingly on the depopulated interior; Público reported in August 2025 that only 40% of the forest-clearing targets in the National Action Plan had been met. The people who live with the risk were counted in the response rhetoric ("we are at war") after being counted out of the prevention ledger (Público, 12 August 2025; ICNF figures).

Test 5 — KEEPS HIS WORD OVER TIME

Campaign promises and stated positions vs. the record since April 2024, with dates.

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for (promises kept or credibly in motion):

  • Tax cuts — delivered in sequence. Successive IRS rate reductions in the 2024 and 2025 budgets, the expanded IRS Jovem, and the start of the promised gradual corporate-tax reduction (IRC cut from 21% to 20% in the 2025 budget) match the AD programme's central fiscal pledge (budget laws, 2024–25).
  • Minimum wage — ahead of his own promise. The October 2024 social-concertation agreement set a path (€870 in 2025, €920 in 2026, rising to €1,020 in 2028) that tracks the programme's €1,000-by-2028 pledge; in September 2026 Montenegro proposed reaching €1,000 already in 2027, a year early (social-concertation agreement, October 2024; government statements, September 2026).
  • Defence — accelerated. The programme's commitment to reach NATO's 2% of GDP was brought forward: at the June 2025 NATO summit his government committed to 2% in 2025 itself (NATO summit reporting, June 2025).
  • No Chega in government — kept in form. His 2024 campaign formula "não é não" ("no is no") ruled out agreements with Chega. After the 2024 election and again after May 2025 — when Chega became the second party with 60 seats and its leader became leader of the opposition — he refused coalition, refused Chega ministers, and called the party "unreliable and unfit to govern" (statements, May 2025). The letter of the pledge has been kept twice, at the price of governing in permanent minority.

Evidence against (promises not kept, or kept only in form):

  • "Não é não" — strained in substance. The pledge was understood by voters in 2024 as no reliance on Chega; the practice since 2025 is that his government's flagship measures pass on Chega votes: the revised Foreigners Law of September 2025 was, in the law-firm and press record, "adjusted by the government with support from Chega" after the Constitutional Court's ruling, as was the nationality tightening; and in December 2025 the government openly sought Chega's backing to pass the labour package (Reuters, 11 December 2025). Montenegro's defence — that voting arithmetic is not an agreement, and that he negotiates with all parties — is on the record. Both the pledge and the practice are documented; the distance between them is the grade.
  • Transparency in his own case — promised, then rationed. He pledged full clarification of Spinumviva, then refused parliament the client list (21 February 2025), disclosed it only after the press had named the main client, and after exoneration described the scrutiny itself as a "totalitarian temptation" (December 2025). The word given in February was "explain everything"; what was delivered was everything, eventually, under compulsion of events.
  • Housing — measures delivered, the promised outcome reversed. He promised to attack the housing crisis; the instruments arrived (Test 4) and prices set records in the opposite direction — +17.6% in 2025 on INE's index — leaving the median young buyer further from ownership at the end of 2026 than at his inauguration, on prices if not on credit terms. A promise graded on its stated aim — affordable access — is not kept by the delivery of its instruments alone.
  • The labour package was not in the shop window. The opposition's documented charge — that the scale of the Work XXI rollback of dismissal and strike rules was not put to voters in the May 2025 campaign — is CONTESTED by the government, which points to its programme's general reform commitments (Al Jazeera/Reuters reporting, December 2025). The specific 100+ measures were published in July 2025, after the election; that sequence is not contested.

Overall Summary

One law: MIXED · Without gripping: HOLDS · The wobble: MIXED · Counts everyone: MIXED · His word: MIXED.

Luís Montenegro's file is the institutional inverse of most in this series: the grip is not the story. In two and a half years no judge, central banker, prosecutor or editor is documented to have been punished for ruling, investigating or publishing against him; the Constitutional Court gutted his flagship law and he had pre-committed to obeying it; parliament dismissed his government and he went to the country and took the answer. What runs the other way is the conduct at the centre and the count at the edges. At the centre: a prime minister whose family company drew a monthly retainer from a state-regulated casino group throughout his premiership, under a share transfer that did not legally do what he said it did, disclosed fully only when journalism forced it — investigated, in the end, by the least coercive instrument available and cleared by it. At the edges: an immigration regime tightened in steps the Constitutional Court twice had to trim, and a generation of young Portuguese handed state-guaranteed mortgages into a market that rose 17.6% in a single year. He holds the seat more loosely than almost anyone graded here. The record asks whether the looseness at the top and the tightness at the border are the same policy — and grades that question MIXED, because the evidence does.
