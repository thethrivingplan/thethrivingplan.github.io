The record · Nov. 3–9, 2026

Trump-aligned officials who resigned after the election

Since the midterm results of Tuesday, November 3, 2026 — in which Democrats won control of both the House and the Senate — seven Trump-supporting government officials have a confirmed resignation on the record. All seven are federal: two in the White House, five in the Justice Department. The list runs from the top down, highest office first.

No Cabinet secretary, no senator, no representative, no governor, and no state or local official meeting the same test was found to have resigned in the window. The big rumored names — Rubio, Hegseth, Grassley, Hassett — did not resign; their stories were denied on the record, and they sit in their own section below, never mixed into the confirmed list.

Tier 1 — White House senior staff

No Cabinet member — no Secretary of State, Defense, or any other — has a confirmed resignation in this window. The two confirmed entries at this level are White House senior staff.

1

Susie Wiles

White House Chief of Staff — Executive Office of the President

Contested — resigned after loss of Trump’s confidence
Trump basis
Trump appointee and longtime Trump aide; she was Trump’s campaign manager in the 2024 election before becoming the first woman White House chief of staff.
Announced
Resignation submitted Thursday evening, Nov. 5; publicly announced by Trump Friday, Nov. 6.
Effective
Her last day was Friday, Nov. 6 — she posted on X that day, “Today is my last day as White House Chief of Staff.” Her successor, Stephen Miller, takes over as chief of staff on Monday, Nov. 9, 2026 — Nov. 9 reporting confirms he takes over Monday as scheduled. Wiles moves to the Trump Political Operation — she is not leaving Trump’s orbit entirely.
Wiles herself

Serving was “the honor of a lifetime”; “While my role may be changing, my commitment to the President and the mission never will.”

Trump, publicly

The departure was “not because of the election”; “It’s just time. Sometimes you change”; “I wanted to make a change… sometimes in life, you have to make moves.”

CNN reporting

Trump “voiced fury” at the midterm strategy and Wiles “was among those Trump blamed for the Republican losses.” Per a person familiar with her thinking, she decided to resign believing Trump should be able to tap someone who had his confidence, after a tough year including cancer treatment — “It wasn’t worth her staying in a job Trump was making clear he didn’t want her in.” USA Today (Nov. 7) likewise reported two sources close to the administration saying Wiles “is being blamed for the GOP’s midterm catastrophe.”

Sources: CNN, Nov. 6, 2026 — cnn.com · Daily Caller, Nov. 6, 2026 — dailycaller.com · USA Today, Nov. 7, 2026 — usatoday.com

2

Richard Walters

White House Deputy Chief of Staff

Voluntary / amicable
Trump basis
Trump White House appointee; former longest-tenured chief of staff of the Republican National Committee and one of outgoing Chief of Staff Susie Wiles’ closest political lieutenants, with a relationship stretching back more than a decade.
Announced
Offered his resignation late Friday, Nov. 6; reported Saturday, Nov. 7 by Axios (“Scoop: First major White House departure as Miller takes charge,” Alex Isenstadt).
Effective
Not reported. Incoming Chief of Staff Stephen Miller said Walters “will continue to help from the outside,” and praised him as “an invaluable member of the White House.”
Stated reason
None specific. Walters told Axios: “Serving President Trump has been the honor of a lifetime and I will always be thankful to Susie Wiles for the opportunity,” and “Stephen is building an incredible and capable team to implement the President’s agenda and I look forward to being part of it from the outside.” A person familiar with the matter described the departure as amicable.

Sources: Primary source is Axios, Nov. 7, 2026. The full Axios text was not directly retrievable in this research; its wording is quoted verbatim by Political Wire, Nov. 7, 2026 — politicalwire.com — and by WLT Report, Nov. 7, 2026 — wltreport.com (WLT Report is a partisan, low-credibility aggregator — rely on the Axios attribution and Walters’ own on-the-record statement to Axios, not on WLT’s framing).

Tier 2 — Justice Department senior officials

Four of the five confirmed entries at this level are Senate-confirmed Justice Department leaders; the fifth, Henry Whitaker, is a counselor to the Attorney General and sits last in this tier. No other agency head was found to have resigned in the window.

3

Harmeet Kaur Dhillon

Assistant Attorney General for Civil Rights, U.S. Department of Justice

Contested — voluntary on her account, forced on CBS’s
Trump basis
Trump appointee — nominated by Trump in December 2024, Senate-confirmed April 3, 2025; a former Republican Party official and a lawyer for President Trump’s campaign.
Announced
Thursday, Nov. 5, 2026. Attorney General Todd Blanche posted on X, Nov. 5: “Today, I accepted the resignation of Assistant Attorney General Harmeet Dhillon, who led the Civil Rights Division with conviction the past 18 months.” She formally submitted her resignation Nov. 5 to President Trump and AG Blanche.
Effective
No separate final day was reported in Blanche’s announcement.
Acting head
The Civil Rights Division now has an acting head — not a permanent successor. Craig Trainor will serve as Acting Assistant Attorney General for Civil Rights, announced Tuesday evening, Nov. 10, 2026 by Attorney General Todd Blanche in a post on X — Trainor was picked by President Trump to serve as acting AAG for civil rights. Trainor currently serves in a Senate-confirmed leadership role at the Department of Housing and Urban Development — Assistant Secretary for Fair Housing and Equal Opportunity, Senate-confirmed last year. He supersedes Jesus Osete, Dhillon’s former Principal Deputy, who was DOJ-listed as Acting AAG for Civil Rights on Nov. 9, 2026 (DOJ Nov. 9 Glendale and Lyft press releases). This is an acting head only — no permanent nominee has been announced.
Caveat — the decision predates the election: Dhillon says she first offered her resignation about three weeks earlier. She told Fox News she informed Chief of Staff Susie Wiles weeks earlier, in early October (last meeting Oct. 13): “I wasn’t asked to resign. I resigned three weeks ago, and Susie asked me to please wait until after the midterms.” She is on this list because the count covers resignations announced or effective Nov. 3–9, and both the formal submission and the public announcement were Nov. 5. On a strict “first communicated” test, she would be a borderline note instead.
Dhillon’s version — voluntary

Returning to her California private practice, Dhillon Law Group; she had “accomplished what I came to do” (resignation letter, quoted by USA Today). In a text to USA Today: “these jobs are tough.” Her statement: “Serving my country as AAG for Civil Rights has been the honor of a lifetime… I want to thank President Trump for giving me this opportunity.”

CBS News version — forced

CBS reported later on Nov. 5 that Dhillon “had been pushed into the resignation by Trump over her less-than-forceful appearance on ‘60 Minutes’” (the Oct. 18 segment on DOJ election monitoring). Dhillon denied being asked to resign, and her October meeting with Wiles predated the broadcast.

Sources: New York Post, Nov. 5, 2026 — nypost.com · USA Today, Nov. 6, 2026 — usatoday.com · Inside Higher Ed, Nov. 6, 2026 (summarizing Bloomberg Law / Fox News / CBS News) — insidehighered.com · Acting-head detail: Bloomberg Law / Bloomberg Tax, Nov. 11, 2026, “Trump Taps Housing Official to Lead DOJ Civil Rights Division” · New York Times syndication, Nov. 11, 2026 · U.S. Department of Justice listing for Jesus Osete as Acting Assistant Attorney General for Civil Rights, and DOJ press releases, Nov. 9, 2026 (Glendale, California Second Amendment lawsuit; Lyft disability lawsuit)

4

John A. Eisenberg

Assistant Attorney General for National Security, U.S. Department of Justice

Contested — reported ordered to resign
Trump basis
Trump appointee — Senate-confirmed and sworn in June 5, 2025. In Trump’s first term he served as Legal Advisor to the National Security Council, Assistant to the President, and Deputy Counsel to the President. One nuance: Bloomberg Law’s sources said he “was never viewed as a Trump loyalist like other DOJ political leaders” — he qualifies as a Trump appointee, not as a reported loyalist.
Announced
Friday, Nov. 6, 2026 — AG Blanche announced on social media that he had accepted Eisenberg’s resignation.
Effective
Not separately reported; no successor was named in the announcement.
Stated reason
None given publicly by Eisenberg or Blanche. Blanche’s public statement was praise only: he “has served with distinction, strengthening our nation’s ability to address threats, both foreign and domestic.” Reuters, as summarized in multiple outlets Nov. 6, reported it was “not clear why either man resigned” (Eisenberg and Quiñones).
Bloomberg Law, Nov. 6

Eisenberg “was ordered to resign” — Blanche “forced out” / “ousted” him after clashes over the pace of White House-directed media-leak investigations. Per Bloomberg, Eisenberg had tried to slow Trump’s demands to compel grand-jury testimony from reporters about sources in critical stories, insisting DOJ protocols requiring other fact-gathering steps be followed first; another person cited a larger concern over his lack of experience managing a large team.

The dispute — Washington Examiner, Nov. 8

Bloomberg’s subpoena claim “was quickly disputed by a senior official’s comments to the Washington Examiner, who said Eisenberg’s exit had nothing to do with the reporter subpoenas.” The Examiner did not report a denial that he was ordered to resign — only a denial of that stated reason.

Sources: Bloomberg Law, Nov. 6, 2026 — news.bloomberglaw.com · Washington Examiner, Nov. 8, 2026 — washingtonexaminer.com · DOJ biography — justice.gov

5

Brett Shumate

Assistant Attorney General for the Civil Division, U.S. Department of Justice

Trump basis
Trump appointee — nominated by President Trump, Senate-confirmed 51–41 on June 9, 2025; he has led the Civil Division since June 2025 and also served in the Justice Department in Trump’s first term.
Announced
Monday, Nov. 9, 2026 — Attorney General Todd Blanche announced on X that he accepted Shumate’s resignation.
Effective
Effective immediately on Monday, Nov. 9, 2026 — CBS News reported the departure was effective immediately that day, described as abrupt and a surprise to Civil Division lawyers, according to CBS sources.
Acting head
The Civil Division now has an acting head — not a permanent successor. Jonathan Guynn is serving as the Civil Division’s Principal Deputy Assistant Attorney General and Acting Assistant Attorney General, effective Tuesday, Nov. 10, 2026, per the U.S. Department of Justice Civil Division Leadership page (justice.gov). Guynn previously led the Torts Branch as a Deputy Assistant Attorney General. This is an acting head only — no permanent nominee has been announced.
Stated reason
None given on the record. Reuters reported Nov. 9 that it was not immediately clear why Shumate was resigning — that remains the prior on-record position. When USA Today asked why, and whether Blanche was unhappy with his work, the Justice Department referred it back to Blanche’s social-media post praising Shumate — including his division’s challenges to local jurisdictions that refused to work with federal immigration enforcement and its settlements ending corporate DEI practices. Single-source CBS reporting — not settled fact: one source familiar with the matter told CBS News that incoming White House Chief of Staff Stephen Miller had been critical of Shumate in the past, believed he was not aggressive enough, and that this factored into the resignation. This entry is classified as a resignation; beyond that single-source CBS account, its voluntariness was not reported as contested, and it is not labeled forced or voluntary beyond that.
The count

Reuters and USA Today count Shumate as the fourth senior Justice Department official to resign since the Nov. 3 midterms, after Harmeet Dhillon, John Eisenberg, and Jason Reding Quiñones.

Sources: Reuters, Nov. 9, 2026 · USA Today, Nov. 9, 2026 · Bloomberg Law, Nov. 9, 2026 · CBS News, reported Nov. 9–10, 2026 (summarized by Archynewsy, Nov. 10, 2026; Particle, Nov. 10, 2026; and Joyce Vance, Nov. 10, 2026 — all citing CBS News) · Acting-head detail: U.S. Department of Justice Civil Division Leadership page, justice.gov (Jonathan Guynn listed as Principal Deputy Assistant Attorney General and Acting Assistant Attorney General, effective Nov. 10, 2026)

6

Jason Reding Quiñones

U.S. Attorney for the Southern District of Florida, U.S. Department of Justice

Resignation under pressure from DOJ leadership
Trump basis
Trump appointee — appointed by Trump to lead the U.S. attorney’s office for the Southern District of Florida, 15 months after Senate confirmation. He led the so-called “grand conspiracy” investigation into officials who investigated Trump. In his own resignation post he wrote, “I am grateful to President Trump for his trust.”
Announced
Friday, Nov. 6, 2026 — announced by AG Blanche on X and confirmed by Quiñones in his own social-media post that day.
Effective
Friday, Nov. 6, 2026 — Quiñones: “I submitted my resignation to the President as U.S. Attorney for the Southern District of Florida, effective today.” The next acting U.S. Attorney is reported to be his first assistant, Yara Klukas (Washington Examiner, Nov. 8, citing a source familiar with the matter).
Stated reason
None given publicly — neither Quiñones nor Blanche gave a reason. Blanche’s public statement thanked him for “leadership in combatting violent crime, drug trafficking, sex trafficking, and transnational crime, including the indictment of Raúl Castro.” Reporting fills in the pressure: CNN (Nov. 6) reported he “resigned… under pressure from department leadership, despite his willingness to pursue some of the most controversial investigations desired by the president,” with top officials blaming him when a promised “grand conspiracy” case failed to materialize within months; AP reported frustrations in Washington over his management, with grand-jury testimony from several witnesses postponed; the New York Post (Nov. 6) reported Blanche had been “frustrated with Quinones for months.”

Sources: CNN, Nov. 6, 2026 — cnn.com · USA Today, Nov. 6, 2026 — usatoday.com · New York Post, Nov. 6, 2026 — nypost.com

7

Henry Whitaker

Senior Counselor / Counselor to the Attorney General, U.S. Department of Justice

Voluntary — leaving for another opportunity
Trump basis
Trump appointee — appointed by Trump in January 2025 as acting head of the Office of Legal Counsel, then counselor to Attorneys General Pam Bondi and Todd Blanche; he was also a senior Office of Legal Counsel official in Trump’s first administration.
Announced
Thursday, Nov. 5, 2026 — the Justice Department said in a court filing that Whitaker “is imminently leaving the government for another opportunity.” Bloomberg Law’s Nov. 9, 2026 report lists Whitaker among the DOJ officials who “have all resigned in recent days.”
Effective
He left the Justice Department on Friday, Nov. 6, 2026, and starts Monday, Nov. 9, 2026 as a partner at the law firm Dechert.
Stated reason
Leaving for another opportunity — the Dechert partnership. Classified here as voluntary.
Caveat — the counts differ: Reuters and USA Today count four senior DOJ resignations since the midterms and do not count Whitaker, a counselor/aide whose departure was reported Nov. 5 as anticipated; Bloomberg Law counts him among those who resigned. He was not in the original Nov. 8 list because at that time reporting said only that he was “imminently leaving,” with no resignation wording or last day; the Nov. 9 Bloomberg reports supply both.

Sources: Bloomberg Law, Nov. 5, 2026 · Bloomberg Law, Nov. 9, 2026 · Bloomberg Tax, Nov. 9, 2026

The tiers below: no confirmed resignations

Checked tier by tier, top to bottom. An empty tier is stated plainly rather than padded — and the near-misses are named, with why each one does not count.

  • U.S. Senators — none

    No senator was found to have announced or effected a resignation Nov. 3–9, 2026. The only senator story in the window was the Grassley speculation, denied on the record — see Rumored, not resigned below.

  • U.S. Representatives — none from Congress

    Mike Johnson (R-LA) did not resign from Congress. On Wednesday morning, Nov. 4, Johnson told staff he will not seek to remain House Republican leader in the next Congress — AP described it as “stepping aside from Republican Party leadership after the GOP’s wipeout.” He won reelection Nov. 3 with about 58% and “is not leaving Congress.” Some social posts mislabel this a resignation; the reputable reporting is a leadership-only decision effective with the new Congress in January. (Senate GOP leader John Thune, by contrast, told Roll Call Nov. 4 he intends to seek reelection as leader — the viral claim that “both leaders resigned the same night” is false on both halves.)

    Source: QNC News / Bloomberg Government, Nov. 4, 2026 — qncnews.com

  • Governors & statewide elected officials — none

    Texas Gov. Greg Abbott lost reelection Nov. 3 to Democrat Gina Hinojosa and conceded — an election loss, not a resignation; he leaves office in January. Hawaii Attorney General Anne Lopez was reported Nov. 6 to be resigning early next month, but Lopez is a Democratic governor’s appointee, not Trump-aligned, so she fails the Trump-basis test. No Trump-aligned governor, attorney general, secretary of state, lieutenant governor, or treasurer resignation was found in Nov. 3–9 reporting.

  • State legislators — none meeting the rules

    Tennessee Rep. Jeremy Faison (R, District 11) was reported Nov. 4 as “stepping down after 16 years,” but it is a scheduled end-of-term departure: Faison announced on Jan. 15, 2026 that he would not seek reelection, and his successor Kenny Cody was elected Nov. 3. New Mexico Rep. Andrea Reeb (R) resigned Oct. 15, 2026 — before the window — was reelected Nov. 3, and announced Nov. 5 she will take the seat for the new term.

    Source: The Tennessean, Jan. 15, 2026 — tennessean.com

  • Local officials — none meeting the Trump-basis test

    Two in-window Republican local resignations were found, but a Republican label alone does not meet the test — no Trump appointment, Trump endorsement, public Trump endorsement, or MAGA-aligned reporting was found for either, so neither is counted:

    · Mona (Durrett) Wilson, Mayor of Dubach, Louisiana (Republican) — resigned Thursday, Nov. 5, effective immediately, in a signed memo dated Nov. 5, after about a week on medical leave. Stated reason: her “health and well-being” and that she could no longer work with Councilwoman Evelyn Graham. She had led the Nov. 3 mayoral field with 121 votes (42%) and also asked to withdraw from the Dec. 12 runoff. Sources: Lincoln Parish Journal, Nov. 6, 2026 — lincolnparishjournal.com; Ruston Daily Leader, Nov. 6, 2026 — rustonleader.com

    · Caleb Stidham, Erie County Treasurer, Ohio (Republican; also Erie County GOP chair) — announced (reported Nov. 8) that he is out as treasurer starting Monday, Nov. 9, to seek appointment Nov. 10 to the Ohio House District 89 seat he won on Nov. 3: a move-up transition. Source: StayTunedSandusky (Sandusky Register), Nov. 8, 2026 — staytunedsandusky.com

    Also excluded: Pasco County, FL Sheriff Chris Nocco resigned late Monday, Nov. 2 (eve of the election) to run for a Florida Senate seat he then won, with the departure first announced in May — pre-window. Lewiston, ME Councilor Scott Harriman resigned after election to the Maine State House, but Harriman is a Democrat — not Trump-aligned.

Rumored — not resigned

These stories circulated in the same window. None is a resignation. Each was denied on the record or amounts to pressure and prediction only — kept separate from the confirmed list, always.

  • Marco Rubio — Secretary of State

    Status: reported, then denied on the record. No resignation. The Independent (Thu Nov. 5) reported Rubio was “on resignation watch” / preparing to resign to position for a 2028 presidential run; Bloomberg separately reported Rubio and advisers were considering his future, with no decision and no timetable. Denials came Nov. 5: Rubio told the New York Times staying to the end of Trump’s term “has always been my plan”; State Dept. Assistant Secretary Dylan Johnson called it “complete and total fake news… The Secretary has not discussed leaving his role”; the White House called it “baseless, false conspiracy theories.” As of Nov. 6, no outlet had a confirmation of any resignation plan.

    Sources: MEAWW fact-check, Nov. 6, 2026 — news.meaww.com · OK Magazine, Nov. 5, 2026 — okmagazine.com

  • Pete Hegseth — Secretary of Defense

    Status: pressure and calls to resign only. He says he is staying. No resignation. Rep. Thomas Massie (R-KY) said the one contingency in which he would not reintroduce his eight impeachment articles is if “Hegseth resigns.” Hegseth responded Nov. 5 in a video: “We don’t back down and that especially applies now,” and said he is committed to remaining at the Pentagon to the end of Trump’s term. Trump said Nov. 4–5 he intends to keep Hegseth, and a spokeswoman said Trump “has full confidence in Secretary Hegseth” — while CBS News reported Trump had privately asked advisers whether Hegseth should stay and that Ron DeSantis had been floated as a possible replacement, with no decision made.

    Sources: USA Today, Nov. 5, 2026 — usatoday.com · Newsmax, Nov. 6, 2026 — newsmax.com

  • Chuck Grassley — U.S. Senator (R-IA)

    Status: speculation explicitly denied. No resignation. After Democrat Rob Sand won the Iowa governorship Nov. 3, speculation circulated that the 93-year-old Grassley might resign early so outgoing Republican Gov. Kim Reynolds could appoint a Republican successor before Sand takes office in January. Grassley’s chief of staff Jennifer Heins Davis posted Thursday, Nov. 5: “Senator @ChuckGrassley is not resigning. He remains committed to delivering for the people of Iowa, who overwhelmingly elected him to a six-year term in 2022.” Grassley himself had said Sept. 25, “I don’t plan on dying in office.”

    Source: Des Moines Register, Nov. 5, 2026 — desmoinesregister.com

  • Kevin Hassett — Director of the National Economic Council, White House

    Status: reported departing, then denied on the record the same day by the White House and by Hassett himself. No resignation. Trump basis: Trump appointee — NEC Director; he served as chairman of the White House Council of Economic Advisers during Trump’s first administration and later as a senior adviser during COVID-19 (Reuters). On Friday, November 13, 2026, Semafor, citing three people familiar with the plan, reported Hassett was departing in the coming weeks and had been inquiring about roles outside the administration since before the November 3 midterm elections. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, who could hold both roles, and his counselor David Zervos are among the people being eyed to replace him, according to Semafor, which also said Hassett’s plans could change.

    The White House denied it on Friday, November 13 (Reuters, by Jasper Ward): “There is no factual basis for this reporting. Kevin Hassett is a key member of President Trump’s economic team and will continue working to advance President Trump’s economic policies,” a White House official said. That White House official’s denial was also given to CNBC the same day. He has not resigned.

    Hassett himself also denied the report on Friday, November 13, 2026: he told CNBC’s Eamon Javers by text message that the Semafor departure report is “not true” (CNBC, Nov. 13, 2026). David Zervos, one of the people Semafor named as a possible replacement, declined to comment when reached by phone after the Semafor story published (CNBC, Nov. 13, 2026). Hassett remains rumored only — denied by both the White House and Hassett himself — not a confirmed resignation.

    Sources: Semafor, November 13, 2026 (as reported by Reuters) · Reuters, November 13, 2026 · CNBC, Nov. 13, 2026

  • “Many more resignations” — a wider exodus

    Status: prediction, not reporting. Former federal prosecutor Bill Shipley predicted on X (quoted by the Washington Examiner, Nov. 8) “many many more resignations from all the Departments before the end of next week”; a current DOJ source told the Examiner they knew of no additional planned departures. Trump himself told CBS’s 60 Minutes (interview excerpted Fri Nov. 6, airing Sun Nov. 8) he expects “maybe a few changes” to his Cabinet and, asked if he expected to fire anyone, “I do, yeah. Little bit” — firings and expectations, not resignations.

  • Donald Trump himself

    Status: no resignation. A viral claim that Trump had promised to resign if Republicans lost the midterms was fact-checked in October 2026 as unsupported, originating with a satire/commentary image, with no authenticated presidential statement. It is noted here only because the claim circulated in this period.

Open questions — what could not be verified

Printed plainly, because an honest gap beats a filled-in guess:

  • Richard Walters’ effective date. Axios (Nov. 7) is the primary source and its full text was not directly retrievable; his entry rests on verbatim Axios quotations reproduced by Political Wire and WLT Report and an Axios summary in the Augerton Digest (Nov. 8). His resignation day — Friday, Nov. 6 — and his on-the-record statement to Axios are consistent across all three.
  • Effective dates for Dhillon and Eisenberg. Neither Blanche announcement, as reported, stated a final day or named successors for the Civil Rights Division or the National Security Division.
  • Completeness at the state and local level. Reporting in the window is dominated by the federal shake-up. Small-jurisdiction resignations (the Dubach, LA mayor is an example of what does surface) may exist that were not indexed by Nov. 9 or that carry no public Trump-alignment record. The honest statement is: none meeting both tests were found — not that none occurred anywhere.
