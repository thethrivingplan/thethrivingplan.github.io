Why? — What it is

A car blueprint that adds one more layer to the line that already made cars survivable: designed absorption. First the field takes the crash. Then the structure. The people don't.

How? — What it means

Every generation of car safety moved the crash away from the body — from the passengers' bones into bent metal. This design continues that exact move, one layer further out, past the metal entirely.

Act. — What it could mean for you

The design is copyleft. You may build it, test it, improve it, and publish your version — the same freedom has to travel with it. Start at the Build Order: rigs and sleds before roads.

LAWS / STANDING

Standing Laws of This Design

Two house laws govern every sheet in this family. This section states what they honestly mean for this car — no more, and no less.

The Three Modes — set down November 21, 2026, under copyleft (CC BY-SA 4.0) — rule every spec that distributes, spaces, proportions, or flows. Golden = spread, never align: each new element into the biggest gap, at the golden angle 137.5077641°. Platinum = align exactly: equal, on the line, 1:1 — the right mode where alignment is the function. Diamond = the weave: align locally, spread globally. The full treatment lives on the Super Suit sheet (System 9) and on Sheet № 0 (§14). Here is this car's own grading:

Golden

Golden — the absorption field (§02, §04)

A crash's energy is taken in across the whole shell and spread over the entire car, never concentrated at the point that struck; distribution is the field's whole job, and the coverage is the same spread on both sides of the windshield — inside and outside meet the same absorption. Where this car distributes, it runs Golden.

Platinum

Platinum — the safety cell and the crumple lines (§02, §04)

The cell protects only by holding its geometry exactly — a cage that must not deform is alignment as the whole function; the crumple zones answer the same way, folding on designed lines in a designed order, stretching the crash over time and distance. Where this car aligns, it runs Platinum.

Diamond

Diamond — the layered whole (§02, §04)

Each layer is exact at its own job — the cell rigid, the crumple folding on its lines — while the field spreads the crash over all of them at once. Local exactness woven under a global spread: the layers are the weave, and the whole design runs Diamond.

No mode

No stamp where it doesn't fit

The Build Order (§05) runs no mode — it is a sequence of gates, not a distribution, a spacing, or a flow; the Open Hows (§06) carry no labels either, because a part that isn't designed yet can't be graded. The modes' own law governs the modes: use it when it works — don't force it.

Adapt and overcome — the second standing law, and a top law: learn your lessons and grow. This design is built to take in new knowledge and get better — and this sheet's own first section is the proof of the law: §01 is a century of cars doing exactly that, rigid to crumple to cell to field, each generation the last one adapted. When better knowledge arrives — a sled number published, a build stage replicated, a field capacity measured — the design is revised to fit it in: fitted when verified better, never forced, and never a from-scratch rebuild of what already works. Today's sheet is the floor, not the ceiling.

01 / HISTORY

The Line So Far

This design did not start with us. It starts with a documented problem — and a documented answer that worked, generation after generation.

1908 – 1950s · DOCUMENTED

Rigid

The first cars — Model T era — were built rigid, with no designed absorption. In a crash, the car stopped and the people inside kept going. It was one of their biggest problems — and they were among the first machines of their kind, so of course they had problems. First versions always do.

→
1950s → · DOCUMENTED

Crumple zones

The fix that changed everything: parts of the car designed to fold. The front and rear crush in a controlled way, stretching the crash over time and distance — the metal takes the energy so the people don't.

→
1950s → · DOCUMENTED

The safety cell

Around the passengers, the opposite idea: a strong, rigid cell that refuses to deform, so the space people sit in stays a space. Crumple outside, cell inside. Each generation absorbed more of the crash for the people inside.

→
THIS BLUEPRINT · DESIGN

The field

The next step of the same line: absorption that starts before the metal. The field takes the crash's energy first; the crumple zones become the second layer, not the only one.

The pattern is the proof of direction: rigid → crumple → cell → field. Nobody designed the field into a Model T because the line had to be walked in order. We are simply printing the next step on the same line — and labeling it, honestly, as the step that is designed but not yet built.

03 / EVERYBODY

Both Sides of the Windshield

Every safety generation before this one protected the people inside the car. A struck pedestrian or cyclist got the bumper. This design refuses that split.

Design

Inside: the occupants

The field takes the crash's energy before the structure. The crumple zones fold as the second layer. The safety cell holds the people's space. Three layers between the crash and the body — where the Model T offered none.

Design

Outside: the people the car meets

The field wraps the whole car — so when a car strikes a pedestrian or a cyclist, what meets them is absorption, not impact. The field answers for the people outside the car too.

Everybody benefits, or it isn't the design. A safety system that only protects the buyer is half a design, and this blueprint does not ship halves.

Tap to open — Why this is the same invention, not a second one

A crumple zone only works in one direction: it protects the car's own occupants by sacrificing the car's own metal. A field has no “own.” It is a region of absorption around the vehicle, and anything entering that region — a wall, another car, a person on foot — meets absorption first.

That is why the umbrella over this whole shelf of blueprints is all transportation — walking included. The person walking is not outside the system. There is no outside the system.

04 / SYSTEMS

Systems — How the Layers Work as One

Design

The vehicle field

The absorption field from the suit's chip, scaled to the vehicle: a shell around the whole car that takes crash energy before the structure does. It is the first layer in every direction — front, rear, side, and toward the ground a person falls on. In the language of the Standing Laws above, the field runs Golden — a hit spread across the whole shell, never concentrated where it struck.

Kept from today

The structure stays a car

Crumple zones and the safety cell are not removed — they are demoted from “the only protection” to “the second and third layers.” If the field ever fails, the car underneath must still be a survivable car. See the fallback open how below. The cell's part of that runs Platinum: it protects by holding its exact geometry, and the crumple zones fold on their designed lines — alignment doing the work (Standing Laws, above).

Design

The worn field — the suit interplay

Occupants may also wear the personal field — the suit. Vehicle field + worn field are two layers of one system, not two products: the car protects everyone it meets; the suit protects its wearer in every vehicle — and on foot, where walking is the vehicle.

Design

One system, every way of moving

Car, bike, ship, walking — the field is personal and portable, and each vehicle blueprint on this shelf is the same idea at that vehicle's scale. Read them together: they are one design with several bodies.

05 / ACT

Build Order — Rigs and Sleds Before Roads

Nobody road-tests an unproven absorption claim with people inside. Each stage must prove its absorption numbers before the next one begins. A stage that misses its numbers stops the line — it does not get averaged away.

  1. Bench the field cell

    One field unit, instrumented, struck by a calibrated impactor. Measure energy absorbed, peak force passed through, and failure mode.

    GATE: PUBLISHED ABSORPTION NUMBERS, REPEATABLE

  2. Sled tests — structure alone, then field + structure

    Crash sleds with instrumented dummies: first the bare crumple-and-cell structure (today's baseline), then the same sled with the field live. The difference between the two runs is the field's measured contribution.

    GATE: FIELD RUN BEATS BASELINE ON EVERY DUMMY READING

  3. Pedestrian & cyclist rigs

    Instrumented pedestrian and cyclist forms struck at urban speeds. The outside of the car is tested as hard as the inside — both sides of the windshield, both sides of the ledger.

    GATE: ABSORPTION INSTEAD OF IMPACT, MEASURED

  4. Full-vehicle barrier tests, field on and field off

    Whole cars into barriers at rising speeds, including a field-disabled run to prove the fallback: with no field at all, the car must still perform like a good modern car.

    GATE: FALLBACK PROVEN — THE CAR IS STILL A CAR

  5. Two-field tests

    Two field-equipped vehicles, closing speeds, offset and head-on. What two fields do when they meet is an open how — this stage is where it stops being one.

    GATE: MULTI-VEHICLE BEHAVIOR MEASURED, NOT ASSUMED

  6. Closed-course, then roads — last

    Only after every number above is published and replicated by an independent builder (copyleft: anyone may replicate) does a person ride in one on a public road.

    GATE: INDEPENDENT REPLICATION
06 / HONEST LABELS

The Open Hows Shelf

Printed at full size, in the open. Each of these is an unknown — and an unknown is not a no. It is work waiting for a builder.

Open How · Unknown

Field capacity at highway energies

Crash energy grows with the square of speed (try the slider above). How much energy a field can absorb, at what rate, and how that scales from the suit's chip to a 1,500 kg car at highway speed is not yet engineered. This is the biggest number the design owes.

Open How · Unknown

Two fields meeting

In a multi-vehicle crash, two absorption fields arrive at the same place at the same time. Do they add, interfere, or hand energy back and forth? Designed answer: none yet. Build Order stage 5 exists to find out.

Open How · Design rule set

Field failure fallback

The rule is already decided even though the engineering isn't: the car must still be a car. Crumple zones and the safety cell stay, at full modern standard, so a dead field means an ordinary survivable crash — never a rigid Model T with a broken promise on top.

Open How · Answered in principle

Cost — and who can build it

What it costs is unknown until stages 1–2 price the field cell. Who may build it is not unknown: anyone. The design is copyleft under CERN-OHL-S — no licence fee, no permission, no gatekeeper. Cost is an engineering question; permission is already answered.
