The Ruler's Test — Wesley W. Simina, President of the Federated States of Micronesia

Subject: Wesley W. Simina — President of the Federated States of Micronesia (FSM) since 11 May 2023. The seat graded is the presidency, and the reason is stated plainly: in the FSM the President is both head of state and head of government, heads the Cabinet, and is in charge of the administration and operations of the National Government (Congress of the FSM / presidency records; CIA World Factbook, final edition January 2026). The premise was verified first. Simina still holds the seat in November 2026: the FSM Government recorded him celebrating Presidents' Day as President on 23 November 2026, dedicating the new FSM Embassy in Washington, D.C. on 16–17 November 2026, delivering the FSM national statement at COP31 in Antalya on 13 November 2026, and meeting FSM citizens in Phoenix, Arizona on 18 November 2026 (FSM Government, gov.fm, November 2026). No change of hands has occurred. Simina, born 10 September 1961, is the FSM's tenth President. He was Governor of Chuuk State from 2005 to 2011, a member of Congress from 2011, and Speaker of Congress from 11 May 2015 to 11 May 2023. The mandate is indirect, and that fact shapes the whole file: the FSM has no political parties, all candidates run as independents, and the President and Vice President are elected by Congress from among its four at-large senators — one per state — for a four-year term, eligible for one re-election (FSM Constitution; Freedom House, Freedom in the World 2024; CRS, The Freely Associated States and Issues for Congress, 10 December 2024). Simina had been re-elected as Chuuk's at-large senator on 7 March 2023 with 12,756 votes (63.27%), and Congress elected him President by acclamation on 11 May 2023, succeeding David W. Panuelo, who had lost his own congressional seat in March; Vice President Aren B. Palik, Kosrae's at-large senator, was re-elected alongside him (2023 general election records; Kosrae State Legislature resolution, June 2023). His current mandate expires on 11 May 2027 and he is eligible for re-election — the 2027 succession horizon: a general election on 2 March 2027 will elect all fourteen congressional seats, including the four at-large seats from which the next Congress will choose the next President (IPU / Congress records; election calendar 2027). He is therefore graded on about three and a half years in office, with roughly six months of the term still to run — a substantial, not a completed, tenure. The state-level governors' role is noted where it bears on the tests: each of the four states — Chuuk, Kosrae, Pohnpei and Yap — has its own constitution, elected governor and legislature and considerable autonomy, and in a federation with a deliberately weak centre the governors are first movers on much domestic ground, notably disaster proclamations, while the President's declaration, funds and vessels follow (Freedom House, 2024; Chuuk and national Sinlaku declarations, April 2026). Graded: November 2026, on the documented public record. Method: The ethic: "How you play the game matters. Play by the rules. Stay true to your word. And if another option becomes available, at least consider it." Each test is graded only on the evidence listed under it. Both shelves are shown — the strongest evidence that he passes, then the strongest evidence that he fails. Allegations are labeled ALLEGATION and never stated as verdicts. Contested items are named as contested, with who disputes them. Conflicting counts are printed as separate sourced figures, never blended. Seat-holders and public figures are named; people below the public eye appear by role only, unless the documented record itself establishes their wrongdoing. The mandate rule applies: governing on the commitments a ruler was seated on is not inherently a failure and is never graded as one — the Test grades the holding, never the platform; marks are lost only for how it is done. Because Simina was seated by Congress and not on a party platform, his graded commitments are the ones he stated himself: his oath and inaugural address, his government's published programs, and his dated public pledges. Grades: HOLDS / FAILS / MIXED / NOT ENOUGH EVIDENCE. This record is correctable: new evidence changes grades, in the open.

Test 1 — GIVES BY ONE LAW

Same rules for allies and opponents, insiders and outsiders — no favorites at the source.

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • The central anti-corruption institution of his tenure was built to apply to everyone, including him — and he built it. Voters approved all nine constitutional amendments in the referendum of 4 July 2023, including establishing the Office of the Independent Prosecutor (OIP), an independent national agency whose prosecutor the President appoints for a six-year term subject to confirmation by two-thirds of Congress; the Independent Prosecutor amendment carried every state, from 80.51% in Pohnpei to 86.5% in Kosrae (2023 constitutional referendum, official results, September 2023). Simina ratified and proclaimed all nine amendments on 4 October 2023 (Freedom House, 2024; referendum records). He then nominated Lucas M. Cupps — a lawyer from Kansas with no FSM family or factional network, a point commentators noted approvingly — as the first Independent Prosecutor; after Congress authorized resubmission of the nomination (Congressional Resolution No. 24-23, 28 May 2025), Congress confirmed Cupps on 19 September 2025 under Congressional Resolution No. 24-37 (FSM Congress, Standing Committee Report No. 24-03, May 2025; FSM Congress confirmation, September 2025). A president who creates, ratifies and staffs an office empowered to investigate the presidency itself has, at the source, written one law that reaches his own desk.
  • The same ratification carried the insider-closing amendments with the outsider-opening ones, without cherry-picking: dual citizenship (approved in all four states, 78.63% in Yap to 87.47% in Pohnpei), a right to a healthy environment, revenue-sharing of fishing and seabed resources with the states, and a change to the presidential-veto override — all nine proclaimed together (referendum results, 2023).
  • The audit referee published against the system, in the open, on his watch. The Office of the National Public Auditor's Report No. 2026-12 (30 September 2026) examined $4.67 million of Congress-funded public projects in Kosrae State from October 2022 to March 2025 and found $126,234.59 in equipment — boats, outboard motors, phones, a laptop, solar streetlights, trucks and mowers — that officials could not verify, with some named recipients denying receipt; the auditors expressly clarified this was not a theft finding, and criticized the Kosrae Congressional Delegation Office for handling procurement and recipient selection in violation of a 2001 FSM Supreme Court ruling that Congress may not implement projects it funds (ONPA Report No. 2026-12, reported in The Kaselehlie Press, 19 October 2026). The Kosrae Governor's office, responding, agreed oversight had failed. The report was published, reported in the national press, and not suppressed.
  • He vetoed an insiders' benefit on stated equity grounds. On 31 July / 6 August 2026 he vetoed Congressional Act No. 24-103, which would have created a monthly housing stipend for exempt employees of the National Government while exempting them from existing housing regulations, citing fiscal impact, implementation and equity concerns, and returned it to the Twenty-Fourth Congress under Article IX, Section 22 of the Constitution (FSM Government, 6 August 2026). The same signing round approved $3.2 million in supplemental appropriations and project reallocations across the states (FSM Government, 7 August 2026).
  • The background indicators describe a system in which the law is generally the same law: the United States Department of State's 2024 Human Rights Report found no credible reports of significant human rights abuses in the FSM and no significant change in the human rights situation (US Department of State, Country Reports on Human Rights Practices for 2024). Transparency International's U4 Helpdesk overview (January 2026) records the FSM's Worldwide Governance Indicators control-of-corruption percentile improving across its series to 62.19 (2024 data) and rule of law to 73.78 (2024 data), while noting the FSM is not covered by the Corruption Perceptions Index — figures recorded here as the indicators state them, not as a verdict on any individual (U4 Helpdesk Answer 2026:2, Transparency International / Chr. Michelsen Institute).

Evidence against:

  • The OIP funding dispute is, on one side's account, a second law in operation: the referee funded on paper and starved in practice when its first case was the President himself. On 8 January 2026 Independent Prosecutor Lucas M. Cupps announced a formal criminal investigation into President Simina and Secretary of Finance and Administration Rose N. Nakanaga over the ALLEGATION that congressionally appropriated funds for the OIP had been unlawfully withheld — Congress had appropriated $250,000 to establish and operate the office — and Cupps argued the failure to release the money had effectively crippled the institution before it could function, leaving him working remotely from Kansas without staff, facilities or operational support (The Kaselehlie Press, 14 January 2026; Chikin Melele reporting, 2026; Cupps letter published in the Marianas Variety, December 2025). No criminal charges had been publicly announced as of grading, and the guilt or innocence of any person in this file is an ALLEGATION until a court rules.
  • The executive's counter-account is also on the record, and it is contested, not settled. In its public statement of 16 January 2026 the Office of the President stated that the $250,000 had been fully allotted, making it legally available; that Cupps had not taken his oath of office in the FSM before an FSM official; that he had demanded the full amount be transferred directly into one of his two personal bank accounts while refusing standard supporting documentation, which the statement said would violate the Financial Management Act and Regulations and Titles 11, 52 and 55 of the FSM Code, which the OIP's own enabling legislation requires it to follow; and that the executive had repeatedly provided the forms and offered assistance, which Cupps had consistently rejected (Office of the President, Public Statement, 16 January 2026, gov.fm). Cupps disputes the administration's characterization, holding that he signed his written oath and began his six-year term in December 2025 and that the executive has not complied with the constitutional obligations the OIP amendment placed on it (Cupps, December 2025). Both disputants are named here because the finding is contested: Cupps on one side, President Simina and Secretary Nakanaga on the other. No court had resolved the dispute in the record assembled here; a November 2026 summary of the file records the matter as potentially headed to the FSM Supreme Court (MSN Sato Nation News summary, 18 November 2026, citing gov.fm, FSM Congress and Chikin Melele).
  • The no-good-choice mechanics apply to the disbursement choice itself, and are recorded here rather than scored as a demerit. The options that actually existed in January 2026 were: (1) transfer $250,000 of public money to a personal bank account without the standard documentation — a course the executive states, citing named statutes, would itself break the financial-management law that binds every branch; (2) refuse any release until the prosecutor used the ordinary allotment process — the course taken, whose real harm is documented above: the nation's first independent prosecutor working from another country with no staff; or (3) seek an early judicial ruling on the OIP's disbursement terms — no documented step of that kind by either side appears in the record assembled here. A ruler put to a choice between breaking the custody rules for public money and leaving a new referee unfunded-in-fact faces no clean door; the choice of door (2) is therefore not held against him under this clause. What remains gradable, and is graded in Tests 2 and 5, is the situation's origin and the honesty and speed of the follow-through: the OIP was his own administration's project, its funding mechanics were foreseeable when the $250,000 was appropriated at his submission, and as of November 2026 — fourteen months after confirmation — the office was still not documented as operational.
  • The Kosrae audit cuts the other way at the system's edge: $126,234.59 unverifiable out of $4.67 million of public-project spending, and a congressional delegation office performing implementation functions a Supreme Court ruling reserves elsewhere, are findings about Congress and state administration rather than about the President personally — recorded on this shelf because Test 1 grades the source's law as lived, and because no documented executive follow-up (referral, prosecution, or corrective directive) to Report No. 2026-12 appears in the record assembled here (ONPA, September 2026; Kaselehlie Press, October 2026). The earlier national record carries the same seam: in March 2023, at the end of the previous administration, the FSM Government filed criminal charges against a Department of Finance & Administration employee by name in a press release alleging conflict of interest, theft, money laundering and unauthorized possession or removal of property (FSM Government, 24 March 2023) — an ALLEGATION-level filing, recorded here only to show that finance-ministry integrity cases predate Simina and were handled through the courts.

Why this grade: In plain words: Simina wrote the strongest one-law instrument in FSM history — a prosecutor who can investigate the President — ratified it, nominated an outsider to hold it, and vetoed a stipend for national-government insiders on equity grounds; and then, when that prosecutor's first investigation was into Simina himself, the money did not move in any usable form, on a procedural justification that is genuinely documented but whose practical effect was to leave the referee unable to work. One law on paper, fully; one law in operation, contested and unfinished. That is MIXED, and the grade would move on a court ruling or on the office actually opening.

Helped / Hurt ledger — Test 1:

  • Helped: FSM citizens in all four states, who gained a constitutional independent prosecutor, dual citizenship, a healthy-environment right and state revenue-sharing in a single ratification (2023 referendum); future defendants of the public purse in every faction, since the OIP's mandate is faction-blind; national-government employees outside the exempt class, who were not placed under a stipend law their President vetoed as inequitable.
  • Hurt: The Office of the Independent Prosecutor as an institution — staff never hired, office never documented as opened — and, through it, any complainant whose case waits on it; Independent Prosecutor Lucas M. Cupps, left to work from Kansas; President Simina and Secretary Rose N. Nakanaga themselves, who carry a publicly announced criminal investigation — an ALLEGATION, uncharged and unresolved as of grading — arising from the funding file; Kosrae project recipients who, on the audit, never received boats, engines, phones and lights recorded as delivered ($126,234.59 unverified, ONPA 2026-12).

Test 2 — HOLDS WITHOUT GRIPPING

Do the courts, the electoral commission, the media, the prosecution, and the central bank keep their autonomy — or is independence punished and control centralized?

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • The referees' independence is the documented norm, not the exception. Freedom House rates the FSM Free, 92 of 100 in Freedom in the World 2026 (Political Rights 37/40, Civil Liberties 55/60), and states that the judiciary is independent and civil liberties are generally respected (Freedom House, country profile, 2026). The US Department of State's 2024 report states that the law provides for freedom of expression including for the press, that the government generally respected it, and that "an independent media, an effective judiciary, and a functioning democratic political system combined to promote freedom of expression" (US Department of State, 2024 Human Rights Report).
  • The press printed the worst headline of his presidency and kept printing. The Kaselehlie Press, the national bi-weekly, carried "Independent Prosecutor opens criminal investigation into FSM President, Finance Secretary" on its front page of 14 January 2026, had earlier carried the Congress's confirmation of that prosecutor (22 October 2025) and the ONPA audit findings against congressional project handling (19 October 2026) — with no documented retaliation, ban, prosecution or closure against the paper in the record assembled here (Kaselehlie Press, 2025–2026).
  • Congress checks him and he works through it, constitutionally. He calls special sessions and submits his priorities to them (3rd Special Session, convened 1 July 2026 at his call; FSM Government, 2 July 2026); he signs most of what Congress passes, including the priority legislation of August and November 2026 (FSM Government, 7 August 2026 and 4–5 November 2026); and where he refuses, he uses the veto the Constitution gives him, returning Acts No. 24-119 and No. 24-103 with written messages explaining constitutional, legal, fiscal and policy reasons, for Congress's further consideration under Article IX, Section 22 (FSM Government, 6 August 2026). His veto of Act No. 24-119 is worth stating precisely: the act would have amended Title 41 on emergency powers and presidential authority, including a $200,000 limit on emergency funding; he objected that it could limit rapid executive response in major emergencies and raised separation-of-powers concerns (veto messages summarized, August 2026). Using a veto openly, with reasons, against a bill about his own powers is the constitutional channel, not a grip on the legislature — but the substance, a President resisting a statutory cap on his emergency spending, is recorded here for Test 2's shelf as well as his.
  • He used the appointment power to arm a referee against himself, and Congress's confirmation machinery ran at its own pace, not his: the Cupps nomination required a resubmission authorization (C.R. No. 24-23, May 2025) before confirmation on 19 September 2025 — the legislature slowing and conditioning the executive's own nominee, in the open (FSM Congress, 2025).
  • Elections ran without him on the ballot and without documented interference: the 4 March 2025 general election filled the ten two-year congressional seats peacefully, mainly re-electing incumbents, at a turnout of 28.67% (International IDEA, 2025; IFES Election Guide, 2025). The Va-PoReg country report (2026) likewise classifies the FSM as a liberal democracy with an independent judiciary and generally upheld civil liberties, while noting information on the microstate is scarce (Va-PoReg Country Report Micronesia, Hannah Arendt Institute, 2026).

Evidence against:

  • The one referee that matters most to this test is the one that cannot work. Whatever the legal merits (Test 1), the operational fact by November 2026 is that the constitutionally independent prosecutor — confirmed fourteen months earlier, funded by a $250,000 appropriation the President himself submitted — had no documented office, staff or disbursement, and its holder was conducting his announced investigation of the President from Kansas (Chikin Melele, 2026; Kaselehlie Press, January 2026). A prosecution service that exists in the Constitution and the newspapers but not in an office is independence punished by inanition if the ALLEGATION is proven, and independence stalled by procedure even on the executive's own account. The executive's stated reason — that the prosecutor had not taken his oath in the FSM and demanded payment to a personal account — is itself contested by Cupps, who states he signed his written oath in December 2025 (Cupps, December 2025; Office of the President, 16 January 2026). Contested, named, unresolved.
  • Judicial and legal capacity is thin enough that autonomy is fragile even where it is respected: the UN country team's compilation for the January 2026 Universal Periodic Review noted a chronic shortage of trained lawyers and public defenders, significant judicial delays, and that no concrete steps had been taken to establish an independent national human rights institution under the Paris Principles; the FSM had ratified no additional core human rights treaty since the previous UPR cycle (OHCHR compilation, A/HRC/WG.6/51/FSM/2, 6 November 2025). These are capacity findings about the state, not acts of gripping by the President — recorded because a referee too thin to act is a Test 2 fact whoever caused it.
  • One episode at the federation's edge shows a referee's decision defied by the legislature, not by Simina, and is recorded with its actor named correctly: in September–October 2023 Congress's Credentials Committee recommended against seating, and Congress declined to seat, Kosrae's duly elected at-large senator-elect Yoslyn G. Sigrah — who had won the July 2023 special election with 697 votes — citing, among other things, an ALLEGATION of biting a police officer, a 2016 misdemeanor charge, and law-practice suspensions, one of which had been successfully appealed; Speaker Esmond B. Moses declared the Kosrae seat vacant and a new special election was called, while Sigrah sued Congress in court and Kosrae's sitting senator raised secession in protest (Kaselehlie Press, 7–9 October 2023; The Fourth Branch interview with Yoslyn Sigrah, 9 October 2023). Simina is not documented as a party to that decision — Congress judges its own members under Article IX — and by the 24th Congress Sigrah sat as a member of its credentials process (Credentials Report, 24th Congress, May 2025). The episode belongs on this shelf as the federation's sharpest recent case of an elected referee-choice overridden, with the presidency's non-involvement stated as plainly as the act.

Why this grade: In plain words: courts, press, elections and Congress all visibly act on their own — the press investigated him in print, Congress conditioned his nominee and received his vetoes back with reasons — and no act of retaliation against any referee is documented. Against that stands a single, heavy fact: the one new referee built to prosecute presidents has never been able to open its doors, in a dispute where the President is an interested party. One stalled referee among many free ones is MIXED, not a grip — and not a hold, either.

Helped / Hurt ledger — Test 2:

  • Helped: The national press and public, who received the investigation, the audit and the vetoes in print and in the open; Congress, whose confirmation, special-session and veto-return processes all ran as the Constitution writes them; voters, whose March 2025 election was administered and certified without documented executive interference.
  • Hurt: The Office of the Independent Prosecutor and anyone awaiting its work (see Test 1); Kosrae's voters of July 2023, whose chosen at-large senator was not seated by Congress — an act of Congress, recorded here with the presidency's non-involvement stated; defendants and litigants across the FSM who face the documented shortage of lawyers, public defenders and judicial capacity (OHCHR compilation, 2025).

Test 3 — ANSWERS THE WOBBLE

When courts, voters, parliament, the street, foreign partners, or events push back, does he adjust openly — or deny and double down?

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • The largest wobble of the tenure was meteorological, twice, and both times the adjustment was fast, open and federal in the right order. In the El Niño drought, after state emergency declarations in Pohnpei, Yap and Chuuk, Simina declared a national emergency on 12 March 2024 and announced a $1.2 million allocation to the Disaster Relief Fund, with three ships deployed and over 21,000 people estimated in urgent need of water and food assistance, especially in outer-island municipalities such as Lekinioch, Piherarh and Onoun where saltwater had intruded into water systems (UNICEF Pacific, Humanitarian Situation Report No. 1, March 2024). In April 2026 Super Typhoon Sinlaku stalled north of Weno for nearly four days (9–12 April), knocking out 60–70% of Chuuk's power and communications; Chuuk's Governor Alexander R. Narruhn proclaimed a state emergency on 12 April, and Simina convened his Cabinet and declared a national State of Emergency the next day, 13 April, activating the National Disaster Committee, waiving procurement rules, allocating up to $500,000 initially from the Disaster Relief Fund and deploying patrol vessels and the FSM vessel Navigator / Micronesian Navigator with relief supplies (Chuuk Disaster and Emergency Operations Center, Situation Report No. 9, 8 May 2026; Presidential Emergency Declaration, 13 April 2026, gov.fm). The FSM then ran a joint damage assessment with the United States that identified approximately $96 million in damages and recovery needs — about 18% of national GDP — which formed the basis for a US commitment to fund the full assessed amount in addition to the Compact's Disaster Assistance Emergency Fund (FSM Government, Arizona outreach report, 22 November 2026; Kaselehlie Press COP31 report, November 2026).
  • On the climate file he changed his argument in public when the facts changed — the other-option test in its exact form. His 2023 UN General Assembly address demanded emissions halved by 2030, peaking by 2025, net zero by 2050, and a Montreal-Protocol-model treaty on methane (UNGA, 19 September 2023). By 2025–2026, with 1.5°C overshoot underway, he did not deny the miss or double down on the old framing: at the UN Climate Summit on 25 September 2025 he announced a new Nationally Determined Contribution and Methane Roadmap to be launched at COP30, renewed the call for a Fossil Fuel Non-Proliferation Treaty, and then launched NDC 3.0 and the FSM's first Methane Reduction Roadmap at COP30 in Belém on 15 November 2025, aligned to the Global Methane Pledge goal of at least 30% below 2020 levels by 2030 (FSM Government, 25 September 2025; FSM Government, 15 November 2025). At COP31 on 13 November 2026 the argument had adjusted again, openly: "Every fraction of a degree counts" — in an era of overshoot, rapid methane and super-pollutant cuts to slow the rate of warming and buy adaptation time, alongside the unmoved 1.5°C threshold (FSM Government, COP31 statement, 13 November 2026; Kaselehlie Press, November 2026). If another option becomes available — here, fast methane action as the lever that can still move near-term warming — the record shows him considering it, adopting it, and saying why, in public.
  • With Congress, pushback produced stated reasons rather than silence: both August 2026 vetoes were returned with messages explaining the constitutional, legal, fiscal and policy considerations, and in the same weeks he signed the Congress's supplemental and project legislation (FSM Government, 6–7 August 2026). On the Yap defence project — a planned expansion of US defence-related seaport and airport works in Yap State under the Compact — he wrote to Yap's Governor acknowledging a "possible setback" on the project rather than announcing over it (FSM history timeline, 2026, summarizing the presidential letter; US Department of the Interior testimony on COFA implementation, 14 January 2026, recording planned expanded seaport and airport projects in Yap State).
  • With foreign partners he held two relationships at once under pressure rather than choosing a camp when pushed: he made a state visit to China on 5–12 April 2024, holding talks with President Xi Jinping on 9 April 2024 and witnessing cooperation documents on Belt and Road, green development, agriculture, health care and infrastructure, with a joint statement reaffirming the one-China principle (Ministry of Foreign Affairs of China, 9 April 2024) — weeks after the United States enacted the renewed Compact on 9 March 2024 (see Test 5), and he continued to work the US relationship through the Compact's JEMCO process, the August 2025 JEMCO budget approvals, and the Sinlaku recovery commitment (US Department of the Interior, 2026).

Evidence against:

  • The OIP wobble was answered with a statement, not with an adjustment that ended it. When the prosecutor he had appointed announced a criminal investigation into him on 8 January 2026, the presidency replied on 16 January 2026 with a detailed public defence — lawful, good faith, funds allotted, forms provided — and a stated willingness to cooperate with any lawful process (Office of the President, 16 January 2026). Explanation is more than denial. But the documented position ten months later was unchanged: no disbursement mechanism agreed, no office opened, no court ruling sought on the record assembled here, and the prosecutor still abroad (November 2026 summaries of the file). On the one pushback that touched his own liability, the wobble was answered in words and left in place in fact.
  • The Chuuk State elections of 2025 mark the federation's roughest recent wobble, and the national response is thin in the record. The UN country team reported that the 2025 elections in Chuuk State were marked by conflicting rulings from electoral commissions and courts, concerns over ballot handling and post-election unrest, including warrantless arrests and political violence, resulting in a declaration of a state of emergency (OHCHR compilation, A/HRC/WG.6/51/FSM/2, November 2025). Chuuk's elections are run by Chuuk's institutions and its governor holds the first responsibility — the state-level role stated plainly — but no documented national-level step by Simina (mediation, inquiry, or public accounting) in that file appears in the record assembled here; it is recorded as an absence, not an act.
  • Adjustment abroad has outrun adjustment at home on his signature file: the climate argument was revised three times in three years (above), while the domestic numbers he himself announced at COP31 — universal electricity access and 70% renewable electricity generation by 2030, 80% by 2035 — are stated by him as targets that "depend on meaningful international support" (COP31 statement, 13 November 2026). That dependence is honestly stated; it also means the wobble of delivery sits with partners, and the domestic record by grading shows plans launched (NDC 3.0, the Methane Roadmap, the National Agriculture Investment Plan signed 20 July 2026) rather than measured movement (FSM Government, July 2026).

Why this grade: In plain words: when nature pushed — drought in 2024, a Category 5 typhoon stalling over Chuuk in 2026 — he declared, funded, deployed and assessed in the open, within a day of the state governors' own declarations, and when the climate facts moved he rebuilt his public argument around the new lever and said so. When the pushback was a prosecutor investigating him, the answer was a well-argued statement and then ten months of standstill. Fast and open on events; static on the file that touches himself. MIXED.

Helped / Hurt ledger — Test 3:

  • Helped: The more than 34,000 people affected in Chuuk and the municipalities of Yap hit by Sinlaku (Chuuk DEOC / UN reporting, May 2026), who received a national emergency declaration, vessels, waived procurement and a fully funded $96 million joint damage assessment; the over 21,000 people in urgent need in the 2024 drought, including the outer-island municipalities named above (UNICEF, March 2024); Yap State, whose governor received a written acknowledgment of a defence-project setback rather than a surprise.
  • Hurt: The nine people killed in the FSM by Sinlaku — printed as the FSM figure of 9 deaths and 1 missing in the consolidated casualty table (Wikipedia compilation of sourced national figures, November 2026), against the separate earlier figures in the FSM history timeline (1, 4, 5 or 7 in successive preliminary reports) and the Kaselehlie Press COP31 report's figure of nine; the figures are printed separately here, never blended, and the preliminary counts are recorded as preliminary. Also hurt: the roughly 4,200 displaced recorded in the FSM history timeline (a separate sourced figure from the 100 displaced in the IFRC-based summary — again printed separately, not blended); and the OIP's prospective complainants, whose referee's standstill (Tests 1–2) is also a Test 3 non-adjustment.

Test 4 — COUNTS EVERYONE

Does the accounting include the four states and their outer islands, the diaspora, women, people with disabilities, and the poor — or are some counted out?

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • Unity across the four states was the stated first priority of the presidency, in terms, from the first day: "Our unity is our strength and our prosperity," he said in his inaugural address of 26 July 2023, committing himself in his oath "to promote and support the unity of this country" and naming the work as tying together Pohnpei, Kosrae, Chuuk and Yap, against the background of Chuuk — the most populous and poorest state, and his own — having repeatedly considered secession, with its independence referendum postponed in 2015, 2019, 2020 and 2022 and not held (FSM Government, Joint Presidential Inauguration, 26 July 2023; PINA, November 2023; Chuuk Political Status Commission record). No secession referendum has been held or revived on his watch, and the federation he inherited intact remains intact.
  • The smallest states and the farthest islands appear in his own itinerary, not only in appropriations. He and Vice President Palik toured the Mortlock Islands of Chuuk State — among the FSM's most remote outer islands — from 28 October to 2 November 2024, attending municipal celebrations and community events island by island (Office of the President reporting, November 2024). His disaster declarations covered outer islands by name (Test 3). The presidency's November 2026 calendar alone runs from Kitti, Pohnpei (Memorial Day commemoration, 27 July 2026) to the diaspora (below).
  • The diaspora is counted as part of the nation, in person and in law. The dual-citizenship amendment he ratified in October 2023 restored and protected FSM citizenship for those with an FSM parent who hold another citizenship — a change whose beneficiaries are overwhelmingly Micronesians abroad (2023 referendum). On 18 November 2026 he held a town hall in Phoenix, Arizona, telling FSM citizens there that "regardless of where Micronesians reside, they remain an integral part of the nation," with his delegation reporting on the Embassy's work on commercial driver's licence (CDL) problems affecting Micronesian citizens in the United States and on Sinlaku recovery (FSM Government, 22 November 2026). Compact citizens' ability to live, work and study in the United States — and the restoration of federal benefits for FAS citizens under the 2024 Compact legislation — is the legal frame that makes that diaspora possible (Compact of Free Association Amendments Act of 2024, P.L. 118-42; AJIL Contemporary Practice, 2024).
  • Women moved from zero to counted during the arc of his speakership and presidency, and his government says so in its own reporting: the FSM elected no woman to Congress until a November 2021 by-election seated Perpetua S. Konman; two more women were elected in March 2023 and re-elected in 2025, giving the 14-member Congress three women (21.4%) with no quota (Congress records; International IDEA, 2025; IFES, 2025).
