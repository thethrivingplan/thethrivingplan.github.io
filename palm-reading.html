Palmistry (old name: chiromancy, from the Greek for “hand” and “divination”) has been practiced for thousands of years, from India to China to Greece. This page keeps it where it belongs: the light side. Curiosity and a good story — never a verdict.

A reading, built from your own hand

Look at your dominant hand — the one you write with — under a good light. Answer four quick questions about what you actually see, and the page will weave the traditional takes into one light-hearted reading.

1 · Your heart line ends…

Find the topmost line across your palm and follow it toward your fingers.

2 · Your head line…

The middle line, the one that starts at the edge of your palm by the thumb.

3 · Your life line sweeps…

The big arc around the ball of your thumb. (Its length was never a countdown — see the myths below.)

4 · Your hand shape is closest to…

Compare your palm's shape to your fingers' length. Not sure? The four shapes are pictured just below.

Four hand shapes

Before the lines, readers look at the hand itself. Palm shape plus finger length sorts most hands into four families, named for the elements.

Earth

Square palm · short fingers

The steady hands. Practical, patient, and happiest with something real to show for the day — a fixed engine, a finished fence, a fed crowd.

“Built to last, and usually the one doing the building.”

Air

Square palm · long fingers

The curious hands. Quick minds, quicker questions — readers of fine print, collectors of ideas, and the friend who explains the thing at the table.

“Asks why, then asks why again, happily.”

Fire

Long palm · short fingers

The sparking hands. Energy first, plan second — warm, bold, restless, and usually the one who gets the whole room moving.

“Already started. Details en route.”

Water

Long palm · long fingers

The feeling hands. Intuitive and imaginative, they read a room the way the rest of us read a menu — instantly, and down to the small print.

“Knows the mood before anyone says a word.”

The mounts: hills named for planets

The soft pads of the palm are its landscape. Readers judge each mount by how full and firm it feels — a plump mount is said to turn that planet's volume up; a flat one, down. Toggle “Show the mounts” above to find them on the hand.

♃

Jupiter

Under the index finger

Ambition, confidence, and the urge to lead. A full Jupiter mount is the hand of someone who volunteers to run the thing — and usually can.

♄

Saturn

Under the middle finger

Patience, depth, and follow-through. Saturn's people are the long-game players — quiet about it, and still standing at the end.

☉

Apollo

Under the ring finger

Creativity, warmth, and a feel for the spotlight. Also called the Sun mount — fittingly, its people tend to light up whatever they're part of.

☿

Mercury

Under the little finger

Wit, words, and quick dealing. A strong Mercury mount belongs to the talker, the trader, the one who can explain anything to anyone.

♀

Venus

The ball of the thumb

Love, warmth, music, and appetite for life. The biggest mount on the hand — a generous one is said to mark a generous heart.

☽

The Moon

Lower palm, little-finger side

Imagination, dreams, and wanderlust. Moon people daydream in high definition and are forever half-planning a trip somewhere.

Old sayings, gently corrected

Even the fun version deserves the true version. Four things people repeat about palms — and what actually holds up.

The old saying“A short life line means a short life.”
The gentle truthEven traditional readers don't claim that — the life line is read for energy, zest, and big chapters of change. Its length is not a countdown, and no honest reader treats it as one.
The old saying“Your lines are carved in stone for life.”
The gentle truthPalm lines genuinely change — slowly, over years, as hands and lives do. Old readers took that as the point: the hand shows tendencies, not a fixed script.
The old saying“Only one hand matters.”
The gentle truthTradition reads both. The dominant hand is “what you've made of it”; the other is “what you started with.” Where the two differ is where readers say the interesting story lives.
The old saying“A reader can tell you exactly what will happen.”
The gentle truthNo line names a date, a person, or an event. What a reading can honestly offer is a story about how someone loves, thinks, and charges at life — told off the map of their own hand.

Read a friend's palm in sixty seconds

The whole art, pocket-sized. It works at kitchen tables, campfires, and anywhere else people hold out a hand and grin.

1

Start with the shape

Square or long palm, short or long fingers — name their element (Earth, Air, Fire, Water) and give them the flattering one-liner from the shapes above. Instant smile, every time.

2

Walk the three great lines

Heart, head, life — in that order, top to bottom. Pick one true detail per line (“your heart line curves up — you lead with feeling”) and let them react. Their reaction is half the reading.

3

Land on a strength

Finish with the fullest mount — Venus for warmth, Apollo for sparkle, Jupiter for leadership — and one kind, specific compliment. Never doom, never dates, never warnings. Light side only.
