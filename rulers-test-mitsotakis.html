The Ruler's Test — Kyriakos Mitsotakis, Prime Minister of Greece

Subject: Kyriakos Mitsotakis — Prime Minister of Greece since 8 July 2019; leader of New Democracy. Won the July 2019 election outright and was re-elected on 25 June 2023 with 40.6% of the vote and 158 of 300 seats. His second term runs to 2027. Graded: November 2026, on the documented public record. Method: Each test is graded only on the evidence listed under it. Both shelves are shown — the strongest evidence that he passes, then the strongest evidence that he fails. Allegations are labeled ALLEGATION and never stated as verdicts. Contested items are named as contested, with who disputes them. Seat-holders and public figures are named; people below the public eye appear by role only, unless the documented record itself establishes their wrongdoing. Grades: HOLDS / FAILS / MIXED / NOT ENOUGH EVIDENCE. This record is correctable: new evidence changes grades, in the open.

Test 1 — GIVES BY ONE LAW

Same rules for allies and opponents, insiders and outsiders — no favorites at the source.

GRADE: FAILS

Evidence for:

  • The OPEKEPE farm-subsidy scandal was not buried when it reached his own cabinet. After the European Public Prosecutor's Office (EPPO) raided the agency in May 2025 and sent a 3,000-page file to parliament, five senior figures resigned in June 2025 — Migration Minister Makis Voridis, Deputy Foreign Minister Tasos Chatzivasileiou, Deputy Rural Development Minister Dionysis Stamenitis, Deputy Digital Governance Minister Christos Boukoros, and Secretary General Giorgos Stratakos. His government abolished OPEKEPE outright and transferred its functions to the tax authority (EPPO statements and Greek government announcements, May–June 2025). The fraud itself is ALLEGATION as to individuals; the resignations and the agency's abolition are documented fact. When EPPO in March 2026 asked parliament to lift the immunity of eleven MPs, three more ministers (Agriculture Minister Konstantinos Tsiaras, Civil Protection Minister Ioannis Kefalogiannis, Deputy Health Minister Dimitris Vartzopoulos) resigned on 3 April 2026.
  • The EPPO was permitted to prosecute the non-political layer at scale: in 2024 and early 2025 it charged some 100 suspects in the subsidy fraud, mostly over pasture claims in Crete, and in the Tempi rail-safety file it prosecuted 23 non-political suspects (EPPO reporting, 2024–2025).
  • On Tempi, a member of his own government was ultimately sent toward trial: former Deputy Minister Christos Triantopoulos was referred by a judicial council in September 2026 to the Special Court on a misdemeanor breach-of-duty charge over the alteration of the crash site, alongside the former Thessaly regional governor and three officials; Triantopoulos denies wrongdoing and says his innocence will be proven. Former Transport Minister Kostas Karamanlis was summoned to testify as a defendant on 27 November 2026 in the breach-of-duty investigation covering his tenure (parliamentary referral, 22 July 2025; Areios Pagos investigation reporting, November 2026).
  • A Greek court did convict in the spyware case: on 26 February 2026 an Athens misdemeanor court found four Intellexa-linked defendants, including founder Tal Dilian, guilty of breaching communications confidentiality and related offenses (combined sentences of 126 years and 8 months, eight years to serve, free pending appeal) — the first spyware-vendor conviction in Europe (court reporting, February 2026).

Evidence against:

  • The constitutional shield of Article 86 — under which only parliament may pursue ministers, and his majority controls parliament — has been applied, in sequence, to every file touching his own ministers. In the wiretapping affair, the 2022 parliamentary inquiry, controlled by his majority, declined to summon Mitsotakis himself or his nephew and general secretary Grigoris Dimitriadis, and concluded without assigning political responsibility; opposition members accused it of obscuring rather than uncovering the truth (inquiry record, September–November 2022).
  • In the Tempi file, the parliamentary inquiry committee established after the crash reported in March 2024 along party lines, the majority finding no criminal responsibility for any politician. The EPPO's chief prosecutor, Laura Kövesi, stated publicly that her office's investigation into the EU-funded rail-signalling Contract 717 was blocked as to the two former transport ministers by Article 86, that Greek authorities placed obstacles before her investigation, and that "if no one lifts the immunity, we cannot do anything about this case"; Mitsotakis responded that Kövesi had exceeded the limits of her competence (EPPO statements, 2023–2024; Kövesi remarks, March 2024 and October 2024).
  • Where ministers were eventually pursued over Tempi, the charges were set at the lowest available level by his majority's own motions: parliament's July 2025 vote adopted New Democracy's proposal of misdemeanor breach-of-duty for Karamanlis over opposition proposals seeking felony charges, and Triantopoulos was fast-tracked past a full preliminary inquiry — a route the government defended as speed ("no cover-up") and the opposition attacked as scope-limiting. CONTESTED as to purpose; the misdemeanor ceiling is documented fact.
  • The wiretapping investigation's judicial endpoint drew the same line at the same place: after Supreme Court Prosecutor Georgia Adeilini — appointed by his cabinet in July 2023 — took the case up to the Areios Pagos in October 2023 citing delays, her deputy's 287-page report of 30 July 2024 concluded that "no government agency, and in particular the National Intelligence Service… or any government official, was involved with the Predator spyware," leaving only private company figures to face misdemeanor charges. Investigative reporting (Inside Story, via OCCRP) documented that at least 27 witnesses who had been surveilled by EYP and targeted with Predator were never interviewed. The February 2026 convictions therefore answer who sold and operated the tool; who ordered the surveillance of the opposition leader, ministers, journalists and the armed-forces chief remains, on the official record, unidentified.
  • The "Petsas list" set the pattern early: in 2020 some €20 million in public-health campaign funds was distributed to media outlets — including, per the European Parliament's February 2024 resolution, non-existent websites and personal blogs — under non-transparent criteria, with some outlets excluded without justification and the recipient list disclosed only after sustained pressure (European Parliament resolution, 7 February 2024; HRW, May 2025).

Test 2 — HOLDS WITHOUT GRIPPING

Do the courts, the media, the intelligence service, the central bank, and municipalities keep their autonomy — or is independence punished and control centralized?

GRADE: FAILS

Evidence for:

  • The courts have ruled against his government at the highest level and the rulings stand. On 5 April 2024 the Council of State, sitting in plenary, unanimously struck down his government's 2021 law barring citizens from ever being informed of national-security surveillance, finding it violated the Greek Constitution, the EU Charter and the European Convention (Council of State plenary decision, April 2024). The European Court of Human Rights has ruled against Greece on pushbacks and rescue failures (Test 4), and no judge is documented to have been removed for any of these rulings.
  • Independent authorities bit, in public, while he governed: ADAE, under its president Christos Rammos, audited EYP's premises in September 2022, confirmed in January 2023 that Labour Minister Kostis Hatzidakis and armed-forces chief General Konstantinos Floros had been under EYP surveillance — flatly contradicting Mitsotakis's denial in parliament the previous month — and moved to fine EYP for obstructing its audit. The Ombudsman's February 2025 Pylos report found "clear indications of culpability" of eight senior coast guard officers. The independent rail-accident authority EODASAAM's February 2025 Tempi report documented systemic state failure and could not explain the fireball by the trains' silicone oils (authority reports, 2023–2025).
  • The central bank has not been touched: Governor Yannis Stournaras has served out his term, been reappointed once (2020), and publicly disagreed with government positions without sanction. Opposition mayors govern Athens and other major cities; opposition newspapers and broadcasters publish investigations of the government daily; elections remain free — his party fell to 28.3% in the June 2024 European elections, a result that was counted and stood.

Evidence against:

  • The grip on the intelligence service was the first act of his premiership and the center of the scandal. On 8 July 2019, the day after winning office, Mitsotakis placed EYP under his own direct control (Law 4622/2019), with oversight exercised by his nephew and general secretary Grigoris Dimitriadis; weeks later his government amended the law to remove the university-degree requirement for the EYP chief so as to appoint Panagiotis Kontoleon — despite Mitsotakis having denied any such law change was planned. The same 2019 law placed the state broadcaster ERT and the Athens News Agency under the prime minister's office (Law 4622/2019; parliamentary record, 2019).
  • The service so controlled then surveilled, on prosecutorial authorization for "national security," the leader of the third-largest party, PASOK's Nikos Androulakis (September–December 2021, while he was running for his party's leadership), financial journalist Thanasis Koukakis, and — per ADAE's confirmations — a serving minister and the chief of the armed forces; the Hellenic Data Protection Authority found 220 Predator-laden messages sent to 92 phones, with several targets overlapping the EYP list. When the scandal broke, Kontoleon and Dimitriadis resigned (5 August 2022); Mitsotakis stated he had not known of the Androulakis surveillance and would never have allowed it (address to the nation, 8 August 2022). Whether he knew remains CONTESTED between the prime minister and the opposition; that the surveillance ran for years from a service answering personally to him is documented.
  • The notification right was removed before the scandal broke and the watchdog was reshaped during it: in March 2021 his government retroactively abolished citizens' right to be told they had been surveilled (the provision the Council of State later voided); and in September 2023, while ADAE was preparing to fine EYP for obstruction, parliament — his majority plus far-right votes — changed ADAE's board composition (OCCRP/Inside Story reporting, September 2023). After the Council of State's April 2024 ruling entitled Androulakis to his file, EYP did not produce it: ADAE's inspectors had already been unable to find the surveillance material in 2022, EYP citing its destruction, and the agency declined to comply with the ruling's follow-through (GovWatch and ADAE correspondence, 2022–2024).
  • The media environment measurably narrowed on his watch. Greece ranked last among EU states in RSF's World Press Freedom Index four consecutive years — 108th of 180 in 2022, 107th in 2023, 88th in 2024, 89th in 2025 (RSF indices). The European Parliament's February 2024 resolution recorded deteriorating media freedom and rule of law; HRW's May 2025 report documented state advertising opacity, concentrated ownership aligned with the ruling party, and government control of state media. Dimitriadis, after resigning, filed civil suits seeking millions in damages against the investigative outlets that reported his alleged Intellexa links — suits press-freedom organizations classify as SLAPPs; Dimitriadis maintains he is defending his name against false claims and denies any connection to Intellexa (CONTESTED characterization; the suits are documented). The 2021 murder of crime reporter Giorgos Karaivaz stood unsolved for five years, with arrests announced only in May 2026.

Test 3 — ANSWERS THE WOBBLE

When courts, voters, prosecutors, or events push back, does he adjust openly — or deny and double down?

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • The Tempi cargo reversal, when it came, was made on camera and against his own prior certainty. On 29 January 2025, after leaked emergency-call recordings reignited the case, Mitsotakis told Alpha TV that his March 2023 statement — that he was "absolutely certain" the freight train carried nothing suspicious — had been premature, that he had repeated assurances from Hellenic Train, the Fire Service and OSE, and that "today there is no certainty… all possibilities remain open," leaving the question to the appellate investigator (Alpha TV interview, 29 January 2025).
  • Electoral wobbles were absorbed, not contested: after the June 2024 European-election result (28.3%, below the 33% benchmark he had publicly set), he reshuffled his cabinet and acknowledged the message; after the February 2025 Tempi protests — hundreds of thousands in the streets, a general strike, polls showing some 72% of Greeks believed the government had tried to cover up the crash — he reshuffled again (14 March 2025) and his majority fast-tracked Triantopoulos toward the judicial council rather than blocking referral outright (government statements and parliamentary record, 2024–2025).
  • Institutional corrections followed some pushes: after the wiretapping scandal broke he accepted the resignations of the EYP chief and his own general secretary within days and passed Law 5002/2022 (December 2022) banning the sale of spyware, raising private-use penalties to felonies, and restoring a route — after three years and with prosecutorial approval — for surveillance targets to be informed; he called it a "brave institutional response."
  • The OPEKEPE wobble was answered structurally as well as personally: the agency was abolished, its functions moved to the tax authority, and accreditation reforms undertaken under Commission pressure (May–June 2025). And in February 2026 he proposed the deepest available correction to the Article 86 shield itself: a constitutional revision transferring preliminary examination of ministers from parliament to an appellate prosecutor, with parliament voting only on prosecution — submitted to parliament in June 2026 and debated through September 2026. Critics answer that the package constitutionalizes his party's program, was carried in this parliament only by government and independent votes, reached nowhere near the required 180, and leaves the final decision to the next parliament (Reuters, 2 February 2026; parliamentary record, June–September 2026; ECPR analysis, 2026 — the characterization CONTESTED, the votes documented).

Evidence against:

  • On Tempi, the adjustment followed two years of denial. Within days of the crash he framed it as "tragic human error"; fifteen days after, he declared himself "absolutely certain" there was no suspicious cargo; questioners were dismissed by his government as conspiracy theorists while the crash site had already been cleared and levelled from 4 March 2023 — some 300 cubic metres of soil removed — destroying evidence the families' experts say can now never be recovered. The cover-up charge itself is an ALLEGATION, denied by the government ("no cover-up, no preferential treatment," its spokesperson restated in September 2026); the site alteration, the two-year certainty, and the reversal only after the recordings surfaced are documented. More than three and a half years after 57 deaths, no trial verdict has been delivered in the main case.
  • On wiretapping, the first instinct was containment: a parliamentary inquiry that did not summon him, a denial in December 2022 that his own minister and the armed-forces chief were under surveillance — contradicted by ADAE within weeks — and, when the EU's chief prosecutor pressed the Tempi file, a public attack on her competence rather than an answer on the immunity question (parliamentary record, December 2022; remarks on Kövesi, March 2024).
  • On pushbacks, the wobble has never been answered at all: the position across six years, restated after the BBC's June 2024 investigation (15 incidents, 43 deaths, nine people witnesses say were deliberately thrown into the sea), was that the reports are "not proved" and the coast guard saves lives — spokesman Pavlos Marinakis, June 2024 — maintained unchanged after the European Court of Human Rights found "strong indications" of a systematic pushback practice in January 2025 (see Test 4).
  • On the farmers — his own rural electorate — the December 2025 answer was dialogue offered on his terms while the blockades ran for weeks through Christmas: he urged "rational discussion" and ruled negotiation could not proceed under ultimatums or blockades, the government conceding accelerated payments (over €1 billion by year-end, per its plan) while refusing core demands such as tax-free diesel (government statements, December 2025).

Test 4 — COUNTS EVERYONE

Does the accounting include the whole flock — migrants in the Aegean, the poor, young Greeks, farmers, his opponents' voters — or are some counted out?

GRADE: FAILS

Evidence for:

  • The domestic flock's material position improved on the headline measures he chose: unemployment fell from roughly 17–18% in 2019 to 8.2% by late 2025, the lowest since 2008; the minimum wage rose from €650 (2019) to €880 (2025), on a legislated path to €950 by 2027; pensions were raised repeatedly after the crisis-era cuts; and some 100,000 Ukrainians were received under temporary protection from 2022 (ELSTAT/Eurostat series; government wage decisions, 2019–2025).
  • Migrants are counted in his government's ledger in one documented way: the coast guard's rescue record, which the government cites in every rebuttal — Greek forces have rescued tens of thousands at sea across his tenure, a figure Frontex and ministry reporting support in scale (Hellenic Coast Guard and Frontex reporting).
  • His opponents' voters are counted where it is structural: elections he lost ground in were certified, opposition mayors and regional governors took and kept office, and the February 2025 protesters — a crowd drawn heavily from beyond any party — policed their general strike without the event being treated as sedition.

Evidence against:

  • Migrants in the Aegean and at the Evros land border are counted out, on findings now made by courts rather than only by NGOs. On 7 January 2025 the European Court of Human Rights found "strong indications" of a systematic practice of illegal pushbacks from the Evros region to Türkiye in the case before it; Amnesty International records that of more than 200 domestic investigations into pushback allegations between 2019 and 2024, none led to charges against officials (ECtHR judgment, January 2025; Amnesty International annual report, 2025).
  • The Pylos shipwreck is the heaviest single entry: on 14 June 2023 the fishing vessel Adriana capsized off Pylos with an estimated 650 people aboard who died; 104 survived. Survivors allege a coast guard tow attempt caused the capsize — an ALLEGATION the coast guard denies. What is documented: the Ombudsman's February 2025 report found a "series of serious and reprehensible omissions" in search-and-rescue duties and clear indications of culpability of eight senior officers, and found crucial evidence had not been disclosed despite repeated requests; the Shipping Ministry publicly attacked the independent authority's findings; only in May 2025 did a naval court prosecutor bring felony charges against 17 officers including the then coast guard chief, expanded in November 2025 to four more including the current chief (Ombudsman report, 3 February 2025; HRW World Report 2026).
  • The counting-out was extended in law in July 2025: a three-month suspension of asylum processing for people arriving by sea from North Africa to Crete and Gavdos — described by Amnesty as unlawful and discriminatory — with the ECtHR issuing interim measures ordering Greece not to remove affected applicants (legislative amendment, July 2025; ECtHR interim measures, 2025).
  • The domestic count has its own exclusions. Roughly 27% of the population remained at risk of poverty or social exclusion in the latest ELSTAT survey even after seven years of growth; Athens house prices and rents rose by roughly half or more over his tenure, pricing young Greeks out of the capital — a failure Mitsotakis himself concedes in terms ("we are not where I want us to be," Wall Street Journal interview, November 2026). Youth unemployment remains around one in five. And the farmers of his own base spent December 2025 in blockade after the subsidy system his government supervised collapsed into fraud investigation — advances 35–40% below expectation, payments frozen for audit — a count of the flock taken late, under duress (ELSTAT 2025; Bank of Greece indices; protest reporting, December 2025).

Test 5 — KEEPS HIS WORD OVER TIME

Campaign promises vs. the record since 2019, with dates. His central 2019 offer was a modernizing, rules-respecting state: lower taxes, investment, "growth for all," and institutions that work.

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • The economic core of the 2019 and 2023 offers was substantially delivered. Taxes were cut as promised: the ENFIA property tax by 22% in 2019 and further thereafter, the corporate rate from 28% to 22%, the bottom income-tax rate from 22% to 9%. The investment grade he pledged to restore was regained — S&P in October 2023, Fitch in December 2023, Moody's last in March 2025. Public debt fell from about 206% of GDP in 2020 to about 146% by end-2025, with Greece running overall budget surpluses in 2024–2025 and growth above the eurozone average (2.3% in 2024, about 2.1% in 2025). The minimum-wage path to €950 by 2027 is on schedule (€880 in 2025, a further rise set for April 2026), and the digital-state promise (gov.gr) is a delivered, visible fact (Eurostat/ELSTAT data; rating-agency actions, 2023–2025).
  • The migration-reduction promise was kept on its own metric: Aegean arrivals fell sharply from 2019 levels and stayed a fraction of the 2015–2019 flows across his tenure — the means are graded in Test 4; the number moved as he said it would (UNHCR arrival data).
  • His 2023 re-election was itself a word kept in form: he had promised a single-party government capable of governing alone, declined coalition arithmetic after the May 2023 simple-proportionality result, went back to the voters in June, and won the majority he had described (election record, May–June 2023).

Evidence against:

  • The growth headline of 2019 — an annual growth rate of 4% — was never reached in any non-rebound year: post-pandemic recovery aside, growth ran at roughly 2% (2023–2025), about half the promised rate, and GDP per head remains below its pre-crisis level, a gap he acknowledged to the Wall Street Journal in November 2026 while asking voters for a "new contract" of growth at twice the eurozone average.
  • The modernizing-state promise broke first and most personally on the EYP: he denied in 2019 that the law would be changed to staff the intelligence service; within weeks the degree requirement was removed for his appointee, the service was placed under his own office, and it was that service which surveilled the opposition leader. The 2021 retroactive removal of the notification right — later voided as unconstitutional — was also his government's act. A promise of institutions that work, kept in the tax office, was not kept in the watchdog house (parliamentary record, 2019–2021; Council of State, April 2024).
  • The Tempi word: in March 2023 he asked the country to trust his certainty that the freight train carried nothing suspicious and that the crash was essentially human error; in January 2025 he withdrew the certainty (Test 3). The railway-safety modernization his governments had pledged — completion of the Contract 717 signalling upgrade, years delayed — was still incomplete on the night of the crash, as EODASAAM documented; the promise and the wreck are the same file (EODASAAM report, February 2025).
  • The wage promise of 2023 — an average salary of €1,500 by 2027 — is running behind the path it requires, with average earnings still well short of the target as the term's final year begins; and the June 2024 European election, where he set his own benchmark of matching 2019's 33% and received 28.3%, stands as the electorate's interim grading of the whole account (ELSTAT earnings data; election results, June 2024).

Overall Summary

One law: FAILS · Without gripping: FAILS · The wobble: MIXED · Counts everyone: FAILS · His word: MIXED.

Mitsotakis's file splits along a single seam: the state he runs as an economy, and the state he runs as a power. On the first, the record is the strongest in this series — taxes cut as promised, investment grade restored, debt down sixty points of GDP, unemployment halved, a second outright majority won. On the second, every test the seam touches fails the same way: an intelligence service taken under his personal control on his first day surveilled the opposition leader, his own ministers and his army chief; the watchdog was reshaped mid-investigation and its court-won disclosure never produced; ministers were shielded by his majority's reading of Article 86 until external prosecutors and a million protesters moved the files, and then only to misdemeanor depth; and in the Aegean the count stops at the waterline — a systematic pushback practice found by the European Court of Human Rights, 650 dead at Pylos, and a denial maintained without amendment for six years. The wobble and the word are mixed because the corrections, when they finally came, were real: a televised withdrawal of a false certainty, an abolished agency, a proposed constitutional surrender of parliament's shield over ministers. But they came after the denial, not instead of it — and the tests grade the whole record, in the order it happened.
