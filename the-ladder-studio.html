What this is — and the honest grading +

The octave math is exact. Halving a frequency drops it exactly one octave; the note name survives every halving, only the height changes. Start at 144,000 Hz and halve: 72,000 → 36,000 are still ultrasound. The first rung inside the textbook audible range is 18,000 Hz (three octaves down — most adults won't hear it; a child might). Then 9,000 · 4,500 · 2,250 · 1,125 · 562.5 — the heart, nearest the middle of hearing · 281.25 · 140.625 · 70.31 · 35.16 Hz, near the floor where tone turns into pulse. Against the piano's grid (A = 440), every rung lands on C♯, sharp by exactly 25 cents — octaves preserve the offset, so the whole family sits a quarter-semitone above the C♯ line, all the way down. That is verified arithmetic, and this studio is tuned to it: crown the ladder's own grid (A = 446.4) with the toggle above and those same rungs read as exact C♯.

Where 144,000 is really from. The number has two real homes. In Revelation, the 144,000 are the sealed — 12,000 from each of twelve tribes: 12 × 12 × 1,000, completeness squared and multiplied; in the text they are a people, and nothing calls them a frequency. In the Maya calendar, one baktun is exactly 144,000 days — about 394 years — a genuine unit from a genuine chain of use. Both are real, old, and checkable.

What is not supported: 144,000 as a frequency is 144 kHz — ultrasound, seven times above the top of human hearing. No ear hears it and no ordinary speaker plays it. Claims that this frequency heals, awakens, or aligns anything are not supported by evidence. What is true is smaller and better: a real number, a real ratio, and — once you octave it down — a real note you can play, record, and build with. That is what this page does with it, and it does not claim more.

How the nine instruments work +

Every voice is synthesized live with the Web Audio API — oscillators, harmonic stacks, filters, and gain envelopes. No samples, nothing fetched. One line each:

VoiceHow it's made
PureA single sine wave — the fundamental alone, with a soft attack and release. The ladder with nothing added.
OrganSine harmonics stacked like drawbars (1×, 2×, 3×, 4×), held at full sustain until the step ends.
PianoA bright harmonic stack struck hard, then decaying exponentially — loud overtones first, fundamental last.
StringsTwo detuned sawtooth waves through a lowpass filter, bowing in slowly — chorus from the detune itself.
FluteA sine with gentle vibrato and a breathy slow attack; a whisper of the octave above, nothing more.
BellInharmonic partials (1×, 2.76×, 5.4×) that decay at different speeds — metal, not a tuned string.
PluckA triangle wave through a filter that slams shut — the Karplus snap of a string let go.
PadThree detuned saws, heavily lowpassed, swelling in and out slowly — weather, not melody.
GlideOne continuous sine voice that slides — portamento — from the last note to the next, never stepping. The glissando voice.

The mic track is the tenth voice and the only real one: your own sound, recorded on this device, looped at step 1.

The two laws under the studio +

1 — Size sets the pitch, shape sets the pattern. Grow or shrink a vibrating thing and its frequency moves in exact inverse ratio to the size — twice as big, half the frequency, one octave down — and the ratio holds at every size of change, with no smallest stair: 1% bigger slides 569 Hz to 563.37; 0.01% bigger slides it to 568.94, still exactly on the line. It doesn't step; it slides, like water, like a glissando. And through the whole slide the pattern never changes — the twelve petals of a vibrating plate stay twelve at every scale. That is why this studio's ladder works at all: change the base number and every rung slides together, the note — the pattern — untouched. The Glide voice is this law with a fader.

2 — A tuning is a rule written while calm. A grid is a choice, crowned once and then measured against. Concert pitch has wandered for centuries — Baroque orchestras sat near A = 415, a full semitone below today; several modern orchestras play at 443–446 because sharp reads as brilliant. This ladder's grid, A = 446.4, sits inside the range humans already use. Crown it and the ladder's numbers are the exact ones while yesterday's “sacred frequencies” become the almosts — 432 becomes 438.28 on this grid, 528 becomes 535.68. Neither grid is nature. Both are rules. The honest move is to say which ruler you're holding — so this studio prints both, and lets you switch.
