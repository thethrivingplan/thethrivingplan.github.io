The Ruler's Test — Godwin Friday, Prime Minister of Saint Vincent and the Grenadines

Subject: Godwin Friday — Prime Minister of Saint Vincent and the Grenadines since 28 November 2025. The seat graded is the premiership, and the reason is stated plainly: the head of state is the Crown, represented by a Governor-General (Dame Susan Dougan) whose office is ceremonial, while executive power — the government, the budget, and command of the state — sits with the prime minister. Friday, born in 1959, is a lawyer who has represented the Northern Grenadines in the House of Assembly since 2001 and led the New Democratic Party (NDP) since 27 November 2016, serving as Leader of the Opposition for nine years before winning office. In the general election of 27 November 2025 the NDP won 14 of the 15 elected seats with 57.74% of the popular vote (37,207 votes) against 42.14% (27,152) for the incumbent Unity Labour Party (ULP), on a turnout of 62.31% — the largest supermajority since 1989, and the ULP's worst result in its history (Wikipedia, 2025 Vincentian general election; CARICOM Election Observation Mission report, 29 June 2026). Friday was sworn in the next day, 28 November 2025, and his cabinet was sworn in on 2 December 2025; he took five portfolios for himself — Prime Minister, Minister of Finance, Legal Affairs & Justice, Economic Planning, and Private Sector Development (Government of SVG, official cabinet list, December 2025). The man named in the original brief, Ralph Gonsalves, no longer holds the seat: his tenure ran from 29 March 2001 to 28 November 2025 (24 years, 244 days; five consecutive election wins), and he is graded here as the completed-tenure context the seat carries — he remains in the House as the ULP's sole member and, since 1 December 2025, as Leader of the Opposition. Friday is graded on roughly twelve months in office, and the file's thinness is stated where it matters. One structural fact hangs over Tests 1–3: election petitions filed on 18 December 2025 challenge Friday's own eligibility to sit at all, on the ground that he voluntarily acquired Canadian citizenship as an adult; the High Court heard the evidence through September–October 2026 and closing submissions on 10–12 November 2026, and Justice Gertel Thom's judgment is reserved, expected early in 2027 (Vincypowa News, October–November 2026). Grades on those tests are expressly provisional on that ruling. Graded: November 2026, on the documented public record. Method: Each test is graded only on the evidence listed under it. Both shelves are shown — the strongest evidence that he passes, then the strongest evidence that he fails. Allegations are labeled ALLEGATION and never stated as verdicts. Contested items are named as contested, with who disputes them. Seat-holders and public figures are named; people below the public eye appear by role only, unless the documented record itself establishes their wrongdoing. Grades: HOLDS / FAILS / MIXED / NOT ENOUGH EVIDENCE. This record is correctable: new evidence changes grades, in the open.

Test 1 — GIVES BY ONE LAW

Same rules for allies and opponents, insiders and outsiders — no favorites at the source.

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • The election that seated him was run by his predecessor's machinery, against his predecessor, and treated both sides alike. The CARICOM Election Observation Mission — invited by the outgoing Gonsalves government — observed over 85% of polling stations and found the 27 November 2025 elections "orderly, peaceful," with established procedures largely respected and no systematic or widespread irregularities reported (CARICOM CEOM preliminary statement, 29 November 2025; final report, 29 June 2026). Gonsalves dissolved Parliament on 28 October 2025, addressed the nation after the loss, and left office the day after the vote; Friday was sworn in on 28 November (International IDEA election summary, 2026; Wikipedia, 2025 Vincentian general election). The source of the seat is a count that was allowed to count him in and his predecessor out.
  • His government's first signature act applied to a whole class, not to a party list. On 6 January 2026 — within six weeks of taking office — Cabinet approved the return of every public officer "deemed to have abandoned their posts" under the previous government's 2021 vaccine mandate (Statutory Rules and Orders No. 28 of 2021), with resumption by 30 January 2026 and arrangements for suitable positions for those who had already resumed (Agency for Public Information notice, 6 January 2026). The officers restored were, as a group, people the outgoing government had cast out and the incoming one had championed; the instrument used was a general approval, not individual pardons for supporters.
  • The law has not been visibly weaponized against the defeated. The NDP alleged large-scale corruption by the ULP throughout its years in opposition; twelve months into government, no investigations or prosecutions of former officials have been announced (St. Vincent Times, reported in Vincypowa News one-year review, 21 November 2026). What the government has published against its predecessor is an audit finding, not a charge sheet: unrecorded departmental liabilities of EC$75 million found in audits, against the EC$20 million expected (Minister of State Lavern King, in Vincypowa News, 21 November 2026). The guilt or innocence of any former official in that file is an ALLEGATION until a court rules; the documented fact is that the new government has so far answered alleged theft with an audit number, not with arrests.
  • The opposition's institutional rights operate. Opposition senators sit in the House on the Opposition Leader's advice; one of them, Senator Carlos James, is simultaneously lead local counsel for the election petitioners seeking to unseat the Prime Minister — a role he performs openly, in court and on television (SVGTV News, 13 November 2026). Gonsalves himself broadcasts a weekly radio programme attacking the government (Star FM, "Morning Comrade," April 2026).

Evidence against:

  • The defining one-law file of the tenure is the government's attempt to rewrite the eligibility rule under which its own leader's election is being challenged — retroactively, and in terms that would decide his case. After electors Carlos Williams and Luke Browne petitioned the High Court on 18 December 2025 to void the elections of Friday and Foreign Minister Fitzgerald Bramble under Section 26(1)(a) of the Constitution — which disqualifies any person who "is, by virtue of his own act, under any acknowledgement of allegiance, obedience or adherence to a foreign power or state" — the government placed two bills on the Order Paper: a Constitution (Amendment) Bill and a Representation of the People (Amendment) Bill that would declare Commonwealth countries, including Canada, not to be "foreign powers," with the redefinition made retroactive to 27 October 1979, the day the Constitution took effect (Vincypowa News, 25 June 2026; Star FM/ULP legal submission, June 2026). The ULP's published legal case states the objection at full strength: the bills "represent not a tidying-up of the law, but an attempt to extinguish a pending legal challenge by legislative force," and the question belongs to the High Court, "not the Cabinet, and not the parliamentary majority" (Star FM, June 2026). The government's position is also recorded at full strength: that the bills "clarify an ambiguity," that Section 25's recognition of Commonwealth citizens as qualified supports the respondents' reading, and that the amendments would not affect the pending petitions (Vincypowa News, June 2026). Both characterizations are contested; what is not contested is the sequence — the bills exist, they are retroactive to 1979, they were tabled by Friday himself in his capacity as Minister of Legal Affairs and Justice while he is a respondent in the petitions, and his gazetted portfolio subjects include "Citizenship" itself (Government Gazette No. 37, 18 August 2026). A law written to fit the ruler's own pending case is the textbook second law for insiders; that it has not been enacted (Test 2) is what keeps this test at MIXED rather than FAILS.
  • The irony on the record sharpens the point. A proposal to let dual citizens hold elected office was part of the 2009 constitutional referendum package; Vincentians voted the package down, and the NDP — Friday included — campaigned against that provision at the time (Vincypowa News, June 2026). The rule his government now proposes to clarify by statute is a rule he previously asked the electorate to keep, and the electorate kept it.
  • An unresolved partisan-employment ALLEGATION stands against the government. In June 2026 Gonsalves alleged that 28 employees of the Youth Empowerment Service (YES) programme were dismissed without proper assessment and replaced by party supporters, some learning of their termination by telephone, and said legal action was being prepared (24/7 Ryders News segment, June 2026). This is an opposition leader's claim, recorded here as an ALLEGATION; no adjudication and no documented government rebuttal appear in the record assembled here. It is the mirror image of the victimization claims the NDP made against the ULP for 24 years, and it is recorded the same way.

Test 2 — HOLDS WITHOUT GRIPPING

Do the courts, the electoral commission, the media, the prosecution, and the central bank keep their autonomy — or is independence punished and control centralized?

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • The most consequential referee in the country is, at grading, actively refereeing against the Prime Minister's personal interest, and he is submitting to it. The election petitions were heard by the High Court in Kingstown through 2026: the petitioners closed their case on 30 July 2026; Friday and Bramble themselves took the witness stand on 29 September 2026 and were cross-examined about their Canadian citizenship; the evidence closed, oral submissions concluded on 10–12 November 2026, and judgment was reserved (Vincypowa News, 30 July, 2 October, and November 2026; SVGTV News, 12 November 2026). No documented act of interference with the court, the judge, or the process appears in the record. The Prime Minister's eligibility will be decided by a judge, in the open, on the unamended Constitution — because, in the end, the government let it be.
  • The grip was not completed even where the votes existed to complete it. With 14 of 15 seats, the NDP could have passed the citizenship bills at any sitting. Instead the sequence was: listed in April 2026; announced as paused on 21 April and referred to a select committee amid demonstrations and legal criticism; withdrawn from the agenda entirely days later; re-listed for first reading on 2 July 2026; and deferred again, as Searchlight reported on 3 July 2026 — and there the matter has rested while the court case ran its course (Vincypowa News, 25 June and October 2026). Twice raised, twice not forced. The bills remain on the shelf of the possible, which is recorded on the other side of this test; the institutional fact is that no referee was overridden.
  • The media environment is observably free and hostile to the government. Vincypowa News publishes detailed promise-by-promise scorecards grading the government down (July and November 2026); the St. Vincent Times and iWitness News cover the petitions and the VAT dispute critically; the Opposition Leader holds a weekly radio platform. No closure, prosecution, license action, or documented pressure against any outlet appears in the record assembled here.
  • The structural referees are regional and untouched. The judiciary is the Eastern Caribbean Supreme Court, with final appeal to the Judicial Committee of the Privy Council; the currency and central bank are the regional Eastern Caribbean Central Bank; the electoral machinery that ran the 2025 election was the same office that had run the elections the NDP spent years criticizing, and its 2025 conduct was validated by the CARICOM mission (CARICOM CEOM report, 29 June 2026). No appointment purge of judges, electoral officials, or prosecutors is documented in his first year.

Evidence against:

  • The concentration of the seat in one pair of hands is the heaviest in the country's modern record. Friday holds the premiership plus Finance, Legal Affairs & Justice, Economic Planning, and Private Sector Development — five portfolios — and the gazetted schedule of his subjects includes Public Debt, Taxation, Customs and Excise, Citizenship, Residency, and Citizenship by Investment Programmes (Government Gazette No. 37, 18 August 2026; gov.vc cabinet list). His deputy, St. Clair Leacock, simultaneously holds the deputy premiership, National Security, Disaster Management, and Immigration. The man whose eligibility is before the court is also the minister for justice tabling the bills about eligibility, and the minister who will design the programme that sells citizenship. Concentration is not capture; but the standard grades how much of the board one hand holds, and this hand holds the money, the law, and the citizenship file at once.
  • The process promised for the bills was not documented as performed. Friday said in April 2026 that the amendments would see broad public involvement before final decisions; between the April withdrawal and the July re-listing there is no public record of a completed select committee process, no published report, and no formal public consultation, and no government statement explaining the re-tabling or its timing — weeks before the July 28–30 court hearing (Vincypowa News, 25 June and October 2026). A consultation promised and not evidenced is a grip on the process even where the outcome was deferred.
  • The predecessor's completed record, as context for what this seat's grip has historically looked like: the 2015 election petitions filed by the NDP spent years in interlocutory litigation — still being fought over strike-out and security-for-costs applications in mid-2017 (Baptiste v Davis, ECSC High Court, 30 June 2017) — and no court voided or upheld the challenged seats on the merits within the 2015–2020 term they concerned. Friday's petitions, by contrast, reached a full trial within eleven months. The comparison is recorded because it is the strongest single evidence that, in his year, the referee has moved faster against the incumbent than it ever moved for him in opposition.

Test 3 — ANSWERS THE WOBBLE

When courts, voters, parliament, the street, foreign partners, or events push back, does he adjust openly — or deny and double down?

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • The April 2026 retreat was open, fast, and against his own immediate interest. When the citizenship bills first appeared on the Order Paper, the response was demonstrations outside Parliament, a mobilization call from the Opposition Leader, and legal criticism that the government was usurping the court. Friday answered in the House itself: on 21 April 2026 he announced the bills would not be debated that day and would go to a select committee for broader public input, and within days the government withdrew both bills from the legislative agenda entirely (Vincypowa News, June 2026; Guardian reporting via asatunews, 21 April 2026). Whatever the motive, the documented behavior is adjustment in public, not denial.
  • On the fiscal wobble, he adjusted his signature promise to expert pushback and said so in budget language rather than pretending nothing had changed. The IMF advised in April 2026 that the fiscal position left no room to lower the standard VAT rate, and its June 2026 Article IV consultation flagged significant fiscal and debt vulnerabilities while projecting 2.5% growth (Vincypowa News accountability review, 2026; IMF Article IV, June 2026). Friday's February 2026 Budget Address had already reframed the VAT cut as a matter "under assessment," and by October 2026 he was defending the deferral in Parliament against the Opposition Leader's attack — "reducing VAT to 13 percent still on cards" (SVG News via Facebook, 27 October 2026). Explanation under pressure is more than denial; the broken date itself is graded in Test 5.
  • On crime — the wobble that is hurting most — the government has answered with announced machinery and public argument rather than silence: a one-year strategic crime plan, more than 120 new police recruits, two additional Coast Guard vessels, a firearms licensing overhaul, FBI assistance, and, announced at the October 2026 Independence celebrations, a National Crime Reduction Task Force co-chaired by the National Security Minister and the Attorney General, with a 90-day security operation set to begin 7 December 2026 (Vincypowa News, 21 November 2026). Friday has also publicly resisted his own deputy's harder line, saying a heavier police presence is needed short-term but policing alone will not end the crisis.
  • In the most literal sense available to a ruler, he answered in person: he took the witness stand in his own eligibility trial on 29 September 2026 and submitted to cross-examination, including about the bills themselves (Vincypowa News, October 2026).

Evidence against:

  • The July re-tabling is the counterweight, and it is documented in sequence, not characterization. Having withdrawn the bills in April under pressure and promised consultation, the government re-listed them for 2 July 2026 — weeks before the 28–30 July hearing — with no published consultation, no select committee report, and no statement explaining the change (Vincypowa News, 25 June 2026). The ULP's reading — that re-listing was meant to alter the legal landscape before the court could rule — is an interested party's characterization and is recorded as such; the government's own explanation is absent from the record, and the absence is itself the finding. The bills were deferred again on 3 July (Searchlight), so the pressure, if intended, was never applied; but a ruler who withdraws a measure as a concession and then quietly re-lists it as the hearing approaches has answered the wobble twice in opposite directions.
  • The first instinct, before any retreat, was legislative. The petitions were filed on 18 December 2025; the documented governmental response across the following months included not only a legal defense — which any respondent is entitled to — but a pair of retroactive bills resolving the constitutional question in the respondents' favor. Adjustment came only after the street and the legal profession pushed. The order of operations matters to this test: litigate first would have been the clean answer; legislate first, retreat second, re-list third is the record as it stands.
  • On the street's other pushback — prices — the government's answer has been explanation it once rejected. Shoppers report sharp supermarket price increases; the government attributes them to oil prices driven by war in the Gulf, an explanation the NDP did not accept from the ULP when in opposition (Vincypowa News, 21 November 2026). And on crime, the outcome line runs against the rhetoric: 45 homicides recorded by 20 November 2026, already past the whole of 2025's 40 (Vincypowa News, 21 November 2026, police figures; Radio Jamaica reported 42 by 23 October 2026). Announced machinery is an answer; it is not yet an adjustment in the numbers.

Test 4 — COUNTS EVERYONE

Does the accounting include the opposition's voters, the Grenadines, the displaced, the young, and the poor — or are some counted out?

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • The downward transfers are real and were delivered early. Public assistance was raised to EC$500 a month (Vincypowa News, 21 November 2026). A 90-day fuel-relief package in May 2026, extended by Cabinet on 19 August 2026, halved the excise tax and customs service charge on gasoline and diesel, waived the service charge on cooking gas, and removed charges on fuel imported by the electricity utility; the 250-kilowatt-hour VAT-free threshold on household electricity was kept; VAT-free shopping days were held on 19 December 2025 and 28 August 2026 (Vincypowa News, 21 November 2026). A law put compassionate gratuities for daily-paid and non-pensionable workers on a legal footing, and the retirement age for those workers was raised to 65 (Government Gazette, Act No. 8 of 2026; Vincypowa News).
  • The pension count was widened with dates attached. In his 27 October 2026 Independence address Friday announced National Insurance Services increases effective 4 January 2027: contributory pensions adjusted for accumulated inflation, benefiting some 7,375 pensioners; the minimum weekly pension up 25%, from EC$80 to EC$100; and the non-contributory pension and Elderly Assistance Benefit up from EC$37.50 to EC$50 a week — plus a promised Social Protection Bill (Vincypowa News, 28 October 2026). Announced, dated, and gradeable in January.
  • The counted-out of the previous government's signature act were counted back in. The public officers deemed to have abandoned their posts under the 2021 vaccine mandate — teachers, nurses, police, and others, over 500 workers on the critics' count — were approved for return as a class in January 2026 (Agency for Public Information, 6 January 2026; the "over 500" figure is the mandate critics' count, recorded as such).
  • The Grenadines' displaced were named a priority in his first month. Hurricane Beryl (1 July 2024) had damaged or destroyed some 90% of buildings on Union Island; on 29 December 2025 Friday pledged housing for residents still displaced, criticizing the inherited pace as too slow (Caribbean National Weekly / St. Vincent Times, 29 December 2025). By 1 July 2026 his Housing Minister put Southern Grenadines recovery at about 60% — Canouan largely back to normal, Mayreau nearly done, Union Island the hardest — and in November 2026 the government announced returning residents would come home by late December, with roofs fixed and new homes completed, while conceding the houses were built before utilities were connected and that electricity and water hookups were still being arranged (SVGTV News, 1 July and 12 November 2026). Friday is himself the member for the Northern Grenadines; the file he is slowest on is his own constituency's sister islands.

Evidence against:

  • The count of the young narrowed even as the rhetoric widened. National scholarships fell by more than 500 in 2026 compared with 2025 as the government changed its award approach, against a Youth Guarantee that promises every young person an offer of a job, training, or internship by the end of the term; youth unemployment is estimated by analysts at 20–25% (Vincypowa News, 21 November 2026). Examination-fee coverage (up to nine subjects) expanded in the same year; the scholarship cut is the documented counterweight.
  • The displaced waited, and at grading are still waiting. Union Island's residents were displaced in July 2024; across two governments the return has been promised for late December 2026 — two and a half years on — with some residents still in shelters through Friday's first year and new homes standing initially without power or water connections (SVGTV News, 12 November 2026; Friday's own studio remarks, October 2026, acknowledging people still in shelters). The delay is inherited in large part — the ULP's prefab programme delivered about 100 units in seventeen months — and the completion, when it lands, will land on Friday's watch; as of grading it is a promise with a date, not a homecoming.
  • The 42% who voted the other way appear in the file mainly through an unresolved ALLEGATION and a symbolic omission. The ALLEGATION: Gonsalves's June 2026 claim that 28 YES programme employees were dismissed without proper assessment and replaced by party supporters — unadjudicated, unanswered on the documented record, and recorded here at allegation strength (Test 1). The omission: reparations — the cause Gonsalves made central to the country's international voice, and a live file for the Garifuna community — was absent from Friday's first Independence address, which the Opposition Leader attacked on exactly that ground (Vincypowa News, October 2026). An omission from a speech is not an exclusion from a budget; it is recorded as the documented signal it is.
  • The poor's arithmetic is contested at the center of the government's best claim. The NDP's October 2025 pledge was to "double Poor Relief" (iWitness News, October 2025). The government presents EC$500 a month as the doubling delivered; Gonsalves counters that his government had already raised the figure to EC$360 before leaving office, making EC$500 an increase but not a double (Vincypowa News, 21 November 2026). Both numbers are on the record; the increase itself is not in dispute, and the "doubling" is graded as contested in Test 5.

Test 5 — KEEPS HIS WORD OVER TIME

Campaign and program pledges vs. the record since 28 November 2025, with dates; his predecessor's completed word stated as context.

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for (promises kept or credibly in motion):

  • The vaccine workers — his clearest word, kept in six weeks. In opposition the NDP promised to reinstate the workers cast out under the 2021 mandate, and Friday had argued against the enabling bill in Parliament in August 2021 in terms the workers' advocates still quote (advocates' published record, November 2026). Cabinet approved the class-wide return on 6 January 2026, with resumption by 30 January 2026 (Agency for Public Information, 6 January 2026). One seam is recorded on the other shelf: the 2021 Public Health Act amendment that removed the word "voluntary" from Section 43B — the legal machinery that made the mandate possible — remained unrepealed as of November 2026, a point pressed by the mandate's critics under the headline "new govt, same old bad law" (advocacy publication, 19 November 2026). The people came back; the law that removed them still stands.
  • No new taxes — kept. The 2026 Budget, presented by Friday as Finance Minister on 9 February 2026 (Estimates laid 29 January 2026), introduced no new taxes and no rate increases, on a total programme of about EC$1.9 billion with capital spending raised to EC$577.3 million; revenue was to come from collection, arrears recovery, and a 20% cut in import duty concessions (Dawgen Global Budget Review 2026; Vincypowa News, 21 November 2026). He also stated the inherited position in the same address: debt of roughly EC$3.5 billion at end-2025, with debt service consuming about 39 cents of each dollar of current revenue — the constraint he now cites against his own VAT pledge, stated by him, on the record, before the IMF said it (Budget Address, 9 February 2026).
  • The public servants' bonus — paid, with its size contested. A flat-rate January 2026 payment was made; Friday said about half of public servants would see their income effectively doubled by it, Gonsalves said only 22% received the equivalent of a full extra month's pay, and the payment has so far been one-off rather than a yearly policy (Vincypowa News, 21 November 2026). The payment happened; the description is contested.
  • Infrastructure — the cruise port deal is signed. In June 2026 the government signed a memorandum with Global Ports Holding for a 30-year concession to develop and run the Kingstown cruise port: up to EC$250 million in phased investment, Vincentians able to hold up to 30% of the concession company, the state keeping ownership of the physical port, operations expected from the first quarter of 2027 (Vincypowa News, 21 November 2026). Gonsalves has confirmed his own government held talks with the same company and criticizes the terms as lacking detail — a criticism of the paperwork, recorded as his position.
  • The predecessor's word, as context for the seat: Gonsalves's 24-year record on promises is genuinely two-sided. His largest promise was kept at the largest scale: the Argyle International Airport, pledged for years and opened in February 2017. His 2009 constitutional referendum — his own project, put to the people — was lost when the yes vote fell short of the required two-thirds, and he accepted the verdict and governed on under the old constitution. Against that: he won the 2020 election and a fifth term on a seats majority while losing the popular vote (ULP 49.59% to the NDP's 50.33%), and governed the full term on it; the republic referendum he floated for 2022 was never held; and the vaccine mandate his government enacted in 2021 was ruled unconstitutional by the High Court in March 2023 — a ruling his government appealed rather than obeyed in spirit, winning reversal in the Court of Appeal on 12 February 2025 (majority; Wallbank JA dissenting), with the unions' further appeal to the Privy Council still the workers' road when Friday simply reinstated them by Cabinet decision (High Court, March 2023; Court of Appeal, 12 February 2025; Agency for Public Information, 6 January 2026). The seat Friday holds was kept warm for a quarter-century by a man whose word was strong on concrete and weak on constraint — and whose final promise, a sixth term, was the one the electorate itself refused.

Evidence against (promises not kept, or receding):

  • The VAT cut — his most repeated word, dated, and missed. The NDP's announcement of 2 October 2025 listed reducing VAT from 16% to 13% among the measures it would deliver within 60 days of taking office (NDP announcement, 2 October 2025; iWitness News, October 2025). Twelve months on, the rate stands at 16%. The February 2026 Budget Address described a general reduction as under assessment; Foreign Minister Bramble has since called it a policy for "future implementation"; the IMF advised in April 2026 there was no room for it; Friday told Parliament in October 2026 it is "still on cards" without a date (Vincypowa News, February–November 2026). A 60-day promise, re-dated to no date at all, is the clearest broken word in the file — mitigated, but not erased, by the fact that the fiscal constraint he cites is documented and was partly of his predecessor's making.
  • The stand-alone security minister — not delivered as written. The NDP's published crime plan promised a dedicated Minister of National Security in a stand-alone role, "giving the role the time and focus it needs." The December 2025 cabinet gave National Security to the Deputy Prime Minister, who also holds Disaster Management and Immigration — in the words of the Vincypowa check against the cabinet list, "That is not a stand alone role. It is four jobs" (Vincypowa News, July 2026). Meanwhile the outcome the promise existed to change has worsened: 45 homicides by 20 November 2026 against 40 in all of 2025 (54 in 2024) (police figures via Vincypowa News, 2026). The 90-day operation announced for 7 December 2026 will be graded when it has a record.
  • Citizenship by investment — the dated launch came and went. In the Budget Address of 9 February 2026 Friday announced "the Government's firm intention to launch a Citizenship by Investment Programme by the middle of 2026." By 12 November 2026 no enabling bill had been published or read in the House, no unit existed, and no application could be filed; in October 2026 the Prime Minister floated the first quarter of 2027 instead, and hedged it (CBI launch tracker, danmerriam.com, updated 12 November 2026, checked against 2026 gazettes and order papers). A "firm intention" with a season attached, missed by two seasons and counting. The delay may reflect the documented international scrutiny of Caribbean CBI programmes; the word, as given, was not kept.
  • Integrity in public life — announced, not yet tabled. The NDP campaigned hard on integrity; Friday announced in March 2026 that an integrity commission would be established during this term, but at one year no integrity legislation has come to Parliament, and no investigation or prosecution arising from the corruption alleged in opposition has been announced (Vincypowa News, 21 November 2026; St. Vincent Times). "During this term" leaves him four years to keep it; the first year is recorded as a promise carried, not a promise performed.
  • The statutory Constituency Development Fund and the jobs number — unverified at grading. The July 2026 scorecard recorded the statutory CDF as "not yet confirmed," and no delivery appears in the record assembled since (Vincypowa News, 13 July 2026). On the five-point plan's first promise — more, better-paid jobs — the government has published no job-creation figures for its first year, so there is no official number to hold it to (Vincypowa News, 21 November 2026). No new minimum wage order was made in 2026; the sector rates in force date from March 2024, under the previous government (Vincypowa News, 21 November 2026). Absences are recorded as absences, not as acts.

Overall Summary

One law: MIXED · Without gripping: MIXED · The wobble: MIXED · Counts everyone: MIXED · His word: MIXED.

Godwin Friday waited twenty-five years in Parliament and nine years as Opposition Leader for a seat he then won in the largest landslide in his country's modern history — and his first year is the record of a supermajority discovering what it cannot do. It cannot cut the VAT rate it promised to cut in 60 days, because the debt it inherited — EC$3.5 billion, stated by Friday himself in his first budget — and the IMF's documented advice stand in the way; it cannot yet show a jobs number, a citizenship-by-investment law, an integrity commission, or a stand-alone security minister; and on the promise to make the country safe again, the homicide count has moved the wrong way, 45 by late November against 40 in all of the year before. What it demonstrably can do, it did fast: the vaccine-mandate workers were back by Cabinet decision within six weeks, public assistance rose to EC$500 a month, a no-new-taxes budget passed, pension increases carry a January date, and a 30-year cruise port concession is signed. The gravest file is not fiscal but constitutional, and it is unfinished: a Prime Minister who holds Canadian citizenship, challenged in court under the allegiance clause, whose government twice placed retroactive bills on the Order Paper that would have settled his own case — and twice, under pressure, did not pass them. The court will now decide, on the unamended Constitution, whether the fifth Prime Minister was ever qualified to sit; he testified in his own trial and let the case run, which is why the grip tests grade MIXED and not worse. The open files that will move these grades are named in the dossier: Justice Thom's reserved judgment, expected early in 2027; whether the citizenship bills return a third time, and in what form; the VAT rate's fate in the 2027 budget; whether the 90-day crime operation bends the homicide line; and whether Union Island's displaced are home by the late-December date his government has set. His predecessor governed for 24 years and left in a day when the count went against him; the system that removed Gonsalves is the same one now grading Friday, and so far — the petitions, the scorecards, the radio programmes, the reserved judgment — it is grading him in the open.
