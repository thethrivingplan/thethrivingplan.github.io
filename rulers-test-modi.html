The Ruler's Test — Narendra Modi

Subject: Narendra Modi, Prime Minister of India (in office since 26 May 2014; third term from June 2024) Graded: November 2026, on the documented public record. Method: Each test is graded only on the evidence listed under it. Both shelves are shown — the strongest evidence that he passes, then the strongest evidence that he fails. Allegations are labeled ALLEGATION and never stated as verdicts. Contested findings are described with who disputes them. Grades: HOLDS / FAILS / MIXED / NOT ENOUGH EVIDENCE.

Test 1 — GIVES BY ONE LAW

Does he apply the same rules to allies and opponents, insiders and outsiders, with no favorites at the source?

GRADE: FAILS

Evidence for:

  • The Supreme Court itself refused to intervene on the general claim of agency misuse: in April 2023 a bench headed by Chief Justice D.Y. Chandrachud dismissed a petition by 14 opposition parties alleging weaponisation of the ED and CBI, holding that politicians have no immunity from investigation and that the remedy lies in the political space and in case-specific court challenges (Supreme Court of India, April 2023; Verdictum case report).
  • The electoral bonds scheme, while it operated, was used by parties across the spectrum — the opposition Trinamool Congress (₹1,592 crore) and Congress (₹1,351 crore) were the second- and fourth-largest recipients (Election Commission of India data published March 2024, per Association for Democratic Reforms).
  • When the Supreme Court struck the bonds scheme down, the government complied: the State Bank of India handed donor and recipient data to the Election Commission, which published it in March 2024 (Supreme Court judgment, 15 February 2024; ECI publication, March 2024).

Evidence against:

  • Official data cited by Human Rights Watch: the Enforcement Directorate filed cases against 193 politicians between April 2015 and February 2025, most of them opposition figures — a more than 600% jump from the 26 politicians investigated in the decade before Modi took office (Human Rights Watch, "'So What's This Democracy We're Talking About?': Modi's Authoritarian Playbook in India," November 2026, citing official data). The opposition's own filing in the Supreme Court put the opposition share of ED political probes at 95% (Supreme Court proceedings, April 2023 — opposition figure, labeled as such).
  • The pattern after defection: HRW documents that ED investigations are "promptly stalled, or charges dropped if the accused switches loyalties" to the BJP — the basis for the opposition's "washing machine" charge (HRW, November 2026). Specific instances of cases going dormant after leaders joined the BJP are a matter of public record in Indian reporting; the general pattern is ALLEGATION in individual cases, documented as a trend by HRW.
  • The anonymous electoral bonds his government created channelled a heavily skewed flow: of roughly ₹16,518 crore in bonds sold, the BJP received about ₹5,594 crore — more than the next several parties combined — and nearly 57% of all bond donations in 2018–2022 (ADR analysis of ECI/SBI data, 2024; Al Jazeera, 15 February 2024). A unanimous five-judge Supreme Court bench struck the scheme down as unconstitutional, holding it violated voters' right to information and that unlimited corporate donations violated equality (Supreme Court, 15 February 2024).
  • Enforcement outcomes are thin relative to enforcement volume: under the UAPA, 19,868 people were arrested in 10,118 cases between 2014 and 2024, with convictions in only 391 cases (government data cited by HRW, November 2026) — a pattern critics cite as process-as-punishment; the government disputes the characterisation.

Test 2 — HOLDS WITHOUT GRIPPING

Do states, institutions — Election Commission, judiciary, media, universities — and allies keep their own autonomy, or is independence punished and control centralised?

GRADE: FAILS

Evidence for:

  • Opposition parties continue to win and hold real power in the states: the Trinamool Congress has repeatedly defeated the BJP in West Bengal, the DMK governs Tamil Nadu, and Congress won Karnataka in 2023 — state governments the centre has not removed (state election results, 2021–2026).
  • The courts have ruled against his government on major questions: the electoral bonds judgment (February 2024) and the Supreme Court's January 2021 stay of the farm laws both went against the executive, and both were complied with (Supreme Court records).
  • Since June 2024 he governs in coalition for the first time: the BJP's 240 seats fell short of the 272 majority, and the government depends on allies such as the TDP and JD(U) (Election Commission results, 4 June 2024; Britannica/CRS summaries). Contested bills in the third term have been sent to parliamentary committees rather than forced through (e.g., the Waqf (Amendment) Bill's referral to a Joint Parliamentary Committee, August 2024).
  • GST Council decisions on the national indirect tax — shared with opposition-ruled states — have been taken overwhelmingly by consensus (GST Council record, 2017–2026).

Evidence against:

  • Human Rights Watch's November 2026 report, based on interviews with over 40 lawyers, judges, journalists and officials, finds the government "weakening constitutional and statutory bodies, such as the Election Commission, Central Information Commission, the anti-corruption ombudsman, and human rights commissions, and interfering in the judicial branch," and bypassing parliamentary oversight through money bills — including the 2019 PMLA amendments that expanded ED powers (HRW, November 2026).
  • In 2023 Parliament changed how Election Commissioners are chosen, replacing the Chief Justice of India on the selection panel (per a Supreme Court interim arrangement) with a Union minister — giving the executive a majority on the panel that picks the election referees (Chief Election Commissioner and Other Election Commissioners Act, 2023).
  • V-Dem classifies India as an "electoral autocracy" (V-Dem reports, 2021 onward) and Freedom House downgraded India to "Partly Free" (Freedom in the World, 2021 onward). The Government of India disputes both characterisations as biased — the dispute is on the record.
  • Media pressure is documented: tax "surveys" of the BBC's India offices followed its 2023 documentary critical of Modi (February 2023), the documentary was blocked in India under emergency IT rules, and India has fallen steadily in the RSF World Press Freedom Index across his tenure (RSF indices, 2014–2026).
  • Federal friction is structural: opposition-ruled states have repeatedly accused the centre of using Governors to block state legislation and central agencies against state governments — most recently the January 2026 ED raids on a political consultancy working for West Bengal's ruling party, which the state's chief minister physically confronted (contemporaneous reporting, January 2026; the ED states the raids were a lawful money-laundering investigation).

Test 3 — ANSWERS THE WOBBLE

When courts, voters, farmers, or events push back, does he adjust openly, or deny and double down?

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • The clearest full reversal by any modern Indian prime minister: after a year of mass farmer protests, on 19 November 2021 Modi announced the repeal of all three farm laws in a televised address and apologised on camera — "While apologizing to the nation, I want to say with a sincere and pure heart that maybe something was lacking in our efforts that we could not explain the truth to some of our farmer brothers" (televised address, 19 November 2021; AP/NPR reporting). Parliament repealed the laws days later.
  • After voters cut the BJP to 240 seats in June 2024 — far below its "400-plus" alliance target — Modi accepted the verdict, formed a coalition, and moderated his programme rather than contesting the result (Election Commission results; International Crisis Group analysis, June 2024).
  • In April 2025 the cabinet approved caste enumeration in the national census — a sharp, open reversal of the BJP's own 2024 campaign position, which had dismissed the caste census as divisive, and of the government's 2021 parliamentary opposition to it (Cabinet Committee on Political Affairs decision, 30 April 2025; Press Information Bureau).

Evidence against:

  • On the Citizenship (Amendment) Act, months of nationwide protest in 2019–20 — among the largest of his tenure — produced no adjustment at all: the law stood, and its rules were finally notified in March 2024 (CAA Rules, 11 March 2024).
  • Demonetization (November 2016) was never revisited or acknowledged as missing its aims, even after the RBI reported 99.3% of the banned currency had returned to the banking system (RBI Annual Report, 2017–18).
  • During the 2025–26 Special Intensive Revision of electoral rolls, when protesters demanded the exercise be scrapped, HRW documents the response as restrictions on movement, internet suspensions, and mass deployment of security forces with thousands detained, rather than any policy adjustment (HRW, November 2026; the Election Commission and government maintain the revision is a routine, lawful clean-up of the rolls — the characterisation is contested).

Test 4 — COUNTS EVERYONE

Does his accounting include the whole flock — Muslims, Christians, Dalits, Kashmiris, the rural poor — or are some counted out of the family?

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • Welfare delivery at genuinely universal scale, formally blind to religion and caste in eligibility: 55.17 crore Jan Dhan bank accounts opened (30.8 crore held by women); over 10.33 crore free LPG connections under Ujjwala; 12 crore household toilets built under Swachh Bharat; free food rations to about 81 crore people under PMGKAY; nearly 4 crore houses under PMAY (Press Information Bureau, Government of India, June 2025; scheme data to March 2025).
  • The poverty outcome is independently corroborated: the World Bank's Spring 2025 Poverty and Equity Brief records 171 million people moved out of extreme poverty in India over the past decade, and India's official multidimensional poverty rate fell from 29.17% (2013–14) to 11.28% (2022–23) (World Bank, 2025; NITI Aayog figures via PIB, 2026).
  • PM-KISAN pays ₹6,000 a year by direct transfer to landholding farmer families across all communities (scheme record, 2019–2026).

Evidence against:

  • The Citizenship (Amendment) Act 2019 wrote a religious criterion into citizenship law for the first time: it fast-tracks citizenship for Hindus, Sikhs, Buddhists, Jains, Parsis and Christians from Pakistan, Bangladesh and Afghanistan who arrived by 31 December 2014 — and excludes Muslims from the same three countries (CAA, Act 47 of 2019). The government states the law "does not affect any Indian citizen" and protects persecuted minorities; the UN High Commissioner for Human Rights called it "fundamentally discriminatory" and intervened in the Supreme Court challenge, which remains undecided as of November 2026 (UN OHCHR intervention, March 2020; Supreme Court proceedings).
  • In Assam, the completed National Register of Citizens excluded 19.06 lakh (about 1.9 million) people in August 2019, leaving their citizenship status unresolved; the register has still not been formally notified (Assam NRC final list, 31 August 2019). No nationwide NRC has been carried out — the Prime Minister stated in December 2019 that none had been discussed; the fear linking CAA to a future nationwide NRC is a contested projection, labeled as such.
  • Kashmir: on 5 August 2019 his government revoked Jammu & Kashmir's constitutional autonomy while the state was under central rule, split it into two union territories, detained its mainstream political leadership including former chief ministers, and imposed a communications shutdown — mobile internet remained off for over five months, among the longest ever in a democracy (contemporaneous reporting, August 2019 onward). The Supreme Court upheld the abrogation in December 2023 while directing that statehood be restored "as soon as possible"; statehood had not been restored as of November 2026 (Supreme Court judgment, 11 December 2023).
  • HRW documents a normalisation of anti-Muslim hate speech by BJP figures and vigilante violence against Muslims and Dalits (including cow-slaughter lynchings) with weak accountability, and anti-conversion laws used against Muslims and Christians (HRW, November 2026). The government rejects the report's framing.
  • HRW further reports that the 2025–26 electoral-roll revision deleted over 130 million names by October 2026, disproportionately affecting women, the poor and Muslims (HRW, November 2026). The Election Commission disputes the characterisation and states deletions follow due process with appeals — CONTESTED, both positions on the record.

Test 5 — KEEPS HIS WORD OVER TIME

Signature promises vs. the record.

GRADE: FAILS

Evidence for:

  • Several manifesto commitments were delivered as promised: revocation of Article 370 (done August 2019), a Ram temple at Ayodhya (consecrated January 2024), the Goods and Services Tax (implemented July 2017), and the toilet-building drive that declared India open-defecation free in October 2019 (BJP manifestos 2014/2019; implementation record).
  • Economic scale promises partially held: India remained the fastest-growing major economy through most of his tenure (IMF assessments), and the welfare-delivery promises — bank accounts, gas connections, rations, housing — were substantially executed (see Test 4).

Evidence against:

  • Demonetization's stated aims vs. outcome: announced 8 November 2016 as a strike on black money, counterfeit currency and terror finance, on the expectation that a large share of the ₹15.44 lakh crore in banned notes would never return. The RBI reported 99.3% of it came back (RBI Annual Report 2017–18). Academic assessment a decade on finds "limited success in meeting its stated objectives," with real short-term output costs (Journal of Economic Perspectives, 2020; NIPFP review, November 2026).
  • Black money from abroad / "₹15 lakh in every account": the 2014 campaign's signature claim produced no mass repatriation; BJP president Amit Shah publicly described the ₹15-lakh figure in 2015 as a "jumla" — an idiomatic expression not meant literally (Amit Shah interview, February 2015). A Special Investigation Team on black money was constituted in May 2014; no per-account deposit ever occurred.
  • Jobs: the campaign benchmark of crores of jobs a year was never met on any series. The government's own PLFS recorded unemployment at 6.1% in 2017–18, a 45-year high (PLFS Annual Report, released May 2019). Precision note: the exact "2 crore jobs a year" figure comes from campaign speeches, not the 2014 manifesto text (Audit India fact-check). Later PLFS rounds show the headline rate falling to 3.2% by 2023–24, while the independent CMIE series continued to read 7–8% — the two series are contested against each other.
  • "Double farmers' income by 2022": the only official measurement series (NSO Situation Assessment Survey) shows farm household income rising from ₹6,426/month (2012–13) to ₹10,218/month (2018–19) — about 59% nominal, roughly 15% in real terms, not a doubling; no post-2019 survey has been published, and the government has given no official accounting of the target (NSO SAS 2018–19; Ministry of Agriculture reply to Parliament, 11 March 2025, which lists inputs but no income figure).
  • The $5 trillion economy target for 2024–25 was missed on schedule (India's GDP reached roughly $4 trillion in 2025, IMF data) — a deadline quietly allowed to pass.

OVERALL SUMMARY

The record splits cleanly in two. On delivery to the counted-in majority, Modi's is the strongest file of any Indian prime minister of the modern era: bank accounts, toilets, gas, rations and houses at a scale corroborated by the World Bank's poverty numbers, and he has shown he can answer a wobble in full — the farm-laws repeal with a public apology, the accepted 2024 verdict, the caste-census reversal. But the five tests ask about restraint, and there the grades fall: enforcement falls overwhelmingly on opponents and stalls for defectors, the institutions that referee the game have been weakened on his watch, a religious test now stands in citizenship law, Kashmir's autonomy was taken while its leaders were detained, and his signature word — black money, doubled farm incomes, demonetization's aims — did not hold on its own stated terms. Grades: One Law — FAILS. Without Gripping — FAILS. The Wobble — MIXED. Counts Everyone — MIXED. His Word — FAILS.
