Strategies — more than one way to play

There is rarely one right way to approach a question. Each strategy below is a real one players use, with its trade-off printed beside it. Coach mode brings the right one to the table at the right moment.

1. Elimination — rule out what you can prove wrong

You rarely need to know the answer. You need to know two answers that can't be right. Cross those off and a blind guess becomes a coin flip.

Trade-off: costs time and only works when you truly know a fact about the wrong options — a guessed elimination can cross off the right answer.

2. Read for the trap

Many questions hide a familiar-but-wrong option: Sydney for Australia's capital, the famous name instead of the right one. If one option feels too obvious, ask why it's there.

Trade-off: sometimes the obvious answer is simply right. Trap-hunting everything turns easy points into overthought misses.

3. Guess beats pass — when the maths says so

Here, a wrong guess costs nothing extra and a pass scores zero. So a guess always beats a pass: one-in-four blind, one-in-two after eliminating two. Passing is only for protecting a streak you care about more than the point.

Trade-off: in games that punish wrong answers, the maths flips. Know which game you're in before you guess.

4. Lifeline judgment — spend them where they change the odds

Fifty-fifty is worth most on a question where you know nothing — it doubles a blind guess. Swap is worth most on a category that's your weak shelf. Extra time only helps if you were close to an answer, not if you're blank.

Trade-off: a lifeline spent early can't save a later question. But a lifeline never spent is worth exactly zero.

5. Play your shelves honestly

Your record (below) shows accuracy by category. In a Category Run, start on a strong shelf to build a streak, then take on a weak one while you're warm — the weak shelf is where the learning is.

Trade-off: comfort runs feel good and teach little. Weak-shelf runs feel worse and move the numbers that matter.

Rules & Settings

The rules are settings. The standard set is printed plainly; change anything and the table labels your game a variant, on the banner above the board — never quietly.

The standard game

  • Four options per question, exactly one right.
  • One point per right answer. No penalty for a wrong answer. A pass scores zero and shows the answer.
  • No clock in Solo and Category runs. Timed Run (20 seconds a question) is a labelled variant.
  • Standard lifelines, one use each per run: Fifty-Fifty, Swap the Question, Extra Time (Timed Run only — it adds 10 seconds).
  • Turning any lifeline off, or playing Timed, makes the run a variant and the banner says so.
How to play — tap to open

Start a run. Read the question, tap an answer. Right or wrong, you get the fact explained in plain words (Light and Coach), then tap Next. Lifelines sit under the options — tap one to use it; each works once per run. Hold any control (or hover with a mouse) and most browsers will show its label; every control also says its gesture in words on its face.

What the tiers mean — tap to open

Gentle — facts most people meet in everyday life. Steady — facts you likely learned once and can reach with a think. Sharp — facts for people who read around the subject. The tier is printed on every question, and your record keeps accuracy per tier so the label stays honest.

About the Bank

Fact discipline: every question sticks to a fact that is well established and not commonly disputed. Where sources commonly disagree — the longest river on Earth is the classic case — the question was left out entirely. A smaller honest bank beats a bigger shaky one.
