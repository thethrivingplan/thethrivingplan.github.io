The Ruler's Test — Tom Emmer, House Majority Whip of the United States House of Representatives

Subject: Tom Emmer (R-MN) — House Majority Whip of the 119th Congress. Premise verified for November 2026: Emmer holds the office today and has held it continuously since 3 January 2023 — he won a contested Republican Conference election for the 118th Congress by 115–106, and was re-elected by his colleagues to serve as House Majority Whip for the 119th Congress, "the third-highest position in House Republican leadership," behind Speaker Mike Johnson and Majority Leader Steve Scalise (majoritywhip.gov biography, 2026; contemporaneous reporting of the November 2022 conference election). The seat graded is the whip's office itself, and its limits are stated plainly at the outset: a whip commands no votes formally. The office's actual powers, on the Congressional Research Service's account, are two — "to take responsibility for the mobilization of party Members for key votes and to serve as a conduit for information between party leaders and party Members" — exercised through a large whip organization of a chief deputy whip, deputy whips, assistant and regional whips who count heads, track members' whereabouts for scheduling, issue advisories, and "whip up" support for the party position (CRS, House Leadership: Whip Organization, RS20499). A whip cannot sign, veto, schedule, or rule anything; the scheduling lever belongs to the Speaker and Majority Leader, graded separately in this series (batch 49). Every member Emmer counts answers first to his or her own district, and can defy the count at the price of a primary, not a punishment the whip can impose. He is graded on his exercise of the counting, marshaling, member-service, and persuasion powers the office actually holds — in the 119th Congress principally, with his 118th-Congress tenure (including his four-hour nomination for Speaker on 24 October 2023, withdrawn the same day after conservatives revolted and Donald Trump attacked him on social media; NBC News, January 2026) as context only. The January transfer, stated plainly: the midterm elections of 3 November 2026 removed his majority, so the Majority Whip's office itself passes to a Democrat when the 120th Congress convenes on 3 January 2027. Emmer won re-election in Minnesota's 6th District on 3 November 2026 with 55.73% to Doug Chapin's 44.16% (St. Cloud Times / MPR–NBC call, 3 November 2026). On 10 November 2026 House Republicans elected him Republican Whip for the 120th Congress — in the minority, the No. 2 post under incoming Minority Leader Jim Jordan — defeating Rep. August Pfluger 103–76 in a secret ballot (Politico reporting via Political Wire and KSTP, 10 November 2026; majoritywhip.gov statement, 10 November 2026). On 18 November 2026 House Democrats elected Rep. Pete Aguilar (D-CA) unopposed as Majority Whip for the 120th Congress, under Speaker-designate Hakeem Jeffries and Majority Leader-elect Katherine Clark (Ballotpedia, 120th Congress leadership elections; Rep. Gabe Vasquez release, 18 November 2026; democrats.house.gov, 18 November 2026). So the man keeps a whip's job in January; the majority office graded here does not stay with him — it goes to Aguilar, and Emmer's grade is for the sitting holder of the 119th, through the lame-duck weeks to noon on 3 January 2027. Graded: November 2026, on the documented public record. Method: "How you play the game matters. Play by the rules. Stay true to your word. And if another option becomes available, at least consider it." Each test is graded only on the evidence listed under it. Both shelves are shown — the strongest evidence that he passes, then the strongest evidence that he fails. Allegations are labeled ALLEGATION and never stated as verdicts. Contested items are named as contested, with who disputes them. Seat-holders and public figures are named; people below the public eye appear by role only, unless the documented record itself establishes their wrongdoing. The mandate rule applies throughout: governing on the platform the voters elected is NOT inherently a failure and is never graded as one — carrying out that platform is evidence on the FOR shelf of Test 5, and marks are lost only for HOW it is done: a second law for his own side (Test 1), gripping the referees (Test 2), counting out those who did not elect him (Test 4). A whip's public record is thinner than a Speaker's — the count itself is taken in private — so where the shelves cannot carry a grade, this dossier strengthens the shelf or grades NOT ENOUGH EVIDENCE rather than inflating. Grades: HOLDS / FAILS / MIXED / NOT ENOUGH EVIDENCE. This record is correctable: new evidence changes grades, in the open.

Test 1 — GIVES BY ONE LAW

Same rules for allies and opponents, insiders and outsiders — no favorites at the source. For this seat: the same whip treatment for every member of his own conference — loyalists, dissenters, and the members whose votes he needs most — since the conference is the only electorate the office serves directly.

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • The clean-slate rule is attested by the dissenters themselves, not by the whip's office. Rep. Nancy Mace (R-SC), who describes herself as "on the higher part of their list of people they have to worry about on certain votes," told The Washington Post that Emmer and his team "have never once held it against me"; Rep. Tim Burchett (R-TN), another frequent leadership opponent, said the same — "They're going to need me on the next go-round" — and that he is now "very fond of him" (Washington Post, 19 September 2026). Each vote treated as a fresh count, with no documented ledger of past defections carried forward as discipline, is one law inside the conference in its working form.
  • His stated operating model is service applied to all comers equally: he runs the whip office as "a customer service office" where the members "are our customers" — "The customer is right, and even when the customer is wrong, the customer is right. Leadership really is not telling people what to do; it's trying to help them do what they came here to do" (Washington Post, 19 September 2026). Members corroborate the manner: Rep. Ralph Norman (R-SC) of the Freedom Caucus — "Tom, he applies the hockey stick… He's straight up. There's no double talk with him" (NBC News, 21 January 2026).
  • No documented case appears in the record assembled here of Emmer stripping a committee assignment, a whip-team post, or member services from a Republican who voted against leadership in the 119th Congress — the formal discipline levers, insofar as they exist, sit with the Speaker and the Steering Committee, and no Emmer-specific use of them is documented. The absence is recorded as an absence, not as proof none occurred in private.

Evidence against:

  • The persuasion escalates by member, and the escalation is the president. Emmer's own account: once holdouts are "whittled down to" a handful, his office calls on President Trump for "much-needed assistance"; to difficult members he says, in substance, you can tell me no, "but now you can tell the president that that's what you want to do" — adding "it's not to intimidate," a characterization the members on the receiving end are not recorded as sharing (Washington Post, 19 September 2026; NBC News, 21 January 2026). A White House official described Emmer as the "tougher cop" against Speaker Johnson's "more patient style" (NBC News, 21 January 2026). A rule whose final argument is a phone call from the president, applied to the movable few and not to the immovable or the already-loyal, is not the same pressure for every member — it is pressure calibrated to weakness, which is the whip's craft and also, on this test, its cost.
  • The attendance rule he announced was absolute in words and uneven in fact. Emmer declared members could miss votes only in "life and death" circumstances; NBC News recorded in January 2026 that the declaration "hasn't been heeded by everyone," and by November 2026 attendance failure was the lame-duck House's defining breakdown (Test 5) (NBC News, 21 January 2026). A law stated for all and enforced against none that the record can name is a second, softer law for those who ignore it.
  • His confidentiality rule cuts both ways and is recorded on both shelves: he declined to name the member who texted Trump that a budget vote would fail, and declined to name the holdout Trump flipped in a late-night call, on a "small town law office" principle — "if you share your clients' business outside of the office, you will not have any business" (NBC News, 21 January 2026). Even-handed silence protects dissenters and loyalists alike; it also means the count's internal bargains — who was promised what — are invisible to the districts those members answer to. The dossier records the practice and its stated reason; what was traded, if anything, is not documented.

Why: The grade lands at MIXED because the two halves of the record are both documented at first hand. Inside the conference, the working rule genuinely appears even: dissenters on the record say their defections are not held against them, and the service model is corroborated by members who vote against him. Against that, the office's decisive instrument — escalation to the president — is by design applied unequally, to the members judged movable, and the one rule he stated absolutely (attendance) was not in fact applied to all. One law in the daily service; a graduated law at the moment of decision.

Helped / Hurt ledger:

  • Helped: frequent dissenters inside the conference (Reps. Mace and Burchett named on the record), whose no votes cost them no standing with the whip office; members generally, served by a count that tells them plainly where they stand.
  • Hurt: movable holdouts on the signature votes of 2025, who faced a presidential pressure call their firmer or more loyal colleagues never received; the districts of members whose private whip conversations are, by the office's own confidentiality rule, never reported back to them.

Test 2 — HOLDS WITHOUT GRIPPING

Do the courts, the electoral commission, the media, the prosecution, and the central bank keep their autonomy — or is independence punished and control centralized? For this seat the referees within reach are the House's own checks — the discharge petition, the motion to vacate, the Congressional Budget Office's scores, and the members' own votes — and the first fact is the office's limit: the whip holds no formal lever over any of them.

GRADE: NOT ENOUGH EVIDENCE

Evidence for:

  • The House's end-run around leadership was used against his team repeatedly in the 119th Congress and the instrument was neither abolished nor rule-changed away on his watch: discharge petitions forced the Epstein-files vote in November 2025 and again reached 218 signatures in November 2026, and in September 2026 Republicans crossed party lines on the floor to demand President Trump curtail the war in Iran (Washington Post, 19 September 2026; Speaker dossier, batch 49). The whip's office exists to prevent exactly those defections; that they happened, publicly, and the mechanisms survived, is the strongest documented evidence that the count did not become a grip on the chamber's own checks.
  • The neutral scorekeeper published against his signature law and was not punished by him on any documented record: CBO's estimates that the One Big Beautiful Bill Act as enacted added $3.4 trillion to deficits and left 10 million more people uninsured in 2034 were debated, not suppressed (CBO, July and August 2025). No documented act by Emmer personally — as distinct from the Speaker, who holds the Rules and Ethics levers — defying a court order, lobbying the Ethics Committee, or retaliating against media coverage appears in the record assembled here. Critical coverage of his whip operation ran in NBC News and The Washington Post in long, interview-based pieces he himself sat for (NBC News, 21 January 2026; Washington Post, 19 September 2026).

Evidence against:

  • The grip this office can apply is on members, and its heaviest documented form is the presidential escalation (Test 1): a member's independent vote is the smallest referee in the system, and the whip's craft is to move it. When the moving is done by summoning the party leader's power over a member's primary electorate — "You're with Donald Trump because your constituents support what Donald Trump is trying to do" (NBC News, 21 January 2026) — the pressure reaches past argument to the member's tenure. That is within the whip's traditional job description; it is recorded here because Test 2 asks whether independence is punished or merely persuaded, and a threatened primary is nearer the first than the second. No member is documented as having in fact lost a committee post, a race, or a service at Emmer's hand for a no vote — the threat's existence is documented, its execution is not.
  • One leadership-collective episode is recorded with its attribution stated carefully: on 24 February 2026 the ROTOR Act — aviation-safety legislation passed unanimously by the Senate, drafted after the January 2025 DCA collision that killed 67 people — was defeated in the House after Republican leadership, Emmer voting no with Johnson and Scalise, closed the vote while members were still arriving, three votes short of passage, as victims' family members watched from the gallery; Rep. Don Beyer charged leadership with "engineering its surprise defeat" and closing the vote early (Beyer release, 24 February 2026). That is an interested opponent's characterization of a leadership act, recorded as his ALLEGATION as to intent; the vote's closure and the leadership team's no votes are documented fact. The gavel and the clock, however, are the presiding officer's and the Speaker's instruments, not the whip's — this episode is evidence about the leadership team Emmer serves on, and only weakly evidence about his own hand.

Why: The grade is NOT ENOUGH EVIDENCE, and the reason is the office itself: the referees this test names mostly sit outside a whip's reach, the levers that were gripped in this Congress (the Ethics process, the National Emergencies Act clock, the shutdown recess — batch 49) were the Speaker's, and no documented record assembled here shows Emmer personally gripping a referee or being the hand on one. What is documented about his own hand — presidential escalation against members' independent votes — belongs principally to Test 1's even-handedness question and is charged there. Under the receipts rule, a thin shelf earns this grade, not a borrowed one from the Speaker's file.

Helped / Hurt ledger:

  • Helped: the discharge-petition signers of both parties, whose end-run survived its successes; CBO and the congressional press corps, unimpeded on the documented record.
  • Hurt: not documented at the level this test requires for the whip personally — recorded as an absence. The members subjected to presidential escalation are charged at Test 1; the ROTOR Act's supporters and the DCA families are recorded above with the attribution limit stated.

Test 3 — ANSWERS THE WOBBLE

When courts, voters, parliament, the street, foreign partners, or events push back, does he adjust openly — or deny and double down? This test also grades whether, if another option becomes available, he at least considered it openly. Considering it and declining with stated reasons is NOT against-evidence. Refusing to look at it at all is.

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • The voters' verdict of 3 November 2026 — the largest wobble a whip can receive — was answered in the open, against himself. At the 10 November 2026 leadership press conference Emmer said: "Today is not a day for celebrating. We lost last Tuesday, and I take it personally, as do my colleagues. I fought for these seats as the NRCC Chair and worked like hell to keep them as the Majority Whip. We're going to do it again" (majoritywhip.gov, 10 November 2026). He then submitted himself to his conference's judgment in a contested election the same day and won it, 103–76 over Rep. August Pfluger (Politico via Political Wire, 10 November 2026) — accepting both the defeat and the vote on his own retention, with no challenge to the results and no attempt to hold the majority office past 3 January 2027 anywhere in the record.
  • Inside the count, adjustment is his documented method: the whip operation treats each vote as a clean slate (Test 1), whittles holdouts by listening — "trying to help them do what they came here to do" — and revises the bill's coalition vote by vote rather than demanding the conference move to leadership's first position (Washington Post, 19 September 2026). When the July 2025 Senate version of the signature bill arrived with provisions some 20 House Republicans objected to, the operation worked a 48-hour adjustment on the floor — the vote held open over six hours while members were persuaded one by one — rather than pulling the bill or denying the problem (Washington Post, 19 September 2026).
  • The no-good-choice clause, applied where it genuinely fits: that July 2025 choice had no good door. The options that actually existed were (a) pass a Senate version many of his members disliked, within roughly 48 hours, with room to lose only three votes; (b) reopen negotiations and miss the president's 4 July deadline the conference had publicly committed to, with the bill's coalition — assembled once, at full stretch — at risk of dissolving; or (c) pull the bill and concede the Congress's central promise in its first year. Each carried real harm. He took the least-harm door actually open (pass it, on 3 July, 218–214) and was honest about the strain while in it. Under the clause's mechanics this is graded as the best possible choice available given the situation in the moment, and it is not held against him; the trap's origin — a deadline set by the president and accepted by leadership, himself included — is part of the record and is noted here rather than charged twice.

Evidence against:

  • The 43-day shutdown of October–November 2025 — the longest full shutdown on record — was answered from his office with blame assigned outward, in advance and throughout. On 30 September 2025, before the lapse began: "I'm telling you right now, if the government shuts down at twelve oh one tonight, it's because of Chuck Schumer and the Democrats in the Senate"; on 7 October 2025 he led a letter pressing Minnesota's Democratic senators to end "the Democrats' government shutdown" and pass the House's clean continuing resolution (Emmer statements via emmer.house.gov, September–October 2025). A partisan monitor, MN-06 Watch, counted 262 occasions across his whip tenure on which he assigned blame for shutdowns, standoffs, or failed votes to others, 258 of them to Democrats — a count by an avowedly critical, reader-funded district watchdog, recorded here as that source's tally with its method stated, not as a neutral finding (MN-06 Watch, October 2026). If another option became available during the shutdown — a House-negotiated extension of the expiring Affordable Care Act subsidies, the substantive dispute — the record shows no open consideration of it from the whip's office; the House itself was in recess for the duration (batch 49), and Emmer's public line never varied from the Senate-must-act position. The wobble was not denied; it was attributed, entirely, to the other side, for 43 days.
  • In September 2026, with the midterms weeks away, he "refused to entertain talk about Republicans losing the majority," telling The Washington Post he was focused on appropriations and already had "the best job in the House" (Washington Post, 19 September 2026). Campaign posture is discounted by this series — every whip projects confidence before an election — but it is the same posture he had taken in laying out a "very close" but winnable map in Dallas in September 2026 while prediction markets gave Democrats an 84% chance of flipping the chamber and the generic ballot favored Democrats by 5.7 points (New York Post, 10 September 2026). The adjustment, when it came on 10 November, was complete and creditable; it came after the verdict, not during the slide.
  • The September 2026 floor week itself was a wobble answered by retreat: some votes fell short, others were abandoned altogether, and the Speaker sent the chamber home after Republicans crossed party lines on the Iran war votes — a week The Washington Post presented as "a fitting snapshot" of the majority's struggle, in the very profile of Emmer's operation (Washington Post, 19 September 2026). The whip's product is a count that holds; that week, on his own profile's telling, it did not.

Why: The grade lands at MIXED because the biggest wobble and the longest wobble were answered in opposite ways. The electoral verdict — the one that ended his majority — he met with personal responsibility, in public, within a week, and submitted to a contested vote on his own future the same day. The shutdown — the longest in the tenure — he met with 43 days of single-direction blame and no documented open look at the option his own chamber could have taken up. Adjustment is his craft inside a count and his failure mode between chambers; the record carries both at full strength.

Helped / Hurt ledger:

  • Helped: his conference's morale and continuity after 3 November 2026 — a whip who took the loss personally and stood for re-election gave the reduced conference (103–76) a settled No. 2 within a week; the July 2025 bill's coalition, held together by floor adjustment rather than ultimatum.
  • Hurt: federal workers and benefit recipients during the 43-day shutdown, whose wait was lengthened by a whip operation committed to the no-negotiation line (the recess decision itself is the Speaker's, batch 49; the whip's contribution was the public blame line and the House count held in reserve, never offered in a negotiation); voters in his own district and nationally who heard, through September 2026, that the majority was not in danger.

Test 4 — COUNTS EVERYONE

Does the accounting include those who did not elect the majority — the minority party's voters, the poor, the unrepresented — or are some counted out? The platform itself is not graded here; the counting is. For this seat the test has a literal form: a whip's count is a list of names, and the question is who is on it.

GRADE: FAILS

Evidence for:

  • Inside his own conference, everyone is counted — including its least reliable members. The clean-slate practice (Test 1) means a member who voted no last week is courted, served, and counted this week with no penalty balance carried: Mace and Burchett, both named frequent opponents of leadership, attest to it (Washington Post, 19 September 2026). A whip who counted out his own dissenters would lose a one-vote majority in a month; Emmer's count demonstrably included them, because the margins (215–214, 216–214, 218–214) leave no one excludable.
  • Democratic votes, when offered, were counted and accepted rather than refused on principle: the March 2025 funding bill passed with a lone Democratic vote picked up (NBC News, 21 January 2026), and in 2026 a procedural vote his team expected to lose was saved by two moderate Democrats, Reps. Jared Golden and Marie Gluesenkamp Perez, voting with the Republicans — which Emmer publicly called "just a stroke of good luck," accepting the help without punishing the occasion (Washington Post, 19 September 2026).
  • The completed FY26 appropriations cycle (batch 49) funded services in every district of both parties, and the whip operation that delivered the House's spending bills in the 119th Congress — passing them, in Emmer's phrase, on Republican votes alone where necessary — delivered full-year funding whose benefits run to districts that elected Democrats as much as to his own (House Appropriations Committee, January 2026).

Evidence against:

  • The count's own stated rule excludes the other party's voters by design, in his words: "I don't count Democrat votes. You're the only ones that I count, and you're the only ones that matter to me" — said to House Republicans at conference meetings in the weeks before the March 2025 funding deadline, and repeated by him to NBC News as the operation's doctrine (NBC News, 21 January 2026). The sentence is about vote-counting, and this dossier does not inflate it into a statement about constituents' worth; but Test 4 grades the accounting a ruler actually keeps, and the accounting this whip keeps — proudly, as strategy — is a list on which 212–215 elected members and the roughly half of the country that sent them do not appear. The bipartisan acceptances on the FOR shelf were windfalls ("good luck"), not entries in the count.
  • The signature count he delivered concentrates its losses, on the neutral scorekeeper's numbers, on people who were never in the count. The One Big Beautiful Bill Act passed his House 215–214 on 22 May 2025 and 218–214 on 3 July 2025 with zero Democratic yeas at the first passage (Clerk of the House, Roll Calls 145 and 190, 2025). CBO's distributional analysis of the enacted law puts the bottom tenth of households at roughly $1,200 a year worse off on average over 2026–2034 and the top tenth roughly $13,600 a year better off, with 10 million more people uninsured in 2034 and $3.4 trillion added to deficits (CBO, July 2025; CBO, Distributional Effects of Public Law 119-21, 11 August 2025). The mandate rule is honored here: the tax and Medicaid provisions were the elected platform, and the platform is not the failure. What Test 4 grades is that the whip's proudest achievement was a count engineered to need no one outside his own conference — and the people bearing its largest scored losses, Medicaid and marketplace enrollees in districts of both parties, were by the count's own doctrine not counted at all.
  • The shutdown count was the same shape held for 43 days: the House's clean continuing resolution, passed on Republican votes, was the only count his office would run, while the costs of the lapse fell on federal workers, benefit recipients, and air travelers nationwide — including in every Democratic district his count excludes (Emmer statements, September–October 2025; shutdown record, batch 49).

Why: The grade lands at FAILS because the exclusion is not an inference — it is the holder's stated doctrine, repeated on the record as a boast, and the two largest exercises of his office (the signature law and the shutdown posture) were both run on counts that needed, and sought, no one outside his conference. The FOR shelf is real but narrower than the test: he counts every Republican, including dissenters, scrupulously — and Test 4 asks about everyone. A whip is a partisan officer, and the dossier has weighed that: the office requires counting your own side first. It does not require declaring the other side's votes beneath counting when a bipartisan count is available, and in March 2025 and again in 2026 his own record shows bipartisan counts working when they were allowed to happen.

Helped / Hurt ledger:

  • Helped: every member of his own conference, dissenters included, whose vote was solicited and whose service requests were honored; Republican voters, whose platform was enacted at one-vote margins precisely because the count excluded no Republican.
  • Hurt: Medicaid and marketplace enrollees — 10 million more uninsured projected by 2034, the bottom tenth of households roughly $1,200 a year worse off on average (CBO, August 2025); the constituents of 212–215 Democratic members, whose representatives were, by the whip's stated doctrine, not in the count on the Congress's largest votes; federal workers and benefit recipients during the 43-day shutdown.

Test 5 — KEEPS HIS WORD OVER TIME

Program pledges and public statements vs. the record since 3 January 2023, with the 119th Congress (from 3 January 2025) as the graded core. Word-keeping and helped/hurt are graded as independent axes: keeping a word can still hurt, and breaking one can still help, and both are recorded in full.

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for (promises kept or credibly in motion):

  • "We're going to prove him wrong" — the funding word, kept. Crossing 1 — kept his word AND it helped people. Facing the first big test of Republican control in 2025, with Leader Jeffries's strategy premised on the GOP failing to pass a funding bill on its own, Emmer told his chief of staff, "We're going to prove him wrong," and told the conference to be ready to pass a spending bill alone (NBC News, 21 January 2026). They did: the March 2025 continuing resolution passed the House on Republican votes (plus one Democrat), the Senate filibuster threat collapsed, and the government stayed open. The same playbook — House passage on his count first — was repeated through the Congress's funding fights. A kept word whose keeping kept the government open helped every federal worker, service user, and taxpayer in both parties' districts.
  • The budget and the bill, on the dates named. Crossing 2 — kept his word AND it still hurt people. In April 2025, when a Republican member texted President Trump "You do not have the votes to pass this," Emmer called the president directly — "Do not do it. We're going to pass this" — and the budget resolution passed 216–214 that day (NBC News, 21 January 2026). On the signature bill he committed to the 4 July deadline; the House passed it 218–214 on 3 July 2025 and the president signed it on 4 July (Clerk of the House, Roll Call 190; Washington Post, 19 September 2026). The keeping earns its credit here in full. The hurt is also recorded in full, not netted away: the law he delivered on time is scored by CBO at $3.4 trillion added to deficits, 10 million more uninsured by 2034, and the bottom tenth of households roughly $1,200 a year worse off (CBO, July–August 2025; Test 4). It is the promise's content, not its keeping, that the other tests grade.
  • The platform itself — the mandate rule's core case. Republicans won the 119th Congress on President Trump's America First platform; the whip's office exists to enact it, and on the documented record the enactment happened at the narrowest margins in modern House history — 215–214, 216–214, 218–214, 214–212 (Secure America Act, 9 June 2026) — a persuasion operation the White House's own legislative director credited to Emmer's "knowledge of the conference, his deep relationships with the membership, his tactical acumen and his expert staff" (NBC News, 21 January 2026). Carrying out what the voters elected is FOR-shelf evidence by the method's own rule.
  • The personal word to his conference after the loss — kept within the week. "I take it personally… We're going to do it again" (10 November 2026) was followed the same day by his standing for, and winning, the conference's contested whip election for the 120th Congress, 103–76 (Politico, 10 November 2026) — the promise to keep working for the seats, submitted immediately to the only electorate that could judge it.

Evidence against (promises not kept, or receding):

  • The attendance word — stated absolutely, not delivered. Crossing 4 — broke his word AND it hurt people. Emmer declared members could miss votes only in "life and death" circumstances (NBC News, 21 January 2026). In the lame-duck weeks of November 2026 the count collapsed on exactly his core responsibility — attendance: 14 Republicans missed the Wednesday votes, forcing postponement of a scheduled rule; 21–22 Republicans were absent the next day; Friday's votes were cancelled and the House sent home early for Thanksgiving, with the Speaker reduced to a 20 November "Dear Colleague" letter pleading for attendance and calling it "a great frustration" (USA Today and contemporaneous reporting, November 2026; Johnson letter, 20 November 2026 — attendance file, batch 49). The members who stayed, and the districts whose lame-duck business (funding past 11 December, the defense bill) was left undone that week, bore the cost. A whip cannot compel attendance — the office's limit is real, and is recorded — but the word was his, stated without the limit, and the record is the opposite of the word.
  • "The Republican side has never been more unified" and the hold-the-House case — campaign words the record falsified. Through 2026 Emmer publicly projected unity and a winnable-to-likely retention of the majority (New York Post, 10 September 2026; Ripon Society remarks, June 2026), while the September 2026 floor record showed votes falling short and abandoned and the conference splitting on the Iran war votes (Washington Post, 19 September 2026). The voters then removed roughly fifty Republican seats. These are weighted as campaign statements, below governing pledges — a whip who predicts his own side's defeat has broken a different duty — but the unity claim in particular was made as a description of his own count, the one thing he is positioned to know, and the count he described was not the count that existed. No Crossing is assigned: the statements were predictions and posture, not commitments, and the dossier records them at that weight.
  • The three-shutdown Congress — a limit on the funding word, recorded honestly. The same operation that kept its March 2025 word presided, as part of the leadership team, over three government shutdowns in this Congress totaling more than 100 days, including the 43-day shutdown of October–November 2025 (Washington Post, 19 September 2026). The House passed its bills on his count in those fights — the word to his conference was kept — and the government still closed, because a whip's count ends at his own chamber's door. Recorded as the boundary of the FOR shelf, not as a separate break: the promise he made was about the House, and the House is what he delivered.
  • Crossing 3 — went against his word AND it helped people: not documented. No clear case appears in the record assembled here of Emmer breaking a stated commitment in a way that helped people — his one famous reversal-adjacent episode, the October 2023 Speaker bid, ended in withdrawal within four hours under pressure, which was a candidacy abandoned, not a word broken for anyone's benefit (NBC News, 21 January 2026). The crossing is recorded as not documented rather than invented, as the method requires.

Why: The grade lands at MIXED because the word-record splits cleanly along the line between the count and everything outside it. Every dated, checkable commitment about a vote — prove Jeffries wrong, pass the budget over a member's warning to the president, deliver the signature bill by 4 July — was kept, several of them at one-vote margins, and the two axes are both printed: the keeping helped (an open government in March 2025) and the keeping hurt (the signature law's scored costs). The words that failed were the ones about the conference itself — attendance "life and death" only, a conference "never more unified," a majority that would hold — and they failed in November 2026 in the same fortnight, on the whip's own home ground of showing up and being counted. A whip who keeps every word about the other side's votes and loses the words about his own side's presence has a genuinely two-sided file, and MIXED is its honest grade.
