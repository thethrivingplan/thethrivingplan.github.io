The Ruler's Test — Andrej Plenković, Prime Minister of Croatia

Subject: Andrej Plenković — Prime Minister of Croatia since 19 October 2016 and leader of the Croatian Democratic Union (HDZ) since 2016; the longest-serving prime minister in Croatia's history and, since 2026, the longest-serving head of government in the European Union. He has led three governments, winning parliamentary elections in 2016, 2020 and 2024. His third government, formed 17 May 2024, is a minority coalition of the HDZ with the Homeland Movement (DP), confirmed 79–61. He survived an opposition no-confidence vote on 21 October 2026 (79–55 against the motion) and remains in office at grading. Graded: November 2026, on the documented public record. Method: Each test is graded only on the evidence listed under it. Both shelves are shown — the strongest evidence that he passes, then the strongest evidence that he fails. Allegations are labeled ALLEGATION and never stated as verdicts. Contested items are named as contested, with who disputes them. Seat-holders and public figures are named; people below the public eye appear by role only, unless the documented record itself establishes their wrongdoing. Grades: HOLDS / FAILS / MIXED / NOT ENOUGH EVIDENCE. This record is correctable: new evidence changes grades, in the open.

Test 1 — GIVES BY ONE LAW

Same rules for allies and opponents, insiders and outsiders — no favorites at the source.

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • The law has repeatedly reached his own ministers, in office, and he did not stop it. On 19 February 2022, construction minister Darko Horvat was arrested by USKOK on suspicion of abuse of office — the first arrest of a sitting Croatian minister — and Plenković dismissed him the same day (USKOK action and government dismissal, 19 February 2022). Horvat's case remains an ALLEGATION in process; he has denied wrongdoing and stated he considers himself not guilty.
  • On 15 November 2024 his health minister, Vili Beroš, was arrested in parallel USKOK and European Public Prosecutor's Office (EPPO) investigations into alleged bribery, abuse of authority and money laundering in medical-equipment procurement; Plenković dismissed him the same day, and the Zagreb County Court confirmed the indictment against Beroš on 22 September 2026. Beroš denies any criminal responsibility; the charges are ALLEGATIONs awaiting trial.
  • Prosecutions of his former ministers have run to verdict. Former EU funds minister Gabrijela Žalac (in office 2016–2019) pleaded guilty in the EPPO's "Software" case and was sentenced on 12 June 2025 to two years' imprisonment for abuse of office and influence peddling over a rigged IT procurement that damaged EU and Croatian budgets by more than €1.3 million, repaying €200,000; on 2 September 2025 she received a further seven-month sentence, again on a guilty plea, for paying private restaurant bills from ministry funds (EPPO statements, June and September 2025). Former state secretary Josipa Rimac likewise pleaded guilty in 2026 in USKOK cases and received a prison sentence.
  • The pattern of exits is itself on the record: by the opposition's count, some 30 ministers left his governments amid scandals between 2016 and 2023, and departures under legal cloud continued in the third term (Beroš, November 2024; agriculture minister Josip Dabro resigned in January 2025 after video of him firing a pistol from a moving car surfaced). Plenković disputes the framing — on 23 November 2026 he stated that only four ministers had left because of problems with the law and publicly offered to resign if ten could be named — but the dismissals and resignations themselves are documented, and in the Beroš and Horvat cases they were immediate.

Evidence against:

  • The source of prosecution was refitted with a man the record had already flagged. In February 2024 his majority appointed Ivan Turudić as State Attorney General (head of DORH). Before the vote, leaked correspondence showed Turudić had exchanged extensive messages with Josipa Rimac — by then an indicted former state secretary — and President Milanović forwarded Plenković Security and Intelligence Agency (SOA) documents indicating that Turudić, as president of the Zagreb County Court in 2015, had repeatedly met Zdravko Mamić while Mamić was under investigation by that court. The President's office further documented that Plenković had not requested a fresh SOA background check on Turudić after 2019, relying on a summary of checks from 2009, 2014 and 2019. Plenković's defense is on the record: the appointment followed the legal procedure (the State Attorney's Council shortlisted candidates; parliament voted), Turudić denied impropriety in the contacts, and the government characterized the criticism as political. Turudić took office in May 2024. (President's office statements, January–February 2024; Liberties Rule of Law Report 2025, Croatia chapter.)
  • The one prosecutor he does not appoint was fought in public. When the EPPO's Zagreb office pursued the University of Zagreb Faculty of Geodesy case — 29 arrests in November 2023 over alleged fraud in EU-funded projects, with a related Culture Ministry contract awarded without tender — Plenković publicly attacked Chief European Prosecutor Laura Kövesi in February–March 2024, insisting the 3D-mapping contract involved no EU funds and that the EPPO was acting outside its mandate; Kövesi's office maintained that €1.6 million of the sum came from the EU Solidarity Fund. Culture Minister Nina Obuljen Koržinek, whose ministry awarded the contract, remained in office (Balkan Insight, 11 March 2024; OBCT reporting, 2024).
  • In the Beroš case the jurisdictional fight was resolved by his appointee, for the national office. On 19 November 2024 Turudić ruled that USKOK, not the EPPO, was competent, requiring the EPPO to hand over its file — a decision legal scholars criticized because the officer deciding the conflict heads one of the two competing offices. The Constitutional Court, asked by 33 MPs to rule on the arrangement, instead referred four preliminary questions to the Court of Justice of the EU on 8 July 2025, including whether a chief state attorney qualifies as the "court" that may resolve such conflicts under the EPPO Regulation; the question is pending (DORH statement, November 2024; Constitutional Court decision U-I-5437/2024, 8 July 2025).
  • His public formula in each ministerial case is distancing rather than accounting: the departing minister "bears political responsibility," while Plenković states he knew nothing of the conduct — a formula applied from Žalac through Beroš, and criticized by opposition and commentators who note that the appointments themselves were his (contemporaneous reporting across the cases; N1 debate coverage, October 2026).

Test 2 — HOLDS WITHOUT GRIPPING

Do the courts, the State Attorney's Office (DORH), media (HRT), the central bank, and municipalities keep their autonomy — or is independence punished and control centralized?

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • The Constitutional Court has acted independently of his government, including against its institutional interests: in July 2025 it declined to simply validate the DORH–EPPO competence arrangement and instead sent the question to the CJEU (above). Ordinary courts convicted his former minister (Žalac) and confirmed the indictment of another (Beroš) — the judicial system processes his own people.
  • No interference with the Croatian National Bank is documented: Governor Boris Vujčić has served throughout Plenković's tenure, delivered euro adoption with the government, and been reappointed through the ordinary process; the central bank's autonomy is not a contested item in the European Commission's rule-of-law chapters on Croatia.
  • Municipal autonomy stands in fact: Zagreb, the capital, has been run since 2021 by the opposition platform Možemo! under Mayor Tomislav Tomašević, re-elected in the June 2025 local elections; the government has not moved against the city's mandate, funding framework, or institutions, and contests with City Hall are fought politically.
  • Judges' and prosecutors' associations, the journalists' association, and the Ombudswoman all operate and criticize the government publicly; the European Commission's 2026 rule-of-law chapter records very low perceived judicial independence but does not find the judiciary captured.

Evidence against:

  • DORH: the Turudić appointment (Test 1) placed the prosecution service under a chief whose documented contacts with a corruption indictee (Rimac) and with a suspect under investigation by his own court (Mamić) were public before the vote, over the objections of the opposition, legal commentators, and the President, who called the process a "brazen farce" and the outcome party control of DORH. That characterization is the President's — an interested political opponent — and is labeled as such; the underlying contacts and the absence of a fresh security check are documented by the SOA material he released. The Liberties Rule of Law Report 2025 concludes the appointment "significantly undermined" the integrity and independence of the justice system and rendered EU anti-corruption recommendations unsatisfactory.
  • The "lex AP": in 2024 his government amended the Criminal Code to create an offence — up to three years' imprisonment — of unauthorized disclosure of the contents of investigative and evidentiary acts. The European Federation of Journalists and the Croatian Journalists' Association called for the amendments to be dropped, warning they would expose journalists' sources and communications to seizure even though journalists themselves were exempted from liability in the revised text; the European Commission's 2024 rule-of-law chapter records that "strong criticism from stakeholders" persisted after adoption. The government's stated rationale — protecting the presumption of innocence, privacy, and the integrity of non-public investigations, in line with what it called democratic standards — is on the record (Government of Croatia, 31 January 2024). The law's popular name derives from Plenković's own initials, given by critics who note it followed a series of scandals broken by leaked communications — including the Turudić–Rimac messages.
  • HRT: the public broadcaster's Director General is elected by the parliamentary majority, and the European Commission's rule-of-law chapters record persistent concerns about HRT's political independence and conflicts of interest year after year; an internal audit found irregularities in dealings with an external provider, and the conflict-of-interest commission penalized the Director General in March 2024. In October 2026 the Sabor's media committee, with majority votes after the opposition walked out, backed Dražen Miočić as the next Director General; the Croatian Journalists' Association and the journalists' union refused to support the process, stating it was not in line with the European Media Freedom Act and that HRT "has been transformed into an institution that operates in the interests of the ruling political party" — the associations' characterization, printed as theirs. RSF's 2026 index ranks Croatia 53rd of 180 and states that government interference in the management of the public television channel persists.
  • State advertising and local media: the Commission has repeatedly found no progress on a fair, transparent framework for distributing state advertising to local and regional media — a standing economic lever over outlets that depend on it (European Commission Rule of Law Reports, 2023–2026).

Test 3 — ANSWERS THE WOBBLE

When courts, voters, the EU, or events push back, does he adjust openly — or deny and double down?

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • He takes electoral defeats without contesting them. His party's candidate Dragan Primorac lost the presidential runoff of 12 January 2025 to Zoran Milanović by 74.68% to 25.32% — the heaviest defeat an HDZ-backed candidate has taken. Plenković's response is on the record: "In the presidential ballot they gave their vote to others. We respect their will. The HDZ has a mandate for 3.5 years [and will] continue to work committedly and responsibly" (statement, 12–13 January 2025). The result was seated without dispute, and the cohabitation continues.
  • On the lex AP he adjusted the text under criticism: after the 2023 draft drew objections from journalists' associations and international stakeholders, the government revised the amendments in early 2024 to expressly exclude journalists and disclosures predominantly in the public interest, and elaborated DORH's public-interest communication duties in line with Council of Europe recommendations. Plenković insisted the changes were "not a result of anyone's pressure" but of "listening to experts" — the adjustment is documented either way; the denial that pressure caused it is also documented (Government of Croatia, 31 January 2024).
  • A flagship fiscal measure was withdrawn when the public pushed back: the property tax announced in 2024 as the centerpiece of a tax reform was dropped in September 2024 after sustained public and coalition resistance, rather than forced through.
  • His government engaged the EU's excessive-deficit and rule-of-law procedures procedurally and delivered the era's defining files through negotiation rather than defiance: euro and Schengen entry (1 January 2023) required meeting criteria set by others, and the OECD accession process — with its anti-bribery and governance conditions — has been pursued on the OECD's terms, with membership expected in 2026.

Evidence against:

  • On Turudić he doubled down completely: presented with the SOA documents by the President before the vote, he requested no new background check, proceeded with the appointment, and treated the documented contacts as a political attack (President's office statements, January–February 2024).
  • On border pushbacks (Test 4) the government's position across his entire tenure has been denial: the Interior Ministry states it has "no information" about documented incidents and that police act lawfully, a position maintained after the ECtHR's 2021 judgment and after Human Rights Watch's November 2026 report alike (Interior Ministry responses, 2025–2026).
  • On the EPPO he answered a competence question with a political attack on the prosecutor personally (Test 1), and his appointee then removed the Beroš file from the EPPO rather than the two offices proceeding in the open.
  • On the October 2026 Gospić hazardous-waste scandal — some 35,000 tonnes of hazardous waste illegally dumped at sites including Gospić, per Europol, with about 20 arrests and tens of thousands protesting in Zagreb in September 2026 — his majority defeated the no-confidence motion (21 October 2026, 79–55) and also voted down the opposition's demand that he report when he first learned of the dumping and why the chief state inspector, himself arrested in a separate bribery investigation in November 2025, had been dismissed. The ALLEGATION aired in debate that he heads a "waste mafia" is an opposition characterization, not a finding; the documented facts are the dumping, the arrests, the protests, and the refusal of the accounting.
  • In the Jankomir psychiatric clinic case — a 32-year-old patient died on 21 October 2026; four medical technicians were arrested on 7 November on suspicion of aggravated murder — Plenković rejected calls for Health Minister Irena Hrstić to resign on the ground that Sveti Ivan is a city hospital (statement, 14 November 2026). The criminal suspicions are ALLEGATIONs in process; the ministerial-responsibility question is the wobble item, and his answer was deflection to the city's ownership.

Test 4 — COUNTS EVERYONE

Does the accounting include Serb minority citizens, migrants on the Balkan route, the poor, his opponents' voters — or are some counted out?

GRADE: FAILS

Evidence for:

  • Minority representation in the Sabor itself is untouched: the eight reserved minority seats, including the SDSS's three, stand; minority MPs other than the SDSS support his government, and the Serb minority's reserved representation was not abolished despite his coalition partner's program questioning the minority representation system.
  • The Serb minority newspaper Novosti, whose state funding his coalition partner DP demanded be abolished, continued to receive its allocation (recorded at €400,000 for 2026 in media monitoring) — the demand was not enacted.
  • His governments' material record reaches the whole population in form: pensions and the minimum wage were raised repeatedly across his tenure, and euro adoption was executed with price-conversion protections; the economic growth he cites (GDP nearly doubled since 2016 on his figures; unemployment below 5%) is population-wide in its accounting.

Evidence against:

  • Migrants on the Balkan route are counted out, on a judicial record. In M.H. and Others v. Croatia (judgment of 18 November 2021), the European Court of Human Rights found violations of Articles 2 (ineffective investigation into the death of six-year-old Madina Hussiny, killed by a train after her family was, on their account, ordered back to Serbia along the tracks), 3 (ill-treatment of the detained children), 5, 34, and Article 4 of Protocol No. 4 (collective expulsion) — a judgment, not an allegation. The documentation has continued for the whole of his tenure: the Danish Refugee Council's Border Violence Monitoring Network, the Council of Europe's Human Rights Commissioner (from 2018), and Human Rights Watch (2023 report "Like We Were Just Animals"; 24 November 2026 report "Doing Europe's Dirty Work," based on 80+ interviews including unaccompanied children, alleging beatings, confiscation of money and destruction of phones) record a continuing practice. The government's position is denial: the Interior Ministry states police do not carry out pushbacks, that people have access to asylum procedures, and that it has "no information" on specific documented incidents (Ministry responses, including November 2026). An EU-funded border monitoring mechanism exists; HRW and the Ombudswoman's office record that it has not curbed the practice and that the independent Ombuds office's oversight role was narrowed in its favor.
  • The Serb minority was removed from government by his choice of coalition. After the 17 April 2024 election, Plenković formed his third government with the Homeland Movement, which had made the exclusion of the Independent Democratic Serb Party (SDSS) a condition — ending some twenty years of Serb minority participation in Croatian governments, in which the SDSS had been his own coalition partner through his first two terms. DP's Ivan Penava and Stjepo Bartulica stated the point publicly ("Pupovac will no longer shape politics in Croatia"). Plenković's stated rationale was arithmetic and stability — the HDZ's 61 seats required a partner — and he retained other minority MPs' support; the documented fact is that the largest minority's party was traded out at the demand of a partner whose program questions minority representation itself, and Plenković accepted the term (election and government-formation record, April–May 2024).
  • The 2025–2026 record adds atmosphere that his government tolerated in its own ranks: Ustaša-era salutes at the July 2025 Thompson concert in Zagreb were met without sanction from the government, and in February 2026 a video of DP deputy prime minister Josip Dabro — by then out of the cabinet but still a DP MP — singing a song glorifying Ante Pavelić strained the coalition; Plenković held the coalition together and ruled out early elections, prioritizing stability (coalition reporting, February 2026).

Test 5 — KEEPS HIS WORD OVER TIME

Stated promises and positions vs. the record since 2016, with dates.

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for (promises kept or credibly in motion):

  • Euro and Schengen — kept, on the date. Croatia adopted the euro and entered the Schengen Area on 1 January 2023, the first country to do both on the same day — the central strategic promise of his second term, delivered on schedule (European Commission and Government of Croatia records, 1 January 2023).
  • OECD — in motion as stated. He named OECD membership "the final step for rounding off Croatia's international position" (30 October 2023); the accession process, opened in 2022, advanced through both OECD Economic Surveys, with the Secretary-General stating in January 2026 that membership is expected in 2026.
  • The economic headline numbers moved as promised in direction: Croatia's credit rating rose into the A category across the agencies in 2024; on the government's figures GDP has nearly doubled since 2016 and unemployment stands below 5% (Government of Croatia statements, 2026).
  • Three election victories on the same platform: he sought and won renewed mandates in 2020 and 2024 on continuity of the same program, and the 2024 government's formation promise — an HDZ-led government without the SDP — was kept in form.

Evidence against (promises not kept, or receding):

  • The anti-corruption promise is the broken centerpiece. He took over the HDZ in 2016 promising to modernize the party and govern cleanly after the Sanader era. A decade on, the documented record is some 30 ministerial departures amid scandals in his first two terms (opposition count; his own November 2026 count concedes four law-related exits and disputes the rest), two ministers arrested in office, a former minister imprisoned on an EPPO guilty plea, a contested attorney-general appointment, and a public jurisdictional war with the European prosecutor. Whatever the correct count is, it is not the record the 2016 promise described — and his November 2026 formulation ("name ten and I resign") grades the promise by a threshold he set himself, a decade later.
  • The property tax was announced in 2024 as a reform he championed, then withdrawn within weeks under pressure (Test 3) — a promise unkept, if openly.
  • The long arc, factually: the diplomat who rebranded the HDZ toward the European center and delivered euro, Schengen and near-OECD membership governs his third term in coalition with a nationalist party his first two terms kept outside, having traded the Serb minority's twenty-year seat in government for the arithmetic; the prosecutor's office and the public broadcaster stand under documented independence questions that the European Commission restates annually; and the border is held by a practice a European court has judged and his government denies exists. His word on Croatia's European position was kept almost to the letter. His word on clean government was not.

Overall Summary

One law: MIXED · Without gripping: MIXED · The wobble: MIXED · Counts everyone: FAILS · His word: MIXED.

Andrej Plenković is the most durable ruler in the European Union, and the record shows both halves of how. The law genuinely bites inside his own house — ministers arrested in office, dismissed the same day, prosecuted, and in Žalac's case imprisoned — and the strategic promises that defined his project, euro and Schengen above all, were kept on the day named. But the grip shows at the two joints that matter most: the prosecution service, refitted in 2024 with a chief whose contacts with suspects were documented before his appointment and who then took the biggest corruption file away from the European prosecutor his prime minister had publicly attacked; and a leaks law, named for his initials by its critics, that chills the channel by which his governments' scandals have repeatedly surfaced. The count fails at the border, where a European court's judgment and eight years of documentation meet a standing official denial, and in the coalition arithmetic that traded the Serb minority out of government after twenty years. He adjusts when voters beat him — the 2025 presidential landslide was accepted in a sentence — and doubles down when institutions check him. The record is correctable: the CJEU's answer on the EPPO question, the Beroš trial, and the OECD's verdict are all still to come.
