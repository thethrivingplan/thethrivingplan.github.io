The Ruler's Test — Ulf Kristersson, Prime Minister of Sweden

Subject: Ulf Kristersson — Prime Minister of Sweden since 18 October 2022 (Moderate Party); a minority cabinet of Moderates, Christian Democrats and Liberals resting on the Sweden Democrats (SD) under the Tidö Agreement of 14 October 2022. His bloc lost the general election of 13 September 2026 (red-green parties 176 seats, Tidö parties 173); he resigned on 17 September 2026 and leads a caretaker government while formation talks continue. Two prime-ministerial votes have failed (Magdalena Andersson, 15 October 2026; Elisabeth Thand Ringqvist, 3 November 2026), SD has withdrawn its demand for cabinet seats and offered to tolerate him, and a Riksdag vote on Kristersson returning as prime minister was scheduled for 30 November 2026. This dossier grades the completed 2022–2026 term. Graded: November 2026, on the documented public record. Method: Each test is graded only on the evidence listed under it. Both shelves are shown — the strongest evidence that he passes, then the strongest evidence that he fails. Allegations are labeled ALLEGATION and never stated as verdicts. Contested items are named as contested, with who disputes them. Seat-holders and public figures are named; people below the public eye appear by role only, unless the documented record itself establishes their wrongdoing. Grades: HOLDS / FAILS / MIXED / NOT ENOUGH EVIDENCE. This record is correctable: new evidence changes grades, in the open.

Test 1 — GIVES BY ONE LAW

Same rules for allies and opponents, insiders and outsiders — no favorites at the source.

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • The law reached his own closest appointment, through independent institutions, to a conviction. Henrik Landerholm — his childhood friend, appointed Sweden's first National Security Adviser in November 2022 — was investigated by the Security Service, prosecuted, acquitted at district court (5 September 2025), and then convicted of gross negligence by the Svea Court of Appeal on 19 October 2026 (80 day-fines, 52,000 SEK) for leaving four classified documents at a conference centre. No intervention in that process by Kristersson is documented; he stated publicly that "the legal process will have its course" (court records; Government Offices statements, January 2025 – October 2026).
  • Enforcement agencies operated without documented political direction throughout the term: Sweden's constitution bars ministers from directing agencies in individual cases (the prohibition on ministerstyre), and no case is documented of a prosecution, tax decision, or police operation against an opponent attributed to government instruction. Opposition parties governed municipalities, won court cases against the state, and ultimately won the 2026 election under rules administered by independent authorities (Election Authority final results, 19 September 2026).
  • When his second National Security Adviser appointment collapsed — Tobias Thyberg resigned within hours of his appointment on 8 May 2025 after media questions about years-old dating-app photographs he had not disclosed in vetting — the exit was immediate and the failure was acknowledged as a vetting failure rather than defended (contemporaneous reporting, May 2025).

Evidence against:

  • The Landerholm file is, at its front end, a favorites file. Kristersson created the National Security Adviser post and filled it with a childhood friend without open competition; security experts criticized the recruitment at the time as vänskapskorruption ("friendship corruption") — a characterization by critics, labeled here as such, which Kristersson disputed, noting Landerholm's prior senior appointments under governments of both blocs (DN reporting, November 2022; expert commentary, 2022).
  • The internal handling shielded the insider before the press exposed him. Landerholm's documented security incidents — a phone left at the Hungarian embassy (December 2022), a notebook left at Swedish Radio (January 2023), and four classified documents left in a conference-centre locker (spring 2023) — were assessed internally by the Government Offices' security department, which concluded no further measures were required and did not report the incidents to the police. Only after DN's revelations in December 2024 did a criminal investigation open; Kristersson defended Landerholm publicly ("I feel safe with this") and Landerholm resigned on 27 January 2025, after the investigation began — not when the incidents occurred (DN, December 2024 – January 2025; Riksdag debate reporting, 2025). All opposition parties reported Kristersson to the Constitutional Committee over the recruitment and the handling.
  • The pattern is appointment-level rather than case-level, and it is the only documented one of its kind in his term — which is why this test grades MIXED and not FAILS: the favoritism is real, and so is the fact that the system he did not control prosecuted his friend anyway.

Test 2 — HOLDS WITHOUT GRIPPING

Do courts, public-service media, the police, municipalities, and the support party keep their autonomy — or is independence punished and control centralized?

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • The courts checked the state repeatedly and were obeyed. Administrative courts overturned police refusals of Koran-burning demonstration permits in April 2023, holding the security ground insufficient; the Supreme Court acquitted 11 climate activists charged with sabotage in July 2025; no judge was removed, transferred, or publicly threatened by the government, and judicial appointments remained on the ordinary merit track (court records, 2023–2025).
  • Public-service media (SVT, SR, UR) kept full editorial independence: no documented instance exists of the government directing, pre-approving, or punishing specific content during the term, and both broadcasters covered the government critically throughout, including the Landerholm affair and the 2026 election the government lost.
  • The police remained operationally independent, and the ultimate check functioned: the Riksdag's arithmetic constrained him throughout (see Test 3), and in September 2026 the electorate removed his majority and he left office through the ordinary process (see Test 3).
  • Municipal self-government was not interfered with politically: opposition-run cities (including Stockholm under Social Democrat leadership for most of the term) governed without central override of their decisions.

Evidence against:

  • The Tidö geometry is a structural grip of a specific kind: power without office. Under the agreement's own text, SD — which in 2022 won more seats (73) than Kristersson's Moderates (68) — received "the same influence on issues in the cooperation projects as the parties in the government," plus a coordination office with its own officials inside the Government Offices, while holding no ministerial responsibility for the outcomes (Tidö Agreement, 14 October 2022). The largest party of the governing bloc thus co-authored policy in migration, crime, energy and welfare while remaining formally in opposition — influence centralized in a party leadership that no voter could remove from a government it was not in.
  • Public-service media was put on a tightening financial leash at SD's insistence. The parliamentary committee for the 2026–2033 permit period split without the cross-bloc consensus that had held since the 1920s; the government proceeded with the majority's funding path (+3% in 2026, +2% annually 2027–2030, +1% 2031–2033) over the broadcasters' documented warnings — SR's CEO calculated a shortfall above 3 billion SEK across the period, and SVT entered a savings program of 355 million SEK eliminating 141 positions — together with new impartiality-scrutiny and text-reporting requirements that SD had demanded and the broadcasters and the State Treasury warned could affect independence (public service committee report, May 2024; government proposal, 2025; broadcaster consultation responses, 2024–2025). SD stated it would have preferred still less money. No editorial interference is documented; the pressure is budgetary and procedural, and it is documented as such.
  • The government repeatedly legislated over the objections of its own legal vetting body, the Council on Legislation (Lagrådet) — a pattern the Swedish Institute for Human Rights and legal commentators describe as a departure from Swedish legislative tradition: the Council's recommendation that the 2024 security-zones law be time-limited was declined; its assessment that anonymous witnesses (in force 1 January 2025) could not be assumed to work was set aside; its rejection of lowering the age of criminal responsibility to 13 was followed first by the government pressing the proposal anyway, and only later by retreat when the Riksdag majority failed (see Test 3) (Council on Legislation opinions, 2024–2026; SIHR consultation responses, 2024–2026).
  • Centralization over municipalities appears once, in the welfare reform: the benefit cap decided 26 May 2026 removes municipalities' ability to grant income support above the national standard except within narrow limits — a deliberate transfer of discretion from elected local councils to a national rule (Riksdag decision, 26 May 2026; in force 1 January 2027).
  • A constitutional amendment to permit revocation of citizenship for dual nationals convicted of crimes seriously harming Sweden's vital interests was submitted to the Riksdag in December 2025; as a constitutional change it requires a second vote after an election and had not taken effect at grading (government submission, 5 December 2025).

Test 3 — ANSWERS THE WOBBLE

When courts, voters, expert bodies, allies, or events push back, does he adjust openly — or deny and double down?

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • The largest wobble of the term was answered in the textbook way. When the final count confirmed his bloc's defeat on 13–16 September 2026 (176–173; turnout 84.9%), Kristersson conceded the result, resigned on 17 September, and has since led a caretaker government within its limits — presenting the 2027 budget bill on 12 November 2026 while the Speaker's process runs, and accepting a formation mandate himself only when the Speaker issued it after two other candidates failed (SVT/Election Authority results; Speaker's statements, September–November 2026; Reuters, 24 November 2026).
  • The Örebro massacre was answered within days and across the aisle. After a gunman killed 10 people at the Campus Risbergska adult-education centre on 4 February 2025 — the worst mass shooting in Swedish history, in Kristersson's own words — the government fast-tracked gun-law tightening (stronger licence vetting, restricted access to AR-15-type semi-automatics) with Social Democrat support, absorbing public criticism from individual SD figures rather than dropping the measure (government statements, 7 February 2025).
  • On the age of criminal responsibility, pushback worked — late, but visibly. The government proposed 13 for the most serious crimes; the Council on Legislation and a large majority of consultation bodies said no, the parliamentary majority was not there, and in June 2026 the government withdrew 13 and returned with 14, which passed in August 2026 as a temporary five-year law with Social Democrat support (Justice Ministry statements, June 2026; Riksdag decision, August 2026).
  • On the reporting duty for undocumented migrants, professional pushback narrowed the instrument: as first envisaged in the Tidö process the duty could have covered welfare-state front lines; the inquiry and the final law exempted healthcare, schools and social services, confining the duty to six national authorities (law adopted 15 June 2026, in force 13 July 2026 — see Test 4 for the counts assessment of what remained).
  • On climate instruments, one partial adjustment is documented: the biofuel reduction obligation, cut from 30.5% to 6% on 1 January 2024, was raised to 10% from 1 July 2025 in response to Sweden's EU emissions obligations (government decision, 2025).

Evidence against:

  • The Climate Policy Council — the government's own independent expert body — delivered the same verdict three years running and was answered with rejection, not adjustment. Its March 2024 report found that policy adopted in 2023 would increase emissions and that the government's climate action plan did not meet the Climate Act's requirements; its 2025 report recorded that emissions did in fact rise in 2024, driven by the fuel-tax and reduction-obligation cuts; its March 2026 report concluded that the term's policy was insufficient and that neither the national 2030 and 2040 targets nor the EU's 2030 requirements would be met under current policy. The government's response in 2026, as in prior years, was to reject the assessment and restate its nuclear-and-electrification strategy (Climate Policy Council reports, 2024, 2025, 2026; TT reporting of the government's response, March 2026).
  • UN pushback on the security zones was answered with restatement. On 5 December 2025 the UN Committee on the Elimination of Racial Discrimination criticized the suspicion-less search zones over documented racial-profiling concerns and called for review; the Justice Minister's office replied that the government considered the legislation compatible with fundamental rights and conventions, and no review was announced (CERD concluding observations, 5 December 2025; government response reported December 2025).
  • The Koran-burning crisis of 2023 was answered by process rather than by either pole: Kristersson condemned the burnings as "deeply disrespectful" while defending the constitutional free-speech framework, refused Turkey's demands that Sweden could not constitutionally meet (extraditions being for the courts), and appointed an inquiry into whether national security could ground permit refusals (August 2023). The inquiry's July 2024 proposal drew heavyweight criticism — the Chancellor of Justice called it a far-reaching infringement departing from the ban on prior censorship, and Säpo, the Police Authority and the Parliamentary Ombudsman were also critical — and no such law had been enacted when the term ended (government press release, 21 August 2023; SOU 2024:52; consultation responses, autumn 2024). Whether this is adjustment or deferral is CONTESTED between the government, which presents the inquiry route as the rule-of-law answer, and critics on both sides who wanted either a ban or a clear refusal.
  • Landerholm was defended until the criminal process, not the facts, forced the exit (Test 1): the wobble — DN's documented revelations — was initially absorbed by the internal assessment that had already cleared him.

Test 4 — COUNTS EVERYONE

Does the accounting include immigrants and their children, gang-affected suburbs, the Sámi, welfare recipients, his opponents' voters — or are some counted out?

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • His opponents' voters were counted in full, in the only count that finally decides: the 2026 election was administered independently, the opposition's 176 seats were certified, and the government they defeated handed over power (Test 3). No franchise rule, district boundary, or election-administration change of his term is documented to have advantaged his bloc.
  • The residents of gang-affected suburbs — overwhelmingly the same immigrant-background communities his migration policy addresses — are the documented main beneficiaries of the term's central crime result: police-confirmed shootings fell from 391 in 2022 to 368 in 2023, 296 in 2024 and 158 in 2025, and to 61 in January–September 2026 against 136 a year earlier; Brå's confirmed lethal violence fell to 84 cases in 2025, the lowest since 2012 (Police Authority and Brå statistics, 2022–2026; Reuters, 31 March 2026). Detonations moved the other way before falling — 194 in 2025, the highest of the period, then 76 in January–September 2026 against 158 — and both directions are recorded here.
  • Ukrainians under temporary protection were held inside the count throughout: Sweden kept the protection regime in force for the tens of thousands who arrived from 2022, extended it in line with EU decisions, and exempted this group from the term's tightening measures (Migration Agency records, 2022–2026).
  • After Örebro — whose dead included Syrian, Bosnian, Eritrean and Iranian residents — the government treated the attack as a national tragedy without distinction: Kristersson visited Örebro, a national minute of silence was held, and the legislative response addressed the shooter's licensing, not the victims' origins (government statements, February 2025).

Evidence against:

  • Asylum seekers and protection holders were counted down by design, and the design reached people already inside. Asylum applications fell ~60% from 2022 to the lowest level since 1985 in 2025 (Migration Agency; Migration Minister Johan Forssell's statements); quota refugee resettlement was cut from 6,400 to 900 places a year under the Tidö Agreement; and in June 2026 parliament enacted, effective July 2026, the abolition of permanent residence for protection beneficiaries and the revocation or refusal of permits for conduct that is not criminal — unpaid debts and undeclared work are the documented examples (Civil Rights Defenders' analysis, August 2026; Riksdag decisions, June 2026). A person can thus live in Sweden for years under protection and remain, by law, permanently temporary and removable for debt.
  • The undocumented — estimated at 30,000–50,000 people — were counted as an enforcement target: the reporting law in force from 13 July 2026 obliges six national authorities (tax, employment, social insurance, pensions, enforcement, prison and probation) to pass information about persons who may lack residence rights to the police, on the threshold of "reason to assume"; the Swedish Red Cross documented that even the debate drove undocumented people away from services, and the law passed 174–172 (AP reporting, 15 June 2026; Red Cross statements, 2024–2026). Healthcare, schools and social services were exempted from the duty itself (Test 3), but the information chain from, e.g., a birth registration at a hospital to the tax authority to the police remains in the law's documented operation (Civil Rights Defenders, 2026).
  • Welfare recipients in large families were counted down in kronor: the May 2026 reform caps income support, limits support for larger households (with a restriction rule from the fourth child), and ends municipal top-ups; the responsible minister stated that nearly 150,000 households would receive lower support. Save the Children assessed the cap as violating the Convention on the Rights of the Child — an advocacy assessment, recorded as such; the government's stated purpose is work incentives and breaking exclusion (Riksdag decision, 26 May 2026; Minister Anna Tenje's statements; Save the Children response, 2026). The companion rule that income support goes only to persons with legal residence rights takes effect with the same package.
  • The Sámi were consulted but not consented. The 2022 Consultation Act requires consultation, not agreement; in December 2025 the UN CERD called on Sweden to review the Act so that consent is actively sought and found Sámi rights inadequately protected. On the ground, the state's own mining company LKAB announced the Per Geijer rare-earth deposit in January 2023 alongside the Minister for Enterprise without prior dialogue with the Gabna herding community whose last migration route the mine would sever, and later obtained EU strategic-project status for it — a process a 2026 peer-reviewed study (Ambio) found failed free, prior and informed consent standards; the community's account and LKAB's negotiated-agreement account are CONTESTED between them. The government also disbanded the long-running Reindeer Grazing Land Committee and replaced it with a new inquiry, against Sámi representatives' criticism, and Sweden remains — as under all his predecessors, a continuity recorded rather than a new act — a non-party to ILO Convention 169 (CERD, December 2025; Ambio/SEI study, 2026; Riksdag committee record, February 2026).
  • Children in gang areas stand on both sides of his ledger: they are the intended beneficiaries of the halved shootings, and they are the subjects of suspicion-less searches in security zones (in force April 2024; CERD's profiling criticism, Test 3), of covert surveillance permitted against under-15s from October 2025, and of the lowered age of criminal responsibility (14, temporary, from 2026; the original 13 proposal, Test 3). The counts question is not whether these children were considered — they were the stated reason for the program — but whether being counted as a risk is the same as being counted; the dossier records the instruments and lets the grade carry the tension.

Test 5 — KEEPS HIS WORD OVER TIME

Stated promises and positions vs. the record since 2022 — and the one pledge from 2018 that frames the term.

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • The Tidö program — the word he actually gave the electorate in 2022 — was substantially executed. The migration "paradigm shift" was delivered in numbers (Test 4: asylum at a 40-year low, returns up ~60% since 2022, 8,312 returns in 2025; a voluntary-return grant raised to up to 350,000 SEK from 2026). The crime program was legislated at scale: security zones (2024), anonymous witnesses (2025), preventive covert surveillance (2023), doubled penalties for gang crime, security custody for dangerous repeat offenders (2026), a widened law on military support to the police (from 1 January 2027), and the criminal-age reduction (Test 3) — and the outcome measure he pointed to, shootings, halved (Test 4). Working-age and pensioner tax cuts were delivered in successive budgets, and the earned-income credit steps were enacted as announced (budgets 2023–2026).
  • NATO was completed on his watch and on his word: he pledged to finish the accession the previous government had begun, absorbed the Turkish and Hungarian conditions that were constitutionally payable (a tougher anti-terrorism law in force 1 June 2023; a counter-terrorism roadmap), refused those that were not (court-decided extraditions), and Sweden became NATO's 32nd member on 7 March 2024. Defence spending rose above the 2% benchmark with cross-party backing, and the multi-year military support framework for Ukraine (75 billion SEK for 2024–2026) was adopted and disbursed (NATO accession record, 7 March 2024; government announcements, 2023–2026).
  • Nuclear power moved from pledge to machinery: a state financing framework for new nuclear was legislated, and Vattenfall advanced its Ringhals project with SMR vendors selected — no reactor was ordered or built within the term, and the dossier records the promise as in motion, not completed (government and Vattenfall announcements, 2025).

Evidence against:

  • The framing pledge of his career was reversed in stages, each stage documented. In 2018, by Holocaust survivor Hédi Fried's account — given in a published meeting and never disputed as to the meeting itself — Kristersson promised her that his party would never cooperate with SD; his later explanation is that he meant the Alliance would not form a government with SD, not that the Moderates would never negotiate with them, and that distinction is recorded as his position, with Fried's account beside it. In December 2019 he held his first formal meeting with Jimmie Åkesson; in 2021 M entered budget cooperation with SD; in 2022 he took office dependent on SD's votes and SD's co-authored program; and in the 2026 campaign he stated that a re-elected right government would include SD ministers — the step the 2022 arrangement had stopped short of (Fried's account, 2018, and its 2022 republication; Kristersson's explanations, 2019–2022; Reuters reporting, 11 September 2026). The 2018 promise, on any reading broader than his narrowest one, was not kept; on his reading it was kept to the letter while its substance inverted. Both readings are on the record; the grade uses the documented sequence.
  • The climate word was not kept on the expert record. His governments affirmed Sweden's climate targets, including net zero by 2045 and the 2030 interim targets; the Climate Policy Council found in three consecutive reports that his policies moved Sweden away from the 2030 targets, with emissions rising in 2024 (Test 3). A government may dispute a council; it cannot both keep the target as its stated goal and be found by its own reviewers, unrefuted on the numbers, to have governed away from it for a full term.
  • Pump prices: the 2022 right's campaign featured prominent pledges by his coalition partners of diesel and petrol several kronor per litre cheaper; the delivered instrument — the reduction obligation cut to 6% — produced a smaller reduction at the pump, and how much smaller is CONTESTED between the parties' figures and independent price comparisons. The dossier records the gap between the campaign's rhetoric and the instrument without adopting either side's arithmetic.
  • The 2026 word to the voters was tested immediately: he campaigned as the right's prime-ministerial candidate on the Tidö record, lost the bloc majority by three seats, and — consistently with his word — resigned rather than contest the arithmetic (Test 3). The subsequent attempt to return as prime minister of a different, Centre-including government is ordinary parliamentary practice under negative parliamentarism, recorded here because it followed a resignation his own statements framed as accepting the voters' verdict; the 30 November 2026 vote will grade that chapter, and it postdates this dossier.

Overall Summary

One law: MIXED · Without gripping: MIXED · The wobble: MIXED · Counts everyone: MIXED · His word: MIXED.

Kristersson's is the low-grip file of the series: no court was packed, no editor was jailed, no result was disputed, and when the voters withdrew his majority he resigned within four days and governed as a caretaker inside the rules while his opponents tried and failed to replace him. The favoritism in his file is real but narrow — a friend appointed, shielded by an internal assessment, and then prosecuted to conviction by institutions his government did not control. What keeps every grade at MIXED is the program the grip was traded for: a support party with co-governing influence and no office; public-service media on a tightening leash; legislation repeatedly carried over the legal council's warnings; an asylum and welfare settlement that counts the undocumented, the protected, and the large poor family down by law; a Sámi consent that is consulted and not sought; and a climate target affirmed in words and missed, on his own council's numbers, in fact. Above all of it stands the 2018 promise and its eight-year inversion — the word against which the word-keeping of the whole term is measured. He governed, by the documented record, as a rule-follower with a program that narrowed who the rules are generous to; the tests grade both halves of that sentence.
