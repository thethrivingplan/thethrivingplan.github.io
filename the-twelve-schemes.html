Two maps of the same sky

Here's the twist the videos build on — and it's a real one. There are two zodiacs, and they no longer line up.

SignMantraSeason Clock datesScheme

Why the gap? Earth's axis wobbles like a top — one slow circle every ~26,000 years, called precession. Since the season-anchored zodiac was fixed about two thousand years ago, the sky has drifted roughly one whole sign against the calendar. So the Season Clock and the Star Map now disagree by about 24 days. Neither is “wrong” — they're two different anchors: one pinned to the equinox, one pinned to the stars themselves. It's the same lesson as the spectrum: the map you're handed and the thing as it actually sits are two different drawings, and it pays to know which one you're reading.

A worked example: a December 28 birthday reads Capricorn on the Season Clock — but Sagittarius on the Star Map. Same person, same sky, two maps.
