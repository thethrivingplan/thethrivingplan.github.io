Where you live — your districts (optional: only if you know them)

Your ballot depends on where you live

Everyone in Arlington votes on the same statewide and county races. A few races depend on your exact address — like which U.S. House district you live in. If you know yours, pick it below. If you don't, just leave “Not sure” — the game will show you every race that could be on an Arlington ballot, and nothing will be wrong. (Most of Arlington is in TX-6; parts are in TX-30. Your official sample ballot, from Tarrant County Elections, shows your exact races.)

District lines are drawn by the state legislature after each census. Where the line falls decides which of these races are on your ballot at all. One street over can be a different ballot entirely — a different set of names you're even allowed to choose between.

Key dates: early voting runs October 19–30, 2026. Election Day is Tuesday, November 3, 2026, 7 a.m.–7 p.m. (The deadline to register was October 5, 2026.) There are no ballot propositions this time — every contest is a person, including three seats on the Tarrant Appraisal District board, the board that decides how homes are valued for taxes. They're in the game too.

How this works — the short version

  • One question at a time. 12 issues, in plain words, with plain answers: Yes, No, Unsure, Skip, or “this is a non-issue for me.”
  • No names and no parties while you answer. You just say what you believe. No candidate's name appears anywhere on this page until your results — so there's nothing to study and no way to pick a person even if you wanted to.
  • Then the names appear. Your answers are compared with what each candidate has actually done, and the game shows you who your answers point to, race by race.
  • Done counts more than said. A match with a real vote or a signed law counts double. A match with something a candidate only promised counts once, and is labeled as a promise. Anything that couldn't be checked counts for nothing.
  • Don't know? Don't care? Say so — right under the question, before the choices. Unsure means “I don't know about this yet” — it and Skip simply don't count. “Non-issue” means “it doesn't matter to me” — it leaves that question out of your results completely.
  • None of these says what I believe. If your stand isn't among a question's choices, pick that instead. It counts as your answer — it shows in your pattern as your stand. Picking it also opens a box where you can say what you believe in your own words, if you want — your words travel with that answer, in your recap, your pattern, and any share that shows that stand. And you can match those words yourself: this page cannot read your words, so it never matches them for you — but right under the box, one optional step asks “does any of these stands say what you wrote?” If you pick one — a choice from the question, or a finer stand on the same issue — that stand becomes your answer everywhere matching happens, labeled as matched by you from your own words, and your words still print in full, exactly as you wrote them. Pick “None of these comes close,” or skip the step, and your words stay unmatched: answered, and counted as not matched for everyone.
  • A pointer, not an order. The game shows you who your beliefs line up with. Who you actually vote for is always your call.
  • Private by default. Nothing is sent anywhere, and nothing is saved on any server.   • Plain on purpose. This page is deliberately plain to avoid all subliminal messaging. During play there are no party labels, no party colors, and no color-coding of candidates.
