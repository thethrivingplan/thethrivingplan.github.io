The Ruler's Test — Teodoro Obiang Nguema Mbasogo, President of Equatorial Guinea

Subject: Teodoro Obiang Nguema Mbasogo — in power since 3 August 1979, President of Equatorial Guinea since 12 October 1982. The premise of the brief is verified, with one correction of form stated plainly: Obiang seized power on 3 August 1979 in a military coup against his uncle, President Francisco Macías Nguema (who was tried and executed weeks later), and ruled first through a Supreme Military Council; the formal title of president dates from 12 October 1982. He still holds the seat in November 2026 — there has been no succession. He was acting publicly as president within days of this grading: addressing the Third Russia–Africa Summit in Moscow on 29 October 2026, and meeting French President Emmanuel Macron in Paris on 11 November 2026, where he asked Macron to intervene personally in the case of Equatorial Guinea's confiscated Paris diplomatic property (Vice-Presidency of Equatorial Guinea official site, 29 October 2026; TVGE newscast and government reporting via AFREEQUE, 11–12 November 2026). Born 5 June 1942, he is 84. His current mandate is his sixth term, from the election of 20 November 2022, in which the National Electoral Commission proclaimed him re-elected with 94.9% of the vote on an official turnout of 98%, for a seven-year term running to 2029 (electoral commission announcement via AFP, 26 November 2022). Freedom House's 2023 country report separately described the same contest as "ostensibly winning 97 percent of the vote" — the two figures are printed here as separate sourced figures, never blended, and both sources agree the contest was not free or fair (Freedom House, Freedom in the World 2023). His ruling Democratic Party of Equatorial Guinea (PDGE) and its allies took all 100 seats in the Chamber of Deputies, all 55 elected Senate seats, and all 588 municipal council positions in the same elections (Freedom House, 2023). The succession positioning named in the brief is also verified: his eldest son, Teodoro Nguema Obiang Mangue ("Teodorín"), has been Vice President since 22 June 2016, appointed by his father, with responsibility for national defence and security; under the constitution the president appoints and dismisses the vice president, who must belong to the president's party, and if the president dies, resigns, or can no longer serve, the vice president serves the rest of the term with no election (constitutional provisions as reported by AFP and AP, 2016–2026; BTI 2026 country report). The Bertelsmann Foundation's BTI 2026 report states that Obiang is believed to no longer manage national politics fully, due to age and declining health, with effective power exercised by the ruling family — a characterization recorded here as that report's assessment, not as a medical finding. Because the seat has not changed hands, this dossier grades Obiang's whole tenure since 1979, weighted to recent years, on the completed record to November 2026; it is not a completed-tenure file, because the tenure is not complete. Graded: November 2026, on the documented public record. Method: The Ethic: "How you play the game matters. Play by the rules. Stay true to your word. And if another option becomes available, at least consider it." Each test is graded only on the evidence listed under it. Both shelves are shown — the strongest evidence that he passes, then the strongest evidence that he fails. Allegations are labeled ALLEGATION and never stated as verdicts. Contested items are named as contested, with who disputes them. Conflicting counts are printed as separate sourced figures, never blended. Seat-holders and public figures are named; people below the public eye appear by role only, unless the documented record itself establishes their wrongdoing. The mandate rule applies: governing on the platform the voters elected is not inherently a failure and is never graded as one — the Test grades the holding, never the platform; marks are lost only for how it is done. In this case the rule's limit is also stated: the 2022 mandate itself was produced by an election no independent assessor in the record calls free or fair, so the platform's content is not graded, but neither can the mandate be cited as freely given. Grades: HOLDS / FAILS / MIXED / NOT ENOUGH EVIDENCE. This record is correctable: new evidence changes grades, in the open.

Test 1 — GIVES BY ONE LAW

Same rules for allies and opponents, insiders and outsiders — no favorites at the source.

GRADE: FAILS

Evidence for:

  • The law has, at least twice in recent years, reached inside the ruling family itself. On 26–27 August 2025 the Bioko Provincial Tribunal convicted Baltasar Ebang Engonga — the president's nephew, former head of the National Financial Investigation Agency (ANIF) — of embezzlement, sentencing him to eight years in prison and a fine of about USD 220,000, alongside five other officials found guilty of diverting public funds claimed as travel allowances (AFP via BBC and multiple outlets, 27–28 August 2025; Africa Yearbook 2025). Prosecutors had sought a combined eighteen years; the court convicted on embezzlement alone.
  • One universal reform was enacted without exception: on 19 September 2022 Obiang signed a new penal code abolishing the death penalty for ordinary crimes, welcomed the next day by the Acting UN High Commissioner for Human Rights; no death sentence had been carried out since January 2014 (OHCHR statement, 20 September 2022; Reuters, 20 September 2022). The limit is printed with it: according to Amnesty International, the Code of Military Justice retains certain death-penalty provisions (Amnesty International, via UPR stakeholder reporting, 2024).
  • The state's anti-corruption machinery exists on paper and has been used against mid-level officials beyond the Engonga case, and the June 2026 fall of his own appointed government was publicly justified in part on the ground that it had "fostered corruption" (PDGE statement via Reuters and AP reporting, 16 June 2026).

Evidence against:

  • The vice president — the president's son and designated successor — is a convicted embezzler under foreign law and has never faced a domestic process. On 28 July 2021 France's Cour de Cassation upheld Teodorín's convictions for embezzling and laundering public funds, cementing French control over about €150 million (USD 177 million) in assets; the United States settled its case in 2014 after he agreed to forfeit USD 30 million, and Swiss prosecutors closed their investigation in 2019 after he forfeited 25 cars auctioned for USD 27 million — a combined USD 237 million in recovered assets across the three countries (Human Rights Watch, 28 July 2021). The US Department of Justice had calculated that Teodorín spent USD 315 million worldwide between 2004 and 2011 on properties, cars, and luxury goods (HRW, 2017, citing DOJ filings). His father's response was not a domestic investigation but promotion: Teodorín remains vice president, in charge of defence and security, and in November 2026 the president personally asked the French president to help resolve the Paris property confiscated as a result of that conviction (AFREEQUE, 12 November 2026).
  • The one case that did reach a family member is contested as succession politics rather than law. Freedom House's 2025 report records that observers speculated the Engonga case "was a politically motivated bid to sideline a potential successor" to Obiang (Freedom House, Freedom in the World 2025). Engonga's arrest in October 2024 was followed, while he was in detention, by the leak of hundreds of sex tapes involving wives and relatives of officials — a public humiliation that reporting across outlets describes as seen to destroy any hope he had of the presidency (BBC via multiple outlets, August 2025). A law that lands on the rival and never on the heir is two laws.
  • The source itself is family property. The US State Department's human rights reporting states that since 1979 Obiang "has dominated all branches of government in collaboration with his clan and political party" (US Department of State, Country Reports on Human Rights Practices). Human Rights Watch's standing country summary: "Vast oil revenues fund lavish lifestyles for the small elite surrounding the president, while a large proportion of the population continues to live in poverty. Mismanagement of public funds and credible allegations of high-level corruption persist" (HRW country page, 2026). Forbes estimated Obiang's personal net worth at more than USD 600 million in 2011, a figure recorded here as an estimate, not an audited fact (Forbes, 2011, via SEC comment filing, 2020).
  • The outcome measures sit at the bottom of the world. Transparency International's 2024 Corruption Perceptions Index, published February 2025, ranked Equatorial Guinea 173rd of 180 countries; the 2025 index scored it 15 of 100, ranking 172nd of 182, tied with North Korea and Syria (Transparency International, CPI 2024 and CPI 2025, via Amnesty International and Africa Yearbook 2025). In 2004 a US Senate investigation had already found that Riggs Bank, which held the government's accounts, transferred millions of dollars to companies apparently owned by government officials, including the president (US Senate Permanent Subcommittee on Investigations, 2004, via HRW).

Why: The grade lands at FAILS because the exception is not incidental — it is the heir. A legal system that convicts the president's nephew while the president's son, convicted of embezzling and laundering public funds by France's highest court and stripped of assets on three continents, is promoted to vice president and defended by his father to the French president in person, is running one law for those out of favor and another for the line of succession. The death-penalty abolition is real and universal, and it is recorded on the for shelf; one universal reform in forty-seven years does not outweigh a source structured as family property.

Helped / Hurt ledger:

  • Helped: The public treasury's claim, vindicated abroad — USD 237 million in assets recovered by France, the US, and Switzerland for the benefit of Equatoguineans (HRW, 2021), though HRW records that as of 2021 neither the US nor Swiss proceeds had yet been returned to the public. Every person under sentence of death for ordinary crimes, helped by the 2022 abolition.
  • Hurt: The Equatoguinean public, whose oil wealth was converted into the assets foreign courts seized. Baltasar Ebang Engonga and the five officials convicted with him — hurt by a process whose documented context (a succession rivalry, a humiliating leak during his detention) leaves its even-handedness contested, as recorded above. Ordinary defendants without family protection, who face the courts described in Test 2 with no Paris or Washington to appeal to.

Test 2 — HOLDS WITHOUT GRIPPING

Do the courts, the electoral commission, the media, the prosecution, and the central bank keep their autonomy — or is independence punished and control centralized?

GRADE: FAILS

Evidence for:

  • The forms of independent institutions exist and occasionally move. Elections are held on a regular cycle; a bicameral legislature sits; the 2011 constitutional reform created the vice presidency and a two-term limit in text (Britannica, 2026). In February 2025, at the UN Human Rights Council, Equatorial Guinea formally accepted 163 of the 218 recommendations presented in its Universal Periodic Review, including pledges on arbitrary detention and judicial independence (Africa Yearbook 2025, Brill, 2026).
  • A limited number of independent media outlets were active and expressed a variety of views in 2024, "but not without restriction" (US Department of State, 2024 Human Rights Report on Equatorial Guinea). Two suspended human rights lawyers had their suspensions lifted in 2025 — Ángel Obama Obiang Eseng in January and Gemma Jones in July (Amnesty International, Equatorial Guinea 2025 report).
  • In April 2026 Pope Leo XIV was permitted to visit inmates at a prison in Bata and to speak publicly about justice — an outside voice heard inside the country's most criticized institution, with the justice minister answering campaigners' criticisms on the record, in denial (AP via Hamilton County Herald, 17 April 2026).

Evidence against:

  • The electoral referee is the ruling party. Equatorial Guinea has no independent electoral body: the head of the National Electoral Commission during the 2022 elections was also the country's interior minister and a member of the ruling PDGE (Freedom House, Freedom in the World 2023). The same man, Faustino Ndong Esono Eyang, proclaimed the 94.9% result in November 2022 and was appointed PDGE secretary-general in May 2025 (AFP, 2022; Africa Yearbook 2025).
  • The judiciary scores zero. Freedom House's 2025 report scores the question "Is there an independent judiciary?" at 0 of 4, and its overview states: "The judiciary is under presidential control, and security forces engage in torture and other violence with relative impunity" (Freedom House, Freedom in the World 2025). The US State Department's 2024 report records credible reports of torture, arbitrary arrest and detention, and transnational repression, and states that in most cases the government did not take steps to identify and punish officials who committed abuses (US Department of State, 2024 Human Rights Report).
  • Parliament is not a check; it is the party. After the 2022 elections the PDGE bloc held every seat in both chambers and every municipal council (Freedom House, 2023). When the entire government resigned on 16 June 2026, it did so because the president was dissatisfied — and the president then reappointed the same prime minister, Manuel Osa Nsue Nsua, announced 19 June 2026 (Reuters, 16 June 2026; Ecofin Agency, 22 June 2026). The referee who failed was re-hired by the same hand three days later; no institution between the president and the outcome moved at all.
  • The aggregate measures are near the floor of the world. Freedom House's Freedom in the World 2026 gives Equatorial Guinea 5 of 100 — 0 of 40 for political rights, 5 of 60 for civil liberties — status Not Free (Freedom House, 2026). The 2022 penal code created an offence of "abusive exercise of fundamental rights," under which activists have been charged (Amnesty International and CIVICUS Monitor reporting, 2024–2026).
  • Control has been extended, not loosened, in the most recent years: in February 2025 military and governmental sources confirmed some 200 Russian instructors had been deployed to take charge of the protection of the president and his family (Amnesty International, Equatorial Guinea 2025 report) — the president's personal security placed with a foreign force answerable to him alone. A nationwide video-surveillance agreement with Huawei was announced in 2025 (Africa Yearbook 2025).

Why: The grade lands at FAILS because every referee named in the test — courts, electoral commission, media, prosecution — is documented as held, not merely influenced: a judiciary scored 0 of 4 for independence, an electoral commission headed by the interior minister of the ruling party, a parliament with no opposition seat, and a presidency whose personal protection has been outsourced to foreign instructors. The for shelf is genuine but thin: forms, pledges accepted at the UN, and individual suspensions lifted. None of it is an institution that can say no to him and make it stand.

Helped / Hurt ledger:

  • Helped: The president, his family, and the PDGE, who face no institutional obstacle between decision and execution. Foreign partners who value a single address for decisions — oil operators who described the political environment in 2025 as "stable and secure" (Africa Yearbook 2025).
  • Hurt: Voters, whose count is run by the incumbent's party. Judges, lawyers, and journalists who practice under suspension and reprisal — Gemma Jones, suspended two years for a social-media post criticizing the judicial system before the suspension was revoked in July 2025 (Amnesty International, 2025; Freedom House, 2025). Detainees held in what the State Department calls harsh and life-threatening prison conditions, with no independent court to appeal to.

Test 3 — ANSWERS THE WOBBLE

When courts, voters, parliament, the street, foreign partners, or events push back, does he adjust openly — or deny and double down? This test also grades whether, if another option becomes available, the ruler at least considered it openly; considering it and declining with stated reasons is not against-evidence — refusing to look at it at all is.

GRADE: FAILS

Evidence for:

  • The longest territorial dispute in the country's history was answered with law, not force, and the answer was accepted through to implementation. Equatorial Guinea and Gabon jointly referred their dispute over the islands of Mbañé, Cocoteros, and Conga to the International Court of Justice; the Court ruled on 19 May 2025 (14–1 in the year's-end account of the president himself) that the 1974 Bata Convention was not a valid legal title and that sovereignty rested on the title Equatorial Guinea inherited from Spain in 1968 (ICJ, Judgment of 19 May 2025, Case No. 179). On 28 July 2026 the two governments signed a Joint Engagement Agreement at the African Union in Addis Ababa committing both to implement the judgment (African Union reporting via Africanews/AFP, July–August 2026). Obiang called the ruling the "best gift" of 2025 (Africa Yearbook 2025).
  • A failure was owned in public, in numbers. On 16 June 2026 the entire government resigned after the vice president announced it had "barely reached 10%" of its targets; the ruling party's statement added that the president had observed the government fostered corruption and failed to diversify the economy (Reuters, 16 June 2026; AP, 16 June 2026). The previous government had stepped down in August 2024 after presidential criticism of its handling of the economy, social cohesion, and corruption — the same open admission, twice.
  • A pardon answered a UN finding. On 4 June 2025 Obiang pardoned 476 prisoners on his 83rd birthday, including the 37 Annobón protesters detained since July 2024 and two South African citizens detained since February 2023 — in both cases after the UN Working Group on Arbitrary Detention had determined the detentions were arbitrary (Amnesty International, Equatorial Guinea 2025 report; AFP, 10 June 2025). In the run-up to the Pope's April 2026 visit, the government released nearly 100 people arrested in a 2022 crackdown on street violence, according to a local lawyer who requested anonymity (AP, April 2026).

Evidence against:

  • The street's clearest recent pushback was answered first with arrests and a blackout, and only a year later with grace. In July 2024 residents of Annobón protested damage they believed dynamite blasting for mining and infrastructure had caused to their houses, farmland, and ecosystem; authorities arrested 42 community members (UNPO account), of whom five elderly women were released after three months, leaving 37 held (UNPO, 2025). The UN Working Group on Arbitrary Detention found the arrests arbitrary under four of its categories (WGAD opinion, June 2025). The island's internet and telecommunications blackout, imposed 20 July 2024, remained in force well into its second year — banking and mobile transfers ceased entirely, and a population of about 5,000 communicated by expensive satellite calls or emigrated (Africa Yearbook 2025). The 2025 pardon freed the people; it was issued as a birthday act of presidential mercy, not as an admission that the arrests were wrong, and Amnesty records no compensation or investigation. The WGAD had asked for both.
  • Disaster was answered with a cause the evidence did not support. After the 7 March 2021 Bata explosions at the Nkoantoma military camp, Obiang stated the blasts were caused by negligently stored explosives ignited by neighbouring farmers' stubble-burning, and ordered an investigation by the "competent courts." The Associated Press, analyzing satellite imagery, found no signs of farming around the base; Human Rights Watch and EG Justice called for an independent international investigation, which was not invited (HRW and EG Justice, 10 March 2021; AP satellite analysis, 2021). The death toll is printed as separate sourced figures, never blended: government officials told HRW 98 people were killed and 615 injured; later consolidated reporting puts the toll at 107–108 dead and more than 600 injured. Residents interviewed by EG Justice said, based on bodies recovered, that the actual number was much higher — recorded here as their account, contested and unverified (HRW, March 2021). The government did declare Bata a catastrophe zone, set up an initial emergency fund of 10 billion XAF (about USD 20 million), declared three days of mourning, and appealed for international aid (government statements, 9 March 2021) — relief was organized; the independent accounting was refused.
  • Electoral pushback has been answered the same way for four decades: the result is proclaimed and the challengers are prosecuted or exiled. In September 2022, weeks before the election, police assaulted the headquarters of the banned Citizens for Innovation party, arresting as many as 275 people including its leader; one officer and at least four opposition activists died (Freedom House, 2023). The Convergence for Social Democracy's description of the 2022 vote as mass fraud changed nothing in the count (Lusa, via reporting, 2022).
  • If another option becomes available, the record shows it is not looked at. The succession option the constitution itself creates — a vice president who serves out a term and an election in 2029 — has been filled in advance by appointment of the president's son, removing the open question rather than considering it. The diversification option has been announced in successive plans (Horizon 2020, Agenda 2035, Horizon 2035, presented again in Moscow on 29 October 2026) while the structure it was meant to change stayed fixed (Test 5). No instance appears in the record assembled here of Obiang publicly weighing an option that would loosen his own hold and declining it with reasons; the options are not declined — they are pre-empted.

Why: The grade lands at FAILS because the pattern across forty-seven years is consistent: pushback that can be answered with force or silence is answered that way first — Annobón's protesters jailed and their island cut off, Bata's cause settled by presidential statement against the satellite evidence, election challengers raided before the vote. The genuine adjustments on the for shelf are real but share one shape: each costs the holder of power nothing. The ICJ ruling was a victory he accepted, not a loss he absorbed; the cabinet that "resigned" was his own appointees, and its head was reappointed within days; the Annobón pardon was framed as mercy from the throne, with the blackout — the actual punishment of the island — left running. A wobble that touches the seat itself has never, in the documented record, produced an adjustment.

Helped / Hurt ledger:

  • Helped: Gabon and Equatorial Guinea both, by a border dispute closed by law and a 2026 implementation agreement rather than by arms. The 476 prisoners pardoned in June 2025, including the 37 Annobón detainees and the two South Africans. Bata's survivors, helped by the emergency fund and international aid the government requested.
  • Hurt: The people of Annobón — some 5,000 residents — who lived under a communications blackout into its second year after protesting environmental damage. The families of those killed at Bata, hurt by an official cause contradicted by satellite analysis and never independently investigated. Opposition members arrested in the September 2022 headquarters raid, and the at least four activists who died in it (Freedom House, 2023).

Test 4 — COUNTS EVERYONE

Does the accounting include the islanders and the mainland peoples, the protesters, the poor, and the strangers delivered into his hands — or are some counted out?

GRADE: FAILS

Evidence for:

  • The oil boom built things that reach everyone who uses them. The World Bank records that Equatorial Guinea's produced capital increased 100-fold between 1995 and 2020 on public investment after the oil discoveries, and that education and health outcomes have improved in recent years, while noting they lag countries of similar income (World Bank, Equatorial Guinea Economic Update, 21 July 2025). Roads, ports, and public buildings from the boom years are documented across the IMF's assessments (IMF, 2015).
  • The death-penalty abolition of 2022 counted everyone equally, including opponents (Test 1). The June 2025 pardon reached beyond his own nationals to two South African prisoners (AFP, 10 June 2025).
  • The state kept functioning for ordinary people through the decline in at least one documented respect: the authorities' efforts to clear arrears brought the debt-to-GDP ratio down to 36.9% in 2024 from 38.5% in 2023 (World Bank, 2025).

Evidence against:

  • The majority of the counted are poor in one of Africa's richest states per capita. The World Bank estimates 57% of the population lived below the poverty line (USD 6.85, 2017 PPP) in 2024, with poverty rising on food prices, sluggish growth, and limited jobs, and unemployment estimated at about 14% (World Bank, 21 July 2025). The African Development Bank, using a different poverty line, records 50.7% in poverty in 2024 — printed as a separate sourced figure from a different measure, not blended with the Bank's (AfDB country outlook, June 2026, via Torch Media Africa). Hydrocarbons still accounted for nearly 46% of GDP and over 80% of revenues in 2024 (World Bank, 2025) — the wealth existed; the counting did not reach most people.
  • The newest count confirms the direction. The World Bank's Economic Update of 4 November 2026 finds the economy contracted an estimated 5.8% in 2025 after modest growth of 0.4% in 2024, driven by collapsing hydrocarbon production, with activity still weak in early 2026 and further contraction of about 1% a year projected for 2026–27 (World Bank, 4 November 2026). Earlier official-adjacent accounts put the 2025 contraction at 6.4% (Obiang's own year-end framing via Africa Yearbook 2025) — separate figures, printed separately.
  • Annobón's people were counted out in the most literal way available: an island province of about 5,000, with a distinct Afro-Portuguese Creole culture, protested environmental damage and was answered with mass arrests, charges including "abusive exercise of fundamental rights," transfer to Black Beach prison in Malabo, and a telecommunications blackout that severed banking and mobile money entirely (UNPO and UN Special Procedures reporting, 2024–2025; Africa Yearbook 2025). Nine UN mandate-holders raised allegations of environmental-rights violations and arbitrary detention affecting the Annobonese people (UNPO, 2025) — the underlying violations are recorded here as the experts' allegations and concerns, with the WGAD's finding of arbitrary detention stated as that body's determination.
  • The strangers delivered into his hands were counted as revenue. In October 2025 Equatorial Guinea agreed to accept third-country nationals removed from the United States, receiving USD 7.5 million in US State Department funds; between November 2025 and April 2026 three flights delivered 32 people from ten countries, who were detained at the Bamy Hotel in Malabo — reported by the Associated Press to be owned by the president's family (Global Detention Project, 2026, citing AP investigation, May 2026). Detainees reported to AP and the Guardian being held without soap, toothbrushes, or clean clothes, with inadequate medical care; by early March 2026, 17 of the first 29 had been returned to the home countries US courts had determined they could not safely be returned to (Global Detention Project, 2026). On 13 May 2026 UN human rights experts warned at least nine detainees faced "imminent" risk of refoulement. ALLEGATION: lawyers and witnesses stated that on 11 September 2026 police hooded and beat two deportees, Ahmed Soliman and Samson Birhane, at the hotel after they spoke to the press (lawyers and rights groups via Standing Record compilation, September 2026); Amnesty International records that both men were later released following advocacy and reporting (Amnesty International USA, 2026 highlights). The US Senate formally requested a statement on Equatorial Guinea's human rights practices, including the treatment of people removed there by the US, on 10 March 2026 (S. Res. 634, 119th Congress).
  • The capital itself was moved away from the counted. On 2 January 2026 a presidential decree made Ciudad de la Paz — the purpose-built city in the mainland jungle province of Djibloho, under construction since about 2011 and criticized by the opposition as the president's personal project — the capital, giving all state institutions one year to transfer (presidential decree, 2 January 2026; Britannica, 2026). The decree's stated reasons were strategic and administrative; the documented context is a city built with oil wealth, in the president's home region, while 57% of the population lived below the poverty line.

Why: The grade lands at FAILS because the accounting has a consistent shape across forty-seven years: the closer a person stands to the ruling family and its home region, the more fully they are counted; the further away — an island province, a poor majority in a rich state, a foreign asylum seeker held in a family hotel — the less. The boom's infrastructure is real, and it is on the for shelf. But a country that took in oil wealth sufficient to raise produced capital a hundredfold, and ends the boom with 57% of its people in poverty, its smallest province blacked out for protesting, and deportees warehoused for USD 7.5 million, has counted some people as assets and others as costs.

Helped / Hurt ledger:

  • Helped: The ruling family's home region of the mainland, which received the new capital and its construction. Contractors and officials attached to boom-era public investment. The US administration, which obtained a deportation destination no court at home had chosen for these people.
  • Hurt: The poor majority — 57% below the World Bank's poverty line in 2024. The roughly 5,000 people of Annobón. The 32 third-country deportees documented by April 2026, at least 17 of the first 29 returned to countries US courts had barred their return to. Young people facing an economy the World Bank projects to keep shrinking through 2027, with the jobs report of November 2026 naming their employment as the central unmet challenge.

Test 5 — KEEPS HIS WORD OVER TIME

Program pledges and public statements vs. the record since 1979, weighted to recent years, with dates. Word-keeping and helped/hurt are graded as independent axes; all four crossings are named where they occurred.

GRADE: FAILS

Evidence for (promises kept or credibly in motion):

  • The death penalty — a word given in 2019, kept in 2022. At the 2019 Universal Periodic Review, Equatorial Guinea's delegation stated the government "had decided to abolish the death penalty and had prepared a decree, which had been put before Parliament for approval" (UN UPR records, via Advocates for Human Rights stakeholder report, 2024). On 19 September 2022 the new penal code abolishing it was signed (OHCHR, 20 September 2022). Crossing 1 — kept his word AND it helped people: every person who would have faced execution for an ordinary crime.
  • Infrastructure under Horizon 2020 — partially kept, at a price recorded in full. The National Plan for Economic and Social Development "Horizon 2020," adopted at Bata in November 2007, promised world-class infrastructure, stronger human capital, a diversified private-sector economy, and quality governance (Decree 69/2021 preamble, reciting the 2007 plan). The government's own evaluation, recited in the same decree, found a "spectacular transformation" in basic infrastructure — and that diversification had not occurred and investments were not having the expected impact on living conditions. Crossing 2 — kept his word AND it still hurt people: the infrastructure pillar was substantially delivered, including the purpose-built capital at Ciudad de la Paz, while the resources it consumed were the same oil wealth whose absence from health, education, and poverty reduction the World Bank documents; the hurt is recorded in Test 4's ledger in full, and the keeping earns its credit here without laundering it.
  • A reversal that helped, recorded on the second axis. His government maintained before the UN Working Group that the Annobón arrests were lawful (WGAD opinion summary, June 2025); the June 2025 pardon reversed that position in practice and freed 37 people. Crossing 3 — went against his stated position AND it helped people: the break with his own government's word is recorded here; the help is recorded in Test 3's ledger in full.

Evidence against (promises not kept, or receding):

  • The term limit — the word was written to exclude the man who wrote it. The 2011 constitutional reform limited the president to two seven-year terms. The BTI's assessment, as reported in 2026 summaries of BTI 2026, is that the two-term period "already expired in 2022," yet Obiang stood again that year and remains in office, the limit having been treated as not retroactive to his prior terms (BTI 2026, via reported summaries). A ruler who accepts a term limit that begins counting only for other people has not kept the word the limit speaks. Crossing 4 — broke his word AND it hurt people: every voter whose constitution told them the seat would turn over.
  • Diversification — promised in 2007, promised again in 2021, promised again in 2026, not delivered. The IMF in 2015: despite the infrastructure, "economic diversification has remained elusive," with hydrocarbons still over 90% of exports (IMF Survey, 15 September 2015). The government's own 2021 decree recited that the economic profile "remained heavily dependent on the exploitation of hydrocarbons" (Decree 69/2021). In 2024 hydrocarbons were still nearly 46% of GDP and over 80% of revenues (World Bank, 2025). On 29 October 2026, in Moscow, Obiang presented "Horizon 2035" — agro-industry, fisheries, tourism, energy, new technologies, private sector — the same list, in a new decade (Vice-Presidency official site, 29 October 2026). His own government's June 2026 resignation statement conceded the failure from inside: barely 10% of targets reached, diversification among the failures named (Reuters, 16 June 2026).
  • The no-good-choice clause, applied by its mechanics. The post-2014 oil collapse is the strongest candidate in this record for a situation with no good choice: when prices fell and production declined, every door — austerity, borrowing, drawing reserves — carried real harm, and the dossier does not grade the choice among those doors against him. What remains gradable, and is graded, is the trap's origin and the honesty inside it. The trap was built during the boom he governed: the IMF warned in 2015, mid-boom's end, that diversification had not happened while the money was there; the World Bank records produced capital a hundredfold up by 2020 while human-capital outcomes lagged peers. A ruler who spends the fat years without building the exit does not get the lean years graded as weather. On honesty inside the trap: the June 2026 admission that his government reached 10% of its targets is the record's most candid sentence, and it is credited in Test 3; against it stand four decades of proclaimed election results — 94.9% on 98% turnout in 2022 — that no independent assessor credits.
  • Crossing 3, second instance sought and not found: beyond the Annobón pardon, no clear documented instance appears in the record assembled here of Obiang breaking his word in a way that helped people. It is recorded as not documented, rather than invented.
  • The anti-corruption word — spoken by the heir, enforced on the rival. Teodorín fronted anti-corruption campaigns, including a 2022 campaign against gang violence and corruption (Freedom House, 2023); the one senior family figure convicted at home is the nephew observers identify as his succession rival, while the vice president's own French conviction stands unexamined by any Equatoguinean court (Tests 1 and 2). The word "anti-corruption" has been kept as an instrument and broken as a law.

Why: The grade lands at FAILS because the two promises that define a ruler's word — that the seat is temporary, and that the people's wealth will be turned into the people's future — were both broken in writing, by his own government's documents: a term limit construed to exempt its author, and a diversification pledge renewed in 2007, 2021, and 2026 while the dependence it promised to end deepened into contraction. The kept words are real and are credited above, each on its own axis: abolition helped; infrastructure helped and hurt at once; the pardon broke a position and freed people. None of them is the word the tenure turns on.

Helped / Hurt ledger:

  • Helped: Those spared the death penalty after 2022. Users of the boom's roads, ports, and public buildings. The 37 Annobón detainees freed by the pardon.
  • Hurt: Every citizen promised a two-term presidency and a diversified economy — which is to say, the whole population of about 1.7–1.8 million, now living through the contraction the World Bank measured at 5.8% in 2025 with poverty at 57%. Future governments, who inherit depleted fields, a purpose-built capital to maintain, and USD 237 million of the family's foreign assets still working their way back through other countries' courts and agreements.

Overall Summary

One law: FAILS · Without gripping: FAILS · The wobble: FAILS · Counts everyone: FAILS · His word: FAILS.

Teodoro Obiang Nguema Mbasogo has held Equatorial Guinea for forty-seven years — longer than the lives of most of its citizens — and the record of that hold is unusually consistent across every test. One law fails because the law's reach stops at the line of succession: his nephew was convicted of embezzlement at home in 2025 in a case observers read as succession politics, while his son and vice president, convicted of embezzling and laundering public funds by France's highest court and stripped of USD 237 million in assets on three continents, has never faced a domestic charge and was defended by his father to the French president in person in November 2026; the people hurt are the public whose oil wealth filled the Paris mansion, and the people helped are that same public only where foreign courts, not his own, acted. Without gripping fails because every referee is held: a judiciary scored 0 of 4 for independence by Freedom House, an electoral commission headed by the ruling party's interior minister, a parliament without a single opposition seat, and a presidency guarded since 2025 by some 200 foreign instructors; the hurt falls on voters, lawyers, journalists, and detainees with no independent door to knock on. The wobble fails because pushback is answered with force or silence first — Annobón's protesters jailed and their island blacked out into a second year, Bata's explosion cause proclaimed against the satellite evidence, challengers raided before elections — while the genuine adjustments (an ICJ dispute closed by law and a July 2026 implementation agreement, a cabinet's public confession of 10% delivery, a pardon for 476) each cost the seat nothing, and the cabinet's head was reappointed within days; the helped are Gabon, the pardoned, and Bata's aided survivors, and the hurt are Annobón's 5,000, Bata's uncounted dead's families, and every challenger. Counts everyone fails because the count follows distance from the family: 57% of the population in poverty at the end of an oil boom that multiplied produced capital a hundredfold, the smallest province severed from the telephone network for protesting, 32 foreign deportees held in a family-owned hotel for USD 7.5 million with at least 17 of the first 29 returned to countries US courts had barred, and a new jungle capital decreed in January 2026 while the economy shrank 5.8%. His word fails on the two promises the tenure turns on — a two-term limit construed to exempt its author, and diversification pledged in 2007, 2021, and again as Horizon 2035 in Moscow in October 2026 while hydrocarbon dependence deepened into contraction — with the kept words credited on their own axes and never used to launder the broken ones: the death penalty abolished as promised in 2022 (kept, and it helped), the infrastructure pillar delivered at a price the poor paid (kept, and it hurt), and the Annobón pardon reversing his government's own position (broken, and it helped). The no-good-choice clause was applied to the post-2014 oil collapse and did not shield him, because the trap — a boom spent without building the exit — was of his own making, which the clause grades separately. The seat has not changed hands, and the succession stands pre-arranged by appointment rather than by any count of the counted: the file closes, as the tenure has not, with the open questions named — whether the 2029 election occurs on schedule, whether the vice president's succession proceeds as the constitution he was appointed under provides, and whether the assets foreign courts recovered ever reach the people they were taken from.
