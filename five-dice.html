Five Dice

Five dice, three rolls a turn, thirteen boxes to fill. Hold the dice that help you, roll the rest, and push your luck — then write the hand into the box it earns most in. Highest total wins.

Built on Yacht, the proven five-dice scorecard game documented in game books since the 1930s and sold commercially from 1956 onward. The rules here stay true to that documented game; the name, the card text, and every word on this page are our own.

Two honest notes. First, an open-ended straight draw (two faces finish it) is worth twice a one-ended draw — that is why pros break up a weak pair to hold four to a straight. Second, the upper bonus is the quiet giant: three of every face up top adds to exactly 63, so “three of a kind in the upper half” is the pace to protect.

Tap to open — how to play, in full

The turn

Roll all five dice. Then, up to two more times: hold the dice you like (tap them) and roll the rest. When you stop — or after the third roll — you must write the hand into one open box on your card. Each box fills once. Thirteen turns fill the card.

The upper half — Ones to Sixes

Each box scores the sum of the dice showing that face. Ones count the 1s, Sixes count the 6s. If the six boxes together reach the bonus target (63 on standard rules — exactly three of every face), the bonus is added once.

The lower half

Three of a Kind — three alike: score all five dice. Four of a Kind — four alike: score all five dice. Full House — three alike plus a pair: 25. Small Straight — four in a run (like 2-3-4-5): 30. Large Straight — five in a run: 40. Five of a Kind — all five alike: 50. Chance — anything at all: score all five dice. A hand that doesn’t make a box scores zero in it — choosing which box takes the zero is half the game.

Extra Five of a Kind (standard rule, can be switched off in settings)

Once your Five of a Kind box holds 50, every further five-of-a-kind pays +100, and that hand may fill the Full House, Small Straight, or Large Straight box at full value, or any other open box by its normal count.

Winning

When every card is full, the highest grand total wins. Solo, your score is measured against your own best on this device.
