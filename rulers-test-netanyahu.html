The Ruler's Test — Benjamin Netanyahu, Prime Minister of Israel

Subject: Benjamin Netanyahu — Prime Minister of Israel 1996–1999, 2009–2021, and since 29 December 2022; Israel's longest-serving prime minister. Since the election of 27 October 2026, in which his bloc won 53 of 120 Knesset seats and no bloc reached the 61 needed for a majority, he heads a caretaker government while opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot holds the president's mandate to form a government (until 6 December 2026). Graded: November 2026, on the documented public record. Method: Each test is graded only on the evidence listed under it. Both shelves are shown — the strongest evidence that he passes, then the strongest evidence that he fails. Allegations are labeled ALLEGATION and never stated as verdicts. Contested items are named as contested, with who disputes them. Where an international body has issued a finding, the finding is stated as that body's, and Israel's and Netanyahu's responses are stated as theirs; this dossier does not adjudicate charges that courts have not adjudicated. Casualty figures are attributed to the body that published them. Seat-holders and public figures are named; people below the public eye appear by role only, unless the documented record itself establishes their wrongdoing. Grades: HOLDS / FAILS / MIXED / NOT ENOUGH EVIDENCE. This record is correctable: new evidence changes grades, in the open.

Test 1 — GIVES BY ONE LAW

Same rules for allies and opponents, insiders and outsiders; no favorites at the source.

GRADE: FAILS

Evidence for:

  • The law's largest case in the country is his own, and it has proceeded against him for six years in the open. Indicted on 21 November 2019 (bribery in Case 4000; fraud and breach of trust in Cases 1000, 2000 and 4000 — all ALLEGATIONs, which he denies, calling the prosecution an attempted coup), he has stood trial since May 2020 as the first sitting Israeli prime minister to be a criminal defendant. The prosecution called 333 witnesses and rested in July 2024; Netanyahu testified in his own defense from December 2024, completing his testimony and cross-examination in June 2026 after 98 hearings; since 4 October 2026 the Jerusalem District Court has sat five days a week on the remaining defense witnesses, including former Attorney General Avichai Mandelblit, the man who indicted him. No verdict has been reached (court record, 2019–2026).
  • The investigative arms of his own government investigated his own office. In the affair Israeli media call Qatargate, police and the Shin Bet arrested two of his close advisers, Yonatan Urich and Eli Feldstein, in March 2025, on suspicions — ALLEGATIONs, unindicted as of late October 2026, though police were reported in September 2026 to have completed their investigation — that they were paid through intermediaries to promote Qatar's image in Israeli media while Qatar mediated hostage talks. Netanyahu was questioned as a witness; he is not a suspect, and has denied receiving any Qatari money ("The claim that I received money from Qatar never happened," in sworn testimony in a separate defamation suit, 2026). Feldstein was separately indicted in November 2024 in the leaked-documents affair, charged with passing a classified document to the German tabloid Bild with intent to harm state security — an ALLEGATION he contests; in December 2025 interviews Feldstein claimed Netanyahu knew of and approved the leak, which Netanyahu's office denies (police, court and press records, 2024–2026).
  • In the Submarines Affair (Case 3000), the attorney general's process did not charge him: Netanyahu was never indicted in the affair, a fact that cuts in his favor on this shelf — the system did not manufacture a case against him where its prosecutors found none (attorney general's decisions, 2018–2019).
  • The attorney general he now opposes has issued binding opinions against him throughout his tenure — on his conflict-of-interest arrangement, on appointments, on dismissals — and those opinions were published and acted on by the courts. The machinery of one law exists and has been used against the ruler personally, in public (attorney general's opinions, 2023–2026).

Evidence against:

  • Mid-trial, he asked to be removed from the law's reach by prerogative. On 30 November 2025 Netanyahu formally requested a presidential pardon from President Isaac Herzog — before any verdict, and expressly without admitting guilt or expressing remorse. The Justice Ministry's Pardons Department concluded in March 2026 that the request did not meet the legal criteria and that granting it in the circumstances would be "extremely problematic." Herzog has not granted it: on 26 April 2026 he said an out-of-court agreement between the parties should be exhausted first, and on 12 October 2026 he said publicly that Netanyahu had not responded to his mediation proposal. U.S. President Donald Trump pressed Herzog repeatedly and publicly to grant the pardon, including in an address to the Knesset on 13 October 2025. A pardon granted on these terms would end, for one man, a process that continues for every other defendant in the country (President's Office statements; Justice Ministry Pardons Department position, March 2026; press reporting, 2025–2026). A Channel 14 report of 28 October 2026 claiming a deal — two more years as prime minister, then retirement and a pardon — was rejected by Herzog's office as an "absolute fake," and is recorded here only as a denied report.
  • A court has found, in terms, that he acted inside a conflict of interest the law forbids. On 21 May 2025 the Supreme Court ruled that the government's dismissal of Shin Bet director Ronen Bar was "illegal and contrary to law," carried out "through an improper process," and that Netanyahu was in a conflict of interest in the matter because the Shin Bet was then investigating his own aides in the Qatargate and leaked-documents affairs (Supreme Court, HCJ 54321-03-25, 21 May 2025). Earlier, Attorney General Baharav-Miara had advised him in February 2023 that his personal involvement in the judicial-overhaul legislation breached the conflict-of-interest arrangement he signed in 2020 to avoid exactly this overlap between his trial and his office; he proceeded anyway. In September 2026 the Supreme Court voided three media-regulator appointments, including one it found incompatible with that same conflict-of-interest arrangement (Supreme Court ruling, 8 September 2026, as reported by Reuters).
  • A state commission warned him personally over a process that bypassed the law's ordinary channels. The commission of inquiry into the Submarines Affair issued Netanyahu a warning letter in June 2024, notifying him it might reach adverse personal conclusions that his decision-making on the submarine and naval-vessel purchases had circumvented established procurement and security processes. Netanyahu disputes the commission's framing; the warning is a documented act of the commission, not a conviction (Commission of Inquiry warning letters, June 2024).
  • In the territory under his government's control, the law is formally two laws. Israeli settlers in the West Bank are tried in Israeli civilian courts; Palestinians in the same territory are tried in military courts — a dual structure documented by every monitoring body that covers it. Within that structure, enforcement against settler violence is documented to be close to nominal: the Israeli organization Yesh Din found that of the hundreds of settler-violence cases it documented since 7 October 2023, only about 2% resulted in indictments, and its long-run data put convictions at about 3% of police files (Yesh Din data, 2025; Reuters, 24 December 2025). Whatever the law says at the source, it does not arrive equally at the two populations living under his government's authority.

Test 2 — HOLDS WITHOUT GRIPPING

Do the courts, the attorney general, the military and security chiefs, media, and the Knesset keep their autonomy — or is independence punished and control centralized?

GRADE: FAILS

Evidence for:

  • The institutions have, repeatedly, bitten him and survived. The Supreme Court struck down his coalition's reasonableness-law amendment on 1 January 2024 — the first time it voided an amendment to a Basic Law — and his government complied in fact: the amendment is not applied. The Court froze the attorney general's dismissal the day it was voted (4 August 2025) and she remains in office in November 2026. It annulled the closure of Army Radio in August 2026. It ruled his Shin Bet chief's dismissal illegal. No judge is documented to have been removed or sanctioned for any of these rulings (court records, 2024–2026).
  • The security chiefs he appointed have defied him in public and kept their posts for long stretches: IDF Chief of Staff Eyal Zamir clashed openly with Netanyahu in August 2025 over the plan to conquer Gaza City, warning of the danger to hostages and of military exhaustion, and remained chief of staff (Israeli press reporting, August 2025). His eventual Shin Bet appointee, David Zini, took office in October 2025 only after a court-sanctioned process — an advisory-committee integrity review and agreements recusing the new chief from the Qatargate and leaked-documents investigations — and the Supreme Court closed the litigation over the appointment in December 2025 (Supreme Court proceedings, HCJ 18133-06-25 and HCJ 427-10-25, 2025).
  • The press that covers him is unsparing and unclosed: the channels and newspapers most critical of him broadcast and publish daily; the protests of 2023 and 2024–2026 filled the streets for months without being banned. The election of 27 October 2026 was administered by the Central Elections Committee, was free on its face, drew 77.9% turnout, and produced a result his bloc did not want (Central Elections Committee final count, 29 October 2026).
  • His coalition partners keep their own spin to a degree unusual in this series: Smotrich and Ben Gvir voted against his October 2025 ceasefire deal inside his own cabinet and kept their ministries; the Haredi parties have repeatedly threatened and withheld support over the draft law. He governs by negotiation with them, not by command (cabinet and Knesset records, 2023–2026).

Evidence against:

  • The 2023 judicial overhaul was, in design, a grip on the referee. As introduced by Justice Minister Yariv Levin in January 2023, it would have given the coalition control of judicial appointments, barred the Supreme Court from voiding Basic Laws, and let a 61-vote Knesset majority override court rulings. Mass protest and a general strike forced a pause in March 2023; the one component enacted, the abolition of reasonableness review, was struck down by the Court on 1 January 2024 (8–7, a fifteen-justice panel), and the Court separately delayed the application of the coalition's "incapacity" amendment — widely described, including by petitioners, as tailored to Netanyahu personally — until after the next Knesset (Supreme Court rulings, January 2024).
  • The grip was re-applied in narrower form. On 27 March 2025 the Knesset passed the Levin–Sa'ar law (67–1, over an opposition boycott) replacing the Bar Association's two seats on the Judicial Selection Committee with two politically appointed lawyers, one chosen by the coalition and one by the opposition, and removing the judiciary's effective veto over Supreme Court appointments — to take effect with the next Knesset, which was seated on 10 November 2026. The attorney general opposed the law; an eleven-justice panel was convened to hear the petitions against it (Knesset record, 27 March 2025; Supreme Court announcement, 2 December 2025).
  • The attorney general's removal was attempted by changing the rules mid-process. After the government opened dismissal proceedings against Baharav-Miara in March 2025, it adopted a June 2025 resolution replacing the established route — recommendation of a public committee — with a ministerial one, and on 4 August 2025 the cabinet voted unanimously to fire her. The Supreme Court suspended the dismissal the same day; as of November 2026 she holds office under the Court's protection, not the government's consent. She is the official whose office prosecutes his trial and who had ruled his involvement in the overhaul and the Bar dismissal unlawful (government decisions and Supreme Court orders, March–August 2025).
  • The security chiefs who pushed back were removed or driven out in sequence. Defense Minister Yoav Gallant — who had publicly pressed for a hostage deal and a Haredi draft law — was fired on 5 November 2024. IDF Chief of Staff Herzi Halevi resigned in January 2025, accepting responsibility for 7 October. Shin Bet director Ronen Bar, whose agency was investigating the prime minister's office and who supported a state commission of inquiry, was fired by cabinet vote on 20 March 2025; the Supreme Court froze the dismissal, then ruled it illegal (Test 1), and Bar resigned effective 15 June 2025. Netanyahu then nominated Zini over the attorney general's written opinion that the nomination was "illegitimate and illegal" because of his conflict of interest; the appointment proceeded only under the court-brokered terms described above (cabinet decisions; Supreme Court rulings; attorney general's opinion, 2025).
  • The media file shows the same hand. In December 2025 his cabinet voted unanimously to close Army Radio (Galei Tzahal), the IDF's civilian news station, by 1 March 2026, with Netanyahu's public endorsement; the High Court froze the closure on 29 December 2025 and annulled it in August 2026, a unanimous panel finding the decision rested at least partly on "improper and invalid" political considerations — Justice Kasher: "It cannot be that someone in a position of power will decide that because they don't like what a broadcaster is saying, that microphone should be 'turned off.'" In June–July 2026, after a Supreme Court ruling on the membership of a media regulator, ministers in his government announced they would not respect the ruling; the cabinet later restated that it would pursue legal means instead; on 8 September 2026 the Court voided three of the regulator appointments (Supreme Court rulings, August–September 2026, as reported by Reuters and Globes). In November 2026, Shin Bet director Zini — Netanyahu's appointee — alleged foreign financing of Arab parties in the election and sought to interrogate Arab politicians including Mansour Abbas and Ahmad Tibi over the attorney general's objection that the evidence was insufficient, a step critics inside and outside the service described as the security agency entering politics (reporting and commentary, November 2026; the allegations of foreign financing are unproven and contested).

Test 3 — ANSWERS THE WOBBLE

When courts, voters, allies, hostages' families, or events push back, does he adjust openly — or deny and double down?

GRADE: FAILS

Evidence for:

  • He has reversed under pressure before, in the open. On 27 March 2023, after firing Gallant for urging a pause in the judicial overhaul triggered a general strike and the largest protests in the country's history, Netanyahu announced a pause in the legislation and later reinstated Gallant. The overhaul's remaining pieces were then fought through the courts rather than forced (statements and record, March–April 2023).
  • Three times, he signed hostage deals he had resisted. November 2023: a pause exchanging 105 hostages for 240 Palestinian prisoners. January 2025: a phased ceasefire under which 33 hostages came home in its first phase. October 2025: after two years of "total victory" language, his cabinet approved the Trump plan's first phase over the votes of his far-right partners, and on 13 October 2025 all 20 remaining living hostages were released (deal texts and records, 2023–2025).
  • He has complied, in the end, with the courts' hardest orders: the Bar dismissal was rescinded by his government in April 2025 once the Court froze it and Bar set his resignation date; the reasonableness ruling stands unapplied; the Army Radio closure was not carried out by other means after annulment (government decisions, 2024–2026).
  • He accepted the loss of power once: in June 2021, after the Bennett–Lapid government was sworn in, he left the prime minister's residence and office and led the opposition for eighteen months. After the October 2026 election he has operated inside the constitutional process — his bloc negotiating, the president's mandate given to Eisenkot on 8 November 2026 without Netanyahu blocking it (records, 2021; President's Office, November 2026).

Evidence against:

  • On the central wobble of his tenure — accountability for 7 October 2023 — the answer has been refusal, sustained for more than 1,100 days. Hamas-led attackers killed some 1,200 people in Israel and took 251 hostages on that day (Israeli official figures). The IDF's own investigations (February 2025) acknowledged the army "severely underestimated Hamas and failed to protect civilians"; Halevi and Bar accepted responsibility and left. Netanyahu has never accepted personal responsibility. On 28–29 October 2023 he posted, then deleted, a statement blaming the security chiefs for failing to warn him, apologizing for the post ("I was wrong") but not for the policy. He has refused to establish a state commission of inquiry — the independent body, appointed by the Supreme Court president, that Israel has used after past national failures — arguing it would be biased, and his government told the High Court the court has no authority to order one. The Court issued an order nisi requiring the government to show cause why a state commission should not be established; the coalition meanwhile advanced the Kellner bill to substitute a politician-appointed "state-national" commission, which the Zulat institute's analysis (December 2025) describes as a body designed by the investigated to investigate themselves — a characterization the bill's sponsors dispute. On 24 October 2026, three days before the election, thousands of bereaved families, survivors and former hostages rallied in Tel Aviv demanding the commission. As of November 2026 there is still none (court filings; Knesset record; AP reporting, October 2026).
  • The refusal is now a bargaining chip. On 21 November 2026, Kan News reported that Likud was conditioning a unity government with Eisenkot's bloc on agreement not to establish a state commission of inquiry; Likud responded that core disputes should be addressed in negotiations. Eisenkot has until 6 December 2026 to form a government (Kan News via Xinhua, 21 November 2026).
  • The hostage wobble was answered late and, on the documented accounts of the mediators and the families, with moving terms. On 31 May 2024 President Biden presented what he said was an Israeli proposal for a phased deal; Netanyahu's office publicly qualified it. In September 2024, after six hostages were murdered by their captors as Israeli forces approached (announced 1 September 2024) and hundreds of thousands protested, Netanyahu held a televised press conference (2 September 2024) insisting on permanent Israeli control of the Philadelphi Corridor — a condition negotiators and the hostages' families' forum said had been added mid-negotiation and was blocking the deal; he also said at that press conference, "I ask for your forgiveness that we didn't bring them back alive." The war continued for another thirteen months before the October 2025 deal, whose terms — full release for a partial withdrawal — resembled the framework available in 2024. Netanyahu disputes the families' and mediators' account, maintaining that Hamas refused the deals and that military pressure produced the releases; the dispute is recorded here as contested, the sequence of dates is not (statements and reporting, 2024–2025).
  • Pushback from inside his own security establishment was answered by removal (Test 2): Gallant fired after publicly demanding a deal and a draft law; Bar fired while supporting an inquiry and investigating the prime minister's office. A ruler who fires the wobblers is answering the wobble by ending it.
  • Court pushback was answered, in 2026, with announced defiance before it was answered with compliance: ministers stated they would not respect the Supreme Court's media-regulator ruling (June–July 2026) before the cabinet walked the position back to legal channels (Reuters reporting via Controversia's documented record, verified 11 November 2026).

Test 4 — COUNTS EVERYONE

Does the accounting include Arab citizens of Israel, Palestinians in Gaza and the West Bank, the hostages and their families, his opponents' voters, and those asked to share the burden of service — or are some counted out?

GRADE: FAILS

Evidence for:

  • The hostages were, in the end, all brought home. All 20 living hostages were released on 13 October 2025; the remains of the deceased were returned in stages, and phase two of the Trump plan was declared in January 2026 after the last hostage's remains were returned. Whatever the delay graded in Test 3, the final count of hostages in Gaza under his government is zero (deal records; reporting, October 2025–January 2026).
  • Arab citizens of Israel vote, organize, and in October 2026 won the largest representation in the country's history — 19 Knesset seats (Joint List 11, Ra'am 8) on a surge of Arab turnout to roughly 80% — in an election administered under his government, whose result the Central Elections Committee certified (Central Elections Committee, 29 October 2026; AIPAC/CNN/Fox reporting of the final count).
  • He has publicly condemned settler violence in terms: on 17 November 2025, after a West Bank village was torched, Netanyahu condemned attacks by "extremists" and said he viewed the riots with severity (France 24, 17 November 2025). His government states that humanitarian aid to Gaza was facilitated throughout the war subject to security screening, and points to the October 2025 deal's provision of some 600 aid trucks a day as evidence the population's needs are addressed when Hamas does not block them (government statements; Crisis Group briefing, 21 October 2025).

Evidence against:

  • Gaza's dead are counted by others because his government does not count them. The Gaza Ministry of Health, whose figures UN OCHA relays and the UN has treated as reliable while noting the ministry operates under Gaza's de facto authorities and does not distinguish combatants from civilians, reported 74,746 killed and 176,234 wounded between 7 October 2023 and 23 November 2026. Israel disputes the figures' composition and methodology; it does not publish a competing civilian count. The International Criminal Court's Pre-Trial Chamber found on 21 November 2024 "reasonable grounds" to believe Netanyahu and Gallant bore criminal responsibility for the war crime of starvation as a method of warfare and for crimes against humanity including murder and persecution — an arrest warrant, not a conviction; Netanyahu called it "antisemitic" and a "modern Dreyfus trial," Israel challenged the Court's jurisdiction, and the United States sanctioned ICC officials. The ICJ, in South Africa's case against Israel (a case against the state, not Netanyahu personally), ordered provisional measures on 26 January, 28 March and 24 May 2024; the merits are undecided. A UN commission of inquiry concluded in September 2025 that Israel had committed genocide in Gaza and cited statements by Netanyahu among those it considered incitement; Israel's foreign ministry rejected the report as distorted and false. Each finding is stated as its issuer's; none is adopted here as this dossier's verdict — but a population whose dead are the subject of three separate international proceedings is not inside the ruler's own count (Gaza MoH via OCHA, 23 November 2026; ICC, 21 November 2024; ICJ orders, 2024; UN Commission of Inquiry, September 2025).
  • The aid record is documented in both directions of time. From 2 March to 19 May 2025 Israel halted all aid entry into Gaza; the UN-backed IPC declared famine in Gaza Governorate on 22 August 2025 — a finding Israel rejected as biased. Since the ceasefire his government credits itself with enabling, Gaza's health ministry counts a further 1,650 killed and 5,975 injured between 10 October 2025 and 16 November 2026, in near-daily strikes Israel says target militants; phase two — Hamas's disarmament, full withdrawal, reconstruction — remains stalled, the Palestinian technocratic committee announced in January 2026 has not taken up governance, and on 17 November 2026 the Board of Peace warned the UN Security Council the truce faces collapse (OCHA Humanitarian Situation Report, 18 November 2026; Board of Peace report, 17 November 2026).
  • In the West Bank, 2025 was the worst year on the UN's record: OCHA documented more than 1,800 settler attacks in 2025, the highest since it began recording in 2006, and 240 Palestinians killed in the West Bank in 2025, including 55 children. Settlement expansion ran at what the UN calls an unprecedented pace — 80 new outposts in 2025 (Peace Now's highest count since 1991), 51,370 housing units approved since late 2022 by Smotrich's count, the E1 project tendered in August 2026 — while indictments for settler violence ran at roughly 2% (Test 1). Smotrich, the minister Netanyahu empowered over West Bank civil administration, said in August 2025 that each new unit is "another nail in the coffin" of a Palestinian state (OCHA; Reuters, 24 December 2025; OHCHR reporting to the General Assembly, November 2026).
  • Arab citizens inside Israel are counted as voters and discounted as victims. In 2025, 252 Arab citizens were murdered in criminal violence — the deadliest year on record, roughly 70% of the country's homicide victims from 21% of its population — while, by the Abraham Initiatives' and press accounts of police data, fewer than a tenth of murders in Arab communities were solved, against roughly two-thirds in Jewish communities. In November 2025 Deputy Attorney General Sharon Afek wrote to the Prime Minister's Office warning the government was failing to act against crime in Arab communities and stalling enforcement legislation; by year's end the National Security Ministry had spent only about half the funds allocated under the 2022–2026 five-year plan for combating crime in Arab society. After the October 2026 election, with Arab parties holding a record 19 seats, Likud claimed on 28 October 2026 that the election had been "stolen by Arab parties that support terrorism," alleging fraud; the Central Elections Committee said the vote was conducted lawfully and there was no basis for claims of systematic interference, and Netanyahu's own post-election speech suggested a government including Arab parties would endanger Israel (Abraham Initiatives data via Times of Israel, December 2025; police figures via AP, February 2026; Central Elections Committee statement; Controversia's documented record, verified 31 October 2026).
  • The burden of service is counted on some and excused for others by design. On 25 June 2024 the Supreme Court ruled unanimously that there was no legal basis to exempt Haredi yeshiva students from conscription and that state funding to institutions whose students evade service must stop. Through 2025–2026, while reservists — his opponents' and supporters' voters alike — served hundreds of days through two wars, his coalition's Bismuth draft bill stalled, and what the Knesset did pass, on 14 July 2026, was a law shielding Haredi draft evaders from arrest; the High Court struck it down unanimously in September 2026. Some 90,000 Haredi men stand listed by the army as draft evaders (Ynet reporting, November 2026). The Haredi parties are inside his count as partners; the reservists' equality claim is the part left uncounted.

Test 5 — KEEPS HIS WORD OVER TIME

Stated promises and positions vs. the record, with dates.

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • His central wartime promise was kept, in full, at the end. He pledged from October 2023 that no hostage would be left in Gaza; on 13 October 2025 all 20 living hostages came home, and by January 2026 every deceased hostage's remains had been returned. The promise took two years and the terms are graded elsewhere; the count itself is complete (deal records, 2025–2026).
  • The pledge that defined his career — that Iran would not be permitted a nuclear weapon and that Israel would act alone if necessary — was acted on at a scale no predecessor attempted. After the twelve-day war of June 2025, Israel joined the United States in the war of 28 February–June 2026 (Operations Roaring Lion / Epic Fury), in which Iran's Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei was killed on the first day and Iran's nuclear and missile infrastructure was struck across 24 provinces; an interim agreement followed on 17 June 2026, though implementation has been rocky and strikes intermittent since. Whether the program is destroyed, as Netanyahu claims, or degraded, as more cautious assessments hold, is CONTESTED between governments and inspectors; that he ordered what he had promised for two decades is not (war records and reporting, February–June 2026).
  • His 2022–2023 commitment to his voters to reform the judiciary was pursued through every reversal and partially delivered: the reasonableness amendment (struck down), then the Judicial Selection Committee law of 27 March 2025, now in force with the new Knesset (Knesset record).
  • His word to his coalition's right wing on Palestinian statehood has been kept in its current form with unusual explicitness: he has stated repeatedly since January 2024 that he will not permit a Palestinian state, and at the September 2025 Ma'ale Adumim signing for E1 construction said, "There will be no Palestinian state. This place belongs to us" (reported remarks, September 2025).

Evidence against:

  • That last kept word reverses his own earlier one. In his Bar-Ilan speech of 14 June 2009 Netanyahu accepted a demilitarized Palestinian state, and in 2020 he accepted the Trump plan containing a Palestinian state in principle. The 2019 pledge runs the other way and was dropped: campaigning in September 2019 he promised to apply Israeli sovereignty to the Jordan Valley and the settlements if elected; in August 2020 annexation was suspended as the price of the Abraham Accords and has never been carried out. Voters who backed each version of the promise got, in sequence, its opposite (speeches and records, 2009–2025).
  • "Total victory" was the war's stated aim — Hamas destroyed, its military and governing capability dismantled. The war ended by negotiation in October 2025 with Hamas armed and, as of November 2026, still controlling part of Gaza (assessed at up to 35% by a Jerusalem Post analysis of 13 November 2026), its disarmament — the deal's phase two — stalled and unenforced. Netanyahu has declared the war's aims achieved; the gap between the phrase and the documented end-state is the record (deal texts; Board of Peace reporting, November 2026).
  • The inquiry promise embedded in his own formula — that questions of responsibility would be answered "after the war" — has outlived the war by a year. The Gaza war ended in October 2025; by November 2026 no state commission exists, his government has told the High Court it cannot be ordered to create one, and his party is reported to be trading its non-creation in coalition talks (Test 3). A word deferred past its own condition is graded as a word not kept.
  • His word to his Haredi partners was not delivered either. The draft-exemption law that was the coalition's founding price was never enacted in the outgoing Knesset: the Bismuth bill stalled through 2025–2026, the June 2024 Supreme Court ruling stood unimplemented, and the substitute arrest-shield law of July 2026 was voided by the Court in September 2026. The issue now sits at the center of the coalition negotiations he is not leading (Knesset and court records; reporting, November 2026).
  • The pre-war policy he sold as security was reversed by events and never owned. For years his governments permitted Qatari cash transfers into Hamas-run Gaza and, by his own reported 2019 explanation to Likud lawmakers, treated a strong Hamas as a counterweight to Palestinian statehood — remarks his office has disputed in interpretation but which the policy itself corroborates in outline. On 7 October 2023 that policy's premise collapsed in the worst attack in the country's history, on the watch of the prime minister who had held the office for most of the preceding fourteen years. He has attributed the failure to the security establishment's warnings process; the state commission that could apportion it remains unestablished at his insistence (policy record, 2018–2023; reporting of the 2019 remarks, contested as to meaning).

Overall Summary

One law: FAILS · Without gripping: FAILS · The wobble: FAILS · Counts everyone: FAILS · His word: MIXED.

Benjamin Netanyahu's file is the series' clearest case of the distinction the Test exists to draw: the institutions held; the holder kept testing them. A sitting prime minister has been tried for bribery for six years and will likely be judged by the courts he could not bend; his attorney general sits in office under a court's protection against his own cabinet's vote; his broadcaster closure was annulled as political censorship; his security chief's firing was ruled illegal in terms that named his conflict of interest. Every one of those sentences is evidence for Israel's institutions — and every one of them exists because he made the attempt: the overhaul designed to capture judicial appointments, the dismissal rules rewritten mid-process for the prosecutor who oversees his trial, the Shin Bet chief fired while investigating his office, the pardon requested mid-trial without an admission, the state commission refused for 1,100 days and then traded in coalition talks. On the count, the hostages all came home — and Gaza's 74,746 dead are counted by a ministry his government dismisses, the West Bank's record year of settler violence drew indictments in 2% of cases, Arab citizens were murdered at a record rate while their cases went unsolved and their record 19 seats were met by his party with the word "stolen," and 90,000 exempt men stood outside a burden reservists carried through two wars. His word is the mixed shelf: the hostages, the Iran strikes, and the statehood refusal were delivered as promised; "total victory," the inquiry after the war, the sovereignty pledge, and the draft law were not. As graded, he remains caretaker prime minister of a country whose voters, on 27 October 2026, gave his bloc 53 seats, his opponents 48, and the citizens he counts least their largest voice ever — and gave none of them, yet, a government.
