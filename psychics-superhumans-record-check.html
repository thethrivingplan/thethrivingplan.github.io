Psychics & Superhumans — the record check

Theresa Caputo, the best-known psychics and mediums, the people from Stan Lee's Superhumans, and the self-published claimants putting their own footage out — each one graded on the documented record: what actually happened, what was tested, what held up, and what didn't. Both sides printed at full size.

The standard this page grades by

"If it works, it works. If they know, they know — and they have to know somehow."

So every person here is graded on two separate layers:

1 · The results layer. Did a specific, documented reading, prediction, or feat produce information or a result beyond what the ordinary channels actually open in that setting explain? A documented result is data, even if its mechanism is unexplained.

2 · The mechanism layer. How did they know, or do it — spirit contact, psi, cold reading, prior research, training, genetics? This is marked cannot verify unless a controlled test or a documented investigation settles it.

Skeptical explanations are claims too, and they're graded the same way: "it was cold reading" or "her team researched the audience" counts as supported only where it's documented in that specific case — a transcript showing the fishing, a sting catching planted information repeated back. TV editing is documented as a general practice and it cuts both ways: it can hide misses, and it means televised "hits" alone can't establish a result.

Research gathered 7 November 2026 from the sources linked throughout. Career, legal, and controlled-test facts are high-confidence (multiple / primary sources). Individual reading anecdotes are printed as attributed reports, not lab records.

Mediums: The James Randi $1M Challenge ran 1964–2015 with roughly 1,000 applicants — none passed even the preliminary stage. No well-known medium in this check has passed a properly controlled, independently replicated test. That doesn't erase individual documented results (graded case by case below), and individual hits don't overturn the controlled record. Both statements stand at full size.

Theresa Caputo: Career fully documented; never took a controlled test. The 2014 "fraud investigation" was a private debunker's campaign amplified by tabloids — no charge, suit, or regulatory action was ever found. Her live record holds both documented miss-streaks and documented specific-feeling private readings.

Superhumans: Here the record does contain lab-verified extraordinary abilities — super-recognizers, a woman whose genetics mean she feels almost no pain, a Guinness-verified human calculator — but they're physiological and cognitive, with mechanisms. The magnetism, no-touch knockouts, and living-on-air claims were tested and failed.

The worst documented record on the roster: Sylvia Browne — 0 correct out of 39 checkable missing-person and murder predictions that could be scored, including telling a kidnapped girl's mother her living daughter was dead.

Part 1 · In depth

Theresa Caputo

Story status: Cannot verify  Conduct on the documented record: Mixed – positive

The career — verified facts

Theresa Caputo (born June 10, 1967, Hicksville, Long Island, NY) starred in TLC's Long Island Medium from 2011 to 2019 — 14 seasons. She is the author of four books — There's More to Life Than This (2013), You Can't Make This Stuff Up (2014), Good Grief (2017), and Good Mourning (2020) — tours as Theresa Caputo Live! The Experience, and hosts the podcast Hey Spirit! She returned to TV on January 25, 2024 with Lifetime's Theresa Caputo: Raising Spirits, and touring remained active with extensive 2026 dates listed by ticket sellers.

She says she has sensed Spirit since childhood — age 4 in her telling — suppressed it, and began practising professionally years before TV; by her own website's statement she was booked two years ahead before the show aired. Her framing: she is a medium, not a fortune-teller — the dead come to her with images, sounds, and feelings she must interpret symbolically. She states on record, in substance: "I'm not asking anyone to believe in me… I don't care what they say about me or if they don't believe in me." She explicitly disavows fortune-telling and makes no medical or financial advice claim.

Her current official site carries a prominent anti-impersonation warning: she does not offer private online or TikTok readings, does not DM fans, does not sell "soul mate drawings" or "energy cleanings," and no team member will contact fans asking for money. The only reading-request route is her official site. Sources: Wikipedia — Theresa Caputo; theresacaputo.com — anti-impersonation warning

Has she ever been tested?

No record exists of Caputo submitting to a controlled or scientific test of mediumship — no Windbridge certification, no Schwartz lab series, no Randi test. That is a verified negative on the public record searched. Magician Criss Angel publicly offered her $1M, then $2M on X, to pass a test ("take my 2 million dollar challenge or stop exploiting the vulnerable"); she did not take it up, and it was reported a decade later as still untaken. The James Randi Educational Foundation gave her its satirical 2012 "Pigasus" Award — an advocacy award by skeptics, not a finding of fact.

So controlled-condition researchers have no Caputo-specific data. Her mechanism claim (spirit communication) is Cannot verify. The skeptics' mechanism claim (cold/hot reading) is graded case by case, below.

The skeptical record, at full size

Cold-reading analyses. Former mentalist Mark Edward, with Inside Edition (2012) and in later writing, analysed her live shows: high-probability open questions ("Who lost an older male relative to heart problems?"), rapid re-targeting of a statement across a large audience until someone claims it, and reinterpretation of misses as "symbols." Massimo Polidoro (Skeptical Inquirer, 2016) additionally alleged she seats people she already knows in front rows — that seating allegation is an attributed assertion with no documentary evidence produced.

Documented failed live reading — Jaime Franchi, Long Island Press (2014). Franchi attended a live show and reported Caputo repeatedly trying to pin messages on audience members until someone connected — asking a woman if her uncle had drowned, being told no, asking others the same, and only landing when someone offered a cancer death where lungs filled with fluid. A named, first-person, contemporaneous journalistic account: a documented miss-streak. The cold-reading mechanism is consistent with it, but not separately proven.

Hershey, PA, April 2014. Audience members quoted by RadarOnline/Enstarz said she "didn't get a single reading correct" and was "grasping at straws" and "fishing." Attributed audience reports — documented as audience dissatisfaction, not as a scored hit-rate.

Andy Cohen (2014 memoir). Cohen reported an off-camera reading after Watch What Happens Live was "a lot of fishing" and she got nothing specific; her team offered a redo, which he refused. Caputo responded (2019) that she spent ~30 seconds with him and his telling was "not totally truthful." Both sides documented; no recording published, so the reading itself cannot be scored.

The "her team researches ticket-buyers" claim — graded. Its best-documented form is debunker Ron Tebo's speculation: when you buy a ticket you give the vendor your name, address, and phone number; the vendor can share it and her staff can investigate. That states a channel that could exist. No evidence was found in any source that it was used — no leaked lists, no staffer testimony, no sting catching planted information repeated (contrast Thomas John, below, where a sting did produce exactly that evidence). For her TV readings, participants were pre-selected from request lists by her own disclaimer, so prior-knowledge channels were open by design — televised hits therefore can't count as results beyond ordinary channels. Verdict: Unsupported for live shows; channel open by design on TV.

The supporter record, at full size

Jaime Franchi's private reading — the other half of her account. Before becoming a skeptic, Franchi reported a successful private reading connecting her with her father that made her "a believer" — specific enough at the time to convince a journalist who later publicly recanted after the live show. Results layer: a documented first-person report of a specific-feeling hit in a one-to-one setting where audience-fishing channels weren't available. A prior-research channel can't be excluded (she wasn't a public figure; there's no evidence of research either). Mechanism: cannot verify.

Client grief outcomes. Thousands of client testimonials — books, show participants, tour audiences — describe comfort, closure, and reduced grief, including widowed clients in RadarOnline's own 2014 critical coverage who defended her, and consistently positive fan accounts at 2026 tour stops. The comfort is documented as reported experience; it doesn't by itself verify information transfer, and skeptics argue comfort "under false pretenses" is harm regardless. Both positions are on the record; the comfort reports themselves are not disputed as reports.

Her own statements about limits. Caputo repeatedly states she cannot control who comes through, cannot guarantee a specific person or message, interprets symbols that can be wrong in interpretation, and does not predict the future.

Charity readings — verified in part. Her long-standing website statement: private and group readings were charged $50–$175, with "100 percent of the money donated to charity" — named recipients included the Long Island Veterans Home via the Stony Brook Foundation and Meals on Wheels, with clients paying the charity directly. The statement and named recipients are documented and were carried verbatim by multiple outlets. The total amounts were not independently audited in any source found — amounts: cannot verify. She also formed a fan club in 2014 ($19.99/year) in part to fight fake-ticket and fake-reading scams, and actively warns fans about impersonator fraud.

The 2014 "fraud investigation" — what actually happened

Ron Tebo, a private "whistleblower" running SciFake.com, told RadarOnline (June 5, 2014), after about a year interviewing dissatisfied clients and colleagues, that Caputo was "like a vulture preying on the most vulnerable," using body-language reading, high-probability guesses, and — he alleged — assistant pre-interviews. Headlines called this a "fraud investigation."

It was not one. It was not a police, FTC, attorney-general, or network investigation. No charge, suit, or regulatory action followed in the record found, and the show returned for Season 5 on schedule (August 3, 2014). As commonly retold, "investigated for fraud" is Misleading. The documented version: a private debunker's campaign amplified by tabloids, plus genuine documented audience dissatisfaction that season. Sources: Bustle (2014); Enstarz (2014)

Business record

  • Private/group readings (historic): $50–$175, all to charity per her statement above. She does not generally offer private readings to the public now; access is via live shows, fan-club lottery/chances, and podcast readings.
  • Live tickets: typically ~$40–$200+ by venue and seating; secondary sellers listed 38+ events in 2026. Fan club: $19.99/year.
  • Waitlist: her own documented claim — booked 2 years ahead before TV, with the waitlist then extending for years and no guarantee of an appointment. Consistent across years of reporting.
  • Complaints, lawsuits, regulatory action: none found against Caputo herself in the record searched — no suit, prosecution, FTC/state action, or BBB action reported by a primary source. ("None found" is not proof none exist anywhere.) Documented complaints are audience dissatisfaction, plus the separate, large problem of impersonators defrauding her fans, which she warns against.
  • Beware: third-party "how to book" sites quoting $300–$600 private readings are SEO content farms with no official basis.
  • Her public door (research only — nobody was contacted): official site theresacaputo.com/pages/readings — the route her own warning names as the only one. Fan club via theresacaputofanclub.com. No personal email or phone is published.
Bottom line — Caputo. No controlled test, ever; TV channels open by design; the live record holds both documented miss-streaks and documented specific-feeling private hits. The charity reading model is documented as stated, the anti-scam warnings are active, and there are no legal or regulatory findings against her. Story: cannot verify. Conduct: mixed–positive on the documented record.

The Randi Challenge — and the case against it

James Randi offered a prize (his own $1,000 at first, then $10k, then $1M from 1996, funded by Rick Adams) to anyone demonstrating a paranormal ability under mutually agreed criteria. About 1,000+ applied; none passed the preliminary test. Notable non-events: Sylvia Browne accepted on air in 2001 and never followed through; James Van Praagh was publicly offered it and did not accept.

Proponent-side criticism, printed: the thresholds were very stringent, and Randi's "I always have an out — I'm right" quip is quoted against its fairness. Defenders' reply, printed: protocols were agreed by claimants in advance, and preliminary bars were set to the claimant's own stated success rates. Both characterisations are documented; readers can grade them directly.

Source: Wikipedia — One Million Dollar Paranormal Challenge

The lab positives — and their critics

Windbridge Institute (Julie Beischel, co-founded 2008) runs triple-blind, later up to quintuple-blind, protocols — no sitter contact, proxy sitters, blinded scoring — and reported in 2007 (Explore) and a 2015 replication above-chance anomalous information reception by screened "research mediums." A 2021 meta-analysis (Tressoldi et al.) pooled a small significant effect while noting heterogeneity and publication-bias risk. Critics (Wiseman, Hyman, CSI, mainstream reviewers): small samples, subjective rater scoring of how general statements are, publication concentrated in parapsychology journals, no independent-lab replication. Proponents' reply: the protocols were designed specifically to answer the classic cold-reading and leakage objections.

Gary Schwartz, University of Arizona (The Afterlife Experiments, 2002) reported selected mediums — including John Edward, Suzane Northrop, and Allison DuBois — scoring well above chance in some blinded-matching conditions (figures up to ~83% in supporter retellings). Ray Hyman's critique ("How Not to Test Mediums," Skeptical Inquirer, 2003) and Wiseman & O'Keeffe (2001): key studies weren't properly double-blind, fraud/leakage precautions were inadequate, sitter endorsements weren't independently fact-checked, failures were reinterpreted non-falsifiably. Schwartz replied that Hyman selectively ignored the total body of data; Hyman rebutted. The exchange is fully published. No independent replication followed.

Framing the mainstream position: the 1988 US National Academy of Sciences review found "no scientific justification" for psi from 130 years of research. Small positive meta-analytic effects continue to be published and disputed.

Source: Wikipedia — Mediumship studies

What the controlled record does and does not establish. It establishes that no claimant has demonstrated mediumship or psi under conditions that exclude ordinary channels and satisfied independent scrutiny — a strong, documented negative about demonstration. It does not logically prove every individual reading in uncontrolled settings used ordinary channels — that must be graded case by case, as this page does. And proponents correctly note that absence of a passed test is not a test of every claimant: most people on this roster were never tested at all. Mechanism for the field as a whole: cannot verify. The demonstrated-ability claim: contradicted by the test record.

Part 4 · Superhuman people

The show, and the wider record

The show in question is Stan Lee's Superhumans — History Channel, August 5, 2010 to September 17, 2014, 3 seasons, 31 episodes — hosted by Marvel creator Stan Lee and field-presented by contortionist Daniel Browning Smith, who travelled the world meeting claimants and putting many through on-camera lab checks. Confirmed. One honest caveat: valid, lab-checked segments aired side-by-side with manipulated and exaggerated ones. The show is entertainment, not a journal. (FOX's Superhuman is a different programme — a 2017 studio competition hosted by Kal Penn with a $50,000 prize; it tested skills in a game format rather than investigating claimants.) Sources: Wikipedia — Stan Lee's Superhumans; Common Sense Media — Superhuman

Here the record is different from the mediums': it does contain lab-verified extraordinary abilities — but they are physiological and cognitive, with mechanisms. Filter works here too.

The wider verified record — strongest verification first

Self-published claimants — the social-media era

The standing rule, stated plainly: self-published video alone cannot verify an ability. The claimant controls the editing, the retakes, the camera angles, the confederates or compliant students, and which attempts get posted. Ordinary explanations therefore stay open in every own-footage case, however impressive it looks. Verification requires a third party who controls the conditions. The claims sort into three honest buckets:

(a) Corroborated beyond their own footage

Wim Hof began as a self-promoter; corroboration came only when Radboud scientists controlled the endotoxin trial — and it attaches to the narrow tested claim, not his whole catalogue. Online-taught memory athletes and mental calculators who then entered official competitions (World Memory Championships, Memoriad, adjudicated Guinness attempts): the competition record, not their channels, is the verification. Self-identified super-recognizers recruited via online screening tests (the UNSW Face Test screened ~23,900 people online) who then confirmed in lab batteries: online test = screening only; lab confirmation = verification.

No other social-era claimant found in this research moved from own footage to independent lab corroboration. That empty set is itself the finding.

(b) Resting only on their own footage — cannot verify

"Magnetic people" of TikTok/YouTube (successors to Magola; e.g. Malaysia's Liew Thow Lin, widely filmed sticking irons and spoons to himself, claiming his son inherited it): every demonstration is on their own terms or sympathetic TV; the friction/skin-oil explanation fits every documented case, and none has passed a talc-controlled third-party test.

Chi / energy healers and "empty force" masters demonstrating on their own students: students falling, twitching, or being "knocked out" without contact, on the master's own channel. The compliant-student channel is fully open — expectation, training, social pressure. Students in George Dillman's own school genuinely fell.

Breatharian / inedia influencers posting "day N without food" content: no continuous third-party observation, and posting gaps defeat the claim's own terms. Dangerous — follower deaths are documented in bucket (c).

(c) Tested, and failed, under third-party conditions

No-touch knockouts. George Dillman promoted no-touch and kiai knockouts through books, seminars, and his own videos, where students collapsed. On National Geographic's Is It Real? (September 2005), instructor Leon Jay could not move or knock out skeptical chemist Luigi Garlaschelli at all. Dillman's on-camera explanation — the skeptic was a non-believer, and tongue/toe positions "nullify" the power — is documented verbatim; as stated, it makes the claim unfalsifiable. Same pattern in Japan: Yanagi Ryuken's no-touch demonstrations on students were followed by his defeat when he accepted a bout with a non-student MMA fighter (Iwakura Tsuyoshi, 2006) — the technique produced no effect. Source: Psi Encyclopedia — Martial Arts and Psi

Breatharianism. Jasmuheen (Ellen Greve) claimed to live for months on "prana" and sold the 21-Day Process in Living on Light. Under 60 Minutes Australia monitoring (1999) the test was stopped at day 4 on medical advice: over 10% dehydrated, dilated pupils, slowed speech, pulse roughly doubled, kidney-failure risk. Documented deaths of followers attempting the process: Timo Degen (Germany, 1997), Lani Morris (Australia, 1999), Verity Linn (Scotland, 1999), and a Swiss woman (2012) — coroner: starvation. US breatharian founder Wiley Brooks likewise charged for instruction while his indefinite-fast claims were never observation-tested. Source: Wikipedia — Inedia

Prahlad Jani (1929–2020) — the genuinely puzzling middle case. He claimed 70+ years without food or water. Under hospital/DRDO observation in Ahmedabad (10 days in 2003; 15 days in 2010, CCTV and medical monitoring), the observing doctors reported he ate and drank nothing, passed no urine or stool, and showed no dialysis need. Results layer: a 10–15 day observed fast, including no water, by an elderly, low-activity man sits at the edge of documented physiology, and the doctors' report is on record — printed here plainly as data. Limits layer: the hospital never published peer-reviewed documentation; his weight dropped slightly; rationalist Sanal Edamaruku documented protocol gaps in 2010 (leaving the CCTV field, meeting devotees, leaving the sealed room to sunbathe). The decades-long claim was not tested and can't be inferred from 15 days. Status: short observation documented-but-unpublished and contested; lifetime claim cannot verify. Not debunked, not verified.

The list

Who makes the list — and the standing watch

Anybody can make this list. It doesn't have to be the heads — it can be someone sharing a motivational speech, someone teaching what they know, someone with a strange gift and a phone. Two doors in: we keep an eye out, and the page stands open, so anybody can also come willingly, on their own choice. Nobody has to be discovered. And we look into people only as far as they allow — what's on their own public record, the stuff they put out there themselves. Nothing past their own door. The decision is made from the record: what they actually did with what they're carrying.

On the list from this sweep — the records that check out

  • Laura Lynne Jackson — the roster's most lab-engaged living medium: she actually submitted to blinded testing (Windbridge certification — a documented fact; its scientific validity is contested), and no adverse findings were found against her. Conduct: shows positive.
  • Super-recognizers — a verified, lab-confirmed ability already doing real work: London Met Police super-recognizers were credited with 600+ identifications from 2011 riot CCTV, and they beat controls on authentic CCTV case material in PNAS-published testing.
  • Jo Cameron — verified by genetics and clinical record: she feels almost no pain, anxiety, or fear, and heals fast. Her case is already teaching medicine something real about how pain works.
  • Daniel Browning Smith — seven Guinness flexibility records, mechanism documented (hypermobile Ehlers-Danlos). The ability is real, and he put it to work presenting everyone else's claims on the show.
  • Scott Flansburg — Guinness-verified calculation speed, and his methods are teachable: he wrote them down in books so other people can learn them. Verified skill, shared.
  • Stephen Wiltshire & Daniel Tammet — verified feats performed in the open, checkable by anyone: city panoramas drawn from memory against the real skyline; pi recited to 22,514 places under supervision.
  • Edgar Cayce (historical) — his dated prophecies largely failed, and that's printed above at full size; but he left a complete, checkable archive of 14,306 readings, charged modestly, gave many readings free, and no fraud finding exists. Transparency like that is exactly what a record check can work with.

Being on this list means the documented record supports it — nothing more, nothing less. Grades can move when new evidence lands; that's the point of keeping the record instead of a verdict.

The standing watch — still developing

  • Miroslaw Magola — still active online and, in 2026 posts, still disputing the history of the Randi prize. His objection is noted on the record; it doesn't supply a controlled demonstration.
  • Wim Hof — 2024 domestic-abuse allegations by a former partner, which Hof denies and has contested via a defamation suit. Contested and unresolved in sources: allegation + denial, not a finding.
  • Matt Fraser — Gerbic's team's video analyses of his group readings continue to be published.
  • Breatharian and no-touch content — still actively posted by new claimants; no new third-party corroboration found as of this research.
  • George Dillman — elderly; the no-touch claim's test record has been closed since 2005. His pressure-point (contact) teaching continues commercially — a separate thing from the failed no-touch claim.

New names surface all the time — a show, a viral account, somebody worth a look. They get the same grading when they arrive: results layer and mechanism layer separately, skeptical claims checked like everyone else's, and the ones whose stories check out go on the list.

What could not be verified in this sweep

  • Caputo's charity amounts — recipients are named; totals were unaudited in every source found. Any Caputo management email or phone beyond the official site.
  • Whether any roster medium besides Jackson, Anderson, Edward, Northrop, and DuBois ever entered a lab protocol. None found is not the same as none occurred privately.
  • Hit-rates for any living roster medium — no complete unedited reading archive exists to score, so all hit/miss claims beyond the cited transcripts and stings are anecdote-grade.
  • Segment-level lab data from Stan Lee's Superhumans (the show didn't publish protocols); Concetta Antico's functional tetrachromacy at the cDa29 standard; Prahlad Jani beyond 15 days.
