The Ruler's Test — Nicolás Maduro, President of Venezuela (2013–2026)

Subject: Nicolás Maduro Moros — President of Venezuela from 19 April 2013 until 3 January 2026, when United States forces captured him and his wife, Cilia Flores, in Caracas in a night raid (Operation Absolute Resolve) and flew them to New York to face the March 2020 Southern District of New York indictment charging narco-terrorism conspiracy and related offenses; both pleaded not guilty on 5 January 2026, and the charges remain ALLEGATIONs before a US court, denied by Maduro. His tenure is therefore graded as complete, and it ended by foreign military seizure, not by vote, resignation, or term limit. The Venezuelan Supreme Tribunal of Justice (TSJ) never declared the presidency vacant: it ruled a "forced absence" — a category the constitution does not contain — and swore in Vice President Delcy Rodríguez as acting president on 5 January 2026; both Rodríguez and Maduro assert that he remains the legal officeholder, while Rodríguez governs in practice (sworn in as interim president by the National Assembly on 5 March 2026; recognized as president by Donald Trump on 8 March 2026), under close US oversight, with no election date set as of November 2026. Maduro is held in New York awaiting trial (a June 2027 trial date has been reported; his head-of-state immunity claim is contested — the US has not recognized him as president since 2019). Graded: November 2026, on the documented public record. Method: Each test is graded only on the evidence listed under it. Both shelves are shown — the strongest evidence that he passes, then the strongest evidence that he fails. Allegations are labeled ALLEGATION and never stated as verdicts. Contested items are named as contested, with who disputes them. Seat-holders and public figures are named; people below the public eye appear by role only, unless the documented record itself establishes their wrongdoing. Grades: HOLDS / FAILS / MIXED / NOT ENOUGH EVIDENCE. This record is correctable: new evidence changes grades, in the open.

Test 1 — GIVES BY ONE LAW

Same rules for allies and opponents, insiders and outsiders — no favorites at the source.

GRADE: FAILS

Evidence for:

  • The anti-corruption machinery was, at times, turned on his own camp at senior level. In the PDVSA-Cripto purge beginning March 2023, dozens of officials and business figures were arrested, and Tareck El Aissami — former oil minister and one of the most powerful men in the government — resigned and was arrested on 9 April 2024 on corruption charges (prosecution statements and contemporaneous reporting, 2023–2024). The charges are ALLEGATIONs before Venezuelan courts; the arrests themselves are documented.
  • Negotiated releases did occur under his government when agreements were struck: in December 2023, under the Barbados/Qatar track, Venezuela released ten detained Americans and a group of Venezuelan political prisoners in exchange for the US release of Alex Saab, a Maduro ally (US and Venezuelan official statements, December 2023).
  • The 2015 parliamentary election was administered and its adverse result formally recognized (see Test 3), and opposition candidates won and took governorships and mayoralties in the 2021 regional elections, which EU observers were admitted to monitor (EU Election Observation Mission final report, 2021).

Evidence against:

  • The founding asymmetry of his tenure was set in December 2015: after losing the National Assembly, the outgoing chavista legislature appointed 13 Supreme Tribunal magistrates in a lame-duck session (23 December 2015), and the reconstituted court then suspended opposition deputies, voided Assembly acts, and in March 2017 (rulings 155 and 156) assumed legislative powers itself — partially reversed only after his own attorney general, Luisa Ortega Díaz, publicly called it a rupture of the constitutional order; she was removed by the Constituent Assembly in August 2017 and fled the country (TSJ rulings; Ortega Díaz statements; contemporaneous reporting).
  • The law's sharpest instruments ran one way for a decade. The UN Fact-Finding Mission (FFM) documented a pattern in which opponents, journalists, military dissidents, and ordinary protesters were prosecuted for terrorism, treason, and "incitement to hatred" under the 2017 Anti-Hate Law, while the FFM found no comparable accountability for security-force killings: of the 25 protest deaths of 29–30 July 2024, the FFM concluded state security forces were involved in at least 12, and reported that "to date, all of the deaths remain in impunity," with the Attorney General's Office having initially asserted state agents bore no responsibility and blamed the opposition (UN Fact-Finding Mission reports, September 2024 and September 2025; OHCHR statements).
  • Electoral law was applied as a filter, not a rule. The comptroller's administrative disqualifications — imposed without trial — barred leading opponents across his tenure, culminating in the 15-year ban on María Corina Machado, winner of the October 2023 opposition primary, which the TSJ upheld on 26 January 2024 despite the Barbados Agreement's commitment (see Test 5). Her substitute registration and that of a further substitute were also blocked, leaving Edmundo González Urrutia as the unity candidate (TSJ ruling, 26 January 2024; CNE record).
  • His own legal exposure was managed by the institutions he controlled: the National Assembly and TSJ treated the ICC's Venezuela I investigation as illegitimate (see Test 2), and in December 2025 — in his final weeks in power — the chavista National Assembly took steps to repeal the Rome Statute after the ICC closed its Caracas office (National Assembly proceedings, December 2025; ICC statements). The US narco-terrorism charges against him personally are ALLEGATIONs he denies; what is documented is that no Venezuelan institution ever examined them.

Test 2 — HOLDS WITHOUT GRIPPING

Do the courts, the electoral authority, the central bank, media, and municipalities keep their autonomy — or is independence punished and control centralized?

GRADE: FAILS

Evidence for:

  • Independent institutions were hollowed rather than formally abolished, and remnants bit occasionally: the Carter Center and a UN panel of experts were admitted as observers for the 2024 election and published findings against the government's interest (Carter Center final report, 2025; UN panel interim report, August 2024); independent digital outlets (Efecto Cocuyo, El Pitazo, TalCual, Runrun.es) continued publishing from inside Venezuela throughout his tenure, under blocking and pressure (RSF and Espacio Público records).
  • The central bank and economic authorities were permitted, from 2019, to run a de facto dollarization and price liberalization that contradicted the movement's own doctrine — an operational autonomy conceded by the presidency itself (see Test 3).
  • Opposition officeholders elected in 2021 (governors and mayors) served their terms; the state did not simply annul subnational government, though winning opposition governorships were in past cycles stripped of powers or given parallel "protectors" (documented for 2017 regional winners).

Evidence against:

  • Every referee institution was captured in sequence. The TSJ after December 2015 (Test 1); the National Electoral Council (CNE), whose rectors resigned en masse in June 2023 and were replaced by a board headed by Elvis Amoroso — the same comptroller who had disqualified Machado (National Assembly appointments, August 2023); the Attorney General's Office under Tarek William Saab, which the FFM found complicit in the impunity pattern (FFM, September 2025); and the Ombudsman's Office, named in the same finding. The 2017 National Constituent Assembly, elected in a vote the voting-system vendor Smartmatic said was manipulated (Smartmatic statement, 2 August 2017 — the government disputed it), sat above all other powers and removed the attorney general.
  • The CNE's conduct on 28–29 July 2024 is the grip in its purest documented form: it declared Maduro the winner with 51.2% (first bulletin; 51.95% in the second bulletin of 2 August), alleged a foreign cyberattack, cancelled scheduled audits, and — as of January 2026 — had still never published the polling-station results that Venezuelan law requires, while the TSJ "certified" the result on 22 August 2024 in a process that cited no station-level data (CNE bulletins; TSJ Electoral Chamber ruling, 22 August 2024; Carter Center final report).
  • Media autonomy was dismantled by regulator and market alike: the press workers' union counted 115 outlets closed in 2013–2018 alone (65 radio, 41 print, 9 television), CONATEL took some 20 more radio stations off air around the 2024 election period, major outlets passed to government-friendly owners early in his tenure, dozens of news websites were blocked, and X was blocked from August 2024 (SNTP counts; IPYS and Espacio Público records; RSF ranked Venezuela 159th of 180 in its 2026 index, reflecting the environment his tenure built).
  • The ICC file belongs here as well as under Test 1: Venezuela sought deferral of the Venezuela I investigation in 2022 on complementarity grounds; Pre-Trial Chamber I authorized resumption in June 2023, finding Venezuela was not genuinely investigating the same persons for the same conduct, and the Appeals Chamber confirmed unanimously on 1 March 2024. Prosecutor Karim Khan was later excused from the situation (September 2025, conflict-of-interest ruling concerning his sister-in-law's role on Venezuela's legal team), and on 1 December 2025 the Prosecutor's Office closed its Caracas office, stating there had been no real progress on complementarity — the investigation itself remains active, with no arrest warrants issued as of November 2026 (ICC records, 2021–2025). The government's position — that it cooperated and that the Court is biased — is on the record; the documented procedural findings above are the Court's.

Test 3 — ANSWERS THE WOBBLE

When courts, voters, foreign partners, or events push back, does he adjust openly — or deny and double down?

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for:

  • His single largest adjustment was electoral and it was real: on 6 December 2015 the opposition won a two-thirds National Assembly majority, and Maduro recognized the result on national television the same night ("I recognize these results exactly as they came from the ballot box"), seating the first opposition legislature of the chavista era (presidential address, 6 December 2015). The adjustment was then reversed institutionally over the following fifteen months (Tests 1–2) — the reversal is graded there; the recognition itself happened.
  • On the economy he reversed his own doctrine under pressure and sustained the reversal: from 2019 his government tolerated and then facilitated de facto dollarization, loosened price and exchange controls, and courted private capital — measures that ended the hyperinflation (annual inflation had peaked at an IMF-estimated ~65,000–130,000% in 2018) and produced modest growth from 2021 (IMF and BCV figures; contemporaneous reporting).
  • He negotiated when cornered, repeatedly: the Mexico talks (2021), the Barbados Agreement (October 2023), the December 2023 prisoner exchange, and oil-license pragmatism with Washington under both the Biden and first Trump administrations. Each adjustment was transactional — priced in sanctions relief or detainee exchanges — but each was documented and delivered on his side in part (see Test 5 for the parts that were not).

Evidence against:

  • Every wobble touching his own hold on power was answered by denial and force. The 2016 recall referendum — a constitutional mechanism with the required signatures — was suspended by the CNE in October 2016 on fraud grounds after regional courts annulled signature sheets. The 2017 protest wave (at least 124 deaths documented by the UN) was answered with the Constituent Assembly. The 2019 Guaidó challenge was answered with blackouts-era repression and mass arrests rather than any negotiated election date.
  • The terminal case is 2024. Confronted with the opposition's published tally sheets — actas from 81.7% of polling stations showing González at ~67% to his ~30%, whose authenticity the Carter Center verified and against which it found the announced result statistically impossible — his government produced no station-level evidence of its own, attributed the discrepancy to a cyberattack for which it published no proof, certified the result through its own court, obtained an arrest warrant against González (2 September 2024; he fled to Spain days later), briefly detained Machado after a protest on 9 January 2025, and inaugurated Maduro on 10 January 2025 behind a security cordon (Carter Center final report; FFM reports; contemporaneous reporting). Maduro publicly offered a full recount through the TSJ; the process that followed cited no polling-station data.
  • The repression that followed the vote was, on the FFM's finding, "a systematic, coordinated and deliberate" plan "orchestrated by the highest civilian and military levels of the government, including President Maduro": more than 2,200 arrests admitted by the authorities themselves in the first week, at least 158 children detained, some charged with terrorism, and 25 confirmed protest deaths (FFM press conference, 17 September 2024; FFM reports 2024–2025). Authorities later released 2,006 of the 2,220 detained in the 2024 post-election protests in tranches — while simultaneously carrying out at least 200 new selective detentions of critical voices (FFM, September 2025).

The final wobble: the 2025 pressure campaign and 3 January 2026. The documented sequence: the US designated Tren de Aragua a Foreign Terrorist Organization (February 2025) and Cartel de los Soles — which Washington alleges Maduro heads, an ALLEGATION he denies and whose very existence as an organization is contested by some analysts — a Specially Designated Global Terrorist group (25 July 2025) and then an FTO effective 24 November 2025; doubled the reward for his capture to $50 million (7 August 2025); deployed a large naval force; and from 2 September 2025 conducted lethal strikes on alleged drug boats in the Caribbean and eastern Pacific (43 strikes and 160 deaths by the end of March 2026, per the EU Agency for Asylum's country reporting). In December 2025 the US seized sanctioned oil tankers, announced a blockade of sanctioned shipments, and struck a target inside Venezuela. A Trump–Maduro phone call in late November 2025 was reported at the time as carrying an ultimatum to leave; Maduro did not leave. His answers throughout were mobilization rhetoric, militia enrolment drives, and the documented September–December 2025 wave of 135 further arbitrary arrests (FFM, March 2026). On 3 January 2026 US forces struck targets across northern Venezuela — roughly 80 people were killed, including 32 Cuban security personnel and at least two civilians — and captured Maduro and Flores at their compound (US and Venezuelan official statements; FFM statement, January 2026). The FFM's dual position is on the record and is printed here at full strength: the operation violated international law, and there are reasonable grounds to believe Maduro is responsible for crimes against humanity — the second finding "does not in any way diminish" because of the first (FFM statements, January and March 2026).

Test 4 — COUNTS EVERYONE

Does the accounting include the whole flock — the opposition's voters, the millions who left, political prisoners, the poor — or are some counted out?

GRADE: FAILS

Evidence for:

  • His government maintained universalist social programs on paper and some in fact: the CLAP subsidized-food distribution reached millions of households (government figures; independent surveys found coverage broad but politicized and nutritionally thin — both findings documented), the mission system continued, and the "Vuelta a la Patria" program repatriated Venezuelans who wished to return (government-reported figures, attributed as such).
  • The diaspora was never formally disenfranchised as a class, and in the 2024 election Venezuelans abroad were legally entitled to vote — though in practice only a small fraction could register or vote at consulates, a restriction documented by election monitors and graded on the against-shelf for its effect.
  • After his capture, the machinery he built released people at a pace his government never had: releases began 8 January 2026 under Rodríguez, an amnesty law covering cases back to 1999 passed on 19 February 2026, and Foro Penal verified 1,104 releases during 2026 — a fact recorded here because it measures, by contrast, the count he himself kept (Foro Penal, 2026 balances).

Evidence against:

  • The largest counting-out is demographic: approximately 7.9 million Venezuelans — more than one in four — left the country during his tenure, the largest displacement in Latin America's modern history, driven by an economic contraction of roughly 75% of GDP between 2013/14 and 2021, hyperinflation, collapsing services, and repression (R4V/UNHCR figures, April 2026 update; IMF estimates). Inside the country, the UN estimated 7–7.9 million people in need of humanitarian assistance. A ruler's flock, counted by feet, voted with them.
  • The 2024 electorate was counted out arithmetically: the CNE's announced result reversed the tally-sheet record without publishing evidence (Test 2), meaning the votes of the majority as documented by the state's own actas were never entered into the official count. The voters of the winning candidate then faced the repression documented in Test 3.
  • Political prisoners were a standing population, not an episode: Foro Penal counts 19,257 politically motivated detentions since 2014. The post-election wave pushed its register to 1,916 prisoners by October 2024 (including 70 adolescents); releases came in negotiated tranches while new selective arrests continued (200+ documented in 2025 by the FFM, plus 135 more in September–December 2025). At his capture, human-rights organizations estimated over 800 political prisoners remained; as of 9 November 2026 — ten months after his removal, under a successor government running an amnesty — Foro Penal still counted 321, of whom 171 had never been convicted and 140 were military personnel (Foro Penal balance, 9 November 2026). The successor's remainder is not his grade; the population he built and left is.
  • Specific communities were counted as enemies: the FFM found reasonable grounds to believe the crime against humanity of persecution on political grounds was committed across his tenure, with victims selected for perceived opposition; its December 2025 report found the National Guard committed crimes against humanity over more than a decade under a centralized chain of command overseen by the president as commander-in-chief (FFM report, 11 December 2025). Foreign nationals were held incommunicado as bargaining assets (at least 150 documented by early 2025, per the FFM) — counted, but as currency.
  • The poor who stayed were counted in rhetoric and discounted in delivery: oil production collapsed from roughly 2.4 million barrels/day in 2013 to about 337,000 in mid-2020 before recovering to around 1 million by 2025–2026; public salaries and pensions lost nearly all value in the hyperinflation years; and the recovery after 2021 was real but concentrated, from the bottom of the contraction (IMF/OPEC figures).

Test 5 — KEEPS HIS WORD OVER TIME

Campaign promises and signed commitments vs. the record since 2013, with dates.

GRADE: MIXED

Evidence for (promises kept or credibly in motion):

  • The electoral calendar, as a calendar, was kept: elections were held on schedule throughout his tenure, including the 2024 presidential election in the second half of 2024 exactly as the Barbados Agreement specified, with the Carter Center and UN panel admitted as observers (Barbados Agreement, 17 October 2023; CNE calendar, March 2024). What was done with the count is graded in Tests 2 and 4; the date itself was honored.
  • Parts of Barbados were performed: an electoral calendar was set, international observers were invited (the EU invitation was later revoked in May 2024 after the EU extended sanctions on individuals — a partial performance, stated as such), some political prisoners were released in late 2023, and the December 2023 exchange was executed as agreed.
  • To his own coalition his core promise was kept without deviation: continuity of the chavista project and no negotiated surrender of power — he said in 2013 he would defend the revolution's hold on the state, and for thirteen years, through every crisis, he did. The tests grade what that continuity cost; here it is recorded that the word, to that audience, was good.
  • His June 2024 signature on the CNE's agreement to respect the electoral authority's announced results was, in the narrowest letter, performed: he accepted and defended the CNE's announcement. González, notably, had declined to sign. The commitment's substance — an announcement that could be verified — is where the failure sits, graded below and in Test 2.

Evidence against (promises not kept, or receding):

  • The Barbados Agreement's central term was broken. The agreement guaranteed "the right of each political actor to choose their candidate for the presidential elections freely and in accordance with their internal mechanisms." The opposition chose Machado in a primary (October 2023, ~93% of the vote). Her ban was upheld by the TSJ on 26 January 2024, substitute registrations were blocked, and the United States formally determined on 17 April 2024 that Maduro's representatives "have not fully met the commitments made under the electoral roadmap agreement," letting the oil license lapse (TSJ ruling; US State Department determination, 17 April 2024). The government's position — that the ban predated the agreement and rested on a lawful comptroller sanction — is on the record and is printed here; the commitment's text did not contain that exception.
  • The 2018 re-election program — an "economic recovery, growth and prosperity" plan launched August 2018 — promised stabilization and prosperity; what followed was the deepest phase of the contraction, hyperinflation into 2021, and the loss of a further two million emigrants. The 2024 campaign's prosperity promise ("Venezuela se arregló" — "Venezuela has been fixed") was made over an economy still roughly three-quarters smaller in real terms than the one he inherited, with inflation still in triple digits in 2025 (IMF estimates).
  • His repeated assurance that Venezuela's elections are "the most audited in the world" stands against his own electoral authority's refusal, unique in the region's modern record, to publish the disaggregated results of the decisive one (Carter Center final report).
  • The 2025 inaugural program — constitutional reform and "seven transformations" — was overtaken within the year by the escalation documented in Test 3; he had also assured Venezuelans through 2025 that the country was at peace and sovereign over its decisions, while the FFM documented 135 arbitrary arrests in the final four months of his tenure.

Overall Summary

One law: FAILS · Without gripping: FAILS · The wobble: MIXED · Counts everyone: FAILS · His word: MIXED.

Nicolás Maduro held Venezuela for thirteen years by converting every referee into a member of his own team — a court packed in a lame-duck session, an electoral council that in the end announced a victory it never evidenced, a prosecutor who found impunity for the state's killings and terrorism charges for its critics — and the record shows the conversion, dated and sourced, at each step. The law therefore never applied to him or his at the source; the grip was total over courts, electoral authority, and media; and the count excluded, in the end, the majority of the 2024 electorate as documented by the state's own tally sheets, roughly a quarter of the population who left, and a political-prison population that still numbered in the hundreds ten months after he was gone. Against that stand the adjustments that keep two tests at MIXED: he recognized the 2015 defeat before reversing it institutionally, he abandoned his own economic doctrine to end a hyperinflation, he negotiated and partly performed at Barbados, and he kept the electoral calendar and his word to his own coalition throughout. His tenure was ended not by any mechanism inside the system he built — the system had none left that could reach him — but by a foreign raid that the UN Fact-Finding Mission calls a violation of international law in the same breath as it records reasonable grounds to believe he bears responsibility for crimes against humanity. Both halves of that sentence are the record, and this dossier prints both. The US criminal charges against him are ALLEGATIONs awaiting trial in New York; the ICC investigation remains open; the grades above rest on the documented public record of the tenure itself, and remain correctable in the open.
